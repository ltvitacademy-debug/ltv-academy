# Lesson 23 — Asset Transfers

**Chapter 5 · Transfers and Retirements · Lesson 23 of 33**

## What you'll learn

- What a transfer actually changes, and what it doesn't
- The difference between a location transfer, an employee/assignment transfer, and a distribution transfer
- How transfers interact with multiple distributions from Lesson 11
- Why transfers matter for property tax and physical audit accuracy

## Transfers move an asset's "where," not its "what"

Lesson 20 drew a clean line between reclassification (the category is wrong) and transfer (the location or cost center is wrong, or genuinely changed). A **transfer** moves an asset — physically, organizationally, or both — without changing its cost or its category. The asset itself, its depreciation method, and its life all stay exactly as they were; only where it sits and who's charged for it change.

## Three kinds of transfer

- **Location transfer** — the asset physically moves: a different building, floor, or plant. This directly updates the location flexfield from Lesson 5, and matters for property tax jurisdiction and for physical inventory accuracy.
- **Assignment transfer** — the asset is reassigned to a different employee or custodian, without necessarily moving physically (a laptop reassigned to a new hire after the previous employee leaves).
- **Distribution transfer** — the cost center or expense account charged for the asset's depreciation changes, affecting which part of the organization bears the expense going forward, independent of where the asset physically sits.

An asset can need one of these, two, or all three at once — Meridian Fabrication Co. moving a CNC machine from its Ohio plant to its Texas plant, reassigning it to a new shift supervisor, and shifting its depreciation expense to the Texas plant's cost center, is a single real-world event that touches all three transfer types.

## Transfers and multiple distributions

Recall from Lesson 11 that a single asset can be split across multiple distributions, each with its own percentage allocation to a cost center. A transfer can apply to the **whole asset** or to **one specific distribution** — if only 40% of a shared machine's expense needs to move to a different department while the other 60% stays put, that's a partial distribution transfer, not a full asset transfer.

## Why transfer accuracy matters beyond bookkeeping

Chapter 1 established that location data drives property tax reporting and physical audits. A transfer that's never recorded — the machine physically moved last quarter, but nobody updated Oracle Fusion Assets — means the location on the books no longer matches reality. That's exactly the gap Lesson 5's physical inventory process is designed to catch, and exactly why transfers need to be entered promptly, not caught up in a batch months later.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Location transfer | Updating an asset's location flexfield to reflect where it physically moved |
| Assignment transfer | Reassigning an asset to a different employee or custodian |
| Distribution transfer | Changing which cost center or expense account bears an asset's depreciation expense |
