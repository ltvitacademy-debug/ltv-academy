# Lesson 16 — Capitalization and Placing Assets in Service · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Not every asset is ready to depreciate the day it's added. This lesson covers construction in process, and the moment that actually changes.

## S2 · STEPS — CIP assets accumulate cost

A CIP asset is added like any other, but flagged as under construction. As invoices come in — progress billings, equipment, labor — costs pile onto the same CIP record instead of creating separate assets.

## S3 · STEPS — CIP never depreciates

No matter how much cost has accumulated or how long it's been sitting there, a CIP asset doesn't depreciate while it's CIP. Depreciation on a half-finished asset wouldn't mean anything — it isn't producing benefit yet.

## S4 · CODE — Capitalizing the asset

Meridian's paint booth accumulates three hundred ten thousand dollars from January through March. It's capitalized April first — that date, not the invoice dates, is what starts the depreciation clock, following a fifteen-year straight-line life.

## S5 · STEPS — Why the distinction matters

If CIP assets depreciated during construction, expense would be overstated while the asset isn't even usable, and understated once it actually is. Holding depreciation until capitalization matches expense to actual use.
