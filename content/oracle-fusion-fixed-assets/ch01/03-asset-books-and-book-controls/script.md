# Lesson 3 — Asset Books and Book Controls · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Every asset you'll ever add belongs to a book. This lesson covers what an asset book actually controls, and the three kinds you'll encounter.

## S2 · STEPS — A container, not a label

An asset book determines which ledger an asset's accounting lands in, which calendar its depreciation follows, and which default rules it inherits. It's not cosmetic — it's the container everything else sits inside.

## S3 · STEPS — Three book classes

Corporate books are primary, used for financial statement reporting — every asset starts here. Tax books depreciate the same assets differently for tax purposes, populated by mass copy, never entered by hand. Budget books forecast future spending without touching real books at all.

## S4 · STEPS — What's fixed at the book level

Several controls are set once, at the book level, for every asset that will ever belong to it: the ledger and chart of accounts, the depreciation calendar, whether mass changes are allowed, and default natural accounts for cost, clearing, and depreciation expense.

## S5 · STEPS — Get it right before adding anything

Categories, depreciation methods, mass additions — everything in this course happens inside a book. Get the calendar or the ledger wrong at setup, and every asset added afterward inherits that mistake.
