# Lesson 4 — Asset Categories and Category Books · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Why doesn't every new asset need its depreciation method typed in by hand? Because of categories. This lesson covers how they're structured and where their defaults actually live.

## S2 · STEPS — Categories group similar assets

An asset category groups assets with the same accounting treatment, built from segments — typically a major category and a minor category, like Computer Equipment and Laptops. One category, one set of depreciation rules to maintain.

## S3 · STEPS — Configured per book, not globally

Here's the detail that trips people up: a category is configured once per asset book, not once for the company. The combination is called a category book, and it's the category book — not the category alone — that carries the actual default rules.

## S4 · STEPS — What a category book defaults

When someone picks a category while adding an asset, the category book hands over the depreciation method, the useful life, the prorate convention, and the default GL accounts — cost, clearing, depreciation expense, accumulated depreciation, gain and loss.

## S5 · STEPS — Designing categories well

Too coarse, and you lose the ability to apply different rules where you need them. Too granular, and setup becomes a burden with no accounting benefit. Group by how assets are actually depreciated and accounted for, not by warehouse convenience.
