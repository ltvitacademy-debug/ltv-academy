# Lesson 20 — Reclassifying Assets · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Sometimes nothing about the physical asset is wrong — just what category it's filed under. This lesson covers reclassification.

## S2 · STEPS — What reclassification changes

Reclassification corrects what category an asset belongs to, with no change to cost and usually no change to location. It's a different transaction than a cost adjustment or a transfer.

## S3 · STEPS — Why category alone changes depreciation

Category books carry the default life and method. Move an asset from a 10-year category to a 7-year one, and its going-forward depreciation changes even though nothing physical moved — and that can trigger the same kind of catch-up calculation as a life correction.

## S4 · STEPS — Telling the three apart

Cost adjustment: the dollar amount is wrong. Reclassification: the category is wrong. Transfer: the location or cost center is wrong. An asset can need more than one in sequence, but each is tracked as its own distinct transaction.

## S5 · CODE — Working the example

Meridian's forty-thousand-dollar tooling, reclassified from a ten-year Machinery category to a five-year Tooling and Dies category after two years. The corrected rate is eight thousand a year; two years should have taken sixteen thousand instead of the sixty-four hundred actually taken — a catch-up of ninety-six hundred dollars.
