# Lesson 19 — Cost Adjustments · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

An asset's cost isn't always final on day one. This lesson covers what actually changes when that cost gets corrected.

## S2 · STEPS — When cost needs to change

A late freight invoice, an uncovered warranty repair, a vendor credit, a billing error caught after the fact — any of these can increase or decrease an asset's recorded cost after it's already in the system.

## S3 · STEPS — Increases versus decreases

A cost increase grows the depreciable basis and can trigger catch-up depreciation for what should have been taken on the higher cost all along. A cost decrease shrinks the basis and can mean the asset was over-depreciated relative to the corrected, lower cost.

## S4 · CODE — Working the increase

Meridian's hundred-twenty-thousand-dollar CNC machine gets a fifteen-thousand-dollar cost adjustment for a late installation invoice. The depreciable basis grows to one hundred thirty-five thousand, and the annual rate recalculates from there.

## S5 · STEPS — Timing and thresholds matter

Many implementations put a window or approval threshold around cost adjustments. An adjustment made years late, after most of the asset's life has already depreciated, produces a much bigger, messier catch-up than one caught early.
