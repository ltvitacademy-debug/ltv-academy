# Lesson 22 — Impairment · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Depreciation plans for value loss. This lesson covers what happens when something unplanned makes an asset worth less than its books say.

## S2 · STEPS — What triggers impairment

Technological obsolescence, physical damage, a discontinued product line, a market shift — any indicator that an asset's carrying value no longer reflects reality triggers a recoverable amount test, under the IAS 36 framework.

## S3 · STEPS — Recoverable amount

Recoverable amount is the higher of two figures: fair value less costs to sell, or value in use — the present value of future cash flows if kept. If it's less than carrying amount, the asset is impaired.

## S4 · CODE — Working the loss

Meridian's packaging line has a two-hundred-eighty-thousand-dollar carrying amount. Its major customer closed, and its recoverable amount is now one hundred ninety thousand. The gap — ninety thousand dollars — is the impairment loss.

## S5 · STEPS — Impairment versus its neighbors

Depreciation is planned and routine. Revaluation is a deliberate policy restatement, up or down. Impairment is unplanned, indicator-driven, and almost always a loss at the time it's recorded.
