## Reports answer the questions this course has been building toward

Nearly every lesson in this course has implicitly raised a question a report exists to answer. Rather than introducing reports as an afterthought, it helps to connect them directly back to what you've already learned:

- **Asset additions report** — every asset added in a period, tying straight back to Chapter 3's addition and mass additions work. Useful for confirming nothing fell through the cracks during a busy posting cycle.
- **Asset cost detail / summary report** — an asset's cost history, including every adjustment from Lesson 19 and every reclassification from Lesson 20, at the individual asset or category-summary level.
- **Reserve ledger report** — accumulated depreciation by asset, connecting straight to Lesson 17's depreciation runs and Lesson 18's catch-up adjustments.
- **CIP report** — every construction-in-process asset still uncapitalized, exactly the population Lesson 16 described as not yet depreciating.
- **Fully reserved assets report** — assets whose net book value has reached zero (or salvage value), relevant to Lesson 26's point about fully depreciated assets at retirement.
- **Retirements report** — every retirement in a period with its gain or loss, the output of Lesson 26's calculation made visible across the whole asset population at once.

## Depreciation projections: the forward-looking exception

Every report above describes something that has **already happened**. The **depreciation projection report** is different: it forecasts future depreciation for a book, category, or individual asset, based on current setup, without actually running or committing anything. This is genuinely useful for budgeting — Meridian Fabrication Co. planning next year's expense budget can project depreciation for its existing asset base before a single period of next year has actually occurred, entirely separate from the budget books mentioned back in Lesson 3.

## Choosing the right report for the question

The practical skill isn't memorizing every report's name — it's recognizing which question you're actually asking and picking the report built to answer it:

- "Did everything I posted this period actually show up?" → Asset additions report.
- "Why did this asset's cost change over time?" → Asset cost detail.
- "How much accumulated depreciation does this category carry?" → Reserve ledger.
- "What's still sitting uncapitalized that shouldn't be?" → CIP report.
- "What will next year's depreciation expense look like?" → Depreciation projection.

## Why reports, not raw screens, drive review

Reviewing hundreds or thousands of individual asset records one at a time isn't how period-end review actually works in practice. Reports aggregate and filter exactly what a reviewer needs to confirm, which is why Lesson 17's exception reports, Lesson 14's mass addition queues, and this lesson's standard reports are really all variations on the same idea: structured output built for a specific review question, not a raw dump of every field on every asset.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reserve ledger | A report of accumulated depreciation by asset, tied to depreciation runs and adjustments |
| CIP report | A report listing assets still in construction-in-process status |
| Depreciation projection | A forward-looking report forecasting future depreciation without committing anything |
