# Lesson 28 — Data Quality Issue Management · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Lesson twenty-eight is the record-keeping layer underneath everything else in this chapter — turning a detected problem into a tracked, closeable issue.

## S2 · STEPS — WHY TRACK, NOT JUST MENTION

An alert fires, or someone notices a red tile. If the only record is a Slack message, it has no owner, no priority, no due date, and no way to check months later whether it actually got fixed.

## S3 · STEPS — WHAT AN ISSUE RECORD NEEDS

A well-formed issue carries a specific description, the affected table or field, what detected it, a severity tied to agreed thresholds, an owner, a root cause once it's known, a status, and a due date.

## S4 · STEPS — THE SIX-STAGE LIFECYCLE

Six stages: new, detected but not looked at; triaged, severity and owner assigned; in progress, root cause and remediation underway; resolved, a fix applied; verified, the original check re-run and confirmed passing; and closed.

## S5 · STEPS — CLOSING THE LOOP

This is what ties the chapter together: monitoring detects and creates the issue, root cause analysis fills in the cause, remediation moves it to resolved, and the cycle only closes once it's actually verified.

## S6 · STEPS — PRIORITIZING WHAT TO FIX FIRST

When there are more open issues than capacity, prioritize by business impact, by blast radius — how many downstream reports a root cause touches — by severity against your thresholds, and sometimes by fixability, to reduce total open issues fast.
