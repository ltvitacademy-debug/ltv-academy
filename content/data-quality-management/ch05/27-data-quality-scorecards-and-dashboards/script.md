# Lesson 27 — Data Quality Scorecards and Dashboards · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Lesson twenty-seven turns Lesson twenty-six's monitoring history into something a stakeholder can read in five seconds — a scorecard.

## S2 · STEPS — FOUR QUESTIONS A SCORECARD ANSWERS

A good scorecard answers four questions for every metric: what's the current value, is that good or bad against a threshold, is it getting better or worse, and who actually owns it when it's at risk.

## S3 · SCREENSHOT — SCORECARDS HUB

Here's a real scorecard interface — Power BI's own Goals feature. Each tile shows a current value, a target, a trend line, and a status pill, all at a glance. It's a general business-goals tool, not built specifically for data quality, but the pattern is exactly what a quality scorecard needs.

## S4 · SCREENSHOT — GOAL EDIT PANE

Opening one goal shows the fields underneath it: current value, final target, and status. Swap "profitability percentage" for "completeness pass rate" and these are the exact fields a data quality metric needs too.

## S5 · SCREENSHOT — GOALS LIST

A scorecard tool also needs to organize many metrics at once — here, a list of scorecards with an owner column for each. That owner column is what turns a red status into an actual next step instead of a dead end.

## S6 · STEPS — SCORECARD VS DASHBOARD

A scorecard answers "are we on track." A dashboard answers "what's actually happening" — breakdowns by table, by rule, by owner, drill-through to the failing rows. A healthy program has both: the five-second check, and the detail underneath it.
