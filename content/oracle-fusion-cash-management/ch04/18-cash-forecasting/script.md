# Script — Cash Forecasting

## Segment 1 (title)

Cash Positioning answers what do I have right now. Cash Forecasting answers a harder question: what should I expect over the weeks and months ahead. This lesson covers the two main approaches Oracle supports.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Transaction based forecasting uses things that already exist in Oracle but haven't settled yet — open Payables invoices not yet due, open Receivables invoices not yet collected. It answers: based on what we already know is coming, what does cash look like? Trend based forecasting analyzes past actual cash flows and projects that trend forward — useful for categories like retail cash sales that don't show up as a clean open transaction but follow a predictable pattern. Most real forecasts blend both.

## Segment 3 (steps)

A cash forecast template defines the structure: which rows, meaning sources of cash flow, and which columns, meaning time buckets like daily, weekly or monthly. A company might run a tight 30-day operational forecast and a separate 12-month strategic forecast from the same underlying data, structured differently.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Oracle supports forecasting in any currency, across different organizations, for multiple time periods — so a single forecast can roll up subsidiaries in different currencies into one consolidated view. But a forecast is a planning tool, not a guarantee. Open transactions can settle late. Trends can break when the future doesn't look like the past.

## Segment 5 (outro)

A fictional example: Harborview Metals Inc builds a 13-week forecast blending open Payables and Receivables with a trend-based miscellaneous cash sales row, consolidated across its US and UK entities into one USD total for the CFO's weekly review.
