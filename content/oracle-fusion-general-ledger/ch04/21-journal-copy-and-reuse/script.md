# Script — Journal Copy and Reuse

## Segment 1 (title)

Chapters seventeen through twenty built real automation. But not every repeat entry deserves it — some journals are only loosely similar to last month's. For those, Copy Journal is the simpler, manual tool.

## Segment 2 (code)

Here's the pattern: a posted February journal, debiting marketing expense and crediting accrued liabilities for thirty-four hundred dollars. Copy it, and you get a new draft with the same lines and amounts — then you edit the date to March and bump the amount to thirty-six hundred to match this month's actual estimate, before completing it as a brand-new journal.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Here's what matters: the copy starts back at square one. Status resets to Incomplete no matter what the original's status was. It has to balance, it has to pass validation against whatever period and accounts you actually land on, and it routes through approval exactly like any other journal. Copying saves retyping — it doesn't skip a single control.

## Segment 4 (steps)

So which tool fits which situation? Exact same accounts and amount every period — standard recurring. Same accounts, amount typed by a person — skeleton. Amount derivable from the ledger itself — formula. One pool split across many targets — allocation. Similar to a past entry but irregular — copy journal.

## Segment 5 (outro)

That's the full toolkit for automating — or deliberately not automating — repeat journal work. Chapter five picks up from here with balances, inquiries, and monitoring: account balances and inquiries, lesson twenty-two.
