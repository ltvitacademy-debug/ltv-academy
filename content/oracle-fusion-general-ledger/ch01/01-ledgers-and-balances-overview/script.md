# Script — Ledgers and Balances Overview

## Segment 1 (title)

Welcome to Oracle Fusion General Ledger. Over the next thirty-seven lessons you'll work hands-on in the General Ledger module: journals, posting, approvals, allocations, multi-currency, and period close. We start with the object everything else sits on top of: the ledger.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Every ledger in Oracle Fusion is defined by four things, sometimes called the four C's: a chart of accounts, an accounting calendar, a currency, and an accounting method. Once you save the ledger, the chart of accounts, calendar, and currency are locked for good. Only the accounting method can still change later.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Most of this course lives inside a primary ledger, the ledger your financial statements come from. A secondary ledger is a separate, independent ledger sharing the same transactions, often used when statutory books need a different chart of accounts. A reporting currency just restates the primary ledger's balances in another currency for reporting — it isn't an independent set of books.

## Segment 4 (code)

Throughout this course we'll use a fictional example company, Solara Fixtures, Incorporated, a mid-size lighting manufacturer. Its primary ledger uses a chart of accounts with company, cost center, account, and intercompany segments, a standard monthly calendar, and U.S. dollars as its functional currency. Every example in this course is illustrative, built around that fictional company.

## Segment 5 (outro)

One more structure worth knowing: a ledger set groups several ledgers that share a chart of accounts and calendar, so you can post journals or close periods across all of them at once. Next up, lesson two: how General Ledger actually stores and summarizes balances.
