# Account Monitor

## From looking something up to watching it continuously

Lesson 22 covered the Inquire on Detail Balances page — a deliberate, one-time lookup: you decide to check an account, you go check it. **Account Monitor** flips that. Instead of checking accounts when you remember to, you define a watchlist of accounts you care about once, set a **tolerance** for each one, and the page keeps watching for you, continuously, as journals post throughout the month.

Think of it as the difference between glancing at your fuel gauge when you happen to look down, versus a dashboard warning light that comes on by itself when the tank gets low. Account Monitor is the warning light for your ledger.

## Setting up a watchlist with tolerances

A user builds Account Monitor by selecting accounts (or ranges of accounts, using parent values) and defining a **tolerance rule** — a threshold that, when crossed, flags the account visually. A tolerance can be defined against:

- A fixed amount (flag if the balance exceeds $50,000)
- A percentage change versus budget
- A percentage or amount change versus the same period last year
- PTD, QTD, or YTD activity, depending on what you're watching for

For **LTV Manufacturing Corporation**, a controller might set up a tolerance on the Travel & Entertainment expense account: flag if PTD actual exceeds 110% of PTD budget. The moment enough journals post in a period to cross that line, the account shows up flagged — without anyone having to go inquire on it.

## Where you'll find it

Account Monitor is surfaced in two places: the **General Accounting Dashboard**, where it's typically one panel among several a user keeps an eye on throughout the day, and the **Financial Reporting Center**, alongside other reporting tools. It updates in near real time as journals post, at every level of the chart of accounts hierarchy — a flagged condition on a detail account can roll up and surface at the cost-center or division level too, depending on how a user has it configured.

## Why this changes how accountants work

Before a tool like this, catching a budget overrun or an unusual balance meant someone remembering to run a report and noticing the number looked off — easy to miss in a busy month. Account Monitor turns that reactive habit into a proactive one: tolerances are defined once, and flags appear on their own. It is also the natural entry point into deeper analysis — a flagged account in Account Monitor is exactly what you'd want to click into next, which is precisely what lesson 24's Account Inspector is built to do.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Account Monitor | A self-updating watchlist of accounts with user-defined tolerances |
| Tolerance rule | A threshold (amount or percentage) that triggers a visual flag when crossed |
| General Accounting Dashboard | A landing page where Account Monitor commonly appears as a panel |

## Recap

Account Monitor replaces one-off inquiries with a continuously updated watchlist: define the accounts and the tolerances once, and the system flags deviations as journals post, at any level of the chart of accounts hierarchy.
