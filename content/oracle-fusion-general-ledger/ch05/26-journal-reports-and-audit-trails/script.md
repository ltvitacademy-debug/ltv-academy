# Script — Journal Reports and Audit Trails

## Segment 1 (title)

A trial balance tells you what the ending balance is. It doesn't tell you which journals built it, or who was responsible for them. That's the gap journal reports and audit trails fill.

## Segment 2 (steps)

The Journal Entries Report lists entries for a ledger and period in order, with source, category, and batch. The Account Analysis Report shows every journal line that touched one specific account. And sequence-based reports confirm no document number in a range is missing.

## Segment 3 (code)

If Utilities Expense at LTV Manufacturing Corporation looked high for March, the Account Analysis Report is where a reviewer goes: every journal line posted to that account that period, in one place, ready to judge whether each one is legitimate.

## Segment 4 (steps)

A report shows what posted. An audit trail shows who did what, and when: who created the journal, who approved it, whether it was edited, who ultimately posted it. That history is retained separately from the financial content itself.

## Segment 5 (steps)

Journal reports and audit trails answer two different halves of the same question. One proves the accounting ties out. The other proves the process that produced it was actually followed.

## Segment 6 (outro)

External auditors want both: numbers that tie out, and a documented trail showing how they got there. Up next, lesson 27: drilling down to subledger detail, connecting these same journal lines back to the original transaction.
