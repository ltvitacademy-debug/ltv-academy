# Script — Account Inspector

## Segment 1 (title)

Account Monitor flagged Travel and Entertainment as over tolerance. Flagging isn't explaining. Account Inspector is the tool you open next to find out why, and it's fully integrated with Account Monitor so you carry the same account straight into it.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Where a plain balance inquiry gives you one number, Account Inspector behaves like an online pivot table. Take a flagged total and slice it by cost center, department, or project, by any segment in the chart of accounts.

## Segment 3 (code)

Here's the difference it makes. A flat balance says Travel and Entertainment is $16,940, over tolerance. Pivot that same number by cost center in Account Inspector, and $11,200 of it sits in Sales alone. The question changes from why is T&E high, to what happened in Sales in March.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Account Inspector isn't just a grid. You can view the same data as a chart, and drill in either direction: from a parent value down a level, or from a child balance straight through to journal lines and subledger transactions.

## Segment 5 (steps)

Three tools, three questions. Inquire on Detail Balances: I know the account, give me the number. Account Monitor: tell me automatically when something crosses a line. Account Inspector: something's flagged, let me slice it to find the real source.

## Segment 6 (outro)

All three read the same underlying balance data, just shaped for a different question. Up next, lesson 25: Trial Balance Reports, the formal report that pulls these balances together for review.
