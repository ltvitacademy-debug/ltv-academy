# Script — Period-End Revaluation

## Segment 1 (title)

Last lesson left a gap open: an invoice booked at one rate, still unpaid when the rate moves. Revaluation is the process that closes that gap, restating foreign-currency balances to what they're worth today.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Revaluation is performed on account balances, not individual transactions, and only on balance sheet accounts holding a foreign-currency balance: payables, receivables, foreign bank accounts. It never touches income statement accounts, which stay booked at their historical rate.

## Segment 3 (code)

At LTV Manufacturing Corporation's March close, that GBP invoice is still unpaid. The rate moved from 1.27 to 1.31. Revaluation recalculates: originally booked at 10,795 dollars, now worth 11,135, an unrealized loss of 340 dollars.

## Segment 4 (steps)

That revaluation journal typically reverses automatically next period. It's a temporary adjustment to a point-in-time balance, not a permanent restatement. Once the invoice is actually paid, the real realized gain or loss gets booked instead.

## Segment 5 (steps)

That's the key distinction: unrealized gain or loss is a paper adjustment that reverses. Realized gain or loss is the actual amount booked when a transaction finally settles.

## Segment 6 (outro)

Revaluation has to run before translation, which restates an entire ledger into another currency for group reporting. Up next, lesson 30: Translation.
