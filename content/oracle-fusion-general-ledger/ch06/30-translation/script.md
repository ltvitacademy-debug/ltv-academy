# Script — Translation

## Segment 1 (title)

Revaluation corrected specific foreign-currency balances inside a ledger. Translation is different: it restates an entire ledger's balances into another currency, for a parent that needs a subsidiary's books expressed in its own reporting currency.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Picture LTV Manufacturing UK Ltd, keeping its books correctly in GBP. The US parent reports in USD. Before the UK numbers can combine with the US numbers, every GBP balance has to become a USD figure, without touching the GBP books themselves.

## Segment 3 (code)

Translation uses two different rates. Balance sheet accounts translate at the period-end spot rate, a snapshot. Income statement accounts translate at the period average rate, since that activity accumulated all period. Here's UK Ltd's cash and sales revenue, each translated at its own rate.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Because two different rates are used, the translated numbers don't naturally balance on their own. That gap gets captured in a dedicated equity account: the Cumulative Translation Adjustment, or CTA.

## Segment 5 (steps)

The CTA isn't an error. It's the expected plug that makes a translated balance sheet balance, and it accumulates in equity over time, never running through the income statement.

## Segment 6 (outro)

The critical point: translation never changes the UK ledger's actual GBP books. It produces a separate USD view, specifically for consolidation. Up next, lesson 31: Intercompany Transactions.
