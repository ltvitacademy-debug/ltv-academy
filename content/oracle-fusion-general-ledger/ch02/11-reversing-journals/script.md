# Script — Reversing Journals

## Segment 1 (title)

We just established that a posted journal's lines can never be edited directly. So how do you undo one? General Ledger creates a reversal — a brand-new journal that cancels the original out.

## Segment 2 (code)

Take our office supplies accrual from lesson six: debit office supplies expense, credit accrued liabilities, twelve-fifty each. The reversal flips it exactly: debit accrued liabilities, credit office supplies expense, same twelve-fifty. Post that, and the original's effect is gone.

## Segment 3 (steps)

There are two ways to flip the amounts. Switch debit and credit is the common one — every debit becomes a credit and vice versa, same accounts. Change sign keeps each line's designation but makes the amount negative instead. Both cancel the original out; which one a company uses is usually a standing configuration choice.

## Segment 4 (steps)

You also choose which period the reversal lands in: the same period, if you caught a mistake before close; the next period, the standard pattern for an accrual; or a specific period if the correction genuinely belongs somewhere else.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Here's the pattern that matters most: when you create an accrual, you can flag it to reverse automatically once next period opens. Book the accrual in March, flag it auto-reverse, and the moment the real invoice posts through Payables in April, the accrual's effect is already cleanly backed out — no one has to remember to do it by hand.
