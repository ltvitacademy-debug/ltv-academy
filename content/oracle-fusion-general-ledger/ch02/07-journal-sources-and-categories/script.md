# Script — Journal Sources and Categories

## Segment 1 (title)

Last lesson, source defaulted quietly to Manual and we moved on. This lesson, source and category get the attention they deserve — because in a real environment, most journals never touch a human hand.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Source identifies where a journal came from. Manual is typed in directly. Spreadsheet comes from an uploaded template. Payables, Receivables, and Fixed Assets are generated automatically by subledger accounting rules. Allocations, revaluation, and translation each generate their own source too. This matters later — approval rules and auto-post criteria both key off source.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Category identifies why a journal exists, not where it came from. Adjustment corrects a balance. Accrual records an expense or revenue before the invoice exists. Allocation redistributes a cost pool. Reclassification moves an amount between accounts. And every single line in a journal has to share the same category — you can't mix an accrual line with a reclassification line in one journal.

## Segment 4 (code)

Put the two together and a reviewer can read a journal before opening a single line. Manual plus Accrual means a person recorded an accrual by hand. Payables plus Standard means routine invoice accounting from accounts payable. Allocation plus Allocation means a mass allocation run generated it automatically.

## Segment 5 (outro)

That source and category pair is exactly what chapter three's auto-post criteria and suspense account rules target — not "all journals," but a specific combination. Next up, lesson eight: journal batches and journal lines.
