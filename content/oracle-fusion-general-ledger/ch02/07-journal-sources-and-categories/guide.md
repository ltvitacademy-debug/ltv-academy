# Lesson 7 — Journal Sources and Categories

## What you'll learn

- Why the source/category combination matters for later lessons on approval, auto-post, and suspense accounts
- How to tell, just by looking at a journal, roughly where it came from and why it exists

## Source: where a journal came from

A **journal entry source** identifies the system or process that created the journal. **Manual** is the source you used in Lesson 6, but most journals in a mature Oracle Fusion environment never touch a human hand:

| Source (example) | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Manual | Typed directly into Create Journal |
| Spreadsheet | Uploaded through the Create Journals in Spreadsheet template |
| Payables / Receivables / Fixed Assets | Generated automatically by subledger accounting rules when those subledgers' transactions are transferred to GL |
| Other predefined sources | Allocations, Revaluation, Translation — each generated by the General Ledger process that produced them |

Sources matter beyond labeling. Chapter 3's auto-post criteria and Chapter 6's revaluation and translation both key off source, and approval rules (also Chapter 3) are commonly scoped to specific sources — for example, requiring approval on Manual journals above a dollar threshold while letting system-generated Payables journals post automatically.

## Category: why a journal exists

A **journal entry category** identifies the *business purpose* of the entry — not where it came from, but what it's for. Common categories include:

- **Adjustment** — a correction to a previously recorded balance
- **Accrual** — recording an expense or revenue before the related invoice exists
- **Allocation** — redistributing a cost or revenue pool (Chapter 4)
- **Reclassification** — moving an amount from one account to another without changing the total
- **Standard** — routine, day-to-day entries that don't fit a more specific category

**Every line in a journal entry must share the same category.** You cannot mix an Accrual line with a Reclassification line in the same journal — if you need both purposes, you create two separate journals.

## Reading a journal at a glance

Put source and category together and a reviewer can tell a lot about a journal before opening a single line:

```
Source: Manual        Category: Accrual        → a person recorded an accrual by hand
Source: Payables      Category: Standard        → routine invoice accounting from AP
Source: Allocation     Category: Allocation      → generated by a mass allocation run
```

This is exactly the kind of pattern Chapter 3's auto-post criteria sets and suspense account rules rely on — they target a specific source/category pair, not just "all journals."

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Journal source | Identifies which system or process created the journal (Manual, Payables, Allocation, …) |
| Journal category | Identifies the business purpose of the entry (Accrual, Adjustment, Reclassification, …) |
