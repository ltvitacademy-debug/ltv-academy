# Lesson 6 — Creating a Manual Journal

## Six fields every journal needs

Whichever way a journal reaches General Ledger — typed by hand, imported, or generated by a subledger — it always carries the same six pieces of header information:

| Field | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Ledger** | Which ledger the journal posts into |
| **Journal Name** | A short, searchable description of the batch |
| **Accounting Date** | Which period the journal posts to (the date's period must be Open or Future Enterable) |
| **Source** | Where the journal originated — defaults to **Manual** when you're typing it in by hand (Lesson 7 covers sources in depth) |
| **Category** | The business purpose of the entry — Adjustment, Accrual, and so on (also Lesson 7) |
| **Currency** | The currency the amounts are entered in |

## Creating a journal, step by step

From the **Journals** work area, **Create Journal** opens a blank header. After the six fields above, you add one or more **journal lines**, each carrying:

- An account combination (every chart-of-accounts segment filled in)
- A **debit** or **credit** amount (never both on the same line)
- An optional line description

```
Journal: Solara Fixtures — March Office Supplies Accrual
Ledger: Solara Fixtures US     Accounting Date: 31-Mar-2026
Source: Manual                 Category: Accrual        Currency: USD

Line 1   01.300.6410.000   Office Supplies Expense   Debit   1,250.00
Line 2   01.300.2210.000   Accrued Liabilities        Credit            1,250.00
```

This fictional entry records $1,250 of March office supplies that haven't been invoiced yet — a classic accrual, which you'll revisit in Chapter 4's recurring journals.

## A journal must balance

General Ledger will not let you mark a journal **complete** unless its total debits equal its total credits, for every balancing segment value represented in the journal (recall from Lesson 5 that Company is Solara's balancing segment). If Solara's Company 01 lines don't net to zero against Company 01, and Company 02's lines don't net to zero against Company 02, the journal stays incomplete no matter how the grand total looks.

## Save versus submit

- **Save** stores the journal as a draft — you can keep editing it, and nobody else sees it as ready for review.
- **Complete / Submit** marks the journal ready to move forward — either straight to posting (if no approval is required) or into the approval workflow covered in Chapter 3.

A journal that fails validation (unbalanced, invalid account, closed period) cannot be completed — Lesson 9 walks through exactly what those errors look like and how to fix them.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Journal header | The six required fields: ledger, name, date, source, category, currency |
| Journal line | One account combination plus a debit or credit amount |
| Complete | Marking a journal ready to post or route for approval |
