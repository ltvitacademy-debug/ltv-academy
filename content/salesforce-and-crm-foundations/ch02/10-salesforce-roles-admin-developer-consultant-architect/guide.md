## Four roles, one ecosystem

Chapters 1 and 2 built toward this lesson on purpose: the CRM concepts
from Chapter 1, the clouds and platform/application split from this
chapter, and AppExchange's partner ecosystem all show up directly in
how Salesforce careers are organized. Most job titles in this space
fall under four broad roles.

## Administrator

The **administrator** (often shortened to "admin") configures and
maintains a Salesforce org for the business using it day to day:
creating users, managing security and permissions, building
automation with point-and-click tools, and customizing the standard
applications from Lesson 7 — mostly without writing code. This is
squarely "customizing a pre-built application," the administrator side
of the platform/application split from Lesson 8, and it's where this
course's hands-on work starts, beginning in Chapter 4.

## Developer

The **developer** builds what point-and-click configuration can't:
custom logic, integrations with other systems, and entirely new
applications built directly on the Salesforce Platform (Lesson 8),
using Salesforce's own programming languages and tools. Where an admin
customizes what's already there, a developer builds something new.

## Consultant

The **consultant** typically works for a partner firm (Lesson 9),
helping a client business figure out *what* to build before anyone
configures or codes anything: gathering requirements, mapping business
processes from Chapter 1 onto Salesforce's data model, and managing
the implementation project itself. A consultant leans on both admin
and developer skills, but the job is centered on the client
relationship and the business problem, not the technical build alone.

## Architect

The **architect** is the most senior, most technical role: designing
how a large, complex Salesforce implementation fits together — data
model, security, integrations with other enterprise systems, and
performance — especially for organizations running many connected
Salesforce products at once. Architects are typically former
developers or consultants who've specialized deeply after years of
hands-on experience.

## How the roles relate, not compete

These aren't competing job titles for the same work — they're
different altitudes on the same ecosystem. A typical implementation
touches all four: a consultant scopes the project, an architect
designs how it fits together, a developer builds the custom pieces,
and an administrator maintains it afterward. Plenty of Salesforce
professionals move between these roles over a career, often starting
as an administrator — which is exactly where this course picks up,
starting in Chapter 3.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Administrator | Configures and maintains a Salesforce org, mostly without code |
| Developer | Builds custom logic, integrations, and new applications on the platform |
| Consultant | Gathers requirements and manages implementations, usually for a partner firm |
| Architect | Designs how a large, complex implementation fits together |
