# Lesson 6 — A Brief History of Salesforce

## Founded on a bet against installed software

Salesforce was founded in 1999 by Marc Benioff, a former Oracle
executive, along with Parker Harris, Dave Moellenhoff, and Frank
Dominguez. At the time, CRM meant software like Siebel Systems:
expensive licenses, long implementation projects, and software
physically installed on a company's own servers.

Salesforce's bet was the opposite: deliver CRM entirely through a web
browser, hosted on Salesforce's own servers, sold as a subscription
instead of a license. The company's early slogan, often shown as a
red circle-and-slash over the word "Software," captured the pitch
directly — no installation, no hardware to maintain, no IT department
required just to get started.

## Why "No Software" mattered

This model — now universally called **SaaS**, Software as a Service —
is the default way almost all business software is sold today. In
1999, it wasn't. Betting an entire CRM company on it was a genuine
risk, and it's the single biggest reason Salesforce could undercut
established, installed CRM vendors on both cost and time-to-value: a
business could sign up and start using Salesforce in days, not the
months or years a traditional implementation required.

## From one product to a platform

Salesforce didn't stay a single CRM product for long. Key moments that
shaped where the company is today:

- **2005 — AppExchange launches**, turning Salesforce into a
  marketplace other companies could build and sell on top of (Lesson 9)
- **2007 — Force.com** opens the underlying platform for building
  custom applications that aren't CRM at all, formalizing the
  platform/application split covered in Lesson 8
- **Acquisitions build out the "clouds"** — Salesforce grew Marketing
  Cloud, Commerce Cloud, and other product lines partly through major
  acquisitions (ExactTarget for marketing, Demandware for commerce,
  Tableau for analytics, Slack for collaboration) rather than building
  every product line from scratch
- **Lightning Experience** — a ground-up redesign of the user
  interface, replacing the older "Salesforce Classic" look most
  screenshots from this course reflect today

## Why this history is worth knowing

None of this is trivia for its own sake. "No Software" explains why
Salesforce orgs are managed through configuration and setup screens
rather than server installations — a theme Chapter 3 covers directly.
The AppExchange and Force.com milestones explain why Salesforce isn't
just one product today, but a platform with an entire ecosystem of
clouds, partners, and specialized roles built around it — exactly what
the rest of this chapter unpacks.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| SaaS | Software as a Service — software delivered over the internet, hosted by the vendor, sold by subscription |
| Force.com | Salesforce's underlying platform for building custom applications, launched in 2007 |
| Lightning Experience | Salesforce's modern user interface, which replaced the older "Classic" interface |
