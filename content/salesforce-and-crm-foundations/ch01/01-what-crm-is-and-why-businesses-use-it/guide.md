# Lesson 1 — What CRM Is and Why Businesses Use It

**Chapter 1 · CRM Fundamentals · Lesson 1 of 20**

## What you'll learn

- What "CRM" actually means, beyond the acronym
- The core problem CRM solves for a growing business
- What a business looks like without one, concretely
- Why this course starts with the concept, not the software

## The acronym, unpacked

CRM stands for Customer Relationship Management. As software, it's a
shared system of record for every interaction a business has with a
prospect or customer — who they are, what they've bought, every call
and email, every open issue. As a practice, it's the discipline of
actually using that system instead of letting the information scatter
across inboxes, spreadsheets, and someone's memory.

Both halves matter. The software without the discipline is just an
expensive address book nobody updates. The discipline without the
software doesn't scale past a handful of customers.

## The problem it solves

Picture a small business before CRM exists: a sales rep tracks leads
in a personal spreadsheet, the owner's inbox holds the only record of
what a customer complained about last month, and nobody can answer
"how many deals are we likely to close this quarter" without asking
around. That works, barely, at ten customers. It breaks completely at
a thousand.

Concretely, without a shared system:

- **Leads go cold** because no one follows up — the only record of
  interest lived in one rep's head or inbox
- **Customers repeat themselves** to every new person they talk to,
  because nothing from the last conversation carried over
- **Deals get double-worked**, or dropped entirely, when a rep is out
  sick and nobody else can see where things stood
- **Leadership can't forecast** revenue, because "what's likely to
  close" exists only as a gut feeling, not a number anyone can total up

## What CRM actually buys a business

A CRM platform turns all of that into shared, structured data: every
account, every contact, every deal, and every service case lives in
one place, visible to whoever needs it, with a history attached. That
turns individual effort into organizational memory — the business
keeps what any one person knew, even after that person moves on to a
different deal, a different team, or a different company.

## Why this course starts here

Salesforce is the CRM platform this course eventually teaches in
detail, starting in Chapter 3. But the concept comes first deliberately:
every button, object, and setting Salesforce ships is in service of
this same underlying job — track relationships, make that tracking
shared, and make it useful. Understanding the job first makes the tool
make sense, instead of memorizing clicks with no idea what they're for.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CRM | Customer Relationship Management — a shared system (and practice) for tracking every interaction with prospects and customers |
| System of record | The one place a business trusts as the accurate, current version of a piece of information |
| Lead | A person or organization who has shown interest, but hasn't yet become a customer |
