# Lesson 2 — The Customer Lifecycle: Marketing, Sales, Service

**Chapter 1 · CRM Fundamentals · Lesson 2 of 20**

## What you'll learn

- The three stages every customer moves through: marketing, sales, service
- What each stage is actually responsible for
- Why the handoffs between stages are where CRM earns its keep
- How this maps to what you'll build hands-on starting in Chapter 3

## Three stages, one relationship

Every customer relationship — whether it's a $20 subscription or a
seven-figure enterprise contract — moves through the same three broad
stages:

1. **Marketing** finds and warms up people who might become customers
2. **Sales** turns an interested prospect into a signed customer
3. **Service** keeps that customer successful, and gets them to stay

Three different teams, three different jobs, but one continuous
relationship. That continuity is exactly what a shared CRM system is
for — without it, each team starts over from zero.

## Marketing: finding and warming up interest

Marketing's job is to generate awareness and interest before anyone
has talked to a human. That's ads, content, email campaigns, events,
and the company website. When someone fills out a form, downloads a
guide, or attends a webinar, marketing has produced a **lead** — a
record of someone who has shown interest but hasn't been qualified as
a real sales opportunity yet.

Marketing's handoff to sales is a judgment call, not a free-for-all:
not every lead is ready for a sales conversation, and a rep calling
someone who filled out a form by accident wastes everyone's time. Most
organizations define what makes a lead "sales-ready" before it gets
handed over.

## Sales: turning interest into a signed deal

Sales takes a qualified lead and works it toward a signed contract.
That means conversations, demos, proposals, pricing, and negotiation —
tracked as an **opportunity**, a specific potential deal with a dollar
value and an expected close date. A rep's whole job, in CRM terms, is
moving an opportunity through defined stages until it's won (or
recording why it was lost).

## Service: keeping the customer, after the sale

The relationship doesn't end at the signature — arguably, it starts
there. Service handles support questions, technical issues, renewals,
and anything that determines whether a customer stays or churns. In
CRM terms, this is usually tracked as a **case**: a specific question
or problem, from open to resolved.

A service team that doesn't know the customer's purchase history, past
issues, or who their original sales rep was ends up making customers
repeat their own story — one of the exact problems Lesson 1 described.

## Why the handoffs matter most

Each stage, on its own, isn't hard to imagine as a separate tool: a
marketing email platform, a sales spreadsheet, a support ticket queue.
What's hard — and what CRM is actually built to solve — is keeping one
continuous, trustworthy record as a person moves from stranger, to
lead, to opportunity, to customer, to a case in the support queue.
That continuity is the entire point of putting marketing, sales, and
service data in one shared system instead of three disconnected ones.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Lead | A person who has shown interest but hasn't been qualified as a sales opportunity |
| Opportunity | A specific potential deal, tracked with a dollar value and expected close date |
| Case | A specific customer question or problem, tracked from open to resolved |
| Churn | A customer who leaves or doesn't renew |
