# Lesson 6 — Manual Sharing

**Chapter 1 · Sharing Building Blocks · Lesson 6 of 24**

## What you'll learn

- When the Sharing button actually appears on a record, and why it's invisible for some objects in some orgs
- What a manual share actually is underneath the UI — a row in the object's Share table with a specific RowCause
- Why manual shares disappear automatically under certain configuration changes, and why that's a feature, not a bug
- Where manual sharing fits (and doesn't fit) in an architect's toolkit relative to the other four classic mechanisms

## When the Sharing button shows up at all

Manual sharing is the only mechanism in this chapter that a user triggers by hand, one record at a time, through the **Sharing** button on a record's detail page. That button, though, doesn't always appear — it only shows up when there's actually something left to grant. If OWD for an object is Public Read/Write, every user already has full access to every record, so there's nothing a manual share could add, and Salesforce simply doesn't show the option. Manual sharing becomes relevant specifically when OWD is Private or Public Read Only, because those are the settings that leave a real gap between what a user can see by default and what the record's owner might want a specific person to see.

Opening the Sharing detail page and clicking **Add** lets the record owner (or anyone with sufficient access) grant a specific user, public group, role, or role-and-subordinates an access level of Read Only or Read/Write on that one record, optionally with the ability for that recipient to also re-share it further with others. This is the most granular of the five classic mechanisms — narrower even than a team, since it has no concept of a reusable membership list or a role on the record, just a flat grant to a chosen recipient.

## What a manual share actually is

Underneath the UI, every sharing grant on an object — manual or otherwise — lives in that object's companion **Share** object (for example, `Opportunity` has `OpportunityShare`). Each row records who the access was granted to, what access level, and critically, a **RowCause** field identifying why the row exists. A manual share carries `RowCause = 'Manual'`. A share generated by a sharing rule carries a RowCause pointing at that rule; a share generated by a role hierarchy grant or a team doesn't appear as an explicit row in quite the same way, but the Apex-managed-sharing mechanism covered in Lesson 8 uses this exact same Share-object pattern with a custom RowCause of its own. Understanding this shared underlying structure is what makes Lesson 8 click later: Apex managed sharing isn't a different system from manual sharing, it's the same `__Share` object mechanism, just written to programmatically with a reason the declarative UI doesn't expose.

## Why manual shares disappear automatically

A manual share is tied to a specific record's specific ownership context, and Salesforce removes manual shares automatically in two situations: when the record's owner changes (the previous owner's manual grants no longer make sense once someone else owns the record), and when organization-wide defaults are widened to the point that the manual share becomes redundant (if OWD moves to Public Read/Write, every existing Private-era manual share is cleaned up, since it no longer adds anything). This is deliberate, not a bug to work around — it keeps the Share tables from accumulating permanently stale, meaningless rows every time ownership or defaults shift. An architect reviewing an org's sharing design should never assume a manual share, once granted, stays in force forever; it's explicitly scoped to the ownership and OWD context it was created under.

## Where manual sharing fits in the toolkit

Manual sharing is the right answer exactly once: a genuinely one-off, ad hoc grant that doesn't fit a repeatable pattern — "the owner of this one deal wants to loop in a specific colleague on this one record, right now, with no expectation this will happen again for other records." The moment that pattern starts repeating (the same two roles need manual shares on records like this regularly), it's a signal the requirement has outgrown manual sharing and belongs in a sharing rule, a team, or Apex managed sharing instead. Architecturally, manual sharing scales to exactly one user's judgment call at a time; it was never meant to be the backbone of a sharing design, and a review that finds manual sharing doing structural, repeatable work is a review that's about to recommend replacing it with something declarative.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Manual sharing | A one-off, record-specific grant made by hand through the Sharing button, available only when OWD leaves a visibility gap to fill |
| `<Object>Share` | The companion object underlying every sharing grant on a given object, holding one row per grant with an access level and a RowCause |
| RowCause | The field on a Share object row identifying why the grant exists — `Manual` for manual shares, a sharing rule's name for rule-based shares, or a custom reason for Apex managed shares |
| Automatic removal | Salesforce's behavior of deleting manual shares when a record's owner changes or when OWD is widened past the point the share adds anything |
