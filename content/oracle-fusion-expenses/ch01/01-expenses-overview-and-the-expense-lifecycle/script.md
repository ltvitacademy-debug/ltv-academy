# Script — Expenses Overview and the Expense Lifecycle

## Segment 1 (title)

Welcome to Oracle Fusion Expenses, the seventh course in the Oracle Fusion Financials Consultant path. You've already built General Ledger, Payables, Receivables, Cash Management and Fixed Assets. Expenses is the module almost every employee touches directly. Let's start with what it does and the lifecycle every expense report follows.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Employees spend the company's money constantly: hotels, client dinners, supplies, conference fees. Somebody has to collect that spending, check it against policy, get it approved, pay it back, and post the right accounting entries. Doing that on spreadsheets does not scale. Oracle Fusion Expenses automates the whole chain, and we'll follow one fictional company, Castellan Supply Co., through this course.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Every expense report moves through the same six stages. Incur: the employee spends money. Capture: they record it in Expenses. Submit: they assemble items into a report and send it in. Audit and approve: rules run automatically and a manager reviews it.

## Segment 4 (steps)

The last two stages. Account: once approved, Expenses builds the accounting distributions. Reimburse: Expenses hands off to Payables, which actually pays the employee or the card issuer. Expenses never pays anyone directly — it's a feeder system, the same way a Payables invoice feeds a payment.

## Segment 5 (steps)

Expenses leans on setup from courses you've already finished. Business units determine which policies apply to which employees. The chart of accounts and General Ledger receive the final entries through Subledger Accounting. Payables performs the actual reimbursement. Four groups of people live in this module day to day: employees, approvers, expense auditors, and the Payables staff watching the handoff.

## Segment 6 (outro)

Keep that six-stage lifecycle in your head: incur, capture, submit, audit and approve, account, reimburse. Every lesson in this course maps onto one of those stages. Up next, lesson two: everything a consultant configures before a single employee can submit a report.
