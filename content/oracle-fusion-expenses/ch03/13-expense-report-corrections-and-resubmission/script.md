# Script — Expense Report Corrections and Resubmission

## Segment 1 (title)

Not every report sails cleanly through audit and approval. This lesson closes Chapter 3 by covering what happens when a report gets sent back, and the practical difference between a rejection, a request for more information, and a correction after the fact.

## Segment 2 (steps)

When a report is rejected, it returns to the employee in an editable state with the reason attached. Nothing has posted yet, since rejection happens before accounting. The employee can edit any item and resubmit - and resubmission restarts audit and approval from the beginning. A rejected-then-resubmitted report isn't assumed fine just because it was reviewed once already.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Request more information is lighter. It pauses the report and asks a specific question - attach the itemized folio, clarify the business purpose - rather than reopening full editing. The employee responds, often just by attaching a document, and the report resumes where it paused instead of restarting the whole sequence. Castellan reserves full rejection for when something is actually wrong, not just unclear.

## Segment 4 (steps)

The harder case: a report that's already been approved, accounted, or even paid, and someone finds an error. Nobody simply edits a posted transaction. If it's accounted but not paid, the invoice goes on hold in Payables while the correction gets worked. If it's already paid, the original entry gets reversed through Subledger Accounting and a corrected item runs through its own mini-lifecycle.

## Segment 5 (code)

Here's a duplicate-item correction. The original eight twenty-five hotel item was paid. A duplicate of the same amount got paid in error on the same trip. The correction deducts eight twenty-five from the employee's next approved expense report, with a note explaining why.

## Segment 6 (outro)

Every correction preserves a full audit trail - what was submitted, what was flagged, what changed, and why - rather than silently overwriting history. A transaction already posted to the General Ledger is reversed and replaced, never quietly edited, the same discipline you've seen in Payables and General Ledger. That closes Chapter 3.
