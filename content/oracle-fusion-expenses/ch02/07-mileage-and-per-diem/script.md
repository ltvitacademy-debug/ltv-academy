# Script — Mileage and Per Diem

## Segment 1 (title)

Most expense types are entered as a receipt amount. Mileage and per diem are different - they're calculated from a rate schedule, because driving your own car or eating while traveling doesn't produce one clean receipt the way a hotel folio does.

## Segment 2 (steps)

When an employee drives their own car, Expenses doesn't reimburse the price of gas - it reimburses a per-mile rate covering fuel, wear, insurance, and depreciation together, the same logic as a government standard mileage rate. A rate schedule can be flat, or vary by vehicle category, number of passengers, or role. Castellan uses one flat rate company-wide, reviewed annually.

## Segment 3 (code)

Here's a mileage claim at Castellan's standard rate: sixty-seven cents a mile. Thirty-eight miles round trip from the regional office to a client site comes out to twenty-five forty-six. No receipt required - there's nothing to photograph. The rate schedule itself is the policy enforcement.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Per diem replaces itemized meal and incidental receipts with one fixed daily allowance tied to destination and dates. Instead of three meal receipts a day for four days, Priya claims one daily amount with no receipts needed. Rate schedules are built by city or country, and Oracle supports meal deductions - if a conference provides breakfast, that day's per diem gets reduced so the company doesn't pay twice.

## Segment 5 (code)

Here's Priya's per diem for her four-day Chicago trip. Full-day rate: seventy-four dollars. Day one, a travel day, pays seventy-five percent. Days two and three pay the full rate. Day four has breakfast provided, so it's reduced by eighteen dollars before the partial-day percentage applies.

## Segment 6 (outro)

A company picks per diem or itemized meals per business unit or trip type, never mixed for the same trip. Castellan uses per diem domestically, since it's simpler to audit, and itemized receipts internationally, where costs vary too widely for one schedule.
