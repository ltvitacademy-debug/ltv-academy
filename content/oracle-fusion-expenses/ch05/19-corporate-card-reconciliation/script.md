# Script — Corporate Card Reconciliation

## Segment 1 (title)

This final lesson closes the loop on corporate cards from lesson nine. Reconciliation is the ongoing discipline of making sure every dollar the card issuer bills Castellan is accounted for somewhere - attached to an approved report, actively in process, or flagged as a genuine exception.

## Segment 2 (code)

Reconciliation compares three numbers that should converge over time. What the issuer billed on its statement. What arrived in Expenses as transactions. And what's actually been attached to submitted, approved reports. Here, the issuer statement and the feed match at one eighty-four thousand six twenty, but only one seventy-nine three ten has made it into approved reports - a five thousand three hundred dollar gap worth investigating.

## Segment 3 (steps)

A consultant sets up a repeatable monthly routine. Pull the Corporate Card Transactions Report for the period. Identify transactions still unassigned past the thirty-day aging threshold. Cross-reference those against the employee-level audit list, since repeat unassigned-transaction offenders often overlap with repeat missing-receipt employees - the same underlying habit of deferring documentation.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Escalate aged, unassigned transactions to the employee's manager with a specific deadline, rather than letting Finance chase indefinitely. And for employees who've left the company with unassigned transactions still outstanding, route to HR and Finance offboarding, since a departed employee can't submit a report after exiting.

## Segment 5 (steps)

Three common exceptions come up. A disputed charge routes to the card issuer's dispute process, outside Expenses entirely. A duplicate feed transaction is a data issue worked with the issuer's technical contact, not an employee error. A terminated employee's unassigned balance gets resolved through payroll offset or direct collection, the same mechanism used for cash advance overages.

## Segment 6 (outro)

A persistent gap signals an operational problem, not a one-off error.
