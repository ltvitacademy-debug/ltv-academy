# Script — Expense Reports and Analysis

## Segment 1 (title)

Lesson fifteen traced one item at a time. Finance leaders also need to look at spending in aggregate - across a department, a quarter, a whole company - to spot trends and enforce policy at scale. Let's look at Oracle's built-in reporting tools for exactly that.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Oracle Fusion Expenses ships several prebuilt reports ready to use immediately. The Expense Audit Report lists every report placed on the audit list, why, and the outcome. Expenses by Business Unit or Cost Center gives aggregated totals against budget. The Corporate Card Transactions Report reconciles card feed data against what's actually been attached to reports. Payment Status reports, shared with Payables, show what's paid, pending, or on hold.

## Segment 3 (steps)

OTBI, Oracle Transactional Business Intelligence, lets a power user build their own analysis on live data without waiting for IT. Instead of a fixed seeded report, someone drags in exactly the dimensions they need - expense type, employee, cost center, date range, policy flag - and crosses them however the question demands.

## Segment 4 (code)

Here's a question no seeded report could answer: total client entertainment spend by region for Q1, limited to items flagged over-policy, broken out by whether each one was waived or sent to audit. OTBI can answer that because it's built on the same underlying data Expenses and Subledger Accounting already captured.

## Segment 5 (steps)

Castellan asks questions like this regularly. Which employees generated the most missing-receipt declarations last quarter? Is average hotel spend trending up faster than the policy limit has been adjusted - a sign the policy itself needs revisiting? What percentage of card transactions get attached within seven days versus sitting unassigned past thirty?

## Segment 6 (outro)

None of this works if expense types were set up sloppily back in chapter one. Miscoded client meals quietly undercount real entertainment spend in every report and every OTBI analysis built on top of them. Reporting can't fix bad classification - it can only reveal, loudly, that it exists. Up next, lesson seventeen: a hands-on troubleshooting lesson pulling together this chapter and the last.
