# Script — Following Revenue into General Ledger

## Segment 1 (title)

The journal entries from last lesson exist inside Subledger Accounting, tied to our transactions. They're not yet sitting in the General Ledger as something a GL user could see on a trial balance. Let's cover that last mechanical step.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Getting there runs through Create Accounting, in two stages. First, create final accounting in Subledger Accounting - taking draft events and finalizing them into complete, balanced entries. Second, transfer those finalized entries to General Ledger and post them, making them part of the period's balances.

## Segment 3 (steps)

These two stages can run together or separately. Some organizations transfer and post immediately; others batch it at set intervals. That's one reason a transaction's accounting can be fully complete in Receivables before it's actually visible in GL.

## Segment 4 (steps)

There's a real difference between draft and final accounting. Draft is a preview - useful for review, but it can't post. Only final accounting is eligible to transfer. A consultant looking at what a transaction posted as needs to know which one they're actually looking at - they can look identical and not be the same thing.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Once Create Accounting runs for our invoice, credit memo, and receipt, each one lands in the receivable, revenue, and cash accounts from the chart of accounts you set up earlier in this path. Up next, lesson twenty-two: reconciling this entire order end to end.
