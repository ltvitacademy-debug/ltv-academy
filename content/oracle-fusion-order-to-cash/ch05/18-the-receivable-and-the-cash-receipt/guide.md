# The Receivable and the Cash Receipt

SO-48217's invoice exists, corrected for the AutoInvoice rejection and reduced by the $2,755.00 credit memo, leaving Harborview owing $52,345.00, due in 30 days. Until that money arrives, LTV Manufacturing is holding a **receivable** — a promise of future cash, not cash itself. This lesson looks at what that open receivable represents and introduces the document that will eventually close it: the cash receipt.

## What you'll learn

- What it means, financially, for a receivable to be "open"
- What aging is, and why it matters for this specific invoice
- What a cash receipt is, as a quick refresher from Accounts Receivable and Cash Management
- What has to happen for SO-48217's $52,345.00 receivable to actually close

## An open receivable is a claim, not cash

From the moment SO-48217's invoice posts, LTV Manufacturing's books reflect that Harborview owes $52,345.00 — but that balance is a legal and accounting claim, not money in a bank account. The company has already given up the physical inventory (depleted back in Chapter 3) and recognized revenue for it (covered in the next lesson), while the actual cash is still 30 days away, assuming Harborview pays on time. This gap between recognizing the sale and receiving the cash is exactly why Order-to-Cash has an entire chapter after billing — the invoice is not the finish line.

## Aging

Every open invoice has an **age** — how long it's been outstanding relative to its due date. Receivables tracks this through aging buckets (current, 1–30 days past due, 31–60, and so on), which collections teams use to prioritize follow-up. SO-48217's invoice, due 30 days from its transaction date, is "current" until that due date passes; if Harborview were late, the invoice would move into an aging bucket and likely trigger a collections process, which is outside this course's scope but is exactly the kind of signal that fed back into the credit check discussed in lesson 8.

## The cash receipt, briefly revisited

You already know, from Accounts Receivable and Cash Management, that a **cash receipt** is the record of money actually arriving from a customer — a specific amount, a specific date, a specific payment method, and eventually an application to one or more open invoices. This course isn't re-teaching receipt classes or methods from scratch. What matters here is the specific link: for SO-48217's receivable to close, a receipt for $52,345.00 (or some combination of receipts) has to arrive and be applied specifically against this invoice, which is where lesson 19 picks up.

## Recap

An open receivable is a claim to future cash, not cash itself, and it carries an age relative to its due date that collections teams track. SO-48217 shows a current, $52,345.00 open balance, due in 30 days. Closing it requires a cash receipt to arrive and be applied to this specific invoice.
