# Script — Applying Cash

## Segment 1 (title)

Twenty-eight days after the invoice date, Harborview pays. Let's look at how that payment actually gets recorded and matched to the right invoice.

## Segment 2 (steps)

A cash receipt involves two separate actions. Creating it records that money arrived - amount, date, method, remitter. Applying it decides which open invoice that money pays off. These can happen together, or a receipt can sit on account, created but not yet applied.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Harborview pays by mailing a check to a bank lockbox, like most of LTV Manufacturing's customers. Lockbox processing handles this at scale: the bank scans checks and remittance data and sends it over as a file. That file gets imported, validated, and used to create and automatically apply receipts, using the invoice number and amount the customer referenced.

## Segment 4 (steps)

When it's not perfectly clean - wrong invoice number, a slightly different amount, one check covering several invoices - the receipt still gets created, the cash is recorded, but Receivables generates recommended matches for a person to review instead of applying automatically. An unapplied receipt isn't a sign something's broken; it's routine collections work.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Harborview's check references our invoice exactly, for the exact fifty-two thousand, three hundred forty-five dollars. Lockbox creates the receipt and applies it automatically, bringing the balance to zero. Up next, lesson twenty: the actual accounting entries this whole cycle generates.
