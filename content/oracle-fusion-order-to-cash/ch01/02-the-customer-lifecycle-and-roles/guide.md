# The Customer Lifecycle and Roles

A sales order does not move itself through Order Management, Shipping, Receivables, and Cash Management. At each stage, a specific role in the business is responsible for an action, and a specific Oracle Fusion screen or process is where that action happens. Before you look at any single transaction in detail, it helps to see the whole lifecycle laid out as stages, with the people and the systems attached to each one.

## What you'll learn

- The six stages of the Order-to-Cash customer lifecycle
- Which business role is typically responsible for each stage
- Which Oracle Fusion area each role works in
- Why knowing "who owns this stage" is the fastest way to troubleshoot a stuck transaction

## The six stages

1. **Order capture** — a customer's request becomes a sales order with agreed items, quantities, and price. Owned by order administration or inside sales, working in Order Management.
2. **Order validation** — the order is checked against credit limits, approval rules, and any holds before it is allowed to proceed. Owned by credit and collections analysts, and sometimes a manager approving in Approval Management (AME).
3. **Fulfillment** — the warehouse picks, packs, and ships the physical goods. Owned by warehouse and shipping staff, working in Inventory Management and Shipping.
4. **Billing** — the shipped order becomes a Receivables invoice. Owned by a billing specialist, working in Receivables, usually through the AutoInvoice process rather than typing invoices by hand.
5. **Collection** — the customer's payment arrives and is matched to the invoice. Owned by a cash applications or collections team, working in Receivables and Cash Management.
6. **Reconciliation** — the accounting generated by the whole cycle is verified against the General Ledger. Owned by an accountant, working in Subledger Accounting and General Ledger.

## Why roles matter to a consultant

It is tempting to think of Order-to-Cash purely as a sequence of system steps. In practice, every handoff between stages is also a handoff between people, and most real-world O2C problems happen at those handoffs: a warehouse worker ships a quantity the sales order didn't authorize, a billing specialist runs AutoInvoice before an order's fulfillment step is complete, or a collections analyst applies a payment to the wrong invoice. As a functional consultant, when something goes wrong, your first useful question is rarely "which table is this field stored in." It is "whose stage was this transaction in when it broke, and what does their screen show them right now."

## One company, one order

Starting next lesson, you'll follow a single sales order, placed by a single fictional customer, through every one of these six stages. Keeping one concrete example in view will make it much easier to see how a credit hold on the order screen becomes a delayed shipment, and how a shipment becomes an invoice line, and how an invoice becomes a journal entry you can trace all the way into the General Ledger.

## Recap

Order-to-Cash moves through six stages — order capture, validation, fulfillment, billing, collection, and reconciliation — and each stage has an owner and a home screen. Most real O2C problems live at the handoffs between stages and between people, not inside any single screen.
