# Script — Following One Transaction: The Plan

## Segment 1 (title)

Reading about six abstract lifecycle stages only goes so far. For the rest of this course, we're going to follow one concrete sales order, from one fictional company to one fictional customer, through every stage of Order-to-Cash. This lesson sets that example up.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Meet the cast. LTV Manufacturing Corporation: a fictional mid-sized manufacturer of industrial control equipment, based in Savannah, Georgia. You'll see this company again in later courses, including the final capstone. And Harborview Industrial Supply: a fictional distributor in Charlotte, North Carolina, an existing customer with an established account.

## Segment 3 (code)

Here's the transaction we'll follow. Order SO-48217: four hundred units of a Model CP-220 Control Panel, list price one hundred forty-five dollars each, fifty-eight thousand dollars total. A five percent volume discount brings it to fifty-five thousand, one hundred dollars, net thirty terms, shipping from Savannah to Harborview's warehouse in Charlotte.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Here's the roadmap. Chapter two: the order gets created, priced, and picks up a credit hold that has to be released. Chapter three: the warehouse ships it, and we look at a damaged-goods return. Chapter four: the shipment becomes an invoice, including what happens when that doesn't go cleanly. Chapter five: Harborview pays, and we trace the accounting into the General Ledger. Chapter six: we review the whole journey and test your judgment on new scenarios.

## Segment 5 (outro)

From here on, "the order" means SO-48217. Up next, chapter two, lesson five: customers and sales orders, the record types that make up this transaction.
