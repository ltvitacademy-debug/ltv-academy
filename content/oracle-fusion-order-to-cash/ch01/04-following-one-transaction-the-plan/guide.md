# Following One Transaction: The Plan

Reading about six abstract lifecycle stages only goes so far. For the rest of this course, you'll follow one concrete sales order, from one fictional company to one fictional customer, through every stage of Order-to-Cash: capture, validation, fulfillment, billing, collection, and reconciliation. This lesson sets up that example so later lessons can simply refer back to it.

**A note on the example:** LTV Manufacturing Corporation, Harborview Industrial Supply, and every order number, item, price, and dollar figure below are fictional, created for this course. They are not drawn from any real company's records.

## What you'll learn

- The fictional company and customer this course follows
- The specific order you'll track through every remaining chapter
- The roadmap of what happens to that order in each chapter ahead

## The company and the customer

**LTV Manufacturing Corporation** is our fictional Oracle Fusion customer — a mid-sized manufacturer of industrial control equipment, headquartered with a distribution center in Savannah, Georgia. This is the same company you'll meet again in later courses in this path, including the final capstone, so the setup you learn here will still matter then.

**Harborview Industrial Supply** is a fictional distributor based in Charlotte, North Carolina, and an existing customer of LTV Manufacturing with an established account and credit history.

## The transaction

On a Monday morning, Harborview's buyer calls in an order:

- **Order:** SO-48217
- **Item:** Model CP-220 Control Panel
- **Quantity:** 400 units
- **List price:** $145.00 per unit ($58,000.00 total)
- **Negotiated discount:** 5% volume discount, bringing the order to $55,100.00
- **Payment terms:** Net 30
- **Ship-from:** LTV Manufacturing's Savannah, GA distribution center
- **Ship-to:** Harborview's Charlotte, NC warehouse

## The roadmap

- **Chapter 2 (Customers and Orders):** SO-48217 gets created, priced with its discount, and — because of its size — picks up a credit check hold that has to be reviewed and released before it can proceed.
- **Chapter 3 (Fulfillment and Shipping):** the Savannah warehouse picks, packs, and ships the 400 units, and we'll also look at what happens when a smaller, separate shipment to Harborview comes back damaged.
- **Chapter 4 (Billing):** the shipment becomes a Receivables invoice, including a look at what happens when that process doesn't go cleanly on the first try.
- **Chapter 5 (Cash and Ledger):** Harborview pays, the cash is applied, and we trace the resulting journal entries all the way into the General Ledger.
- **Chapter 6 (Practice):** the whole journey gets reviewed end to end, plus new exception scenarios to test your judgment.

## Recap

From here forward, "the order" means SO-48217, from LTV Manufacturing Corporation to Harborview Industrial Supply: 400 units of Model CP-220 Control Panels, net $55,100.00 after discount, Net 30. Every remaining lesson in this course builds on this one transaction. Next up, Chapter 2 begins with lesson 5: customers and sales orders, the record types that make up SO-48217.
