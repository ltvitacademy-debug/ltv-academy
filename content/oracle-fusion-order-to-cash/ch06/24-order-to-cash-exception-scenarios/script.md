# Script — Order-to-Cash Exception Scenarios

## Segment 1 (title)

SO-48217 hit exactly two exceptions. Real Order-to-Cash work has many more kinds. Let's walk through four new, independent scenarios to broaden what you can recognize.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Scenario one: the short shipment. A customer orders five hundred units; the warehouse can only pick four hundred sixty. Fusion invoices what actually shipped, not what was ordered. The remaining forty either back-order for later or need a decision from the customer.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Scenario two: a price override. A salesperson wants fifteen percent off, well beyond the standard automatic discount. That's not an automatic rule anymore - it's a manual override that needs a real approval before the order can proceed, unlike a credit hold the system applies on its own.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Scenario three: a disputed invoice. A customer insists they were billed for a quantity they never got. You don't just write it off - you compare the invoice back to the shipment it came from. If they're right, a credit memo corrects it. If they're wrong, you resolve the dispute by showing them the evidence.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Scenario four: a duplicate payment. A customer pays the same invoice twice. You never just delete the second receipt - real cash arrived, so it becomes an on-account credit or a refund. Up next, lesson twenty-five, the final lesson: troubleshooting practice that pulls all of this together.
