# Script — Shipping Confirmation

## Segment 1 (title)

The control panels are picked, packed, staged, and the truck leaves. But in Fusion, nothing downstream happens until something tells the system the shipment actually occurred. That's shipping confirmation, and it's one of the most important single events in the whole cycle.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Confirming a shipment does several things at once. It locks in the actual shipped quantity, which can differ from what was planned. It triggers inventory depletion - the goods come off the books because they're no longer physically there. And it starts Interface Trip Stop, the process that eventually makes the line eligible for invoicing.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Before confirmation, a shipment is still just a plan. After it, things become true that can't easily be undone - inventory's gone, and the clock toward invoicing has started. That's exactly why a change after shipping needs a return instead of a simple edit. Ship confirmation is the point of no return.

## Segment 4 (steps)

It's also a real failure point. If a shipment leaves the dock but never gets confirmed, the order sits stuck, inventory looks like it's still on hand when it isn't, and the customer never gets billed for goods they already have. "Why hasn't this invoiced" very often starts with checking whether ship confirmation actually happened.

## Segment 5 (outro)

For our order: four hundred units confirmed, today's date, against Harborview's delivery. Inventory depletes, Interface Trip Stop runs, and the line becomes eligible to invoice. Up next, lesson thirteen: what happens when some of what Harborview received doesn't stay received.
