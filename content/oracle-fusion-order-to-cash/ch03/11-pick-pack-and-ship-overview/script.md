# Script — Pick, Pack and Ship Overview

## Segment 1 (title)

Once SO-48217's line is released to the Savannah warehouse, three physical steps stand between it and the loading dock: picking, packing, and preparing for shipment. Let's walk through each one.

## Segment 2 (steps)

A released line usually isn't picked by itself - it's grouped into a pick wave, a batch of work released together, often by ship date or carrier. That lets the warehouse plan an efficient run instead of chasing one line at a time. Our order joins the next outbound wave for Savannah.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Picking is pulling the ordered quantity from its storage location. A pick task tells a worker exactly what to pull and from where, and confirming it decrements available inventory. For us, that's four hundred units of the control panel. If fewer are actually available - a short pick - that has to get resolved before the shipment proceeds as planned.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Packing groups picked items into shippable containers and records what went into which one - cartons, pallets, whatever the carrier requires. That pack information often feeds directly into shipping documents and freight calculation, and sometimes generates the packing slip that travels with the shipment.

## Segment 5 (outro)

With picking and packing done, the line is staged and ready. The last physical step - loading the carrier and recording that the shipment actually left - is ship confirmation. Up next, lesson twelve: that exact step, and what it starts in motion.
