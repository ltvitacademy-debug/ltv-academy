# Script — Order Pricing and Discounts Overview

## Segment 1 (title)

When Harborview's order line was entered, nobody typed in a price. The system calculated one, using a price list and discount rules set up long before this order existed. Let's see how that actually works.

## Segment 2 (steps)

A price list is a defined set of prices for items, by currency and sometimes by customer segment. Every order line prices against a specific list, chosen based on the customer or business unit. For our order, the Model CP-220's list price is one hundred forty-five dollars per unit - that's where the fifty-eight thousand dollar list total for four hundred units comes from.

## Segment 3 (steps)

The list price isn't necessarily final. Manual discounts get applied directly by a person, within their limit. Automatic discounts fire whenever an order meets a defined condition - like five percent off when quantity hits three hundred units or more. And negotiated agreements can override the price list entirely for a specific customer.

## Segment 4 (code)

That automatic rule is exactly what happens here. Four hundred units clears the three hundred unit threshold, so a five percent volume discount applies on its own. Fifty-eight thousand dollars times five percent is two thousand, nine hundred dollars off, leaving a net fifty-five thousand, one hundred dollars. Nobody had to request it.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Going outside the standard rules - a bigger one-off discount, a price below a floor - usually needs its own approval, separate from a credit check. SO-48217 doesn't need that; its discount is a standard rule. Up next, lesson eight: the credit check and approval process this order does have to go through.
