# Script — Manual Sharing and Teams

## Segment 1 (title)

OWD, roles, and sharing rules are all systematic — they apply the same way, automatically, to every matching record. This lesson covers the one layer that isn't: manual sharing.

## Segment 2 (screenshot: layers diagram)

Manual sharing sits at the very top of the stack — the most granular, least automated layer. A record owner, or anyone with sufficient access, shares one specific record with one specific user or group, by hand, through that record's own Share button. No rule, no hierarchy, no criteria.

## Segment 3 (steps: one record, one grant)

It's the deliberate, individual counterpart to everything else in this chapter. A classic example from Salesforce's own training: a recruiter going on vacation shares their open job applications with a colleague covering for them — too narrow and too temporary to justify building a whole sharing rule for it. And there's a real gotcha — if the record's owner changes, the previous owner's manual shares can quietly stop applying.

## Segment 4 (screenshot: picker UI)

The interaction itself should look familiar — an available list, a selected list, Add and Remove moving entries between them. Salesforce reuses this exact picker pattern everywhere it grants access to specific users or groups, manual sharing included.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Manual sharing is a one-time grant on one record. Up next: Account, Opportunity, and Case Teams — standing, collaborative access that keeps working across many records, not a single ad-hoc share.
