# Script — Account, Opportunity, and Case Teams

## Segment 1 (title)

Manual sharing was one record, one grant, by hand, every time. This lesson covers the standing version of that same problem — teams.

## Segment 2 (screenshot: Opportunity Team related list)

Here's the Opportunity Team related list, right on the Contacts tab of an Opportunity — team members with their roles, and two ways to populate it: add a default team in one click, or add specific members by hand.

## Segment 3 (screenshot: Add Team Members)

Adding someone isn't just flipping on access. Each member gets a Team Role — a free-text label like Sales Engineer — and an Access Level, Read Only or Read/Write, set independently of whatever their profile or the role hierarchy already gives them. That independence matters: a team member's access can exceed the org's default opportunity access entirely.

## Segment 4 (steps: three objects)

The same pattern applies across three objects. Account Teams suit a long-term relationship with one account. Opportunity Teams are a temporary group formed to close one specific deal. Case Teams collaborate to resolve one case. Different objects, identical mechanics underneath.

## Segment 5 (screenshot: Splits tab)

Teams sit next to related but distinct features on the same record — Opportunity Splits, for example, divide revenue credit among team members. Splits and teams solve different problems, credit allocation versus record access, even though they live on adjacent tabs and usually involve the same people.

## Segment 6 (outro)

Teams give standing, reusable collaboration. Up next: Public Groups and Queues — the reusable containers sharing rules and teams both share with.
