# Financial Reporting Center Tour

Chapter 2 is dedicated to a single page inside General Accounting that does a lot of work: the Financial Reporting Center. This is where Oracle's seeded financial statements live, where your own Financial Reporting Studio reports get published, where Account Groups show up with their Sunburst visualization, and where Smart View reports, OTBI content, and BI Publisher reports are all cross-referenced in one place. This lesson is a tour — what's on the page, and how the pieces relate to each other.

## What you'll learn

- How to navigate to the Financial Reporting Center
- The main areas of the page and what each one holds
- How Account Groups and Smart View reports surface inside this same center
- Why this page is the natural home base for a General Accounting consultant

## Getting there

The Financial Reporting Center is opened from inside the General Accounting work area (sometimes labeled General Ledger, depending on configuration), typically through a Financial Reporting Center link or task in the Navigator under General Accounting. Once open, it presents a single page that functions as a hub rather than a single report.

## What's on the page

The Financial Reporting Center organizes several different kinds of content side by side:

- **Financial Reporting Web Studio reports** — formatted statements such as Income Statements, Balance Sheets, and Trial Balances, built in Financial Reporting Studio and published here so they can be opened, run, and drilled into.
- **Account Groups** — curated sets of key accounts a user wants to monitor, displayed with the Sunburst visualization tool (covered in lesson 7).
- **Smart View reports** — Excel-based queries and reports that have been uploaded here so others can download and open them in Smart View.
- **OTBI analyses and dashboards** — ad hoc content surfaced alongside the formatted reports, so a user doesn't have to leave the center to see both.
- **BI Publisher reports** — pixel-perfect output that can also be launched from this same hub.
- **Business Intelligence mobile apps** — content formatted for mobile consumption of key financial metrics.

In other words, the Financial Reporting Center is less "one more report" and more a front door that gathers output from every tool this course covers, specifically for the General Accounting / Financial Reporting audience.

## Running versus authoring

Everything in the Financial Reporting Center can be *opened and run* by a user with the right access, which typically means picking a point of view (ledger, period, scenario) and viewing or exporting the result. *Authoring* new Financial Reporting Studio content is a separate activity, covered starting in lesson 8, that happens in the Financial Reporting Web Studio editor, with the finished report then published back into this center.

## Why this matters for your day-to-day work

As a consultant, the Financial Reporting Center is frequently the first place you point a client who says "I just need my monthly financials." Rather than explaining four different tools, you can often say: open the Financial Reporting Center, here are your statements, here's your trial balance, here's the account group you're tracking. Knowing this page well — what's already there, and where new content should get published — is one of the most immediately useful skills in this course.

## Recap

The Financial Reporting Center is a hub inside General Accounting that brings together Financial Reporting Studio statements, Account Groups, Smart View reports, OTBI content, and BI Publisher reports in one place, for running (not necessarily authoring).
