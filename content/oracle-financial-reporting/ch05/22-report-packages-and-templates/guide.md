# Report Packages and Templates

So far, Smart View has been about one person exploring data. This lesson covers its other job: **Report Packages**, part of Oracle's Narrative Reporting, which give several people a secure, structured way to jointly produce a single polished deliverable — a board package, a regulatory filing, a management report combining commentary and numbers from multiple contributors.

## What you'll learn

- What a Report Package is and the problem it solves
- The three development phases: author, review, sign-off
- Word- and PowerPoint-based packages versus PDF-based packages
- Smart View's specific role in the review phase

## The collaborative problem Report Packages solve

Picture a quarterly board package: five different people each own a section — one writes the executive summary, one owns the financial statements, one owns a risk discussion, and so on — and the final document needs consistent formatting, needs everyone's section reviewed by the right stakeholders, and needs a clear record of who signed off before it goes out. Doing this by emailing Word documents back and forth quickly becomes unmanageable and leaves no reliable audit trail of who approved what.

Report Packages provide a secure, collaborative, and process-driven approach for defining, authoring, reviewing, and publishing financial, management, and regulatory reports — essentially a structured container that coordinates multiple contributors toward one finished document, rather than a single person's workbook.

## Three phases: author, review, sign-off

A Report Package's development can include up to three distinct phases:

1. **Author** — contributors draft their assigned sections, which may mix financial data (potentially pulled live from a connected source, the same way an ad hoc Smart View worksheet does) with written narrative commentary.
2. **Review** — designated reviewers examine the content and leave comments on their assigned areas, without necessarily having edit rights to the content itself — the review phase enables report stakeholders to review and provide feedback on the content in the report package.
3. **Sign-off** — once review feedback is addressed, designated approvers formally sign off, creating a recorded trail of who approved the final version before it's published or distributed.

Not every Report Package needs all three phases configured — a simple package might skip straight from authoring to publishing — but the framework exists specifically for deliverables where a trail of review and approval matters.

## Word/PowerPoint packages versus PDF packages

Report Packages come in two underlying formats, and this matters for how Smart View fits in:

- **Word- or PowerPoint-based packages** can be reviewed directly using Smart View — reviewers enter, review, and respond to comments inside the familiar Word or PowerPoint interface, with Smart View handling the connection back to the underlying reporting system.
- **PDF-based packages** are reviewed through the Narrative Reporting web interface instead, not through Smart View.

Knowing which type a given package is tells you immediately which tool a reviewer needs open.

## Recap

Report Packages give multiple contributors a structured, auditable workflow — author, review, and sign-off — for producing one collaborative financial or regulatory deliverable, with Smart View specifically handling the review of Word- and PowerPoint-based packages (PDF-based packages route through Narrative Reporting's web interface instead).
