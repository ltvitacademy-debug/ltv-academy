# Script — Trial Balance and Account Analysis Reports

## Segment 1 (title)

The Income Statement and Balance Sheet are the polished output. Behind them sit two detail reports a consultant uses constantly during close to verify those statements before anyone outside accounting sees them.

## Segment 2 (steps)

A Trial Balance lists every account with its debit or credit balance for the period, with total debit and total credit columns at the bottom. The entire point is that check: total debits must equal total credits. If they don't, something in the underlying journals is wrong, and it has to be resolved before the formal statements can be trusted.

## Segment 3 (steps)

A typical Trial Balance shows account, beginning balance, period activity, ending balance, and a grand total row confirming debits equal credits across the whole ledger. It's a working document for the accounting team, not something handed to an external reader.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Account Analysis goes the other direction. Where a Trial Balance shows every account side by side, Account Analysis takes one account and shows every transaction that moved its balance during the period, the detail a Trial Balance deliberately leaves out. That's the report you pull when one balance looks wrong and you need to see exactly what posted to it.

## Segment 5 (outro)

In practice these get used in sequence: Trial Balance confirms things balance overall, Account Analysis investigates anything that looks off, and the formal statements publish once both checks are clean. Up next, lesson twenty-six: Payables reports, aging and payments.
