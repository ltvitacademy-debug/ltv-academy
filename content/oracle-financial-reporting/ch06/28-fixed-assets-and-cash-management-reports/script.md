# Script — Fixed Assets and Cash Management Reports

## Segment 1 (title)

Two more modules, two more sets of reports a consultant must know: Fixed Assets, tracking what a company owns and how its value declines, and Cash Management, tracking what's actually in the bank.

## Segment 2 (steps)

The Asset Register is Fixed Assets' core report, documenting buildings, equipment, vehicles, computers, with acquisition cost, date, category, location. It's the authoritative detail behind the Fixed Assets line on the Balance Sheet, the same relationship a Trial Balance has to the GL overall.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Depreciation reporting shows the expense recognized per asset or category, and it matters on two statements at once. It reduces net book value on the Balance Sheet, and shows up as depreciation expense on the Income Statement, reducing Net Income. During close, you're confirming depreciation actually ran, for the right assets, posting the expected amounts.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Cash Management's job is different in character, confirming Oracle Fusion's recorded cash position matches what the bank reports. Bank statement reconciliation compares recorded transactions against the bank's own statement, checking off matches and surfacing anything that doesn't, like an uncleared check or an unrecorded bank fee.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Both modules follow the same pattern as this whole chapter, a summarized statement line is only as trustworthy as the detailed report supporting it. Up next, lesson twenty-nine: the month-end reporting package, pulling everything together into one close deliverable.
