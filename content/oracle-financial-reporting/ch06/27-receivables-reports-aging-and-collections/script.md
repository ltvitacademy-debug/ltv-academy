# Script — Receivables Reports: Aging and Collections

## Segment 1 (title)

Receivables mirrors Payables on the other side of the business. Instead of what the company owes, these reports track what customers owe the company. Let's cover aging, statements, and collections.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Receivables has its own aging report, bucketed the same way as Payables, current, then past-due ranges. But the urgency runs the opposite direction. An aging Payables balance is delayed outgoing cash. An aging Receivables balance is incoming cash that hasn't arrived yet, directly affecting how much cash the company has to operate with.

## Segment 3 (steps)

A customer statement summarizes one customer's account activity, invoices, payments, running balance, formatted the way a bank statement formats yours. These are typically BI Publisher jobs specifically because they're customer-facing, needing a consistent, professional layout every time, for potentially hundreds of customers at once.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Beyond that, an invoice register lists invoices issued during a period, and a receipts register lists payments received, both useful for audit and reconciliation, the Receivables-side equivalent of Payables' payment registers.

## Segment 5 (outro)

A collections team's daily work is organized around the aging report, oldest and largest balances first, escalating as accounts age further. Checking aging constantly throughout the day is exactly OTBI's job; the formal statement that eventually goes out is BI Publisher's.
