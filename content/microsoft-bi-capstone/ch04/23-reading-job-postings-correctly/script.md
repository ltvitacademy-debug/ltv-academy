# Script — Reading Job Postings Correctly

## Segment 1 (title)

Most job postings list more than any real candidate will actually have. This lesson covers how to read one correctly — what's really required, what's a red flag, and how your skills translate onto its language.

## Segment 2 (steps: must-have vs. nice-to-have)

Sort every requirement into two piles. "Required" and "must have" language, plus a specific years-of-experience threshold on a core skill, is a realistic bar. "Preferred," "a plus," and "familiarity with" language is helpful but not disqualifying — don't screen yourself out over it.

## Segment 3 (steps: red flags)

Watch for expert-level requirements stacked across five or six unrelated tools at a junior salary, no salary range listed at all, and vague responsibilities like "wear many hats" making up the bulk of the description. None of these disqualify a posting on their own, but two or three stacked together is a reason to be cautious.

## Segment 4 (steps: mapping your skills)

A posting's vocabulary and your resume's vocabulary rarely match word for word. SSIS maps to "ETL" or "data pipelines." Dimensional modeling maps to "data warehousing" or "star schema." SSRS maps to "paginated reporting." Translate before deciding whether you're a fit.
