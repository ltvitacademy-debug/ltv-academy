## Separating must-have from nice-to-have

Most job postings are written by a hiring manager describing their
ideal candidate, not the minimum bar for the role — which means most
postings list more than any real candidate will actually have. Reading
one correctly means sorting its requirements into two piles instead of
treating the whole list as a pass/fail gate:

| Signal | Usually means | Example phrasing |
|---|---|---|
| "Required," "must have," a specific years-of-experience threshold tied to a core skill | Must-have — you likely need this to be a realistic candidate | "3+ years of T-SQL required" |
| "Preferred," "a plus," "nice to have," "bonus," "familiarity with" | Nice-to-have — helpful, not disqualifying | "Experience with Azure a plus" |
| A long list of tools with no distinction made between them at all | Often padding — treat the ones central to the job title as must-have and the rest as aspirational | A "BI Developer" posting listing a dozen unrelated tools with no priority given |

A candidate coming out of this program with a finished SQL
Server-to-Power BI pipeline is a realistic match for most "must-have"
lists in this field, even without matching every "nice to have" bullet
on the page.

## When a posting is really asking for something else

Not every posting titled "BI Developer" wants what this program taught.
Read past the job title into the actual body of responsibilities and
requirements:

- A posting heavy on **Python, cloud pipeline tools, and "data
  engineering"** language, with reporting mentioned only in passing,
  is really a data engineering role wearing a BI title.
- A posting heavy on **statistics, forecasting, and "insights"**
  language, with almost no mention of ETL or warehouse design, is
  closer to a data analyst role.
- A posting that spends most of its space on **database
  administration, backups, performance tuning, and uptime**, with
  reporting as an afterthought, is a DBA role, not a BI developer role.

None of these are bad postings — they're just not this role, and
applying anyway wastes your time and the employer's. Matching the
actual body of the posting, not just its title, is what tells you
whether it's worth pursuing.

## Red flags

A few patterns in a posting are worth noticing before you invest time
applying:

| Red flag | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Unrealistic requirement stacking | "Expert" level in five or six unrelated tools, at a junior-sounding salary, usually signals a role that was never scoped properly |
| No salary range listed at all | Increasingly rare and increasingly a signal the range is lower than the responsibilities suggest, or the company hasn't done the work to figure it out |
| Vague responsibilities ("wear many hats," "other duties as assigned" as the bulk of the description) | Often means the role hasn't been defined yet, or it's really three jobs being sold as one |
| A generic, copy-pasted description with no detail specific to the company or team | Sometimes a sign the posting is a placeholder or a low-priority req, not an active, funded search |

None of these are automatic disqualifiers on their own — but two or
three of them stacked together are a reasonable signal to be cautious
about how much time you invest in that application.

## Mapping your resume language to the posting

This program built you a real skill set. The posting's language and
your resume's language don't always use the same words for the same
thing — matching them is part of reading the posting correctly:

| Your skill from this program | What a posting might call it |
|---|---|
| SQL Server / T-SQL | "SQL," "relational databases," "RDBMS experience" |
| SSIS | "ETL," "data integration," "data pipelines" |
| Dimensional modeling / the warehouse | "Data warehousing," "star schema," "data modeling" |
| SSRS | "Paginated reporting," "enterprise reporting" |
| Power BI | "BI tools," "data visualization," "dashboarding" |

When you see the posting's phrasing, mentally (or literally, in your
resume draft) translate it to your actual capstone experience before
deciding whether you're a fit — the posting's exact vocabulary and
your resume's exact vocabulary rarely need to match word for word.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Must-have requirement | A requirement phrased as required or mandatory — a realistic bar for applying |
| Nice-to-have requirement | A requirement phrased as preferred or a bonus — helpful, not disqualifying |
| Requirement stacking | A red flag where a posting demands expert-level skill in an unrealistic number of unrelated tools |
| Keyword mapping | Translating a posting's vocabulary to your own resume language (or vice versa) to judge real fit |
