# Script — CRM Analytics vs. Native Reports

## Segment 1 (title)

You already know native reports and dashboards. CRM Analytics doesn't replace them, it does a different job. This lesson is about telling the two apart, so you pick the right tool without guessing.

## Segment 2 (screenshot: CRM Analytics dashboard)

Here's a real CRM Analytics dashboard. Key numbers down the left, filters across the top, a funnel, a trend line, and a stacked bar by sales rep with a quota line. Select a stage, and the other widgets re-slice instantly. That's faceting. In a native dashboard, each component runs its own report, and clicking a chart doesn't filter its neighbors.

## Segment 3 (code: side by side)

Side by side. Native reports read your live records at the moment you run them. CRM Analytics reads datasets that were loaded ahead of time, on a schedule. Reports stay with Salesforce data, while datasets can blend in outside sources. Reports give you filters and formulas. CRM Analytics adds faceting, bindings, recipes, and a query language called SAQL.

## Segment 4 (steps: which one)

So which one? If the need is operational, say a rep's open opportunities this week, a report is faster to build and always current. If you need blended data, large volumes, or deep interactive exploration, that's CRM Analytics. Many teams use both. Remember the trade-offs: datasets are only as fresh as their last refresh, and CRM Analytics is typically licensed separately, so it has to earn its cost.
