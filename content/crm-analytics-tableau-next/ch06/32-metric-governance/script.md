Defining a metric centrally solves half the problem. The other half is keeping it correct as the business changes. Someone must own it, someone must approve changes, and everyone must know where the official version lives. That is metric governance.

For consumers, a governed metric is simple. This real metric page shows the current value, the change against the prior period, a time range, and filters, with a Follow button for updates. People see one trusted number. Governance is the work that protects that trust behind the scenes.

Decide three roles for every important metric. An owner, usually a business function like revenue operations, accountable for the definition. A small group of editors allowed to change the model. And consumers, who use the metric but cannot alter it. In Tableau Next, keeping the editor role rare is how you protect definitions.

Reference, do not copy. An asset can live in one workspace and be referenced from others without duplication. This popup shows a referenced semantic model pointing back to its home workspace. A central team can share a core model while other teams build on it. The official version stays in one place.

Use a lightweight change process. Propose the change with a business reason. Review it with the owner. Publish it and update the description. Announce it, so people understand period comparisons. And retire the old version, rather than leaving two competing definitions. Govern the metrics that matter, and keep it proportionate.
