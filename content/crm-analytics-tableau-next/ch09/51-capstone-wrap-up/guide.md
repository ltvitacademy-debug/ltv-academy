# Capstone: Wrap-Up & Portfolio Presentation

You've built the app, defined the metrics, connected usage data, and secured it. This final lesson turns that work into something you can show: a ten-minute executive presentation for Cobalt Ridge's CRO, and a portfolio write-up that proves to a hiring manager that you can do the whole job. It also closes the CRM Analytics and Tableau Next course. All numbers are the illustrative figures from earlier in the capstone.

## What you'll learn

- How to reduce a whole project to one page of numbers, each with a verdict
- A five-part structure for a ten-minute executive presentation
- How to turn a forecast into a specific ask
- What belongs in a portfolio write-up, including honest limits

## Start with the one-page result

An executive won't read your recipe. Give them a handful of numbers, each with a verdict:

- **Bookings:** $5.2M of the $8.0M target, 65%, with the Americas at $2.9M, EMEA at $1.5M, and APAC at $0.8M
- **Pipeline:** $6.0M open against a $2.8M gap, about 2.1x coverage
- **Conversion:** 27% win rate, and 31 open deals idle for 30+ days worth $1.9M
- **Retention risk:** 17 of 96 renewing accounts under 40% seat activation, holding $2.3M of $12.4M

## The five-part presentation

1. **Headline.** The verdict in one sentence: "We're on track for about $6.8M against an $8.0M target unless we act on two things."
2. **Evidence.** Two or three dashboard components, not all of them. Show the region split and the stalled-deal list.
3. **Risk.** What the data can't tell us and where you're least sure.
4. **Ask.** The specific decision you need.
5. **Next.** What you'll do this week regardless.

## Turn numbers into a verdict

A forecast is arithmetic plus an honest assumption. Closed won is $5.2M. Expected from open pipeline, at the 27% trailing win rate, is about $6.0M x 27% = $1.6M. That totals roughly $6.8M, or about $1.2M short of the target. Notice what the dashboard's coverage tile hid: 2.1x sounds comfortable, but at a 27% win rate you'd need roughly 3.7x coverage (1 divided by 0.27) to expect to close the gap.

Be clear about the assumption. The win rate here is by deal count, applied to dollars, so it's a rough rule, not a forecast model. Say so out loud, which builds credibility rather than costing it.

Then make the ask. "Have the three regional VPs re-engage the 31 stalled deals ($1.9M) this week, and have Customer Success contact the 17 at-risk renewals ($2.3M) before their renewal calls." A verdict without an ask is just commentary.

## The portfolio write-up

Write it as a short case study with five sections:

- **Problem:** who asked, what decision it supports
- **Data and model:** sources, dataset grain, the metric definitions and their owners
- **Decisions:** why a recipe, why predicates instead of sharing inheritance, why a shared key instead of identity resolution
- **Results:** your own screenshots from your own org, clearly labeled as sample data
- **Limits and next steps:** what you'd improve

Use your own screenshots and your own data. A Trailhead image is a good learning aid, but it isn't evidence of your work.

## Close with honesty

State the limits plainly: the figures are illustrative, the dashboard and metric layer needed a manual parity test, usage data is a day behind, and product names such as Data Cloud (now Data 360) and their menus change between releases. Limits stated by you read as judgment. Limits discovered by the interviewer read as a gap.

## Recap

One page of verdicts, five presentation parts, a forecast that ends in an ask, and a five-section portfolio piece with honest limits. You now have the skills to build, define, connect, secure, and present analytics in the Salesforce ecosystem. Next: the Salesforce Analytics Career & Capstone course.
