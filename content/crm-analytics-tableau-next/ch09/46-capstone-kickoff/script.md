# Script — Capstone Kickoff

## Segment 1 (title)

Welcome to the capstone. Until now you've learned this course in pieces. Now you'll combine it into one project: a CRM Analytics app, a central metric layer, a Data Cloud connection, and proper security, ending in an executive presentation. You'll apply what you know, not relearn it.

## Segment 2 (steps)

The company is Cobalt Ridge Software, a fictional B2B software company with about twelve hundred employees, three thousand two hundred customer accounts, and three regions: the Americas, EMEA, and APAC. Every figure here is an illustrative example. Your org's numbers will differ, and that's fine, because the method is what matters. Your Chief Revenue Officer wants one place where every rep, every regional VP, and the CRO see the same trusted numbers.

## Segment 3 (code)

Five questions. Are we on track against an eight million dollar quarterly target? Does open pipeline cover the gap? How do bookings split by region? Which open deals have gone quiet? And which upcoming renewals show weak product adoption? Each question is measurable, and someone will ask each one every week.

## Segment 4 (code)

Here's the example baseline to check your work. Closed won so far: five point two million from sixty-four deals, sixty-five percent of target. Open pipeline: six million, roughly two point one times coverage of the remaining gap. Win rate: twenty-seven percent. And on renewals, seventeen accounts under forty percent seat activation, holding two point three million dollars of annual revenue.

## Segment 5 (steps)

Four build stages. First, the app: a recipe, a dataset, and a dashboard. Second, the metric layer: define each headline number once, with an owner. Third, Data Cloud: bring in product usage and compute adoption. Fourth, security: make sure each person sees only their own slice. Then a presentation and a portfolio write-up close it out.

## Segment 6 (code)

One caution about names. Salesforce renamed Data Cloud to Data 360 in late 2025, and menus and features shift between releases. This course keeps the familiar name with that note attached. Whenever a step depends on your org, trust the pickers on your screen and current Salesforce documentation over memory. Use a Developer Edition or sandbox org, and name everything clearly.
