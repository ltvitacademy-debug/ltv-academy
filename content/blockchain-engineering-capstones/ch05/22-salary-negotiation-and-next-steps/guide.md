## Why no specific numbers appear here

Salary figures go stale fast, vary enormously by country, metro area, company stage, and even which chain a company builds on, and this catalog has no reliable way to verify a precise number as current for your specific market. Instead of quoting a figure that might already be wrong, this lesson teaches the same research process this catalog's other career-prep courses use — building your *own* range from live, current sources.

## Why blockchain pay varies even more than most tech roles

- **Titles aren't standardized at all.** "Blockchain Developer," "Smart Contract Engineer," "Solidity Developer," and "Web3 Engineer" can mean nearly identical work at different companies — or very different scopes under the same title.
- **Company stage swings compensation enormously.** A funded protocol team, a large exchange, and an early-stage project with a token-heavy compensation structure can offer wildly different base-to-token ratios for similar day-to-day work.
- **Token-based compensation adds a real variable cash pay doesn't have.** Some blockchain roles include token grants or vesting alongside (or instead of) a traditional bonus — research this as its own line item, not folded into a base-salary number.

When researching your own range, search under several of these title variants, and note whether a posting's total compensation includes any token component.

## Building a range, using this path's full research toolkit

| Source type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Aggregated salary sites | Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Payscale, LinkedIn Salary |
| Government labor data | Your country's official labor statistics agency |
| Direct market signal | Live job postings in your target companies, searched under several blockchain-role title variants |

Build a low-to-high band from at least two independent sources, for your actual experience level and the kind of company you're targeting (protocol team vs. exchange vs. early-stage) — never a single remembered number without a source behind it.

## Answering "what are your salary expectations"

The same principle holds here as everywhere else: whoever names a number first anchors the conversation. Redirect to the posted or budgeted range where you can ("I'd love to hear what range you had in mind for this role"); if pushed, answer with your own researched range.

## Evaluating and countering a real offer

```
Thank you for the offer -- I'm genuinely excited about this role
and the team.

Based on my research for Smart Contract Developer / Blockchain
Engineer roles at companies at a similar stage, similar positions
tend to run somewhat higher on the base, and I wanted to ask about
the token allocation as well.

Is there room to move the base toward that range, or adjust the
token vesting schedule to bridge the gap?

I'm ready to move forward once we're aligned.
```

Look at the whole package before responding: base, any token grant and its vesting schedule and cliff, signing bonus, remote/hybrid flexibility, and the timeline to your first compensation review — not just the headline base number.
