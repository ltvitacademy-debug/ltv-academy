# Script — Chart of Accounts Structure and Segments

## Segment 1 (title)

Everything from the last three chapters exists to support one final piece: the chart of accounts, the classification scheme every transaction gets coded against. This lesson starts chapter five with how it's structured.

## Segment 2 (code)

A chart of accounts is built from segments — ordered pieces like Company, Cost Center, and Natural Account. Every transaction gets one value from each segment, and the full combination is called an account combination, the complete address a dollar of activity posts to.

## Segment 3 (code)

A chart of accounts structure defines the shape: how many segments, their order, their widths. A structure instance is an actual deployment of that shape, tied to specific value sets — two ledgers can share a structure but use different value sets for Cost Center.

## Segment 4 (steps)

How many segments and in what order is decided once, during design, and should not be revisited casually. Too few segments and you can't report what you need; too many and every screen and report gets harder to use.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Once a structure is defined, it gets frozen, then deployed, before any ledger can use it. Next up, lesson twenty-one: segment labels — balancing, cost center, and natural account.
