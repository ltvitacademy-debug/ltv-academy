# Finding Your Way Around Setup and Maintenance

Almost every lesson left in this course happens inside one work area: **Setup and Maintenance**. If Enterprise Structures and Chart of Accounts has a home screen, this is it. Before diving into legal entities, ledgers, and segments, spend this lesson learning how Setup and Maintenance is organized, so you are not hunting blindly every time a task tells you to "go configure" something.

## What you'll learn

- How to reach the Setup and Maintenance work area from the Navigator
- The three-level organization: offerings, functional areas, and tasks
- How to search for a specific setup task instead of browsing the whole tree
- What an "implementation project" is and why it matters later

## Reaching Setup and Maintenance

From the home page Navigator, under the **My Enablement** or **Tools** area, you will find **Setup and Maintenance**. Clicking it opens a work area built around one purpose: configuring Oracle Fusion applications before (and after) go-live. Everything you do in this course — defining a chart of accounts, creating a ledger, setting up a business unit — happens through tasks launched from this one screen.

## Offerings, functional areas, and tasks

Oracle Fusion organizes setup work in three nested levels:

```
Offering               (e.g. Financials)
  └─ Functional Area    (e.g. Enterprise Profile, Ledger and Reporting)
       └─ Setup Task     (e.g. Manage Chart of Accounts Structures)
```

- An **offering** is a large product area, such as Financials, Procurement, or Project Management. You license and enable offerings at the top level.
- Each offering breaks down into **functional areas** — logical groupings like Enterprise Profile, Ledger and Reporting Configuration, or Subledger Accounting.
- Each functional area contains individual **setup tasks** — the actual clickable steps, like "Manage Chart of Accounts Structures" or "Manage Legal Entity."

This course lives almost entirely inside the **Financials** offering, and mostly inside two functional areas: **Enterprise Profile** (legal entities, business units, locations) and **Ledger and Reporting Configuration** (ledgers, chart of accounts, calendars, currencies).

## Searching instead of browsing

The full task list for even one offering can run into hundreds of tasks. Rather than expanding every node, use the **search box** at the top of Setup and Maintenance and type part of a task name — for example, typing "chart of accounts" surfaces every task with that phrase, regardless of which functional area it technically lives in. This is the fastest way to find a specific task once you know roughly what it's called, and it is how most working consultants actually navigate day to day rather than drilling through the tree every time.

## Implementation projects, briefly

An **implementation project** is a named, scoped bundle of setup tasks you create to track a specific rollout — for example, "Phase 1: Enterprise Structures." It lets a team check tasks off, assign owners, and export/import setup between environments. You do not need one to complete the individual tasks in this course, but you will see the concept again when Functional Setup Manager comes up in Lesson 4, because FSM is the engine that generates these task lists in the first place.

## Recap

Setup and Maintenance is organized as offering → functional area → setup task, almost everything in this course sits inside the Financials offering, and the search box is usually faster than browsing.
