# Script — Accounting Calendars and Periods

## Segment 1 (title)

Every ledger needs a calendar, one of its four C's. This lesson covers what an Oracle Fusion accounting calendar actually consists of, and why these choices ripple through every period-end close for the ledger's whole life.

## Segment 2 (code)

An accounting calendar breaks a fiscal year into periods. Defining one means specifying the period type, how many periods per year, start and end dates for each, period names, and the fiscal year's start month, which isn't always January.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Most implementations use one of two patterns. Monthly, Gregorian calendars: twelve periods matching calendar months, the most common and simplest to reconcile. Thirteen-period calendars: equal-length periods, often four or five weeks, common with retailers and manufacturers on a 4-4-5 pattern.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Each period needs a clear, unambiguous name, like Jan-25, because users see that name on every transaction and report for years. Consistency matters more than cleverness — a confusing naming pattern causes real reporting headaches later.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Once a calendar has live transactions posted, changing its structure is disruptive at best, sometimes effectively impossible. Get it right before go-live. Next up, lesson sixteen: period types and adjusting periods.
