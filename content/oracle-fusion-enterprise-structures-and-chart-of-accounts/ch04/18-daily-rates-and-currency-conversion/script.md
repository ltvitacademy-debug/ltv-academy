# Script — Daily Rates and Currency Conversion

## Segment 1 (title)

Conversion rate types define the category of rate. This lesson covers the day-to-day mechanics: how actual numeric rates get loaded, kept current, and applied when a Spot or Corporate rate is needed.

## Segment 2 (code)

A daily rate is a specific numeric exchange rate between two currencies, for one rate type, effective on one date — one euro equals one point oh eight US dollars, Corporate rate, effective a given day. These are managed through Manage Daily Rates.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Most companies don't type rates in by hand every day. A Daily Rates Import and Calculation process can pull rates from an external feed automatically, keeping Spot and Corporate rates current without manual entry — essential at any real multi-currency volume.

## Segment 4 (code)

Sometimes a direct rate between two currencies isn't available, but rates to a common third currency are. A cross rate rule derives a rate between A and B by routing through that third currency — a Thai baht to euro rate computed through US dollars, for example.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Daily rates aren't a set-it-up-once structure — they need fresh data every operating day. Next up, lesson nineteen: opening the first accounting period, where this chapter's setup finally becomes usable.
