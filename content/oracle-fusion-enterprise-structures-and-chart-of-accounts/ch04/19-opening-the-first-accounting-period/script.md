# Script — Opening the First Accounting Period

## Segment 1 (title)

A beautifully configured calendar is still useless until a period is actually open. This lesson closes chapter four with period statuses, and the specific steps around opening a ledger's very first period.

## Segment 2 (code)

Every period carries one of five statuses: Never Opened, Future Enterable for posting ahead of schedule, Open for normal activity, Closed but reopenable if needed, and Permanently Closed, which can never be reopened, preserving audit integrity.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Periods generally move left to right through these statuses over their lifetime — never opened, then open when its time arrives, closed at month-end, and eventually permanently closed once audits are complete.

## Segment 4 (code)

Before opening the first period, the whole chapter's work needs to be in place: the calendar defined and assigned, the ledger created with its chart of accounts and currency, and at least one legal entity assigned with its balancing segment value.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Period status isn't a one-time setup step — it's actively managed every accounting cycle, opening the next period and closing the prior one on schedule. That completes chapter four. Chapter five turns to the chart of accounts itself.
