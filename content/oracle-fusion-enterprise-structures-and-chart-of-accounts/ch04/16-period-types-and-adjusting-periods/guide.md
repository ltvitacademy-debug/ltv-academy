# Period Types and Adjusting Periods

Not every accounting entry fits neatly inside a regular monthly or weekly period. Year-end audit adjustments, final true-ups, and certain closing entries often need a period of their own — one that exists specifically to hold them apart from ordinary operating activity. That is the **adjusting period**.

## What you'll learn

- What a period type is and how it relates to the calendar from Lesson 15
- What an adjusting period is, concretely, and why it exists
- How many adjusting periods a calendar typically has, and when they're used
- How adjusting periods interact with regular financial reporting

## Period type, briefly revisited

A **period type** (Month, Week, Quarter, and so on) is the category of regular period a calendar is built from — Lesson 15 covered how a calendar strings a sequence of these together into a fiscal year. Every regular period in a calendar shares the same period type; a monthly calendar has twelve month-type periods, for example.

## What an adjusting period is

An **adjusting period** is a special, additional period layered onto the regular calendar, used to record entries that should not appear in a normal operating period's balances — classic examples include year-end audit adjustments proposed by external auditors, or final closing entries management wants visible separately from the last regular period's day-to-day activity. A company might, for example, define its calendar with twelve regular monthly periods plus one adjusting period at year-end specifically for audit entries.

```
Regular Calendar:  Jan | Feb | Mar | ... | Nov | Dec
Adjusting Period:                                    | Adj-Dec
                                                        (year-end audit entries only)
```

## How many, and when

Most companies that use adjusting periods define just one, positioned at fiscal year-end, since that is when audit adjustments are most common. Some calendars (particularly certain 13-period patterns) can define more than one adjusting period if there is a real business reason — for example, distinguishing interim quarter-end adjustments from the final year-end adjustment. As always, more adjusting periods means more complexity to maintain; most companies do not need more than one.

## Reporting with and without the adjusting period

Oracle Fusion lets reports and balance inquiries include or exclude adjusting period activity depending on what question is being asked. A standard operating report for December would normally exclude the adjusting period, showing only "regular" December activity; a full-year financial statement intended to reflect audited, final numbers would include it. This flexibility is exactly why adjusting periods exist as a separate period rather than simply being folded into the last regular period — folding them in would make it impossible to see pre-audit and post-audit numbers separately.

## Recap

A period type is the category a calendar's regular periods share, while an adjusting period is a special additional period, usually just one at year-end, that isolates audit and true-up entries from ordinary activity.
