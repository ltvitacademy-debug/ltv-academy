# Script — Period Types and Adjusting Periods

## Segment 1 (title)

Not every accounting entry fits neatly inside a regular period. Year-end audit adjustments and final true-ups often need a period of their own, set apart from ordinary activity. That's the adjusting period.

## Segment 2 (steps)

A period type, like Month or Week, is the category of regular period a calendar is built from. Every regular period in a calendar shares the same type — a monthly calendar has twelve month-type periods.

## Segment 3 (code)

An adjusting period is a special, additional period layered onto the regular calendar for entries that shouldn't appear in a normal operating period's balances — year-end audit adjustments, for example, attached after the last regular month.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Most companies define just one adjusting period, at fiscal year-end, since that's when audit adjustments are most common. Some 13-period calendars define more than one, but extra adjusting periods mean extra complexity to maintain.
