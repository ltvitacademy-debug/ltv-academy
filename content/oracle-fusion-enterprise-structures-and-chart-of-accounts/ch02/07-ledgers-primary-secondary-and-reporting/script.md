# Script — Ledgers: Primary, Secondary and Reporting

## Segment 1 (title)

If the legal entity is the "who," the ledger is the "book." This lesson covers the three kinds of ledger Oracle Fusion supports, and the four defining characteristics every ledger needs.

## Segment 2 (code)

Every ledger is defined by its chart of accounts, its calendar, its currency, and its subledger accounting method — sometimes called the ledger's four C's. Two ledgers sharing all four are candidates for a ledger set. Differ in even one, and they're accounting for things differently.

## Segment 3 (steps)

The primary ledger is the mandatory, central record-keeping ledger. Every accounting configuration is built around exactly one. It's directly linked to subledger transactions from Payables, Receivables, and the rest.

## Segment 4 (steps)

A secondary ledger is optional and always tied to a primary ledger — it maintains an alternative accounting representation, maybe a different GAAP or a different chart of accounts. A reporting currency is simpler: the same ledger, just restated in another currency.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Primary ledger: mandatory and central. Secondary ledger: an alternate accounting representation. Reporting currency: the same book, translated. Next up, lesson eight: ledger sets, for managing ledgers that share enough of these characteristics together.
