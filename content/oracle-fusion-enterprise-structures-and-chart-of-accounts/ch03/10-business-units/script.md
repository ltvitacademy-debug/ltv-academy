# Script — Business Units

## Segment 1 (title)

Legal entities and ledgers answer "who legally exists" and "what book records it." Neither one actually processes a transaction. That job belongs to the business unit, which you'll touch constantly in every later course.

## Segment 2 (code)

A business unit is an operational, transaction-processing organization that performs specific business functions. It processes invoices, orders, and requisitions. It's assigned to exactly one primary ledger, and usually associated with one legal entity.

## Segment 3 (steps)

The relationship isn't strictly one-to-one. A single legal entity might contain several business units — manufacturing and distribution, say. A single business unit might even process transactions for more than one legal entity, acting as a shared service provider.

## Segment 4 (steps)

New consultants sometimes treat legal entity and business unit as the same thing. They're not. Legal entity is about statutory obligations. Business unit is about how the company actually organizes its operational work — and one legal entity can run several business units.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Business units are also the lens that determines which company policies apply to a transaction, through reference data sets, covered in lesson twelve. Next up, lesson eleven: business unit functions and assignments.
