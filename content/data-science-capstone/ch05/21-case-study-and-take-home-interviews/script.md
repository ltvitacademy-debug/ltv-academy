# Script — Case Study & Take-Home Interviews

## Segment 1 (title)

Case studies and take-homes are where an interview starts to look like the job. There's no single right answer. Interviewers watch how you frame a vague problem, what you check before trusting the data, whether you start simple, and whether your recommendation fits the business. If you finished the Harvest Table capstone, you've already done a full version. Formats vary, so ask what to expect.

## Segment 2 (steps: live vs take-home)

A live case is a conversation, often thirty to sixty minutes. They want your structure and reasoning, not a finished model. A take-home is an assignment you do on your own time, usually a dataset and a question, followed by a discussion. Ask about the time limit, and what they'll be looking for.

## Segment 3 (steps: the outline)

Here's an outline for a churn case. One, clarify the decision: who acts, and what do they do differently? Two, define the target precisely: what counts as cancelling, over what window. Three, check the data for gaps and leakage. Four, start with a baseline. Five, build and validate with a time-based split. Six, choose metrics tied to action. Seven, translate into value. Eight, state limits and next steps.

## Segment 4 (code: value calculation)

A model that scores well can still lose money if the action is costly. With hypothetical numbers, contacting the top twenty percent of customers lost four hundred dollars. Contacting a sharper top ten percent earned seven hundred sixty, and catching a few more cancellers raised that to seventeen hundred twenty. The point is connecting thresholds to cost, benefit and uncertainty.

## Segment 5 (steps: take-home habits)

For take-homes, time-box your work and say what you skipped. Lead with a one-paragraph recommendation. Make it reproducible with a README and fixed seeds. Start simple, show your checks, be honest about limits, and never post a company's data or solution publicly without permission.

## Segment 6 (code: leakage)

One more habit: look for leakage out loud. A cancellation-survey response, a final-month refund, or any field recorded after the prediction date shouldn't be a feature. Naming that risk early shows experience.
