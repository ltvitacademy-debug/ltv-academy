# Script — Behavioral Interviews & Communication

## Segment 1 (title)

Behavioral questions sound soft, but they carry real weight. Data scientists spend much of their time explaining and persuading people who aren't data scientists. Interviewers ask, tell me about a time you, to predict how you'll act on their team. The best preparation is a small bank of true stories from your projects. Nothing here should be invented.

## Segment 2 (steps: STAR)

Use the STAR structure. Situation and task, briefly. Then your actions, which get most of the time. Then the result and what you learned. Aim for about two minutes. Two common errors: too much background, and saying we throughout, so the interviewer can't tell what you did.

## Segment 3 (steps: project decisions)

Decisions make better stories than tasks. Look through your capstone for real moments. A judgment call about metrics, like recall over precision. A problem you caught, such as leakage or duplicates. A simpler model you chose because it was easier to explain. A limitation you disclosed. Or feedback from practice that changed your approach.

## Segment 4 (code: before and after)

Now, explaining a model to a business audience. Focus on the decision, not the math. Instead of gradient boosting and area under the curve, say: the model scores each customer on how likely they are to cancel. Contacting the top group reaches more leavers than picking at random. It isn't perfect, so we should test whether outreach works.

## Segment 5 (steps: explaining pattern)

A good pattern has five parts. The decision. What the model does in one sentence. How it compares to a simple alternative. What it can't do. And the recommended next step.

## Segment 6 (steps: hard questions)

For failure, weakness and disagreement questions, be candid. Choose a real, modest mistake, own it, and show what changed. Name a real weakness you're improving. Describe how you sought to understand the other view. And prepare three questions of your own for them.

## Segment 7 (outro)

Practice out loud with a friend or mentor. Next is the final lesson of the course: salary and offer basics.
