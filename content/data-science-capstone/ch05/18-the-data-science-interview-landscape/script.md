# Script — The Data Science Interview Landscape

## Segment 1 (title)

Data science interviews have a reputation for being unpredictable. There's no single standard process, but the same building blocks show up again and again. Knowing them lets you prepare for categories instead of guessing questions. What follows are common patterns, not a promise about any one employer, so always ask your recruiter what to expect.

## Segment 2 (steps: the rounds)

Commonly the process moves through these stages. A recruiter screen. A conversation with the hiring manager or team. A SQL or coding screen. Statistics and machine learning questions. A case study or take-home. Then a final loop, often with a presentation. Not every company uses every round, and the order varies.

## Segment 3 (code: role flavors)

The mix changes with the role. Analytics-focused roles lean on SQL, statistics, experiments and communication. Machine-learning-focused roles lean on modeling, evaluation and code. Research-flavored roles go deeper on math. Junior roles often stress SQL, statistics and communication. Read the description, not just the title.

## Segment 4 (steps: the pitch)

In the recruiter screen, prepare a sixty-second answer to tell me about yourself. Three beats: where you come from, what you built, and what you want next. Mention the Harvest Table churn project in one sentence, with its result, and say plainly that the data was fictional.

## Segment 5 (steps: what they evaluate)

Underneath every round, the same questions repeat. Can you get the data? Can you reason about it? Can you build something sound? Can you connect it to a decision? And can you communicate honestly about limits?

## Segment 6 (steps: four-week plan)

A realistic plan is four weeks. Week one, polish your resume, GitHub and pitch. Week two, SQL and pandas practice out loud. Week three, statistics and machine learning review, plus one practice take-home. Week four, mock interviews and behavioral stories. Keep applying throughout.

## Segment 7 (outro)

Rejections are common and aren't a verdict on you. Next, we work through statistics and machine learning interview questions.
