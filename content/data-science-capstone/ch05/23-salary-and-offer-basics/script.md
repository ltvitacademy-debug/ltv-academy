# Script — Salary & Offer Basics

## Segment 1 (title)

You have the skills, the portfolio and the interview practice. The offer conversation is the last step, and it's where many first-time candidates accept the first number without asking a single question. This lesson is a starting point, not financial or legal advice, and every figure varies by city, employer and year.

## Segment 2 (code: pay signals)

Current postings suggest a broad pattern for this path. Junior data scientists and analysts are often in the range of seventy-five to one hundred ten thousand dollars. Senior roles may reach one hundred thirty to one hundred eighty thousand or more. Staff or principal roles can exceed two hundred thousand, but that takes years of results. These are a compass, not a promise.

## Segment 3 (steps: research)

Research before you talk numbers. Collect ranges from postings for the same title and city. Compare two or three sources. Ask mentors what's realistic locally. Then write your own range: a minimum, a target, and an ambitious number you can justify.

## Segment 4 (steps: total compensation)

Think in total compensation. Base pay, bonus, and whether it's a target or guaranteed. Equity, which is uncertain. Benefits like retirement match and time off. One-time items, like a signing bonus. And growth: training budget, certifications, and remote options.

## Segment 5 (code: comparing offers)

A simple calculation shows why the highest base isn't always the highest package. With illustrative numbers, an offer with a hundred thousand base totaled exactly that, while a ninety-two thousand base with bonus, match and signing totaled about one hundred eight thousand. Bonuses are often targets, and signing bonuses are one-time, so use this to organize questions, not to decide.

## Segment 6 (steps: negotiating)

When the offer arrives, thank them, and ask for a day or two. Make one clear counter with a reason. If the base is firm, ask about the signing bonus, start date or certification budget. Get the final terms in writing. Never accept on the spot, lie about salary, or invent a competing offer.

## Segment 7 (outro)

That completes the Data Scientist path. From SQL to a presented, deployed model, you've built real work. From here, you can go deeper into quantitative research, build AI applications, or branch into data engineering.
