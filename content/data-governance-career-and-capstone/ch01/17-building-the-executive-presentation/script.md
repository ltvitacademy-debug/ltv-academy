# Lesson 17 — Building the Executive Presentation · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Fourteen deliverables and a strategy document still aren't a
presentation. This lesson compresses all of it into a story Dana
Whitfield can deliver in minutes.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (what executives listen for)

Boards listen for four things: time compressed to seconds, not
slides; risk stated in dollars, not compliance jargon; a roadmap that
reads as momentum, not a wishlist; and one specific ask instead of
vague support.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (beats 1-2)

The hook opens exactly where Lesson 1 did: a six-week DSAR, two
conflicting order histories, a problem that already reached the
board. The cost of inaction follows immediately — staff hours already
spent, framed against UK GDPR's real penalty ceiling.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (beats 3-4)

What we built isn't fourteen lesson titles — it's four outcomes:
named owners, one reconciled golden record, governed AI pilots
instead of unmonitored ones, and a real architecture for Summit. The
roadmap presents four phases as proven momentum, not a plan still on
paper.

## S5 · STEPS CARD (beat 5 — the ask)

The ask is three specific things: fund the Lesson 14 architecture
into production, staff Marcus Ibe's governance office for its
permanent operational work, and renew Dana Whitfield's mandate for
the next phase.

## S6 · STEPS CARD (why the hook repeats)

The same incident opens every version of this presentation because
the room already half-remembers it — it reached the board once
already. That makes the ask land as finishing something known, not
funding something new.
