# Lesson 24 — The Governance Interview Landscape

## The common rounds

Not every company runs every round, and the order varies, but the same building blocks show up again and again:

1. **Recruiter screen (20-30 minutes).** Fit, logistics, interest, work authorization. Not technical, but you can be filtered out here.
2. **Hiring manager or team conversation.** Your background, your capstone, and how you think about the team's actual governance problems.
3. **Technical / data quality screen.** SQL and profiling questions, live or timed. Lesson 26 practices this directly.
4. **Scenario-based governance conversation.** Open-ended "how would you handle..." questions about ownership, conflicting definitions, or access requests. Lessons 25 and 27 practice this.
5. **Stakeholder or communication exercise.** Sometimes a short presentation, sometimes a role-play of explaining a policy to a skeptical business stakeholder.
6. **Case study or take-home.** For example, "draft a glossary entry for this ambiguous term" or "design a RACI matrix for this scenario."
7. **Behavioral and values conversation.** Often woven into several rounds rather than held as one separate conversation.
8. **Offer.** Comes later in the process; Lesson 32, later in this chapter, covers that stage specifically.

## Role flavor changes the mix

Job titles are inconsistent, so read the actual description rather than assuming what a title means.

| Role flavor | Common emphasis |
|---|---|
| Data Steward | Domain knowledge, day-to-day quality and definition disputes |
| Governance Analyst | SQL and profiling, documentation, process discipline |
| Governance Manager / Lead | Stakeholder management, KPI reporting, operating-model design |
| Privacy / Compliance-focused | Classification, regulation (GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA), access governance |

A steward-flavored role will lean heavily on scenario and domain questions; a governance-manager-flavored role will lean on stakeholder and communication rounds; a privacy-flavored role will test classification and access-model reasoning specifically.

## What interviewers are usually evaluating

Across every round, a handful of underlying questions repeat:

- **Can you find and describe a real data problem?** SQL, profiling, reading a results grid correctly.
- **Can you reason about ownership and conflict?** Who should decide, versus who should just execute.
- **Can you document something so someone else can use it?** A glossary entry, a lineage diagram, a policy that a non-technical reader can follow.
- **Can you work with people who don't report to you?** Governance work runs almost entirely through influence, not authority.

## A preparation plan

A realistic three-week version, adaptable to your own timeline:

- **Week 1.** Resume, LinkedIn, and portfolio polish (Lessons 20-22). Draft and time your 90-second capstone pitch (Lesson 23).
- **Week 2.** SQL and data quality practice daily (Lesson 26 as a starting set, then more on your own). Review the role-flavor table above against real job postings.
- **Week 3.** Scenario-question practice out loud (Lessons 25 and 27), one mock interview with a friend or mentor, and a review pass through every portfolio artifact (Lesson 22's checklist).

Keep applying throughout — don't wait until you feel fully ready before submitting applications.
