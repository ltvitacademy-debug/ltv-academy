# Lesson 19 — Data Governance Career Paths and Roles · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Welcome to Chapter Two: Career Preparation. This lesson maps out the actual job titles in a data governance career, and — more importantly — which ones match the work you've already done in this path.

## S2 · STEPS — Day-to-day roles

Four titles cover most of the hands-on work. Data steward: the day-to-day custodian of one domain's quality and definitions. Governance analyst: writes quality rules, profiles tables, maintains the glossary. Lineage analyst: documents how data moves and investigates when a number looks wrong. Quality analyst or engineer: a deeper, more technical version of the analyst role, focused on SQL checks.

## S3 · STEPS — Specialist, platform, and leadership roles

Beyond the day-to-day roles: metadata and catalog analysts own the glossary and dictionary. Privacy and compliance analysts classify data and design access models. MDM analysts build golden records. Many postings name the platform directly — Purview, Snowflake, Unity Catalog, Fabric. And governance managers run the whole operating model: councils, KPIs, stakeholders.

## S4 · STEPS — Matching roles to what you've built

Match the posting's emphasis to your own work. Ownership and dispute resolution maps to Foundations' roles chapter. SQL quality checks map to Data Quality Management. Glossary and catalog work maps to Metadata Management. Tracing data across systems maps to Data Lineage and Impact Analysis.

## S5 · STEPS — Where people actually come from

There's no single governance degree. People land here from BI and reporting analyst roles, database administration, business analysis, QA and data-quality testing, and compliance or audit backgrounds. What gets you hired is being able to show the specific governance work, not the prior title.
