## The day-to-day roles

These are the titles you'll see most often on job boards, and the ones this path has already prepared you to talk about with specifics, not generalities:

- **Data Steward.** The day-to-day custodian of quality and definitions for one business domain — resolving disputes about what a term means, keeping the glossary current, chasing down data quality issues. This is the **Responsible (R)** role in the RACI structure that Data Governance Foundations, Lesson 17 covers.
- **Data Governance Analyst.** Writes and runs data quality rules, profiles tables, maintains glossary and catalog entries, and supports the operating model day to day. This title covers a lot of the hands-on work from Data Quality Management and Metadata Management and Business Glossary.
- **Data Lineage Analyst.** Documents how data moves and transforms across systems, performs impact analysis before changes, and runs root-cause analysis when a number looks wrong — the work from Data Lineage and Impact Analysis.
- **Data Quality Analyst / Engineer.** Specializes in profiling, writing SQL checks, and automating quality monitoring — essentially a deeper, more technical version of the analyst role, focused on the Data Quality Management course's material.

## Specialist, platform, and leadership roles

- **Metadata / Data Catalog Analyst.** Owns the business glossary, data dictionary, and catalog adoption — the work from Metadata Management and Business Glossary, Chapters 2 through 5.
- **Data Privacy / Compliance Analyst.** Classifies sensitive data, designs access models, and manages retention and data-subject requests — the work from Data Security, Privacy and Classification.
- **Master Data Management (MDM) Analyst.** Builds golden records, matching and survivorship rules, and governs reference data — the work from Master and Reference Data Management.
- **Platform-specific governance titles.** Many job postings name the tool directly: "Microsoft Purview Governance Analyst," "Snowflake Data Governance Engineer," "Databricks Unity Catalog Administrator," "Fabric Governance Lead." Same underlying skills, applied to a specific platform's catalog and access controls.
- **Data Governance Manager / Lead.** Runs the governance operating model itself: councils, KPIs, stakeholder communication, and program maturity — the work from Data Governance Program Management. This role is usually **Accountable (A)** at the program level, not just within one domain.
- **Data Governance Architect / Director.** The long-term destination for this path, combining deep technical breadth with enterprise strategy. Lesson 34, later in this chapter, covers that path specifically.

## Titles vary — read the description, not just the title

No two companies use these titles identically. A "Data Governance Analyst" at one company might spend all day writing SQL checks; at another, the same title might be almost entirely glossary and documentation work. Read the actual bullet points in a posting and match them against what you've built in this path, rather than assuming a title means one fixed thing.

## Where people actually come from

There's no single "governance degree." People land in these roles from backgrounds like: BI or reporting analyst, database administrator, business analyst, QA or data-quality tester, and compliance or audit. What gets you hired isn't the prior title — it's being able to show the specific governance work: a glossary you built, SQL checks you wrote, a lineage diagram you traced, a RACI matrix you can defend.
