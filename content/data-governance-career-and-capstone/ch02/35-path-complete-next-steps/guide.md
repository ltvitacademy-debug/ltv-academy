## How the four groupings built on each other

**Core Foundations (Courses 1-6)** gave you the vocabulary and the
fundamentals: what governance is and isn't, how to measure and improve
data quality, how to document meaning through metadata and a
glossary, how to trace lineage and impact, how to manage master and
reference data as a single source of truth, and how to classify and
protect sensitive data. Nothing after this group would have made sense
without it.

**Platform Implementation (Courses 7-12)** took every one of those
concepts and made them concrete on the tools governance actually runs
on in a real job — Microsoft Purview, Microsoft Fabric, Databricks
Unity Catalog, Snowflake, Power BI, and the governance services native
to Azure and AWS. This is the group that turns "I understand data
classification" into "I've actually configured a classification rule
in a real platform."

**Advanced & Specialized Governance (Courses 13-15)** pushed past
day-to-day execution into the newest and most senior territory in the
field: governing AI and machine learning systems, running a governance
*program* rather than just governance *tasks*, and designing
governance architecture at an enterprise, cross-platform level — the
rungs this course's Lesson 34 mapped onto a realistic career ladder.

**The Capstone (Course 16, this course)** brought every one of those
fifteen courses together on one realistic scenario in Chapter 1, and
then turned that finished work into something you can actually use to
get hired, present, and grow in Chapter 2 — resumes, portfolios,
interview practice across every technical area this path covered,
certifications, compensation, your first 90 days, and the career
ladder beyond it.

## What finishing this path actually means

You can now take an organization's messy, undocumented, inconsistently
defined data and build a real governance program around it: identify
what matters, define it clearly, document where it lives and where it
flows, classify and protect what's sensitive, implement all of that on
real platforms, extend it to AI systems, run it as an actual program
with stakeholders and KPIs, and describe how it should scale at an
architecture level. That is not a small thing, and it is not a
beginner's skill set — it's the complete, defensible foundation of a
real data governance career.

## Where you go from here

There's no assigned next course, and that's intentional — this is the
end of the path, not a pause in it. A few honest directions, without
ranking them: put the capstone and everything you built in front of
real job postings; keep deepening on whichever platform or specialty
from Courses 7-15 matches where you want to work; pursue one of the
certifications from Lesson 30 if it supports a direction you're
already leaning toward; or simply start doing the work, since the
first 90 days from Lesson 33 will teach you more than any additional
course could at this point. All of them are valid. None of them are
required before you're ready — you already are.
