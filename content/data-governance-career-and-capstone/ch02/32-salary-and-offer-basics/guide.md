## Why no specific numbers appear in this lesson

Salary figures go stale fast, vary enormously by country, metro area,
industry, and company size, and this catalog has no reliable way to
verify a precise number as current for your specific market. Rather
than quote a figure that might be wrong by the time you read it, this
lesson teaches the research process other career-prep courses in this
catalog already use — building your *own* range from live, current
sources, rather than trusting one invented or outdated figure.

## Why governance pay varies more than some other data roles

Data governance titles and compensation vary unusually widely for a
few concrete reasons worth knowing before you research your own range:

- **Titles aren't standardized.** "Data Governance Analyst," "Data
  Steward," "Data Quality Analyst," and "Information Governance
  Specialist" can describe very similar work at different companies —
  or very different scopes of work under the same title.
- **Regulated industries tend to formalize and compensate the role
  more.** Financial services, healthcare, and insurance companies
  often have mature, well-funded governance functions because
  regulation requires it — a governance role at a heavily regulated
  company can look different from the same title at a company without
  that pressure.
- **The role sometimes sits inside IT, sometimes inside a dedicated
  data/analytics function, and sometimes inside compliance or legal** —
  which department it reports into affects both the pay band it's
  measured against and what skills get valued most.

When researching your own range, search using several of these title
variants, not just one, and note which industry and department each
posting sits in — that context matters as much as the number itself.

## Building a range before you're in an offer conversation

The research habit is the same one this catalog's other capstone
courses teach, applied to governance-specific title variants:

| Source type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Aggregated salary sites | Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Payscale, LinkedIn Salary |
| Government labor data | Your country's official labor statistics agency (for example, the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics) |
| Direct market signal | Live job postings in your metro area that list a range, searched under several governance-adjacent titles |

Build a low-to-high band from at least two independent sources, for
your actual metro area and your actual experience level — not a
single national average, and not a number remembered from somewhere
without a source behind it.

## Answering "what are your salary expectations"

The same principle this catalog's other career-prep courses teach
applies directly here: whoever names a number first anchors the rest
of the conversation. Where possible, redirect to the budgeted range
("I'd love to hear what range you had in mind for this role"); if
pushed, answer with your own researched range rather than a single
invented figure.

## Evaluating and countering an offer

Once an offer exists, you have real leverage you didn't have during
interviews. A respectful counter has the same consistent parts
regardless of role: thank them for the offer, state your researched
range (not a demand), give a specific reason grounded in your research
or the role's scope, and stay collaborative rather than issuing an
ultimatum.

```
Thank you for the offer -- I'm excited about this role.

Based on my research for Data Governance Analyst roles in
this market, similar positions tend to run somewhat higher
on the base.

Is there room to move the base toward that range, or adjust
other parts of the package to bridge the gap?

I'm ready to move forward once we're aligned.
```

Before accepting or countering, look at the whole offer, not just the
base number: signing bonus, bonus target, remote/hybrid flexibility,
the timeline to your first review, and any certification or
conference-reimbursement budget — a real benefit in a field where
Lesson 30's certifications can be expensive to pursue on your own.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Range (salary) | A realistic low-to-high band for a role in a specific market, built from research — never a single invented number |
| Title variance | The way governance roles use inconsistent titles across companies, which widens the range you need to research |
| Total compensation | The full value of an offer, including base, bonuses, and benefits — not just the base salary figure |
