# Lesson 33 — Your First 90 Days in a Governance Role

**Chapter 2 · Career Preparation · Lesson 33 of 35**

## What you'll learn

- Why the first 90 days of a governance role is mostly about learning
  the landscape, not fixing it
- A realistic shape for days 1-30, 31-60, and 61-90 that applies
  across most governance analyst and steward roles
- Why trying to overhaul policy in week two is one of the most common
  new-governance-hire mistakes
- How to pick one small, visible win early without overstepping a
  role you're still learning

## The instinct to fix everything immediately is the wrong one

A new governance hire usually walks into an organization with real,
visible problems — inconsistent definitions, undocumented lineage,
access that's never been reviewed. The instinct to fix all of it
immediately is understandable, and it's also the most common mistake
in the role's first 90 days. You don't yet know which problems are
already being worked on, which ones are politically sensitive, or
which "obvious fix" would quietly break something three teams depend
on. The first 90 days is mostly about earning the standing to make
changes later, by first understanding the organization accurately.

## Days 1-30: learn the landscape

The first month is almost entirely about listening and mapping, not
producing:

- **Meet the people whose names you'll use constantly** — your direct
  manager, the data owners for your assigned domains, the stewards
  already in place, and the engineering or BI contacts you'll work
  with most.
- **Read everything that already exists** before suggesting anything
  new — existing glossary entries, data dictionaries, classification
  schemes, past governance council minutes if you can get access.
- **Ask why things are the way they are**, not just what they are. A
  confusing-looking process often has a real reason behind it that
  isn't obvious yet.
- **Start a running list of questions and observations** — don't act
  on them yet, just capture them so nothing gets lost once you do know
  enough to act.

## Days 31-60: start contributing on scoped work

The second month is where you start producing, but on work that's
scoped and low-risk rather than broad and structural:

- **Take on a defined piece of existing work** — documenting one data
  dictionary entry, writing one glossary definition, running one data
  quality check someone already asked for — rather than proposing a
  new initiative from scratch.
- **Get your first contribution reviewed and approved** through
  whatever process already exists, even if it feels slow. This builds
  trust in your judgment before you ask people to trust bigger changes.
- **Start noticing patterns across the individual pieces of work**
  you're doing — the same kind of question keeps coming up, or the
  same team keeps needing the same kind of help.

## Days 61-90: own something end to end

By the third month, you should be ready to take ownership of something
small but complete, rather than only contributing pieces:

- **Propose one scoped improvement** based on a pattern you noticed in
  month two — not a sweeping overhaul, one concrete fix with a clear
  owner and a clear before/after.
- **Run a small piece of the governance process yourself** — leading
  one review meeting, owning one classification decision end to end,
  presenting one update to a stakeholder (the skills from Lesson 29).
- **Ask for feedback explicitly.** A direct "how is this going so
  far, and what would you want me doing differently" conversation with
  your manager at the 90-day mark is more useful than waiting for a
  formal review cycle to surface the same information later.

## Picking a first small win

A good first win is specific, visible, low-risk, and attributable to
you without requiring anyone else's cooperation to fail — something
like cleaning up one confusing glossary entry that's been bothering an
analyst team, not "redesign the classification scheme." Small, real,
and finished beats ambitious and half-done every time in a first 90
days.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Landscape learning | The listening-and-mapping work of the first 30 days, before proposing changes |
| Scoped contribution | A defined, low-risk piece of existing work taken on to build trust before attempting something larger |
| First small win | A specific, visible, finished piece of work early in a role that establishes credibility |
