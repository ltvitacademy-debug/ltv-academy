## Writing a governance LinkedIn headline

LinkedIn gives you roughly 220 characters for your headline, and most people waste it repeating their current job title. Use it for searchable terms instead:

"Data Governance Analyst | Business Glossary, Data Quality, Lineage | Built an enterprise governance program end to end"

That one line gives a recruiter three things to search on and one concrete claim to click through to.

## The About section

Three short paragraphs works well:

1. **Who you are and what you're targeting.** One or two sentences on your background and the kind of governance role you want.
2. **What you built, with results stated honestly.** Name two or three specific deliverables — the glossary, the SQL quality checks, the lineage documentation — each with one concrete detail (a count, a scope, a technique), not a vague claim.
3. **What you're looking for and how to reach you.**

## What goes in Featured

A data science portfolio usually centers on a GitHub repository full of runnable code. A governance portfolio is different, and that difference is worth being upfront about rather than forcing a mismatch: most of what you build in governance work is **documents and diagrams** — a glossary, a data dictionary, a RACI matrix, a lineage diagram, a policy, a strategy deck — alongside a smaller amount of actual SQL. Your Featured section should reflect that honestly:

- A link to a portfolio deck or document (PDF, Notion page, or similar) containing excerpts of your real deliverables
- A link to a small code repository containing just your SQL data quality checks, if you have one
- Optionally, a link to or screenshot of your lineage diagram or governance operating-model chart

## Disclosing a training scenario honestly

The capstone in this course is built around a fictional scenario — LTV Global — used specifically so you could practice the full scope of a governance program without needing access to a real company's live systems. Say so plainly in your portfolio and on LinkedIn: "Training capstone, fictional organization." This protects you in interviews, where you'll be asked follow-up questions about every artifact, and it's a strength, not a weakness — it shows you can build the full scope of a governance program, not just one narrow piece of one.

## Common mistakes

- Listing a skill in your headline you can't defend for five minutes in an interview
- A portfolio that's all policy prose and no concrete artifact (no actual glossary entries, no actual SQL)
- Forgetting to disclose that the capstone data and organization are fictional
- Different numbers or claims across your resume, LinkedIn, and portfolio document

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Featured section | LinkedIn's area for pinning links to work samples, directly below your About section |
| Portfolio deck | A document or slide deck presenting excerpts of real governance deliverables, used in place of a code repository |
| Fictional-scenario disclosure | Honestly stating that a capstone's organization and data are a training exercise, not a real employer |
