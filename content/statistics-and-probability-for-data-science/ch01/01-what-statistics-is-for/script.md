# Script — What Statistics Is For

## Segment 1 (title)

Welcome to Statistics and Probability for Data Science, step three of the Data Scientist path. You already know how to query data and wrangle it in Python. This course answers the next question: what can you honestly conclude from data once you have it? No prior statistics is assumed.

## Segment 2 (steps: three jobs)

Statistics does a few jobs. First, it describes: an average, a spread, a chart, a summary of what you actually have. Second, it infers: using the data in front of you to say something about a larger group you never fully observed. Third, it quantifies uncertainty: attaching an honest "how sure are we" to every conclusion. That last job is what separates a data scientist from someone with a calculator.

## Segment 3 (code: population and sample)

Here is the core problem in code. We build an illustrative population of ten thousand order values, then pretend we can only afford to look at fifty at a time. We print the true population mean, then the mean of three different random samples.

## Segment 4 (code: output)

The population mean is sixty point four four. The three samples give sixty point seven, fifty-eight point seven seven, and fifty-eight point three eight. Nobody made a mistake. Every sample is a slightly different random slice, so sample averages wobble. In real work you only ever see one sample and must decide how far to trust it. That question drives most of this course.

## Segment 5 (steps: road ahead)

Here is the road ahead. We start by describing data honestly, then learn the language of probability, then see how samples relate to populations, and finally learn hypothesis testing, a disciplined way to separate a real effect from noise. Along the way, you'll practice in numpy, scipy, and pandas.
