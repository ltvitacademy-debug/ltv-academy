You have a correct analysis, but that is only half the job. A result nobody can understand or act on has no value, and in a hiring process the write-up is often the only thing a reviewer reads. So let's finish the capstone properly.

First, a results table. Keep every number that supports your conclusion in one place: the estimate, the ninety-five percent interval, the p-value, and whether it passes the Bonferroni bar. Churn is down two point eight points and passes. Spend shows no detectable change and does not. Report both, including the one that did not work out.

Second, one honest chart. Show churn for both groups with ninety-five percent intervals, and start the bars at zero. Notice the two error bars overlap slightly, yet the test on the difference was significant. Overlapping intervals do not mean a difference is unreal, so put the interval for the difference on the slide.

Third, the stakeholder summary. Lead with the answer: the series cut churn by about two point eight points. Then the evidence, a randomized test of five thousand customers. Then what we did not find, and the caveats: a wide interval, ninety days only, and illustrative data. Finish with a recommendation. Skip the jargon.

Fourth, package it. A reader should be able to run your work, so include a README, the data script, the analysis script, the results table, and the chart, and say clearly that the data is simulated.
