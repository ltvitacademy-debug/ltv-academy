# Script — Sales Pipeline Reporting

## Segment 1 (title)

Everything so far was a tool. This chapter puts those tools to work on real questions — starting with the most common one in any sales org: how healthy is the pipeline?

## Segment 2 (steps: the four questions)

A sales leader is really asking four things. How much is open — total pipeline value. Is it enough — pipeline coverage against quota. Is it moving — stage velocity. And what's at risk — deals stuck or past their expected close date. Every pipeline report should answer at least one of these clearly.

## Segment 3 (code: the report foundation)

The foundation is simple. Start from an Opportunities report type, filter Stage to exclude Closed Won and Closed Lost, group by Stage, and summarize Sum of Amount. Switch to Matrix and add a second grouping — Owner or Close Month — and you've gone from "how much" to "whose, and when."

## Segment 4 (steps: stuck deals)

Pipeline value alone hides a real problem. A deal sitting in Negotiation for ninety days looks identical to one that arrived yesterday on a plain stage total. Days in current stage, a standard Opportunity field, is the filter that catches that — turning a snapshot into an early-warning report.

## Segment 5 (steps: the dashboard)

A working pipeline dashboard combines four pieces. A funnel chart for pipeline by stage. A metric widget for the one number — total open pipeline. A table for deals at risk. And usually a bar chart by owner, so a manager sees both the aggregate picture and exactly where to focus.

## Segment 6 (outro)

Pipeline reporting is reports and dashboards doing their actual job — turning raw opportunity data into decisions. Next up: the same approach, applied to service and support instead of sales.
