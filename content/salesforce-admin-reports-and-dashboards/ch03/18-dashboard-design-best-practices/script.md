# Script — Dashboard Design Best Practices

## Segment 1 (title)

Every tool from this chapter can build a technically correct dashboard that's still useless, because nobody can tell what it's saying at a glance. This lesson is the habits that fix that.

## Segment 2 (screenshot: well-organized dashboard)

Here's a real dashboard doing it right. Key totals — pipeline, open pipe, won deals — sit right at the top, above the fold. Supporting trend charts and detail tables come after. The most important number is the first thing a viewer sees, not the eighth.

## Segment 3 (screenshot: the builder, for restraint)

And here's the builder for that same dashboard. Notice what's not crammed in — a handful of widgets, each answering one clear question, with View Report links underneath for anyone who needs more than the dashboard shows at a glance.

## Segment 4 (steps: the habits)

Four habits do most of the work. Lead with what matters most, above the fold. Choose restraint over completeness — every widget is one more thing competing for attention. Keep chart types and color meaning consistent across widgets, so a pattern learned once transfers everywhere. And name widgets for what they tell a viewer, not just the report behind them.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Get those four right and a dashboard earns a spot people actually open every morning, instead of one they learn to scroll past. That closes dashboards. Next up: putting reports and dashboards to work on real business questions, starting with sales pipeline.
