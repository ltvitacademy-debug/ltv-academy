# Script — Report Folders and Sharing

## Segment 1 (title)

A report nobody else can see isn't much better than a personal spreadsheet. In Salesforce, who sees a report comes down to one thing: the folder it's saved in.

## Segment 2 (screenshot: folder list)

Here's the real folder list on the Dashboards tab — the Reports tab works the same way. Created by Me, Private, Shared with Me, All Folders down the left side. Every report and dashboard lives in exactly one of these, and that folder is what controls its audience.

## Segment 3 (screenshot: Share folder — adding access)

Open a folder's menu and choose Share, and you get this dialog. Pick Share With — a role, a public group, a user — search for the name, set an access level, and click Share to add them.

## Segment 4 (screenshot: Share folder — who can access)

Scroll down the same dialog and Who Can Access lists everyone already sharing this folder, with their current access level right next to their name. Change it with the dropdown, or click the X to revoke it entirely — nothing else to configure.

## Segment 5 (steps: access levels)

Three access levels are worth knowing cold. Viewer is read-only. Editor can build and modify reports in the folder. Manager has full control, including deciding who else gets access. Pick the lowest level that still lets someone do their job.

## Segment 6 (steps: inheritance and privacy)

Two more things to remember. Subfolders inherit their parent folder's sharing automatically, so a shared folder's children are shared too unless you deliberately change that. And anything not ready for an audience belongs in a private or hidden folder — the moment a report lands in a shared folder, it's visible to everyone that folder is shared with.

## Segment 7 (outro)

Get folder sharing right and you control exactly who sees what, with almost no ongoing maintenance. Next up: getting reports to people automatically, on a schedule, without anyone having to go looking for them.
