# Lesson 11 — Endorsement: Promoted and Certified · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Every workspace fills up with reports and datasets that all look equally trustworthy — until you open them. Power BI's endorsement system fixes that with a visible badge that follows content wherever it goes. There are three levels, and the first is Promoted.

## S2 · SCREENSHOT (promoting content)

Any owner of a dataset or report can set it to Promoted, right from that item's settings — no special permission needed. It's a self-service signal: the person who made it is saying "this is ready to use."

## S3 · SCREENSHOT (certifying content)

Certified sits one level above. The setting looks almost identical, but for most users it's grayed out — certifying content requires a permission an admin has to grant first, because it represents an organizational review, not just one person's opinion.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (three levels)

So there are three levels. None, the default, with no trust signal either way. Promoted, any owner's own call. And Certified, an org-level review that requires a granted permission — exactly who gets that permission is next lesson's topic.

## S5 · SCREENSHOT (badges everywhere)

These badges aren't confined to one settings screen. The same Promoted and Certified badges show up in search results, in a workspace's content list, in the app itself — wherever a user encounters the item, they see the same signal.

## S6 · OUTRO CARD

That consistency is the whole point — a split-second trust decision without opening the file. Next lesson: certified datasets specifically, and who in your organization should actually hold the power to grant that top badge.
