# Lesson 9 — Sharing and Permissions · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Sharing and permissions — how one report reaches one person, without touching the whole workspace.

## S2 · STEPS — Two different ways to grant access

Power BI has two distinct ways to let someone see content. Workspace access gives a role — Admin, Member, Contributor, Viewer — across everything in a workspace. Item-level sharing hands one specific report or dashboard to someone who might have no workspace access at all. Sharing is the more common day-to-day action, and the one most prone to governance drift, because it's easy to share something once and forget.

## S3 · SCREENSHOT — Sending a link

Share opens the Send link dialog. The link-type selector at the top is the real access control — it determines who can even use the link, before any names are entered: people in your organization, specific people, or people with existing access.

## S4 · STEPS — Two toggles worth checking

Below the link type, two more settings matter for governance. Allow recipients to share this item — resharing. And allow recipients to build content with the data — build permission, letting them create new reports off the same semantic model. Neither should be left on autopilot.

## S5 · SCREENSHOT — Managing and auditing access

The same dialog's options menu exposes Manage permissions — the screen that actually lists who has access and how they got it. Send link only grants access going forward; Manage permissions is where you see, and revoke, everything already granted.

## S6 · SCREENSHOT — Workspace access, for contrast

Workspace access is managed separately, from the workspace's own header bar — Manage access controls the broader workspace roles from Lesson 3, a coarser grant than sharing one item.

## S7 · STEPS — A governance lens on sharing

Default to the narrowest link type that still works. Treat resharing and build permission as deliberate choices, not defaults left untouched. And revisit Manage permissions periodically — especially for anything shared with the whole organization, since that's also the easiest to forget about.
