## This course, chapter by chapter, proven in one case

- **Chapter 1 — The Incident Response Lifecycle.** NIST's phases, named directly in the case:
  preparation already in place, detection and analysis at 2:47 PM, containment, then eradication
  and recovery.
- **Chapter 2 — Preparation: Building an IR Plan.** The plan, playbook, and RACI that meant an
  on-call analyst knew exactly what to check and who to call, instead of improvising on the spot.
- **Chapter 3 — Detection & Analysis Deep Dive.** The triage checklist, timeline reconstruction,
  and scoping discipline that turned one alert into a two-entity, confirmed incident.
- **Chapter 4 — Containment Strategies.** The evidence-safe order of operations — capture, then
  isolate, then disable — that protected what Lesson 30's forensics needed.
- **Chapter 5 — Eradication & Recovery.** The judgment call that SYSTEM-level persistence meant a
  rebuild from verified-clean backup, not live cleanup, plus the validation that came before
  WKSTN-FIN-14 went back into production.
- **Chapter 6 — Post-Incident Activity.** The lessons-learned meeting and after-action report,
  with four owned, dated follow-up items instead of a narrative no one acts on.
- **Chapter 7 — Threat Hunting Methodology.** The hypothesis-driven loop and MITRE ATT&CK map
  that found Daniel Oyelaran's account — a compromise the original alert never caught — and the
  habit of turning that find into a permanent rule.
- **Chapter 8 — this capstone.** All seven of the above, run together as one incident, because
  that is the only way any of them were ever meant to be used.

## The other six courses, underneath all of it

None of Chapter 8's case study works without what came before this course. **How IT Teams Work**
is the ticketing and escalation discipline behind every "who do I call" decision. **IT, Networking
& Cybersecurity Fundamentals** is what makes an IP address, a geolocation, and a sign-in log
readable in the first place. **Windows, Linux & Security Administration** is the scheduled-task
and account-permission knowledge that made `MicrosoftEdgeUpdateTaskMachineUA` recognizable as an
attacker's work, not Windows' own update task. **Network Security, Endpoint Protection & Threat
Detection** is Defender for Endpoint's isolation action and the attack patterns it's built to
catch. **SOC Operations, SIEM & Log Analysis** is the alert-triage workflow this whole case ran on
before it ever touched Sentinel specifically. **Microsoft Sentinel, Defender XDR & KQL** is the
analytics rule, entity mapping, investigation graph, and every KQL query in Lessons 29 through 31.
This course is where all six of those become one incident, handled start to finish.

## What you're job-ready for

A graduate who can do what Lessons 29 through 31 just did — triage a real alert, scope an
incident past the one entity it names, contain without destroying evidence, find and remove an
actual root cause, validate before returning to production, write a report with owned follow-up
items, and run a hypothesis-driven hunt that catches what the SIEM missed — has the working
toolkit of a **Junior SOC Analyst** or **Cybersecurity Analyst**. That is not a certificate
claim; it is the specific, demonstrated set of judgment calls this entire seven-course stage was
built to produce.
