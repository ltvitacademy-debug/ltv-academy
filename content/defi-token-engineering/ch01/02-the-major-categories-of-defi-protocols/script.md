# Script — The Major Categories of DeFi Protocols

## Segment 1 (title)

DeFi isn't one thing — it's five categories of protocol, built on top of each other. Know these five, and you can place almost any new protocol you encounter without having to learn it from scratch.

## Segment 2 (steps: the five categories)

DEXs and AMMs swap tokens by formula instead of an order book — Uniswap and Curve. Lending and borrowing protocols like Aave and Compound let you supply for interest or borrow against collateral. Derivatives and perpetuals, like GMX and dYdX, trade price exposure without holding the asset. Stablecoins hold a steady value, whether fiat-backed like USDC or crypto-collateralized like DAI. And yield aggregators like Yearn automate moving capital between all of the above.

## Segment 3 (code: one dollar's journey)

Watch one dollar move: swap USDC for ETH on an AMM, supply that ETH as collateral on a lending protocol, borrow a stablecoin against it, then deploy that stablecoin into a yield vault that itself deposits into an AMM pool. Four categories, one sequence, no bank and no approval anywhere in it.

## Segment 4 (code: why these two categories)

Every category ultimately solves one of two problems: how strangers trade without an order book, solved by AMM math, and how strangers lend without a credit check, solved by requiring collateral instead of trust. Derivatives, stablecoins, and yield products are all built from those two primitives — which is exactly why this course spends the next two full chapters on AMMs and lending specifically.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Five categories, two load-bearing primitives. Next up: composability — how these categories actually stack together, like the money-lego example you just saw.
