# Script — What Makes DeFi Different

## Segment 1 (title)

A bank holds your money and promises to give it back. A DeFi protocol never holds it at all — your wallet signs every transaction directly against public code. That one mechanical difference is where everything else in this course starts.

## Segment 2 (code: custodial vs self-custodial)

Open an account with a bank or a centralized exchange, and you're trusting a company's solvency and goodwill — FTX's users learned in 2022 that a frozen withdrawal button can erase "ownership" overnight. A self-custodial protocol can't freeze your wallet or reject your application, because there's no account and no company in the loop. You hold the asset; the contract just executes.

## Segment 3 (steps: the four pillars)

Four properties define DeFi: non-custodial, so the protocol never takes control of your asset; permissionless, so no approval process gates who can use it; transparent, so every trade and every line of code sits on a public ledger; and composable, so protocols stack like building blocks, which Lesson 3 covers in depth.

## Segment 4 (code: trust-minimized, not trustless)

"Trustless" is the marketing word. The honest one is "trust-minimized" — you still trust the code has no critical bug and that no admin key can drain it. DeFi doesn't eliminate trust, it relocates it from legal promises to verifiable code, and audited protocols have still lost hundreds of millions to exploits.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Non-custodial, permissionless, transparent, composable — and trust relocated, not removed. Next up: the major categories of protocols built on top of those four properties.
