# Script — Utility vs. Governance Tokens

## Segment 1 (title)

Utility tokens perform a function the protocol needs -- gas, access, collateral. Governance tokens grant a say over how the protocol changes. Most real tokens do both jobs at once.

## Segment 2 (steps: utility token use cases)

Gas and fee payment, access or feature unlocks, staking collateral, and fee discounts. None of these require any voting rights -- a utility token's value comes from needing it, not controlling anything.

## Segment 3 (steps: governance token use cases)

Proposal rights, voting power weighted by tokens held or delegated, treasury control, and parameter changes like interest rate curves or emission schedules -- decided by vote instead of a centralized team.

## Segment 4 (code: the fee switch)

A fee switch routes a share of protocol revenue to token holders, usually through buyback-and-burn. Twenty percent of five hundred thousand dollars in monthly fees is a hundred-thousand-dollar buyback budget -- at two dollars fifty a token, that burns forty thousand tokens.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Utility is about needing the token; governance is about controlling the protocol; a fee switch is how governance decisions turn into real, revenue-funded burns. Next up: vesting schedules and cliffs -- how token allocations actually unlock over time.
