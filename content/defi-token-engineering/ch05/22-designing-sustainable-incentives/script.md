# Script — Designing Sustainable Incentives

## Segment 1 (title)

The question behind every incentive design: where does the yield actually come from? Pull together Chapter 4's emissions and this chapter's supply math, and a few warning signs separate sustainable designs from unsustainable ones.

## Segment 2 (steps: red flags)

Rewards paid entirely in the protocol's own inflationary token with no path to real revenue, advertised APY far beyond plausible usage revenue, no token sink removing supply, and new deposits structurally required to keep paying existing depositors.

## Segment 3 (code: real yield vs inflationary yield)

Protocol A advertises 150 percent APY backed almost entirely by fifteen million dollars in new token issuance against fifty thousand dollars of real revenue. Protocol B advertises a smaller 8 percent, but every dollar paid out already exists as trading-fee revenue, independent of the token's price.

## Segment 4 (steps: principles behind sustainable design)

Pay real yield from fees wherever possible, cap and taper emissions instead of leaving the tap open, build in token sinks like burns and lockups, and match lock duration to reward duration so farm-and-dump is structurally discouraged.

## Segment 5 (outro)

A design only works as long as new capital arrives faster than old capital exits -- real yield doesn't have that dependency. Next up: modeling token supply and demand as a system, not just a single formula.
