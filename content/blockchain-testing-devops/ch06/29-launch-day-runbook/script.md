# Script — Launch-Day Runbook

## Segment 1 (title)

Twenty-eight lessons -- testing, CI/CD, deployment, hosting, monitoring, incident response, audits, phased rollouts -- all converge on one day. This lesson is that day, as an actual runbook, not a recap.

## Segment 2 (steps: the morning of)

Before anyone broadcasts anything: re-run Lesson 26's checklist live, that same morning, not from memory of how things looked last week. Monitoring dashboards are open on a screen the whole team can actually see. And every multisig signer is confirmed online and reachable -- not just technically available somewhere.

## Segment 3 (steps: the deployment itself)

Deploy with Lesson 28's cap set deliberately low -- this is day one, not day ninety. Verify the contract on the block explorer immediately, confirming source matches bytecode, before anyone announces anything. A live but unverified contract invites exactly the wrong kind of attention.

## Segment 4 (steps: the first hours)

In the first hours: watch whether alerts fire, or don't -- Lesson 23's calibration gets its first real test against real activity. Resist moving the cap early just because nothing's gone wrong yet; evidence takes longer to gather than excitement does. And keep the communications channel warm, ready to post, even when there's genuinely nothing to report yet.

## Segment 5 (code: if something goes wrong)

If something does go wrong: confirm it's real, pause through the multisig, acknowledge publicly within the hour, investigate using the alert history, and run the postmortem before ever reopening. Every single step here is a lesson this course already taught -- launch day doesn't invent anything new, it just asks you to execute it for real.

## Segment 6 (outro)

That's the end of Blockchain Testing, DevOps & Deployment -- testing, CI/CD, deployment, hosting, monitoring, and now launch itself. The final course in the Blockchain Engineer path is Blockchain Engineering Capstones, where everything from this course and the ones before it comes together in real, end-to-end projects.
