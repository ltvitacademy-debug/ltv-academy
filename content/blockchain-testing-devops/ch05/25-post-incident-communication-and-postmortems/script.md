# Script — Post-Incident Communication & Postmortems

## Segment 1 (title)

Lesson 24 ended with a pause buying the team time and stopping new damage. The pause doesn't tell users what happened. That's a separate job, with its own discipline -- and it closes out this chapter.

## Segment 2 (steps: the first hour)

In the first hour, say something true, fast. Acknowledge you're aware and investigating -- silence reads as denial. State only what's actually confirmed, not speculation about cause or scope. And commit to a specific time for the next update, not a vague "we'll update you soon."

## Segment 3 (steps: anatomy of a postmortem)

A real postmortem has four parts. A timeline, built from Lesson 24's alert history -- what happened, in order. The impact, in specific numbers, not vague language. The actual root cause, the real mechanism, not just "a bug." And remediation -- what concretely changed so this specific failure can't repeat.

## Segment 4 (code: postmortem skeleton)

Public postmortems from real incidents tend to follow close to this shape: a summary, a timeline in UTC, impact, root cause, what went well, what didn't, and remediation items with an owner and a due date each. It's a document, not a press release.

## Segment 5 (steps: blameless, not toothless)

Blameless doesn't mean toothless. The goal of the process is understanding what let this happen at the system level, not finding a person to blame -- but every remediation item still needs a named owner and an actual due date, or it never gets done.
