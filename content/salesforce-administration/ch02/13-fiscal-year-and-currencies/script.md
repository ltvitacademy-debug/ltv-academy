# Script — Fiscal Year and Currencies

## Segment 1 (title)

Two more org-wide settings round out this part of the chapter, and both are decisions you make once and live with: Fiscal Year, which defines what "this quarter" means everywhere, and Multiple Currencies, which lets a global company record deals in the currency they were actually sold in.

## Segment 2 (screenshot: Company Information - Fiscal Year)

Both settings have their switch on Company Information: Fiscal Year Starts In, and Activate Multiple Currencies. A standard fiscal year just needs a start month. A custom fiscal year supports non-calendar periods, but once you enable it, you can never turn it back off.

## Segment 3 (screenshot: Conversion Rates)

Every org has one corporate currency — what everything ultimately rolls up into for org-wide reporting. Activating Multiple Currencies doesn't change that; it lets you mark additional currencies active, each with its own conversion rate back to the corporate currency.

## Segment 4 (screenshot: New Currency form)

Adding a new active currency means picking a currency type, a conversion rate, and decimal places. And once it's active, it can never be deleted — only deactivated. That's worth knowing before you activate currencies just in case.

## Segment 5 (steps: the maintenance burden)

Conversion rates aren't live — they're numbers an admin maintains by hand. If a real exchange rate moves and nobody updates the record, every opportunity in that currency rolls up at a stale number. This is ongoing work, not a one-time setup step.
