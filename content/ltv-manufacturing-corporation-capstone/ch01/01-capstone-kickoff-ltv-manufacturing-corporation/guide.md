# Capstone Kickoff: LTV Manufacturing Corporation

**Chapter 1 · Design and Configuration · Lesson 1 of 25**

Every course in this path handed you one piece: Enterprise Structures and Chart of Accounts, General Ledger, Payables, Receivables, Cash Management, Fixed Assets, Expenses, the two end-to-end process courses, Subledger Accounting, Financial Reporting, Security, Implementation Lifecycle, and Troubleshooting. This capstone is where every piece becomes one job. You are the Oracle Fusion Financials Consultant for a single fictional company, from the first configuration decision through a real month-end crisis.

## What you'll learn

- What this capstone asks of you, and why it runs as one continuous project instead of 25 unrelated exercises
- The four-chapter structure: design and configure, run the business, survive January 31, wrap up
- How to work it: your own Oracle Fusion practice environment, lesson by lesson, in order
- What "done" looks like at the end of lesson 25

## One company, start to finish

Every lesson from here forward refers back to the same fictional company — **LTV Manufacturing Corporation** — the same company you designed a chart of accounts for in Enterprise Structures and Chart of Accounts, and the same company whose purchase and sales transactions you followed in Procure-to-Pay and Order-to-Cash. Lesson 2 pulls every detail about that company into one canonical reference you'll use for the rest of this course. If you remember nothing else from those earlier courses, that's fine — lesson 2 rebuilds the full picture from scratch.

## The four chapters

1. **Chapter 1 — Design and Configuration (lessons 1-8).** You review the company, its processes, and its requirements, then design and build its enterprise structure: ledgers, legal entities, business units, chart of accounts, calendar, suppliers, customers, bank accounts, and fixed asset setup. Nothing here is a transaction yet — it's the foundation everything else stands on.
2. **Chapter 2 — Running the Business (lessons 9-17).** You run real transactions through the configuration you just built: a purchase from requisition through payment, a sale from invoicing through cash receipt, an asset capitalized and depreciated, a bank statement reconciled, and the accounting posted to the General Ledger. This is where the business actually operates for a month — January.
3. **Chapter 3 — The January 31 Challenge (lessons 18-23).** On January 31, the CFO calls: the books don't balance and the period won't close. You investigate, correct, reconcile, document, and present — the centerpiece of this entire capstone.
4. **Chapter 4 — Wrap-Up (lessons 24-25).** You review your work against the original requirements and assemble everything into a capstone portfolio you can walk an interviewer through.

## How to work this capstone

- **Work in your own Oracle Fusion practice environment**, lesson by lesson, in the order given. Chapter 3 only makes sense if you actually built Chapter 1's configuration and ran Chapter 2's transactions yourself — the problems you'll investigate are planted inside work you did, not abstract exercises.
- **Keep a running notes document.** Lesson 2's lab asks you to start a requirements document; you'll add to it in nearly every lesson through Chapter 4.
- **Don't skip ahead to Chapter 3** expecting it to stand alone. The specific account numbers, supplier names, transaction amounts, and people you'll meet investigating the crisis are the same ones you configured and transacted with in Chapters 1 and 2.

## What "done" looks like

By lesson 25 you will have: a complete enterprise structure and chart of accounts for a two-entity manufacturer, a month of real transactions across Payables, Receivables, Cash Management, Fixed Assets, and the General Ledger, a diagnosed and corrected month-end close with six real, interconnected problems resolved, a full deliverables package (Income Statement, Balance Sheet, AP Aging, AR Aging, Reconciliation Report, Issue Log, Implementation Documentation), and a presentation you could give to a real hiring panel.

## Recap

This capstone is one continuous project — one fictional company, LTV Manufacturing Corporation, carried through configuration, operation, crisis, and resolution — built across four chapters and 25 lessons. Work it in your own practice environment, in order, and keep notes as you go.
