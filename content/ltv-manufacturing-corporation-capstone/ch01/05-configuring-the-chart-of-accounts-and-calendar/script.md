# Script — Configuring the Chart of Accounts and Calendar

## Segment 1 (title)

This is the lesson every later chapter leans on. The five-segment chart of accounts design from Enterprise Structures and Chart of Accounts gets configured for real here, plus the calendar both ledgers share.

## Segment 2 (code)

Five segments: Company, Cost Center, Account, Intercompany, Future. Company values are 1000 for the US parent and 2000 for the Canadian subsidiary — the balancing segment that hooks directly into each legal entity. Cost centers 410 Assembly, 420 Fabrication, and 510 Corporate Overhead exist in both entities.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Fourteen natural accounts carry this entire capstone: cash, receivables, inventory, fixed assets and accumulated depreciation, payables, accrued liabilities, intercompany receivable and payable, revenue, overhead allocation, utilities, depreciation expense, and a brand-new account — 7850, Freight Out. Remember that one; it matters later.

## Segment 4 (steps)

The Intercompany segment mirrors the Company segment's values, restricted so an entity can never name itself as its own intercompany partner. The calendar is a standard Gregorian monthly calendar, fiscal year 2026 — and Period 1, January 2026, is this configuration's very first month-end close, ever.
