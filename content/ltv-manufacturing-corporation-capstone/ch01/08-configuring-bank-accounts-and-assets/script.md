# Script — Configuring Bank Accounts and Assets

## Segment 1 (title)

This is the last configuration lesson before Chapter 2 puts the structure to work. Two pieces remain: the bank accounts LTV disburses from and collects into, and the Fixed Assets setup needed before a single asset can be capitalized.

## Segment 2 (code)

Two bank accounts: Regions Bank in Savannah, USD, tied to the US entity and enabled for electronic payment file transmission; Royal Bank of Canada in Windsor, CAD, tied to the Canadian entity. Each links to account 1110, Cash Operating, at its entity's own Company segment value.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Five fixed asset categories, each with a genuinely different useful life: Manufacturing Equipment at ten years, Office Equipment at five, Furniture and Fixtures at seven, Computer Equipment at three, Building and Leasehold Improvements at fifteen. Two asset books, one per legal entity.

## Segment 4 (steps)

The category list isn't decorative — assigning an asset to the wrong category produces a real, calculable depreciation error. Keep Manufacturing Equipment and Office Equipment in mind specifically; the gap between their useful lives resurfaces as one of Chapter 3's six planted problems.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Chapter 1 is complete — two entities, two ledgers, two business units, a shared chart of accounts and calendar, suppliers, customers, banks, and assets. Up next, Chapter 2 begins with lesson 9: purchasing, from requisition to purchase order.
