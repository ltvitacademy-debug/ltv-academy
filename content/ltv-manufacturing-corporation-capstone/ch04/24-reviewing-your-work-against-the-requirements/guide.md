# Reviewing Your Work Against the Requirements

**Chapter 4 · Wrap-Up · Lesson 24 of 25**

Lesson 2 gave you Elena Marsh's five requirements and asked you to keep them. This lesson closes the loop: going line by line through that list, confirming what you actually built satisfies what she actually asked for.

## What you'll learn

- How to audit finished work against original requirements, not against what felt like a lot of effort
- Where this capstone's work maps to each of Elena's five requirements
- The gap between "the system works" and "the requirement is met"
- Why this review belongs in your capstone portfolio, not just in your head

## The five requirements, reviewed

**Requirement 1 — a two-entity enterprise structure.** Built in lessons 3-4: two legal entities, two primary ledgers (USD and CAD), two business units, one per real legal entity. **Met** — and the structure was exercised for real in Chapter 2's transactions, not just configured and left untouched.

**Requirement 2 — a chart of accounts tracking cost centers and supporting intercompany elimination.** Built in lesson 5: the five-segment design with Company as the balancing segment, Cost Center carrying Assembly/Fabrication/Corporate Overhead in both entities, and Intercompany mirroring Company's values. **Met** — and proven under real pressure in lesson 20-21, when the intercompany journal's absence was exactly what broke the January 31 close, and posting it was exactly what fixed it.

**Requirement 3 — supplier, customer, bank, and asset master data.** Built in lessons 6-8: four suppliers, three customers, two bank accounts, five asset categories, two asset books. **Met** — every one of these was actually used in Chapter 2, not configured as an unused formality.

**Requirement 4 — a full month of real transactions.** Built across lessons 9-17: purchasing through payment, invoicing through cash receipt, asset capitalization through depreciation, bank reconciliation, and subledger-to-GL posting. **Met** — and this is also where, candidly, six real problems entered the data, which is part of what "real" transactions actually look like.

**Requirement 5 — a clean first month-end close.** This is the one that didn't go cleanly the first time. By lesson 21, the close is fully corrected, reconciled, and completed — **met, but only after a genuine investigation and correction cycle**, not on the first attempt.

## The gap between "works" and "meets the requirement"

A system that technically runs isn't automatically a system that meets its requirements. LTV's Oracle Fusion instance processed every Chapter 2 transaction without a single system error — and still failed Requirement 5 on the first attempt, because a clean close requires more than transactions posting; it requires every subledger actually reconciling to the GL. Reviewing against requirements, not against "did anything crash," is what catches that gap before a client does.

## Why this belongs in your portfolio

An interviewer asking about this capstone isn't just asking "did you configure Oracle Fusion" — they're asking whether you can hold yourself accountable to what a stakeholder actually needed. Being able to say "here's the original requirement, here's what I built, here's where it didn't go cleanly the first time, and here's how I fixed it" is a stronger answer than "everything worked perfectly," because it's a true answer that shows judgment.
