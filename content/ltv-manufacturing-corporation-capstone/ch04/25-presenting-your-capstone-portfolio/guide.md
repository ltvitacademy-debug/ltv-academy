## Assembling the portfolio

Your portfolio for this capstone has four parts, each one already built:

1. **The company and requirements** (lessons 1-2) — a one-paragraph summary of LTV Manufacturing Corporation, its two-entity structure, and Elena Marsh's five requirements.
2. **The configuration** (lessons 3-8) — the enterprise structure diagram, the chart of accounts design, and a short list of the master data built.
3. **The operations month and the crisis** (lessons 9-23) — the transaction summary, the six-item issue log from lesson 22, and the final deliverables package: Income Statement, Balance Sheet, AP Aging, AR Aging, Reconciliation Report, Issue Log, and Implementation Documentation.
4. **The review** (lesson 24) — the five requirements, confirmed met, with an honest note on which one took real work to satisfy.

Put these four parts in a single document or deck, in this order. An interviewer should be able to read it in five minutes and understand exactly what you built, what went wrong, and what you did about it.

## Answering "walk me through a project you're proud of"

Use this structure, out loud, in under two minutes: "I built a complete two-entity Oracle Fusion Financials implementation for a fictional manufacturer — enterprise structure, chart of accounts, suppliers, customers, and a full month of real purchasing, receivables, and asset transactions. At month-end, six separate issues surfaced — an unreversed accrual, a misapplied receipt, a miscategorized asset, a bank-side duplicate payment, a Subledger Accounting configuration gap, and an unposted intercompany journal. I investigated each one back to its root cause, corrected it, reconciled every subledger to the GL, and delivered a full reporting package — including an issue log documenting exactly what was wrong and how I verified the fix."

## Why the crisis is a strength, not a weakness

A new consultant's instinct is to hide anything that "went wrong," even in a learning project. Resist that instinct here. The six-problem month-end crisis is the single most interview-relevant part of this entire capstone, because it's the part that proves you can do the job real consultants are actually hired to do: not just configure a system correctly the first time, but diagnose and fix one when it isn't. Every module course before this one taught you a piece. This capstone is the only place in the whole path that proves you can use all of them together, under realistic pressure.
