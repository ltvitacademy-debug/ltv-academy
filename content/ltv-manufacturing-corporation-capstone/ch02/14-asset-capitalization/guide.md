# Asset Capitalization

**Chapter 2 · Running the Business · Lesson 14 of 25**

The Fabrication department takes delivery of a new CNC lathe. This lesson capitalizes it as a fixed asset — and plants the third of Chapter 3's six problems, a wrong asset category that will quietly distort depreciation for as long as it goes unnoticed.

## What you'll learn

- How Derek Shaw capitalizes a new piece of manufacturing equipment
- The specific category and cost center decisions this capitalization requires
- The mistake made here, and why it doesn't look like a mistake at the time
- What this sets up for lesson 15's depreciation run

## The new asset

LTV's Fabrication department takes delivery of a new CNC lathe to replace an aging machine on the production floor:

- **Asset:** FA-10452, CNC Lathe
- **Cost:** $145,000.00
- **Placed in service:** January 2
- **Physical location:** Fabrication floor, Savannah plant
- **Funding source:** capital expenditure, already approved in the prior year's budget

## Capitalizing the asset

Derek Shaw, LTV's fixed assets accountant, enters the new asset into the **LTV US Corporate Book** (lesson 8):

- **Asset number:** FA-10452
- **Description:** CNC Lathe, Fabrication floor
- **Cost:** $145,000.00
- **Asset category:** selected as **Office Equipment** — 5-year useful life, straight-line
- **Cost center / Company:** 410 (Assembly), 1000 (US)
- **Date placed in service:** January 2

**This is where the mistake happens, twice over.** The correct category for a CNC lathe is **Manufacturing Equipment — Machinery**, 10-year useful life — not Office Equipment's 5-year life. And the correct cost center is **420 (Fabrication)**, where the machine physically sits — not 410 (Assembly). Derek, working from a capital spending list organized by approval date rather than asset type, picks the category and cost center from the row above it on his worksheet, a generic office furniture purchase approved the same week, and carries that row's category and cost center forward by mistake.

## Why this passes without a hitch

Capitalizing FA-10452 doesn't fail, error out, or produce any warning. Office Equipment is a perfectly valid category; 410 is a perfectly valid cost center. The asset capitalizes cleanly, debiting Fixed Assets — Machinery & Equipment (1710) for $145,000.00 under 1000-410, and nothing about the transaction itself looks wrong. The only way to catch this is to know, independently of the system, what a CNC lathe actually is and where it actually sits — exactly the kind of functional knowledge a consultant brings that the system itself can't enforce.

## What this sets up

Office Equipment's 5-year life depreciates faster per month than Manufacturing Equipment's 10-year life. Lesson 15 runs January depreciation on this asset exactly as capitalized here, and the dollar difference it produces is precise and calculable — which is exactly how you'll recognize it in Chapter 3.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Asset category | A default grouping (useful life, depreciation method, accounts) assigned to a capitalized asset |
| Placed-in-service date | The date depreciation begins accruing on an asset |

## Recap

FA-10452, a $145,000.00 CNC lathe placed in service January 2, is capitalized into the wrong asset category (Office Equipment instead of Manufacturing Equipment) and the wrong cost center (Assembly instead of Fabrication) — a clean, error-free transaction that is nonetheless wrong. Next up, lesson 15: depreciation, where this mistake turns into a specific dollar amount.
