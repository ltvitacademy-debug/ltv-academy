# Script — Asset Capitalization

## Segment 1 (title)

The Fabrication department takes delivery of a new CNC lathe. This lesson capitalizes it as a fixed asset — and plants the third of Chapter 3's six problems, a wrong asset category that quietly distorts depreciation.

## Segment 2 (steps)

FA-10452, a CNC lathe, $145,000, placed in service January 2nd on the Fabrication floor. Derek Shaw capitalizes it into the LTV US Corporate Book — but selects Office Equipment, a five-year life, and cost center 410, Assembly, instead of the correct Manufacturing Equipment, ten-year life, and cost center 420, Fabrication.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Working from a capital spending list organized by approval date rather than asset type, Derek carries forward the category and cost center from the row above — a generic office furniture purchase approved the same week — by mistake.

## Segment 4 (code)

Capitalizing FA-10452 doesn't fail or warn. Office Equipment is a valid category; cost center 410 is a valid cost center. The asset debits Fixed Assets — Machinery and Equipment for $145,000 under 1000-410, and nothing about the transaction looks wrong on its own.
