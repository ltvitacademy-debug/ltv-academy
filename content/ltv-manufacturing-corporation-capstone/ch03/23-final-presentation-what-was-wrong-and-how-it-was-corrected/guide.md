# Final Presentation: What Was Wrong and How It Was Corrected

**Chapter 3 · The January 31 Challenge · Lesson 23 of 25**

Two days after her call, Elena Marsh gathers Victor Okafor, Sarah Lindqvist, Chen Liu, Mateo Rios, Grace Olsen, and Derek Shaw for a 30-minute readout before the board meeting. This lesson is that presentation — the moment every piece of Chapter 3 comes together into something a non-technical executive can actually use.

## What you'll learn

- How to structure a findings presentation for an executive, not a fellow accountant
- The order that builds confidence instead of confusion
- How to present six problems without sounding like six separate failures
- What to say when asked "will this happen again?"

## Structuring the presentation

A findings presentation to a CFO is not a lesson in Oracle Fusion mechanics. Elena doesn't need to hear about Account Rules or auto-reverse flags first — she needs the headline, then the proof, in that order:

1. **The headline:** "All six issues are identified, corrected, and verified. Period Jan-26 is closed and reconciled in both ledgers. Here's what happened and what changed."
2. **The pattern, not six unrelated failures:** every issue traces back to one of three categories — a setting that should have been checked (the auto-reverse flag, the Account Rule mapping), a step that should have been completed (applying the receipt correctly, submitting the intercompany journal, capitalizing to the right category), or an external event outside LTV's control (the bank's duplicate transmission). Naming the pattern, rather than listing six isolated mistakes, is what turns a presentation into something Elena can repeat to her board with confidence.
3. **The numbers, before and after:** AP Accrued Liabilities down $18,400.00 to its correct balance; Harborview's two invoices now showing their true status; January depreciation corrected from $2,416.67 to $1,208.33 on FA-10452; cash reconciling exactly after the bank's $9,200.00 reversal; $4,250.00 of freight revenue now posted; $27,750.00 of intercompany overhead correctly allocated.
4. **What's different going forward:** a brief, concrete answer to "will this happen again" for each category — a period-close checklist addition for auto-reverse flags on manual accruals, a change-control step requiring new accounts to be paired with their Account Rule update, and a reminder that journal batches aren't done until they're submitted, not just saved.

## Answering "will this happen again?"

This is the question every CFO asks after a crisis like this one, and it's the question a weak consultant answers badly — either overpromising ("never again") or getting defensive. The honest, useful answer: these specific six issues won't recur because of the specific process changes above, but a first-ever month-end close on brand-new configuration will always surface *something*; the measure of a good implementation isn't zero surprises forever, it's catching them fast, understanding them completely, and fixing them before they reach the board.

## Why order matters

Presenting the fix before the cause, and the cause before the mechanics, respects the audience's time and their actual decision: Elena doesn't need to approve a journal entry, she needs to decide whether she trusts the numbers enough to put them in front of her board. Everything in this presentation exists to earn that trust, in the order that earns it fastest.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Findings presentation | A structured readout of what was wrong, why, and what changed, aimed at a decision-maker rather than a technical peer |
| Process change | A change to how work gets done (a checklist, a control, a habit), distinct from a one-time correction to a single transaction |

## Recap

The final presentation leads with the headline, groups six problems into three understandable patterns, shows the numbers before and after, and answers "will this happen again" honestly. Elena leaves with what she actually needed: confidence in the numbers and a defensible answer for her board.
