# Script — The CFO's Call: The Books Do Not Balance

## Segment 1 (title)

It's January 31st. Elena Marsh calls. This is the capstone's centerpiece beginning — not a fix, not yet an investigation, just the moment the problem becomes real and gets named out loud.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Six symptoms, in her own words: AP aging doesn't match the GL. Harborview's biggest invoice looks overdue despite a payment she saw. Depreciation on new equipment looks heavy. A nine-thousand-two-hundred-dollar bank item won't clear. Some Receivables transactions never made it to the GL. And intercompany between the US and Canada doesn't tie.

## Segment 3 (steps)

"The books don't balance" is a CFO's phrase, not a diagnosis. Debits still equal credits inside everything that actually posted — double-entry doesn't silently break. What it really means: subledgers don't reconcile to the GL, reports don't tie, and known items sit open. Translating that phrase into which specific reconciliation is broken is the core skill here.

## Segment 4 (code)

Each symptom maps to a problem already sitting quietly in the books: the AP mismatch traces to December's unreversed accrual. The AR confusion traces to a misapplied receipt. Heavy depreciation traces to a miscategorized asset. The bank item traces to a duplicated EFT. Missing Receivables traces to a Subledger Accounting gap. Intercompany traces to an unposted journal.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Up next, lesson nineteen: investigating AP, AR, and Assets — the first three symptoms, traced to their causes.
