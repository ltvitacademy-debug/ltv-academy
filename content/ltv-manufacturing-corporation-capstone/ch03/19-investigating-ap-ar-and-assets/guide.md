# Investigating AP, AR and Assets

**Chapter 3 · The January 31 Challenge · Lesson 19 of 25**

Three of Elena Marsh's six symptoms get traced to their actual root causes in this lesson: the AP aging mismatch, the AR confusion on Harborview's account, and the heavy depreciation number. Nothing gets fixed yet — lesson 21 does the fixing. This lesson is pure investigation.

## What you'll learn

- How to investigate an AP-aging-vs-GL mismatch back to a specific journal
- How to trace an AR aging anomaly back to a specific receipt application
- How to confirm a depreciation number against the asset record that produced it
- Why confirming the theory always comes before touching anything

## Investigating AP: the aging mismatch

Elena's complaint: AP aging doesn't match what the GL shows as owed. Starting from the GL, you check account 2150 (Accrued Liabilities) for Company 1000, cost center 420, and find **two** entries tied to the same PO: JE-GRNI-1231 ($18,400.00, December, never reversed) and the accounting generated by INV-MER-88341's validation ($18,400.00, January). The AP subledger's own aging report only reflects the validated invoice — it has no concept of a manual GL accrual journal. The GL's Accrued Liabilities balance is therefore **$18,400.00 higher than it should be**, carrying a stale accrual that the real invoice already superseded.

**Root cause confirmed:** JE-GRNI-1231's auto-reverse flag was never set (lesson 10), so the December accrual never cleared out of January, double-counting the Meridian bearings liability.

## Investigating AR: the Harborview confusion

Elena's complaint: Harborview's big invoice looks unpaid despite a payment she saw, and something else looks overpaid. Pulling Harborview's AR aging detail by invoice instead of by total balance immediately shows it: INV-HV-30144 ($55,100.00) is open and overdue; INV-HV-29890 ($6,100.00) shows a $49,000.00 credit balance. Checking receipt application history for RCPT-50231 confirms it was applied in full to INV-HV-29890, not INV-HV-30144.

**Root cause confirmed:** Mateo Rios's January 28 receipt application (lesson 13) was applied to the wrong invoice. The total cash and total AR balance were never wrong — only the open-item detail was.

## Investigating Assets: the depreciation number

Elena's complaint: depreciation on new equipment looks heavier than expected. Opening FA-10452's asset record in the LTV US Corporate Book shows category **Office Equipment**, 5-year life, cost center 410. Cross-checking against the capital approval paperwork for a CNC lathe confirms it should be Manufacturing Equipment, 10-year life, cost center 420 — exactly the mismatch planted in lesson 14.

**Root cause confirmed:** FA-10452 was capitalized under the wrong category and cost center, overstating January depreciation by $1,208.34 and misstating it to Assembly instead of Fabrication.

## Why confirm before touching anything

In each case, the investigation stopped at *confirming* the root cause with evidence — the actual journal, the actual receipt application history, the actual asset record — before proposing any correction. Reversing a journal, unapplying a receipt, or reclassifying an asset based on a guess, rather than confirmed evidence, is how a consultant turns one clean problem into two messy ones.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Open-item detail | The invoice-by-invoice breakdown underneath a subledger's total control account balance |
| Root cause confirmation | Verifying the actual originating transaction, not just the symptom, before correcting anything |

## Recap

Three root causes are now confirmed with evidence: the unreversed GRNI journal (AP), the misapplied Harborview receipt (AR), and FA-10452's wrong category and cost center (Assets).
