# Lesson 16 — Governance Councils and Committees · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Lessons 13 through 15 gave you four roles, each tied to a person and a domain. This lesson
covers what happens when a problem doesn't fit inside one domain's boundaries: the council
and committee structure above them.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (why roles alone aren't enough)

Data rarely respects domain boundaries. A dispute between the customer owner and the orders
owner about what counts as an active account has no single authority that covers both.
That's exactly what a standing, cross-functional governance body exists for — a forum
that's already there before the dispute happens, not improvised after.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (the layered structure)

Most mature programs separate this into layers. A governance council — senior leaders —
sets strategy, sponsors the program, and makes the highest-level trade-offs. A governance
committee is the operational layer underneath it: policy, cross-domain disputes, day-to-day
decisions. And working groups handle the detailed, domain-specific work, escalating only
when they're stuck.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (what the committee is for)

The committee itself does four concrete things: approves the policies Chapter 4 covers,
resolves disputes no single owner can settle, formally empowers owners and stewards so those
assignments are visible and legitimate, and prioritizes issues when there's more to fix than
there's time for.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

Not every organization needs three full layers — a smaller company can compress this into
one committee under an executive sponsor. What matters is that something standing and
cross-functional exists. Next lesson makes every one of these roles precise, on paper, with
RACI. See you there.
