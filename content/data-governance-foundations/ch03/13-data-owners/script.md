# Lesson 13 — Data Owners · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Chapter two gave you the frameworks and operating models. Chapter three names the actual
people inside them, starting with the role everything else depends on: the data owner.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (what an owner is accountable for)

DAMA-DMBOK defines a data owner as the individual responsible for definition, policy, and
practice decisions about data within their area of responsibility. In practice that's four
things: approving access, setting quality thresholds, resolving disputes between teams, and
owning the outcome when something in their domain goes wrong.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (business role, not IT role)

Here's the mistake almost every organization makes the first time: handing ownership to IT
by default, because IT is who technically touches the data. DAMA-DMBOK is explicit that this
is backwards. A database administrator can tell you how a table is structured; only a
business leader can tell you what the numbers inside it actually mean. Ownership is a named
business leader's job, one owner per domain, who can delegate daily work without giving up
accountability.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (owner vs. steward)

That delegation is exactly the relationship between owner and steward, which Lesson 17's
RACI model makes precise: the owner is Accountable — the A — and the steward is Responsible
— the R. Same domain, two different kinds of accountability.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

One named owner, four accountabilities, and a business role IT should never default into.
Next lesson: the steward who does the work the owner is accountable for. See you there.
