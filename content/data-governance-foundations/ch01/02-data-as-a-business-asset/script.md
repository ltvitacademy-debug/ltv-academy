# Lesson 2 — Data as a Business Asset · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

"Data is our most valuable asset" is in almost every company's values statement. This lesson asks: do they actually treat it that way?

## S2 · STEPS — What makes something a real asset

A real asset passes four tests. It has a named owner. It has a measurable value, and a measurable cost if it's lost. It's actively maintained, not left to decay. And it's weighed explicitly when decisions get made — budgets, cuts, migrations.

## S3 · STEPS — Where most organizations fail these tests

Most company data fails all four. No single person can say who owns the customer table. Nobody can price what a bad dataset actually costs. Fields go stale for years with no review. And data is almost never on the table when a system gets retired — until something breaks downstream.

## S4 · STEPS — Why the gap exists

Three real obstacles, not excuses: data is intangible, so it doesn't sit on a balance sheet like a building does. It's distributed — thousands of people touch it daily, not a handful of controlled entry points. And its value is contextual — worthless in one use case, critical in another.

## S5 · STEPS — What actually changes when this mindset sticks

When an organization genuinely treats data as an asset, three things become routine: a named accountable owner for every dataset — that's Lesson 13. Quality measured and reported like a financial metric — that's Chapter 4. And data considered explicitly before a system gets retired, not discovered as a surprise months later.

## S6 · OUTRO

Next lesson: now that we've called data an asset, how is governing it actually different from managing it, or ensuring its quality? Three terms people use interchangeably — and shouldn't.
