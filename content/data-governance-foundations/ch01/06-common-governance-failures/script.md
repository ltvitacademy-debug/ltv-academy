# Lesson 6 — Common Governance Failures · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Governance programs almost never fail loudly. This lesson covers the five quiet, specific ways they actually fail.

## S2 · STEPS — Failures one and two

No executive sponsorship — run by a mid-level team with no one who can mandate compliance across departments. And governance treated as a one-time project — a launch happens, then quietly stops, because it was scoped like a project instead of an ongoing discipline.

## S3 · STEPS — Failures three and four

Tool-first thinking — a catalog or quality tool gets bought and treated as "doing governance," skipping the harder work of actual ownership and decision rights. And no clear decision rights — two departments disagree on a definition, and there's no authority to resolve it, so it just persists.

## S4 · STEPS — Failure five

Governance disconnected from business value — the program measures itself by internal activity, policies written, meetings held — instead of any outcome the rest of the business actually cares about. Warning sign: budget season comes, and no one outside the data team can say what it did for them.

## S5 · STEPS — Why these are organizational, not technical

None of these five involve a technology gap. They're failures of sponsorship, structure, and measurement — which is exactly why this course spends so little time on tools and so much on frameworks, roles, and policy.

## S6 · OUTRO

That closes Chapter One. Chapter Two starts with the actual named frameworks organizations use to avoid these failures — beginning with DAMA-DMBOK.
