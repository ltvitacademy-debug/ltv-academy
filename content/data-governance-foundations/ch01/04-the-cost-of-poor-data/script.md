# Lesson 4 — The Cost of Poor Data · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Not a vague inconvenience — a measured, material cost. This lesson puts a real number on poor data quality.

## S2 · STEPS — A real, cited number

Gartner's data quality research has put the average cost of poor data quality at 12.9 million dollars a year, per organization. That's from Gartner's own survey-based research, cited on Gartner's data quality pages. Treat it as an order of magnitude, not a number that fits your company exactly — the point is that this is measured, not guessed.

## S3 · STEPS — Where the cost actually shows up

It rarely shows up on a line item called "bad data." It's wasted labor — analysts reconciling conflicting numbers instead of doing real work. Bad decisions built on bad numbers — an inflated forecast, a wrong budget allocation. Compliance exposure, when mismanaged personal data turns into a regulatory problem. Customer-facing failures — wrong addresses, duplicate accounts. And opportunity cost — usually the biggest, least-measured category of all.

## S4 · STEPS — Why the real number is higher

Most of these costs never get traced back to their root cause. A lost renewal from a billing error gets filed under "churn" — not under the broken record that actually caused it. That's why 12.9 million is best treated as a floor, not a ceiling.

## S5 · STEPS — Start estimating your own

Pick one recurring data problem. Estimate the hours per month people spend working around it. Multiply by a fully-loaded hourly cost. That single number is usually enough to get a governance conversation taken seriously.
