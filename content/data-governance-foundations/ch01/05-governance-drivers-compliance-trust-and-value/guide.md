# Lesson 5 — Governance Drivers: Compliance, Trust and Value

**Chapter 1 · What Data Governance Is · Lesson 5 of 30**

## What you'll learn

- The three distinct reasons organizations actually fund governance programs
- Real regulatory examples behind the "compliance" driver
- Why "trust" is a harder sell than compliance but often the bigger win
- How "value" reframes governance from a cost center to an enabler

## Three different reasons to fund the same work

Nobody funds "governance" in the abstract. Every real governance program gets approved because it serves one (usually more than one) of three concrete drivers: **compliance** (avoiding legal and regulatory consequences), **trust** (making data something people actually believe and use), and **value** (making data generate more business value, faster). Knowing which driver is actually paying for your program changes how you pitch it, measure it, and prioritize what gets governed first.

## Driver 1: Compliance

This is the most concrete and easiest driver to get budget for, because the cost of ignoring it is explicit and legal. Real examples: the EU's **GDPR** imposes fines up to €20 million or 4% of global annual revenue for serious violations. US state laws like **CCPA/CPRA** in California give consumers enforceable rights over their data, with penalties for non-compliance. **HIPAA** governs health data in the US with its own civil and criminal penalties. Sector-specific rules (financial services, in particular) add more layers still. Compliance-driven governance tends to focus narrowly on regulated data categories — personal data, health data, financial records — rather than the organization's data as a whole, which is both its strength (clear scope, clear urgency) and its limitation (it can stall once the regulatory checkbox is satisfied).

## Driver 2: Trust

Trust is harder to put a dollar figure on, which makes it a harder initial sell — but it's often what actually sustains a governance program after the compliance deadline passes. Trust means: when someone pulls a number from a report, they believe it without needing to double-check it against a shadow spreadsheet. It means a new analyst can find the authoritative source for a metric instead of guessing which of four "customer" tables is real. Low trust has a specific, visible symptom: people quietly rebuilding their own versions of reports because they don't trust the official one. That duplicated effort is trust's cost, even though it rarely gets logged anywhere as a governance problem.

## Driver 3: Value

Value-driven governance treats governance as an enabler, not a brake. The pitch here isn't "stop bad things from happening" — it's "good data, consistently governed, lets the business move faster." Concrete examples: an AI/ML initiative that actually works because its training data is trustworthy and well-defined. A faster, more confident executive decision because the underlying numbers aren't in dispute. A new product or market-entry analysis that doesn't have to start by cleaning up data from scratch. Value-driven governance is the hardest to pitch upfront (it's speculative, not a deadline) but it's the driver that turns governance from "the department that says no" into something the business actually wants more of.

## Using this framework

When you're scoping or pitching a governance effort, name the driver explicitly. A compliance-driven effort gets a hard deadline and narrow scope. A trust-driven effort gets measured by adoption and reduced duplication. A value-driven effort gets measured against the specific business initiative it's unblocking. Conflating the three is how governance programs end up with vague goals and no clear way to know if they succeeded.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Compliance driver | Governance funded to meet legal/regulatory obligations (GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA) |
| Trust driver | Governance funded to make data something people believe and use without double-checking |
| Value driver | Governance funded to accelerate business outcomes, not just reduce risk |
