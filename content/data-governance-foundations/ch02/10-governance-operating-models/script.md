# Lesson 10 — Governance Operating Models · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Frameworks define what good governance covers. An operating model is a different question entirely — how it actually runs day to day.

## S2 · STEPS — Framework vs. operating model

DMBOK defines what needs governing — it doesn't say who governs it or where authority sits. Two organizations can both follow DAMA-DMBOK and run completely different operating models. The framework is the what. The operating model is the how.

## S3 · STEPS — What every model has to define

Where do decisions get made — centrally, by business unit, or in between? Who holds the authority — a dedicated team, business-unit owners, a council? How does policy actually get enforced — mandate, incentive, or escalation? And how does it scale as the organization grows?

## S4 · STEPS — Same framework, different models in practice

A large, decentralized conglomerate and a small single-product company can both genuinely practice DMBOK's eleven knowledge areas — but the conglomerate needs decisions pushed out to each business unit, while the small company can centralize almost everything. Same framework, completely different operating model.

## S5 · STEPS — The stakes of getting it wrong

A centralized model forced onto a decentralized company creates a bottleneck — every decision waits on a team that can't understand every business unit's data. A decentralized model with no coordination, at a company that needs consistency, recreates the exact conflicting-definitions problem governance exists to prevent.
