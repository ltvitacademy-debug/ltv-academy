# Lesson 12 — Choosing an Operating Model · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

"It depends" is the right answer to which operating model to choose — but it depends on specific, answerable questions.

## S2 · STEPS — Five questions

How many meaningfully distinct business units are there? How much cross-cutting data genuinely needs to be consistent? What's the current governance maturity? Is there real executive sponsorship to back a centrally-enforced standard? And how fast does each part of the business actually need to move?

## S3 · STEPS — A worked example

A mid-size healthcare company: a hospital network, a telehealth platform, a billing subsidiary. Three distinct units — leaning federated. All three touch HIPAA-regulated patient data — a strong consistency need. Maturity is low, this is their first real effort. But there's a new Chief Data Officer with real backing.

## S4 · STEPS — What the framework points to

Start federated, but scope the centrally-governed piece narrowly — patient-data compliance first — and expand central scope only as maturity grows. Not a from-scratch, big-bang federated rollout on day one.

## S5 · STEPS — Closing Chapter Two

Chapter Two covered the vocabulary — DMBOK — the named frameworks beyond it — DCAM, COBIT, ISO 38505 — how to measure where you stand, and how governance actually gets structured and chosen.

## S6 · OUTRO

Chapter Three moves from structure to people: owners, stewards, custodians, councils — who actually holds these roles, starting with data owners.
