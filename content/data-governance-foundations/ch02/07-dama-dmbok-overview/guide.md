## What DAMA and the DMBOK actually are

**DAMA International** (the Data Management Association) is a global, vendor-neutral professional association for data management practitioners. Its flagship publication, the **DMBOK** — the **D**ata **M**anagement **B**ody **o**f **K**nowledge — is the most widely cited reference framework in the data management and governance field. The current edition, DMBOK2, organizes the entire discipline of data management into eleven knowledge areas, with data governance positioned deliberately at the center.

## The DAMA Wheel

DAMA's own published diagram — known simply as "the DAMA Wheel" — is the clearest single picture of how the framework is structured. Data Governance sits at the hub. The other ten knowledge areas are arranged as spokes around it, each representing a distinct discipline within data management.

The placement isn't decorative. It's the framework's central argument: none of the other ten areas can be done well without governance supplying the decision rights, accountability, and policy that make them consistent and trustworthy.

## The ten knowledge areas, briefly

- **Data Architecture** — the overall structural blueprint for how data assets fit together
- **Data Modeling and Design** — designing the models (conceptual, logical, physical) data is stored in
- **Data Storage and Operations** — the operational work of storing, performing, and maintaining data systems
- **Data Security** — protecting data from unauthorized access, loss, or misuse
- **Data Integration and Interoperability** — moving and synchronizing data consistently across systems
- **Document and Content Management** — managing unstructured and semi-structured content (documents, records)
- **Reference and Master Data** — maintaining a consistent "single source of truth" for core entities like customers and products
- **Data Warehousing and Business Intelligence** — enabling analytics and reporting on organizational data
- **Metadata Management** — managing "data about data": definitions, lineage, and context
- **Data Quality** — ensuring data is accurate, complete, consistent, and timely

## Why this framework matters even if you never adopt it formally

Most organizations don't implement DMBOK2 cover to cover — it's dense and comprehensive by design, closer to a reference encyclopedia than a quick-start guide. What matters for this course is that DMBOK's vocabulary and structure have become the field's shared language: when a job posting mentions "master data management" or "metadata management" as a discipline, it's almost always using DAMA's framing, whether or not the company has ever read the book. Lesson 8 compares DMBOK against other named frameworks — DCAM, COBIT, ISO/IEC 38505 — each suited to a different kind of organization and starting point.
