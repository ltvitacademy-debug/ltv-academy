# Lesson 9 — Data Governance Maturity Models · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

How capable is an organization, right now, at governing its data — not in intentions, in actual repeatable practice? That's what a maturity model measures.

## S2 · STEPS — What it actually measures

Maturity models score an organization against defined levels, from ad hoc and reactive at the bottom to optimized and continuously improving at the top. The value isn't the score itself — it forces a structured, honest assessment instead of a gut-feel claim.

## S3 · STEPS — Three real, named models

Gartner's framework — five levels, Aware through Optimized, across seven building blocks. IBM's model — eleven governance disciplines across four stages. CMMI's Data Management Maturity model — the same five-level process-maturity structure CMMI uses in software engineering, applied to data across six key themes.

## S4 · STEPS — Where organizations actually land

Across all these models, the honest finding is the same: most organizations cluster in the lower-middle levels — aware governance matters, reacting to problems as they come up, but without proactive, organization-wide discipline. That's the real, documented starting point most programs begin from.

## S5 · STEPS — Using it honestly

The failure mode to avoid: treating a maturity score as a badge to report upward instead of a diagnostic to act on. It's only useful if it identifies specific gaps and feeds directly into a roadmap. A score with no action plan attached is governance theater.
