# Lesson 30 — Foundations Case Study · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

This is the last lesson. Everything from here is one fictional
company, walked through all five chapters of this course, in order —
so you can see how the pieces actually fit together.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (the company and the problem)

Meridian Fulfillment Co. — a fictional, illustrative company invented
for this lesson, not a real business. Three systems hold a customer
address field, nobody owns which one is authoritative, and shipments
are going to the wrong place often enough that it's showing up in
complaints. That one problem is the thread through every chapter.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (chapters 1-2)

Chapter one names this as a governance root cause behind a data
quality symptom — trust and operational cost, not a compliance
mandate. Chapter two says Meridian doesn't need the full DAMA-DMBOK
treatment — just an operating-model decision: centralized enough to
pick a winner when addresses disagree, federated enough to keep each
business unit's day-to-day stewardship.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (chapter 3)

Chapter three's roles stop being abstract. A data owner — the
operations director who already fields the complaints. A data
steward — the analyst who already unofficially knows which records
are suspect. A RACI that says who flags, who decides, who's
consulted, who's informed. And an executive sponsor — the VP who
raised this in the first place.

## S5 · STEPS CARD (chapter 4)

Chapter four doesn't need a full regulatory treatment here — there's
no specific law driving this. It needs one policy naming the CRM as
the authoritative source, one standard defining a valid address
record, and one enforcement step: the other two systems sync from the
CRM nightly instead of keeping independent copies.

## S6 · STEPS CARD (chapter 5 sequence)

Chapter five's full sequence, in order. Readiness: high pain, high
sponsorship, low policy, low tooling. Business case: the problem, the
scope, the resources, the anticipated value. Pilot: customer address
data, a baseline complaint count, a ninety-day window. Roadmap: this
pilot is days sixty-one through ninety of a longer plan. Communication:
the sponsor frames it as how we stop shipping to the wrong address,
not as a new rule to follow.

## S7 · STEPS CARD (judging it)

At day ninety, one number decides it: did address complaints actually
drop against the baseline. If yes, the same five-step sequence repeats
for the next domain. If not, the team checks which step broke down.
The sequence doesn't change — only the domain does.

## S8 · OUTRO CARD

Congratulations — you've finished Data Governance Foundations. You
now have the full sequence: what governance is, which frameworks and
roles and policies make it real, and how to actually get a program
funded, piloted, and heard. Next in the Data Governance path: Data
Quality Management — where the quality side of this course gets its
own full treatment.
