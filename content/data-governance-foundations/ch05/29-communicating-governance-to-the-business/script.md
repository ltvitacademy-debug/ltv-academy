# Lesson 29 — Communicating Governance to the Business · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Lessons seventeen and eighteen covered RACI and executive sponsorship
— the structure of governance communication. This lesson is about the
actual words — the part that trips up even well-staffed programs.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (the word problem)

"Data governance" is part of the problem. To a business audience,
"data" sounds like a tactical IT concern, and "governance" sounds like
bureaucracy — rules on top of work that was going fine without them.
None of this course's substance survives if the first sentence makes
someone tune out.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (plain definition)

Have one jargon-free sentence ready. Data governance is the rules,
roles, and responsibilities for how data gets created, stored,
accessed, and used — so the numbers people rely on are accurate,
consistent, and there when they're needed. No metadata, no lineage, no
framework names, unless you translate the term first.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (enabler framing)

The single biggest shift is framing. "You can't access this data
anymore" becomes "here's the faster, approved way to get it." "This
policy restricts what you can do" becomes "this is what makes the
self-service dashboard safe to roll out." Same program — only one
version gets a willing audience.

## S5 · STEPS CARD (channels)

Match the channel to the audience. All-staff for the whole
organization, with the sponsor visibly behind it. Manager briefings so
people leaders can answer their own teams. Office hours and embedded
stewards for day-to-day users. And one-on-ones, sponsor-led, for a
specific skeptical stakeholder.

## S6 · OUTRO CARD

A plain sentence, enabler framing, the right channel, and real
evidence from a pilot if you have it — that's how governance actually
gets heard. Next up: the final lesson — pulling every chapter of this
course together into one case study.
