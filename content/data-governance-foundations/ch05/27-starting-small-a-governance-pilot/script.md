# Lesson 27 — Starting Small: A Governance Pilot · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Lesson twenty-six got you a yes. The temptation now is to go big
immediately — full operating model, every role, a complete policy
library, company-wide, all at once. Resist that. Start with a pilot.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (why pilot)

Scaling governance too fast scales your mistakes right along with it
— a confusing RACI, an unrealistic policy, a tool nobody adopted. A
pilot finds those mistakes while the cost of being wrong is still
small. And it produces a true story — a specific, measurable win — far
more persuasive than any framework diagram.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (three criteria)

Not every domain makes an equally good pilot. Three criteria, used
together: high value, so a win is worth talking about. High pain, so
stakeholders already feel the problem and show up engaged. And high
feasibility — small enough that a small team can actually finish in a
defined window.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (five pieces)

A real pilot needs five pieces. A defined objective, in one sentence.
A baseline measurement, captured before anything changes. A named
owner — an actual person, not an abstract team. A fixed time window,
typically sixty to ninety days. And a planned decision point at the
end: scale it, adjust it, or stop it.

## S5 · STEPS CARD (pitfalls)

Four ways pilots fail even when the idea was sound: too ambitious a
scope that fails the feasibility test, no executive visibility so a
real win has no audience, no baseline so there's nothing to compare
against, and no defined end so it just drifts.

## S6 · OUTRO CARD

High value, high pain, high feasibility — five pieces, a fixed window,
a real decision at the end. That's a pilot built to actually prove
something. Next up: how a successful pilot becomes a full roadmap.
