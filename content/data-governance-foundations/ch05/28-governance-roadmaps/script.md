# Lesson 28 — Governance Roadmaps · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Last lesson's pilot proved something in one domain, in one fixed
window. A roadmap is what turns that single proof point into an
organization-wide program — sequenced in phases, not repeated
everywhere at once.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (five-phase shape)

A realistic roadmap has roughly five phases. Days zero to thirty:
discovery — goals, stakeholders, pain points. Days thirty-one to
sixty: foundation — first owners, first policies, first catalog
entries. Days sixty-one to ninety: the pilot itself, running inside
the roadmap. Months three to six: expand to two or three more
domains. Months six to twelve: scale into business as usual.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (every phase needs three things)

A roadmap that's just colored boxes on a timeline is decoration, not
a plan. Every phase needs a named owner for that phase's work, a
defined output — an artifact or capability that now exists — and a
measurable milestone anyone can check against reality.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (keeping it honest)

Roadmaps fail quietly two ways: marching forward on schedule even when
a phase's milestone wasn't actually hit, or treating the whole thing
as aspirational — a wall poster nobody checks. Revisit it at the end
of every phase and adjust the next one based on what actually
happened.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

Discovery, foundation, pilot, expand, scale — each phase owned,
defined, and measured. That's a roadmap built to survive contact with
reality. Next up: putting all of this into language the rest of the
business will actually listen to.
