# Lesson 25 — Assessing Governance Readiness · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Chapter two gave you maturity models — where an organization sits on
a governance continuum. Now it's time to actually find out where
yours sits, before you design anything.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (why assess first)

Skipping the assessment is the number-one reason governance programs
stall. A team buys a catalog tool, writes a charter, forms a council —
and six months later nothing's changed, because nobody checked for
sponsorship, capacity, or whether the business even agreed there was a
problem. Technology was never the gap. Readiness was.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (six dimensions)

A real readiness check rates six independent dimensions: current data
practices, executive sponsorship, organizational structure, existing
policy, technology and tooling, and regulatory pressure. Rate each one
Low, Medium, or High on its own — an organization can be strong on
one and weak on another, and that combination tells you something.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (who's in the room)

An assessment run entirely by IT produces a technology inventory, not
a readiness assessment. You need the prospective executive sponsor,
a few business data owners who can name real pain points, IT and data
engineering, compliance or legal, and a handful of frontline data
consumers. Fifteen minutes with each beats a survey nobody fills out.

## S5 · STEPS CARD (gap list)

The assessment only matters if it produces a gap list — current
rating, target rating, and the one-line gap, per dimension. That list
feeds directly into next lesson's business case and the lesson after
that's pilot selection. The gaps are the justification.

## S6 · STEPS CARD (traps)

Three traps to avoid: mistaking tooling for readiness, skipping
business engagement so IT rates itself too generously, and treating
the assessment as a one-time event instead of something you redo after
a leadership change or a new regulation.

## S7 · OUTRO CARD

Six dimensions, the right people in the room, and a gap list at the
end — that's a real readiness assessment. Next up: turning that gap
list into a business case someone will actually fund.
