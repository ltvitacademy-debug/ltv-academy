# Lesson 23 — Policy Enforcement · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

A policy can be drafted, reviewed, approved, and published — the full Lesson 22 lifecycle —
and still not have changed a single person's behavior. This lesson covers the piece that
actually makes it real: enforcement.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (the gap)

An unenforced policy is functionally a suggestion. It's a statement of intent competing
against whatever's more convenient in the moment, and convenience usually wins. Enforcement
is what closes that gap between what's written down and what actually happens.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (organizational enforcement)

Some enforcement is organizational, not technical: training people on the rule and having
them formally acknowledge it, tying a manager or owner's accountability to whether their
domain actually follows it, periodic audits and spot checks, and a clear escalation path
back to the governance committee.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (technical enforcement)

Increasingly, governance backs policy with technical controls applied at the point data is
actually touched. Role or attribute-based access control blocks unauthorized access
automatically. Automated classification tags the sensitive fields a rule actually applies
to. Real-time monitoring flags a violation as it happens. And an audit trail makes all of it
verifiable after the fact, not just assumed.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

Enforcement is what turns a document into a real rule. Next lesson covers why many of these
rules exist in the first place: the regulatory landscape. See you there.
