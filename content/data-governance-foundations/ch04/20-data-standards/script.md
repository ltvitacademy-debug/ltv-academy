# Lesson 20 — Data Standards · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Lesson 19 gave you the policy — the why. This lesson gives you its necessary partner, the
piece that makes a policy actually checkable: the data standard.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (policy vs standard)

A standard offers the technical detail for executing a policy effectively. Where the policy
states what's required and why, the standard defines the exact, concrete rule — formats,
structures, terminologies, thresholds — that makes the requirement consistently achievable.

## S3 · CODE CARD (real companion standard)

Following straight from last lesson's confidentiality policy, a companion standard might
read: all customer account numbers must be 10 digits, starting with two letters identifying
the region. Notice it doesn't restate why that matters — the policy already covered that. It
just makes the rule checkable.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (four categories)

Standards typically fall into four categories. Format standards — exact structure for a
field. Terminology standards — the one approved term for a concept, which the next lesson
covers in full. Quality standards — a specific measurable threshold. And security standards
— the exact control that satisfies a policy's security requirement.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

Policy without a standard is too vague to apply consistently; a standard without a policy
has no stated reason to exist. Next lesson goes deep on the terminology category —
definitions and naming standards. See you there.
