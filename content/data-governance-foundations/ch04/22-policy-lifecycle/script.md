# Lesson 22 — Policy Lifecycle · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Lesson 19 treated a policy as a finished document. It isn't — a policy moves through a
lifecycle, from first draft to eventual retirement, and skipping stages is exactly how
organizations end up with rules nobody follows.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (stages 1-3)

The first three stages: drafting, where the owner identifies the need and a steward authors
a first controlled version naming its owner and a review date. Review, where legal,
compliance, IT, and the governance committee assess accuracy and conflicts. And approval,
where the committee approves, rejects, or sends it back for changes.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (stages 4-6)

The next three: publication, getting the approved policy to the people actually expected to
follow it, not just filed somewhere. Review and revision, triggered either by the scheduled
date or by a real change. And retirement — formally removing an outdated policy from
circulation.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (retirement skipped + triggers)

Retirement is, in practice, the stage organizations handle worst. It's far easier to simply
stop thinking about an old policy than to formally retire it — and the result is obsolete
rules sitting next to current ones with no clear signal which is which. A lifecycle isn't
complete until retirement gets the same formality as approval did.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

Six stages, and retirement deserves just as much rigor as drafting did. Next lesson covers
what actually makes a published policy matter day to day: policy enforcement. See you there.
