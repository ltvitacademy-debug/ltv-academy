## S1 · TITLE CARD

Lesson 20 named terminology as one of the four standard categories. This lesson goes deep on
exactly that: data definitions and naming standards.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (the problem)

Ask five teams to define active customer and you'll often get five different answers —
active in the last 30 days, the last 12 months, has a current subscription, has ever
purchased, hasn't unsubscribed. None of those is wrong by itself. But when a sales report
and a finance report both say active customers while counting different things, nobody can
trust either number without first asking which definition was used.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (business glossary)

That's exactly what a business glossary solves — a living, governed reference with one
approved definition per term. A good entry has four parts: the term itself, a definition
specific enough that two people apply it the same way, the accountable owner who decided it,
and the mapping to the actual physical data element it represents.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (metadata layers + ISO 11179)

That glossary lives inside the business metadata layer — definition, classification,
retention — as distinct from technical metadata like column type or table size, which
custodians care about instead. And naming conventions aren't arbitrary; many organizations
anchor them to ISO slash IEC 1-1-1-7-9, the international standard for naming and describing
metadata elements consistently.
