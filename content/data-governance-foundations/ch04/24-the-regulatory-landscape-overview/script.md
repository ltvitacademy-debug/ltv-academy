## S1 · TITLE CARD

Chapter 4 has covered policies, standards, naming, lifecycle, and enforcement. This closing
lesson covers a big part of why many of those rules exist in the first place: the regulatory
landscape.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (caveat)

One important caveat before anything else: this is an orientation to the shape of a few
widely-referenced regulations, not legal advice, and not a comprehensive survey of global
privacy law. If your organization has real compliance obligations, that needs actual legal
counsel — what this lesson gives you is just enough vocabulary to follow why a policy
exists.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (three regulations)

Three regulations come up constantly. GDPR covers personal data broadly for EU residents,
with a generally opt-in consent model. CCPA and its expansion, CPRA, cover California
consumers with a generally opt-out model for selling or sharing data. And HIPAA protects
health information specifically, inside US healthcare contexts, for covered entities and
their business associates.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (opt-in vs opt-out)

If you remember one structural fact, make it this: GDPR defaults to opt-in — consent before
processing. CCPA defaults to opt-out — it collects by default, and the consumer has to act
to stop it. An organization compliant with one of these is not automatically compliant with
the other — the mechanics are genuinely different, not just the paperwork.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

Regulation is one of the main reasons a policy exists, and one of the main triggers that
sends it back through its lifecycle. That closes Chapter 4. Next chapter turns everything so
far into an actual governance program, starting with assessing readiness. See you there.
