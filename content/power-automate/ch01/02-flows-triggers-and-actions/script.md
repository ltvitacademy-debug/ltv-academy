# Script — Flows, Triggers and Actions

## Segment 1 (title)

Every flow in Power Automate, no matter how complicated it gets, is built from exactly two kinds of pieces: one trigger and one or more actions. Castlebridge Logistics' shipping approvals run through a flow that is, underneath everything, just that same shape. Let's get it firmly in your head before you build your first real flow next lesson.

## Segment 2 (screenshot)

A trigger is the event that starts a flow — nothing runs until it fires. Every connector ships its own set of prebuilt triggers. This is a partial list from just the Office 365 Outlook connector: a new email arriving, an email being flagged, a calendar event being created. SharePoint, Teams, and hundreds of other connectors each have their own.

## Segment 3 (screenshot)

An action is a step the flow performs after the trigger fires. You add your first one by selecting the plus sign below the trigger card, which opens this panel: Favorites, AI capabilities, Built-in tools, and every connector's actions underneath. You'll use this exact panel next lesson to add an approval action to Castlebridge's shipping flow.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Triggers come in three types. Automated triggers fire on an event in a connected service, like a new item appearing in a SharePoint list. Instant triggers fire manually, such as a dock worker tapping a button in the mobile app to flag a damaged pallet. And scheduled triggers fire on a recurring schedule you define, like a 6 AM summary of overnight deliveries.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Every flow needs at least one trigger and one action to even save. Hold onto that shape. Up next, Lesson 3: building your first real flow, an approval that fires on a new SharePoint item, start to finish.
