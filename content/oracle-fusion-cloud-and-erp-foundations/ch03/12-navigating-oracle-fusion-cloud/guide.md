## The Navigator

The **Navigator** is the master menu for the entire application, opened from an icon in the global header, usually positioned next to the Oracle logo. It lists every **work area** a signed-in user has access to, organized into groups by product family — Financials, Procurement, My Enterprise, Tools, and others, depending on what the company has implemented and what that user's security roles permit. Clicking a work area in the Navigator takes you directly to its landing page. If the home page's springboard (Lesson 11) is a handful of favorite shortcuts, the Navigator is the complete directory everything else lives inside.

## Simplified UI: a deliberate design choice

Older enterprise software, including Oracle's own E-Business Suite, was often built around dense forms: many fields on one screen, keyboard-driven navigation between fields, and a steep learning curve. Oracle Fusion Cloud was built instead around what Oracle calls a **simplified user interface** — fewer fields visible at once, more guided, click-driven navigation, and a design philosophy closer to a modern consumer web application than a 1990s terminal screen. This isn't cosmetic. It's a deliberate bet that a workforce increasingly used to simple consumer apps will be more productive, and need less training, in a system that works the same way.

## Favorites and Recent Items

Nobody wants to dig through the full Navigator every single day for the three work areas they use constantly. Oracle Fusion supports marking work areas, reports, or specific pages as **Favorites**, which then appear as quick links without re-navigating the full menu structure each time. The system also automatically tracks **Recent Items** — the pages a user has visited lately — so returning to something you were just working on doesn't require retracing your steps through the Navigator. Lesson 14 covers Favorites in more depth, alongside saved searches.

## Why navigation skill matters here

A new Oracle Fusion user who only knows how to reach one screen at a time, by memorized clicks, will be slow and will struggle the moment a business process spans multiple work areas — which, per Chapter 1, is most of them. A consultant who understands the Navigator's structure, knows how to use Favorites deliberately, and can explain navigation to end users during training is delivering real value beyond just configuring the system correctly.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Navigator | The master menu listing every work area a user can access, by product family |
| Simplified UI | Oracle's modern, click-driven interface design, replacing dense legacy forms |
| Favorites | User-marked shortcuts to frequently used work areas or pages |
| Recent Items | Automatically tracked list of recently visited pages |
