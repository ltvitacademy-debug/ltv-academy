# Script — How Business Processes Flow Through an ERP

## Segment 1 (title)

Lesson 1 said an ERP is built from integrated modules. Now let's make that concrete by following two real processes end to end — the two you'll hear about constantly as a Financials consultant: Procure-to-Pay and Order-to-Cash.

## Segment 2 (steps: procure-to-pay)

Say Blue Harbor Manufacturing needs to buy steel. A requisition requests it, a purchase order commits to buying it, a receipt confirms it arrived, an invoice gets matched against the PO and receipt before approval, and finally accounts payable pays the supplier. That's Procure-to-Pay, crossing Procurement, Supply Chain, and Financials without anyone retyping a thing.

## Segment 3 (steps: order-to-cash)

Now a customer orders finished goods. A sales rep enters the order, the warehouse ships it and inventory drops, accounts receivable invoices based on what actually shipped, and the customer's payment gets applied against that invoice. That's Order-to-Cash.

## Segment 4 (code: both roads lead to the GL)

Here's the pattern to remember: whatever module a process starts in, it ends in the same place.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Every business process, whatever module it starts in, eventually produces an accounting entry in the General Ledger — the financial heartbeat of the company. Next up, Lesson 3: a closer look at the modules themselves — Finance, Procurement, Supply Chain, and HR — and how they integrate.
