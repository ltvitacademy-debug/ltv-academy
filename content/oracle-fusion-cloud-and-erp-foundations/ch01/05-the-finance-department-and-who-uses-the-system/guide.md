## Roles inside finance

- **Accounts Payable (AP) clerk / specialist.** Enters and matches supplier invoices, resolves exceptions, and processes payments. Lives inside the Payables module.
- **Accounts Receivable (AR) clerk / specialist.** Manages customer invoices, applies incoming payments, and chases overdue balances. Lives inside Receivables.
- **General Ledger (GL) accountant.** Reviews and posts journal entries, reconciles accounts, and supports the period close.
- **Controller.** Owns the accuracy of the financial statements and the integrity of the close process; typically the AP, AR, and GL teams' manager.
- **FP&A (Financial Planning & Analysis) analyst.** Builds budgets, forecasts, and variance analysis — often a heavy user of Oracle's reporting tools rather than its transaction screens.
- **CFO.** Ultimately accountable for the company's financial health and the numbers that go to the board and investors.
- **Internal and external auditors.** Don't process transactions, but regularly review them — which is why controls like the three-way match from Lesson 2 matter so much.

## People outside finance who still touch the system

A finance-focused ERP isn't used only by finance. Remember the integration story from Lesson 3:

- **Procurement buyers** create and approve purchase orders that eventually become AP invoices.
- **Warehouse and shipping staff** record receipts and shipments that feed both inventory accounting and AR invoicing.
- **Sales representatives** enter the orders that start the Order-to-Cash cycle.
- **Every employee**, in most companies, submits their own expense reports directly into the system.
- **Managers** approve requisitions, expense reports, and other transactions that route to them for sign-off.

## Where a functional consultant fits

As an Oracle Fusion Financials Consultant, you won't be the person entering invoices or chasing overdue receivables day to day — that's the AP and AR teams' job. Your job is to understand what each of these roles needs the system to do, configure Oracle Fusion to support those processes correctly, train the people who'll use it, and troubleshoot it when something breaks. That means every one of the roles above is effectively a future stakeholder you'll need to interview, listen to, and design for.
