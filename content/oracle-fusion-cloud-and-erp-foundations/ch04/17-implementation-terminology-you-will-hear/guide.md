# Implementation Terminology You Will Hear

**Chapter 4 · Implementation Basics and Careers · Lesson 17 of 20**

Walk into your first Oracle Fusion implementation project meeting and you'll hear a wall of acronyms and jargon, often without anyone pausing to explain them. This lesson is your glossary, built in advance, so none of it catches you off guard.

## What you'll learn

- The vocabulary describing an implementation's overall lifecycle
- Terms describing how requirements get captured and tested
- Terms describing the people and roles involved
- Why fluency in this vocabulary is itself a hireable skill

## Lifecycle terms

- **Implementation.** The overall project of configuring and deploying Oracle Fusion Cloud for a specific company — not a single task, but the whole effort from kickoff to go-live and beyond.
- **Go-live.** The date a company starts actually running its real business in the new system.
- **Cutover.** The specific window of activity right around go-live where the company stops using its old system and switches operations to the new one, often including final data loads and validation.
- **Hypercare.** A defined period immediately after go-live with extra support staffing, because new issues surface fastest in the first weeks of real usage.

## Requirements and testing terms

- **Fit-gap analysis.** Early in a project, comparing what the company needs ("fit") against what the standard system does out of the box, identifying anywhere a gap exists that needs a configuration decision, a process change, or an extension.
- **CRP (Conference Room Pilot).** A structured walkthrough where the configured system is demonstrated against real business scenarios in front of stakeholders, often held multiple times across a project to validate configuration decisions before they're locked in.
- **UAT (User Acceptance Testing).** The phase where actual end users, not just the project team, test the configured system against real scenarios before go-live, formally signing off that it meets their needs.
- **Data migration.** Moving a company's historical data — customers, suppliers, open invoices, account balances — from the old system into the new one, usually one of the highest-risk parts of any implementation.

## People and roles

- **SI (Systems Integrator).** The consulting firm hired to lead or support an implementation, as opposed to the customer's own internal staff.
- **Functional consultant.** Covered in depth in Lesson 19 — configures the system and understands the business processes.
- **Technical consultant.** Also covered in Lesson 19 — handles data loads, integrations, and extensions.
- **Project sponsor.** The executive on the customer side ultimately accountable for the project's success, usually someone like a CFO or VP of Finance for a Financials implementation.

## Why this vocabulary is a hireable skill

A candidate who can use these terms correctly in an interview signals real project exposure, not just screen familiarity. Equally, a consultant who doesn't know what a CRP or a fit-gap analysis is will struggle to follow a project plan or a status meeting on day one. This vocabulary isn't decoration — it's the shared language an entire implementation team uses to coordinate.

| Term | Meaning |
