## S1 · TITLE CARD

Lesson 2 previewed "special category" PII. Now let's draw the sharper
line this chapter has been building toward: sensitive data versus
confidential data. They're not the same thing.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (sensitive data)

Sensitive data is data that harms the person it describes if exposed.
Health records, biometric data, sexual orientation, religious beliefs,
financial account numbers. The harm here is personal — embarrassment,
discrimination, fraud. A leaked bank account number hurts the person
it belongs to far more than the company that stored it.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (confidential data)

Confidential data is different: information a business protects for
its own reasons, with no individual victim required. Trade secrets,
unreleased merger plans, source code, internal financials before
they're public. If a competitor gets your product roadmap, nobody's
privacy was violated — but the business damage can still be severe.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (overlap, not identical)

These overlap but aren't identical. A health record is sensitive but
usually not confidential. A merger plan is confidential but not
sensitive. And some data is genuinely both — an executive's salary and
health file matters to them personally, and to the company that
doesn't want it public. Which category applies changes who has a say,
and which rules apply.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

Chapter 2 builds classification labels — public, internal, confidential,
restricted — directly on this distinction. A scheme that only thinks
about business risk under-protects personal health data; one that only
thinks about personal harm under-protects the company's unreleased
numbers. Next up: Chapter 1 wraps with the major regulations.
