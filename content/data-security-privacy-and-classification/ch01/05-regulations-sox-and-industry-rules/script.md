# Lesson 5 — Regulations: SOX and Industry Rules · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE CARD

This is an orientation overview, not legal advice. SOX isn't a privacy
law — it's a financial-reporting law. But the controls it demands are
exactly what data governance builds. Plus three sector rules you'll
hear named constantly: PCI DSS, GLBA, and FERPA.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (SOX origin and scope)

Sarbanes-Oxley passed in 2002, after accounting scandals like Enron and
WorldCom. It applies to publicly traded companies and their auditors.
The goal: make sure financial reports are accurate, and that the
controls behind them can actually be trusted.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (Section 302, 404, PCAOB)

Section 302 makes officers personally certify their financial reports
are accurate. Section 404 requires management to assess internal
controls, with independent auditor attestation. SOX also created the
PCAOB, to oversee audits of public companies directly.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (why this is governance work)

Section 404 in practice means: who can change financial data, is every
change logged, are duties properly separated. That's access control,
audit trails, and classification — the same infrastructure data
governance builds for every other kind of sensitive data.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

Three more names worth knowing: PCI DSS for payment card data, GLBA for
financial-services customer information, FERPA for student education
records. Each one governs a specific data category. Next lesson:
Privacy by Design, and the seven principles behind GDPR's own
Article 25.
