## S1 · TITLE CARD

A privacy impact assessment is a structured review of a planned data
processing activity, done before it launches, to identify privacy
risks and decide how to address them in advance. This closing lesson
covers what one actually contains.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (GDPR Article 35)

GDPR's Article 35 requires a Data Protection Impact Assessment
specifically when processing is likely to result in a high risk to
individuals — large-scale sensitive data processing and systematic
monitoring are the examples the regulation calls out. Not every
project needs the formal version, but the practice is useful far more
broadly.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (five questions)

A usable assessment answers the same core questions regardless of
template. What data, and why, exactly. Is it necessary and
proportionate — could the same purpose be met with less. What are the
actual risks to individuals, not just "could we get breached." What
mitigates those risks. And who signed off, confirming it was genuinely
done.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (applied to Brightfield)

Run Lesson 28's dashboard project through this, and "what data, and
why" alone would have surfaced that a regional sales dashboard didn't
need row-level customer records at all. "Necessary and proportionate"
would have flagged the broad access grant before it was ever made —
caught at design time, not three months later during a recertification
cycle.
