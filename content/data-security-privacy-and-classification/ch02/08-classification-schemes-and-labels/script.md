# Lesson 8 — Classification Schemes and Labels · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Classification needs actual tiers to sort data into. Most organizations
land on some version of the same small scheme — here's the common
pattern, and where it varies.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (the four common tiers)

Four tiers show up again and again: Public — fine for anyone to see.
Internal — for employees, not damaging if it leaked. Confidential — real
harm if disclosed, like customer records. Restricted, or Highly
Confidential — the most sensitive tier, where a leak causes severe harm.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (what distinguishes a tier)

The label is just a name. What makes it meaningful is three questions:
who can access it, what happens if it leaks, and what controls apply.
Public needs none of that; Restricted needs the strictest controls the
organization has — multi-factor authentication, tightly scoped access,
mandatory audit trails.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (labels vary; government is different)

Exact names and tier counts vary — three tiers, five tiers, different
labels — and that's fine, as long as they're small in number and
consistently applied. One real scheme is genuinely different: the US
government's Confidential, Secret, and Top Secret system for national
security information. Legally defined, its own clearance process — don't
confuse it with commercial data classification.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

The transferable skill isn't memorizing one vendor's label names — it's
recognizing the pattern: a small number of tiers, each with a defined
answer for access, consequence, and controls. Next lesson: you can't
classify what you haven't found — discovering sensitive data in the first
place.
