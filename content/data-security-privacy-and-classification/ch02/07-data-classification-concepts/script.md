## S1 · TITLE CARD

Chapter One gave you the vocabulary — sensitive data, PII, the major
regulations. This chapter turns that vocabulary into something you can
actually apply: data classification.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (what classification is)

Data classification is sorting data into a small number of sensitivity
tiers, so handling rules — who can access it, whether it's encrypted, how
long it's kept — attach to the label automatically, instead of being
decided case by case, every single time.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (why it exists)

Without classification you're stuck choosing between two bad options:
protect everything at the highest level, which is safe but wildly
expensive, or protect everything lightly, which is cheap but leaves your
most sensitive data exposed. Classification lets you right-size controls
to actual risk.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (connecting back to Chapter 1)

This is where Chapter One's ideas stop being abstract. "This data is
sensitive" becomes a label — Confidential, Restricted — stamped on an
actual table. "This table has PII under GDPR" becomes a concrete rule a
system can enforce. The label is the bridge from policy to practice.
