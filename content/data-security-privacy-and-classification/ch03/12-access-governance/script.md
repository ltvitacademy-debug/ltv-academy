Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Role-based access control, least privilege, segregation of duties, access
reviews, privileged access — this chapter covers five real controls. Access
governance is the program that coordinates all five: who decides, who grants,
who checks.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (four questions)

Access governance answers four questions for every system worth protecting.
Who can request access? Who has the authority to approve it? Who actually
grants it? And who checks, afterward, that the grant was correct and is
still needed?

## S3 · STEPS CARD (Joiner-Mover-Leaver)

Most access changes trace back to three events. Joiner — a new hire gets
starting access tied to their role. Mover — someone changes teams and needs
new access, and critically, needs their old access removed. Leaver — someone
exits, and every grant they held gets revoked, fast.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (four roles)

Every access decision involves four roles. The requester identifies the
need. The approver — usually the data owner — decides if it's justified.
The provisioner executes the grant. The auditor confirms, independently,
that it was approved and is still appropriate.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

Next up: role-based access control — the single most common way
organizations actually implement the access governance program this lesson
described, mapping people to roles instead of granting permissions one
person at a time.
