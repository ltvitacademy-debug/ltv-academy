# Lesson 23 — Data Retention · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Left alone, most systems either keep everything forever or get "cleaned
up" by an engineer without checking who still needs the data. Neither
is a decision. Data retention is deciding, in advance, how long each
category of data is kept.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (two failure modes)

Over-retention feels safe — "we might need it someday" — but every
extra year of customer data sitting around is another year of breach
exposure. Under-retention is the opposite problem: deleting records
before a legal or tax obligation expires. And a legal hold suspends
the whole schedule the moment litigation is reasonably anticipated.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (four parts of a schedule)

A usable retention schedule answers four questions, not one: what
category of data this is, how long it's kept — stated as a real
duration — what trigger event starts the clock, and what disposition
action happens when the period ends: delete, anonymize, or archive.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (trigger event)

The most common mistake is skipping the trigger event. "Seven years"
means nothing without a starting point. Seven years from when a record
was created, versus seven years from when the customer relationship
ended, produces very different deletion dates for someone who stayed
ten years.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

Next lesson: what happens when that clock runs out, or when someone
asks you to delete their data early — data deletion and the right to
erasure.
