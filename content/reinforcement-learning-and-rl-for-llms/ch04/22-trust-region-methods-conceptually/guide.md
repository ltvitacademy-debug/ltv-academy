# Trust Region Methods, Conceptually

This is lesson 22 of the Reinforcement Learning & RL for LLMs course, opening Chapter 4, Policy Optimization Methods. Chapter 3 solved "how do we get a good, low-variance gradient estimate." This chapter solves a different problem: once you have that gradient, how large a step should you actually take with it? Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) was the first widely-used answer, and understanding it conceptually is the key to understanding PPO, which this chapter builds to next.

## What you'll learn

- Why an ordinary gradient step size can silently destroy a policy's performance
- The probability-ratio surrogate objective that trust region methods optimize
- The KL-divergence constraint that keeps each policy update "nearby" in a meaningful sense
- Why TRPO, while foundational, was impractical enough to motivate PPO

## The step-size problem, specifically for policies

In ordinary supervised learning, taking too large a gradient step usually just means a temporarily worse loss — the next batch of data looks similar, and training recovers. In policy optimization, a too-large step is far more dangerous: a single bad update can shift the policy's action distribution so much that it starts gathering *qualitatively different, much worse* experience. The next batch of training data is no longer "a noisier version of before" — it was generated by a policy that forgot how to act well, and there may be no path back.

This is the central insight trust region methods act on: it's not just the size of the parameter update that matters, it's how much the resulting *policy's behavior* actually changed. A small change in θ can sometimes cause a huge change in π_θ's behavior (or vice versa), so constraining the step directly in parameter space isn't the right lever to pull.

## The surrogate objective

TRPO works with a **surrogate objective**: instead of maximizing expected return directly (which is expensive and high-variance to even evaluate exactly), it maximizes an estimate built from importance-sampled probability ratios between the new and old policy, weighted by the advantage:

```
maximize  E[ π_θ(a|s) / π_θ_old(a|s) · A(s,a) ]
```

The ratio π_θ(a|s) / π_θ_old(a|s) says "how much more (or less) likely is the new policy to take this action than the old policy was." When this ratio is close to 1, the surrogate is a good local approximation of the true change in expected return. The farther θ strays from θ_old, the less trustworthy that approximation becomes — which is exactly the problem the constraint below exists to prevent.

## The trust-region constraint

TRPO maximizes that surrogate objective *subject to* a constraint on how much the policy's behavior is allowed to change, measured with KL-divergence between the old and new action distributions, averaged over the states visited:

```
maximize    E[ π_θ(a|s) / π_θ_old(a|s) · A(s,a) ]
subject to  E[ KL(π_θ_old(·|s) || π_θ(·|s)) ] ≤ δ
```

δ is a small constant — the "trust region" radius. Rather than trusting the surrogate objective everywhere, TRPO only trusts it within a region where the old and new policies are provably close in a *behavioral* sense (KL-divergence), not just a parameter-space sense. This is the "trust region" the method is named for: the region in which the surrogate objective is a good enough approximation of the real objective to optimize safely.

## Why TRPO isn't what's actually used in practice

Solving that constrained optimization problem exactly requires approximating the KL constraint with a second-order (curvature) expansion and applying conjugate gradient methods to solve for the step — line search, Fisher-information-matrix-vector products, and a fair amount of numerical machinery, all of which is expensive and intricate to implement correctly. TRPO was a major theoretical advance and is still exactly what many papers compare against, but almost nobody trains with it directly. Lesson 23 introduces the algorithm nearly everyone actually uses instead: Proximal Policy Optimization, which gets most of TRPO's safety guarantees from a dramatically simpler mechanism.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Surrogate objective | An importance-sampled approximation of the true policy improvement, built from a probability ratio times advantage |
| Probability ratio | π_θ(a\|s) / π_θ_old(a\|s); how much more or less likely the new policy is to take an action than the old one |
| KL-divergence constraint | A limit on how much the new policy's behavior can differ from the old policy's, measured in output distributions, not parameters |
| Trust region | The region around θ_old within which the surrogate objective is trusted to approximate the real objective well |

## Recap

Trust region methods recognize that step size should be measured in terms of how much policy *behavior* changes, not how much the parameters move, and they enforce that with a KL-divergence constraint around a probability-ratio surrogate objective. TRPO's exact solution is powerful but expensive. Next up, Lesson 23: Proximal Policy Optimization, the practical algorithm that approximates this same idea far more cheaply.
