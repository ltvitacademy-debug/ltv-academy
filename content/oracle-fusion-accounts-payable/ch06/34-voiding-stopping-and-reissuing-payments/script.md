# Lesson 34 — Voiding, Stopping, and Reissuing Payments · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2.5-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Sometimes a payment goes wrong after it's already created. Which tool you reach for depends almost entirely on timing.

## S2 · STEPS

Voiding cancels a payment before it's left the organization's control — a check printed wrong, a payment created in error and never sent. It's purely internal, and it unwinds the payment completely, putting the invoice back to unpaid as though nothing happened.

## S3 · STEPS

A stop payment is different. It's for a check already sent, or an EFT already issued, that hasn't cleared the bank yet. Unlike a void, this requires action with the bank itself — placing a hold on that specific check number so it's refused if presented. It usually carries a bank fee and isn't instant.

## S4 · CODE

Here's an illustrative example. Harbor Point Logistics was issued a four-thousand-two-hundred-dollar check last week. The supplier calls to say it never arrived. After confirming it hasn't cleared, a stop payment is placed with the bank, the original payment is marked stopped, and the invoice goes back to unpaid.

## S5 · STEPS

Then comes the reissue: a brand-new payment for the same forty-two hundred dollars, same invoice, same supplier — but this time by EFT instead of a check, to avoid the same risk happening twice.

## S6 · OUTRO

Before it's gone, void it. After it's gone but not cleared, stop it. Either way, reissue to finish the job. Next up, lesson thirty-five: supplier refunds, money flowing the other direction.
