# Lesson 31 — Quick Payments · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2.5-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Most payments run through a scheduled batch. This lesson covers the exception: the quick payment.

## S2 · STEPS

A quick payment is a single, immediate payment to one supplier, created directly from the invoice, skipping the batch selection process entirely. It exists for when waiting for the next scheduled payment run isn't acceptable — a supplier on hold elsewhere needing an urgent authorized payment, a rush invoice that has to be paid today, or a one-off manual check a batch wouldn't naturally cover.

## S3 · CODE

Here's an illustrative example. Harbor Point Logistics has a forty-two-hundred-dollar invoice that must be paid today to release a shipment stuck at the dock. Rather than waiting for tomorrow's scheduled payment run, the AP clerk creates a quick payment directly against that invoice: wire as the method, the primary disbursement account, paid the same day.

## S4 · STEPS

Creating one means picking invoices for a single supplier, then specifying the payment method, the disbursement account, and the payment date, right there. No proposed-payment review step, no batch file generation — it processes immediately as its own transaction.

## S5 · STEPS

It trades the efficiency of batching for speed. That's exactly why it should stay the exception — using quick payments for everything would defeat the entire point of running Payment Process Requests.

## S6 · OUTRO

One supplier, one or a few invoices, right now. Next up, lesson thirty-two: the Payment Process Request itself, the batch flow quick payments are built to bypass.
