# Lesson 28 — Withholding and Retainage Concepts · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2.5-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Two more special concepts close out this chapter: withholding tax, and retainage. Both mean the amount paid can legitimately be less than the invoice.

## S2 · CODE

Withholding tax is a legally required deduction remitted to a tax authority instead of paid to the supplier, driven by setup on the supplier record. Harbor Point Logistics invoices ten thousand dollars for transportation services subject to ten percent withholding. Payables calculates the withheld amount automatically during validation: one thousand dollars withheld, nine thousand actually paid to the supplier.

## S3 · STEPS

Retainage works differently. It's a percentage of each progress payment on a long-duration contract, deliberately held back until the work is fully done, as leverage to make sure the contractor finishes to standard. It's set up on complex, progress-payment purchase orders billed across multiple invoices over time.

## S4 · CODE

Meridian Office Supply also runs an office-renovation contract: fifty thousand dollars, ten percent retainage, billed across two progress invoices. Twenty thousand billed, two thousand held, eighteen thousand paid. Thirty thousand billed, three thousand held, twenty-seven thousand paid.

## S5 · STEPS

Across both invoices, forty-five thousand dollars gets paid now, and five thousand accumulates as retainage. Once the renovation is complete and accepted, a retainage release invoice pays out that held-back five thousand.

## S6 · OUTRO

Different purposes — a legal tax obligation versus a contractual control — but the same pattern: invoiced and paid aren't always the same number, by design. That's Chapter 5 complete. Next up, Chapter 6: Payments, starting with payment methods and payment process profiles.
