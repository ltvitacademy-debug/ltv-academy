# Lesson 25 — Four-Way Matching and Matching Tolerances · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2.5-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Four-way matching adds one more checkpoint on top of three-way matching, and this lesson also ties together how tolerances govern every matching level.

## S2 · STEPS

Three-way matching confirmed goods were received. Four-way matching adds inspection and acceptance: someone has to confirm the goods aren't just present, but good. The comparison now runs across quantity ordered, received, accepted, and invoiced. Organizations reserve this extra step for high-value or regulated purchases, because it adds real process time.

## S3 · CODE

Here's an illustrative example. Meridian Office Supply, a fictional supplier, delivers fifty ergonomic chairs at one hundred eighty dollars each. The warehouse receives all fifty. Quality inspection accepts only forty-seven — three arrive with a manufacturing defect and get rejected.

## S4 · CODE

Meridian invoices for the full fifty. All fifty were received, but only forty-seven were accepted — so invoiced quantity exceeds accepted quantity, and the invoice holds until the defective units are replaced and accepted, or the invoice is corrected down to forty-seven.

## S5 · STEPS

Every matching level depends on tolerances to decide what's close enough. A tolerance set bundles several limits together: how far quantity can exceed the PO, how far it can exceed the receipt, and how far price can drift, either by percent or by a flat dollar amount. Each type of violation creates its own distinctly named hold, so whoever clears it knows exactly what to fix.

## S6 · OUTRO

Four checkpoints at most, tolerances deciding what passes through automatically either way. Next up, lesson twenty-six: prepayments, and applying money paid before an invoice even exists.
