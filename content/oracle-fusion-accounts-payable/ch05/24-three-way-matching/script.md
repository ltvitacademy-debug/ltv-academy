# Lesson 24 — Three-Way Matching · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2.5-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Two-way matching compared a PO and an invoice. Three-way matching adds a third document: the receipt.

## S2 · STEPS

A receipt is the record created when goods physically arrive and someone logs them into the system. Three-way matching now compares quantity ordered on the PO, quantity actually received, and quantity and price invoiced. The question it answers that two-way matching can't: did we actually receive what we're being billed for? An invoice can only validate for as much as has been received, not just what was ordered.

## S3 · CODE

Here's an illustrative example. Harbor Point Logistics, a fictional supplier, ships a PO line for two hundred pallets of packaging material at eight dollars each. The warehouse logs a receipt for only one hundred eighty — twenty are still in transit. Harbor Point invoices for the full two hundred, matching the PO price exactly.

## S4 · CODE

Price matches. Quantity matches the PO. But only one hundred eighty have actually been received. Three-way matching catches that gap and places the invoice on a quantity received hold, until either the remaining twenty pallets arrive, or the invoice is corrected.

## S5 · STEPS

Receiving rarely happens all at once. A single PO line is often received across several shipments, and the system tracks the running total against it. If those last twenty pallets show up next week as a second receipt, the held invoice can pass on revalidation without anyone touching the invoice itself.

## S6 · OUTRO

Three-way matching is the standard for physical goods, because a receipt now stands between the PO and the payment. Next up, lesson twenty-five: four-way matching and how tolerances tie all of this together.
