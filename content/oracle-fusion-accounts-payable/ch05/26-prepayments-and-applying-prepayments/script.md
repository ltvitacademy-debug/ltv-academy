# Lesson 26 — Prepayments and Applying Prepayments · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2.5-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Sometimes you pay a supplier before there's a normal invoice to pay against. That's a prepayment, and this lesson covers how to pay it, and later apply it.

## S2 · STEPS

A prepayment is its own invoice type in Payables, not a note on a future bill. There are two kinds: temporary, meant to be applied against a future standard invoice, and permanent, which isn't applied to anything later. We're focused on temporary, because applying it is the whole point.

## S3 · STEPS

The sequence matters. First you create the prepayment invoice for the deposit amount. Then you validate it like any invoice. Then — and this is required — you pay it in full. Only a fully paid prepayment is eligible to be applied later, whether that happens automatically or manually.

## S4 · CODE

Here's an illustrative example. BrightPath Consulting Group, a fictional supplier, requires a thirty percent deposit before starting a twenty-thousand-dollar engagement. A six-thousand-dollar prepayment is created and paid. When the full engagement invoice for twenty thousand arrives, the six-thousand-dollar prepayment is applied against it.

## S5 · CODE

The net amount still payable drops to fourteen thousand dollars. Without applying that prepayment, the organization risks paying the full twenty thousand on top of the deposit it already sent — paying for the same work twice.

## S6 · OUTRO

Create, validate, pay, then apply — and only a fully paid, unapplied prepayment for the same supplier qualifies. Next up, lesson twenty-seven: credit memos and debit memos.
