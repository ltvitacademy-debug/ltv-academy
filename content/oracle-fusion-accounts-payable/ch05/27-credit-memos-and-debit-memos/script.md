# Lesson 27 — Credit Memos and Debit Memos · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2.5-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Credit memos and debit memos both reduce what you owe a supplier. The difference between them is simply who creates the document.

## S2 · STEPS

A credit memo comes from the supplier, acknowledging an overcharge, a return, or a pricing error on their own invoice. A debit memo is raised by your own organization instead — often while a dispute is still being worked out, so your position is on the record even before the supplier agrees. Both are entered as negative-amount invoices.

## S3 · CODE

Here's an illustrative example. Solace Robotics, a fictional company, received a fifteen-thousand-dollar invoice for a batch of components. Ten percent of the batch turned out defective. The supplier issues a credit memo for fifteen hundred dollars, acknowledging the return.

## S4 · CODE

Net amount now owed: thirteen thousand five hundred dollars. If the supplier had been slow to issue that credit, Solace Robotics' own AP team could instead record a debit memo for the same amount, documenting the dispute internally while collections continued.

## S5 · STEPS

Either memo type can be matched back to the original purchase order or the original invoice it corrects, the same way a standard invoice is matched. That keeps the reduction tied to a specific transaction instead of floating as an unexplained negative balance against the supplier.
