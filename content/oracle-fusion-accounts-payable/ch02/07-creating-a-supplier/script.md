# Script — Creating a Supplier

## Segment 1 (title)

With the supplier, site, and contact model in your head from last lesson, creating a supplier is really just filling in that hierarchy one level at a time. Let's walk the Create Supplier flow in order.

## Segment 2 (steps)

It starts small: supplier name, a business relationship, and tax identification. The business relationship matters more than it looks. Prospective means the supplier can go through sourcing and qualification but can't be paid yet. Spend Authorized means they're cleared for real purchase orders and invoices. A new vendor often starts Prospective and flips to Spend Authorized once they clear qualification.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Fusion separates addresses from sites so you never type an address twice. You create one or more addresses on the Addresses tab first, each with its own internal address name. Then on the Sites tab, you build a site by picking one of those addresses and giving the site its own unique site name. That's how one physical address can end up backing two differently-named sites.

## Segment 4 (code)

Past name and tax ID, the full profile has tabs worth knowing by name: Organization, Business Classifications, Products and Services, Transaction Tax, Income Tax, and Payments, where the bank account eventually attaches. You can save a supplier before all of those are filled in - which is exactly how a new vendor ends up unable to be paid on their first invoice.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Brightfield's fictional new vendor, Hearthstone Logistics, goes in as Spend Authorized, with one address and one pay site built from it - still needing a business-unit assignment before any invoice can hit it. Up next, lesson eight: supplier addresses and business classifications in more depth.
