# Script — Supplier Registration and Qualification Overview

## Segment 1 (title)

So far this chapter assumed someone at Brightfield manually types a new supplier into existence. In practice, plenty of suppliers start differently - they register themselves, and procurement decides whether to let them in. This lesson covers that front door, and the screening that usually sits behind it.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Supplier registration lets a potential trading partner submit their own information directly - company details, tax ID, addresses, banking, classifications - instead of Brightfield's team typing it all in by hand. Until that registration is reviewed and accepted, the supplier exists in a restricted state: they can log in and respond to negotiations or surveys, but they can't be paid and have limited visibility into anything else.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Supplier Qualification Management adds a formal screening layer on top. Rather than letting any self-registered company straight into the Supplier Master, it distributes a qualification survey covering things like certifications, insurance, or safety record, scores the responses against predefined criteria, and lets procurement decide whether to accept based on that score.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Put together: a company registers, Brightfield routes it through qualification, procurement accepts or rejects it, and an accepted registration becomes a real supplier record - usually starting Prospective and moving to Spend Authorized once its site, address, and bank account are complete. Not every supplier needs this formal path; a buyer can still manually create a low-risk, one-off vendor.

## Segment 5 (outro)

The point of qualification is keeping unqualified or unwanted suppliers out of the Supplier Master in the first place, rather than cleaning up after the fact. Up next, lesson eleven: supplier maintenance and merging, what happens to a supplier record after it's created.
