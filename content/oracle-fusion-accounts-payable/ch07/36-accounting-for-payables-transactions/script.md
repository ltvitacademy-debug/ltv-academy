## S1 · TITLE CARD

Welcome to Chapter 7. Every transaction we've covered eventually has to become a real accounting journal. This lesson is about the engine that does that.

## S2 · STEPS

That engine is called Subledger Accounting, shared across every subledger instead of each one building its own logic. It works from accounting rules tied to an accounting method assigned to a ledger — configurable rules, not hardcoded account numbers.

## S3 · STEPS

Not every action creates accounting. Only specific accounting events do: invoice validation debits an expense or asset account and credits the AP liability. Payment debits that liability and credits cash. Prepayment application reclassifies between a prepayment asset and whatever it offsets.

## S4 · CODE

Here's an illustrative example. Cascade Industrial Parts invoices twelve hundred fifty dollars for bearing assemblies, validated with no holds. That creates a journal: debit inventory or expense twelve fifty, credit accounts payable liability twelve fifty.

## S5 · CODE

When that invoice is later paid by EFT, a second, separate journal happens: debit the AP liability twelve fifty, credit cash twelve fifty. Two separate events, two separate journals — which is exactly why a validated but unpaid invoice still shows up as a liability on the balance sheet.

## S6 · OUTRO

Rules-driven, not hardcoded, so the same engine handles every ledger, business unit, and category correctly. Next up, lesson thirty-seven: actually running Create Accounting in Payables.
