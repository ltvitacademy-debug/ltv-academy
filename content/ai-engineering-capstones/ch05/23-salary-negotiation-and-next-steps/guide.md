## Why no specific numbers appear here

Salary figures go stale fast, vary enormously by country, metro area, company stage, and even which part of the AI stack a role focuses on, and this catalog has no reliable way to verify a precise number as current for your specific market. Instead of quoting a figure that might already be wrong, this lesson teaches the same research process this catalog's other career-prep courses use — building your *own* range from live, current sources.

## Why AI engineering pay varies even more than most tech roles

- **Titles aren't standardized at all.** "AI Engineer," "Machine Learning Engineer," "Applied AI Engineer," "Generative AI Developer," and "AI Solutions Developer" can mean nearly identical work at different companies — or very different scopes under the same title. Some of these roles lean toward building applications on top of existing models (closer to this path's focus); others lean toward training or fine-tuning models from scratch. Read the actual responsibilities in a posting, not just its title.
- **Company stage swings compensation enormously.** A funded AI-native startup, a large tech company's AI platform team, and an early-stage product adding AI features can offer very different pay for similar day-to-day work.
- **Equity adds a real variable cash pay doesn't have.** Many AI roles, especially at startups, include equity alongside (or instead of) a larger bonus — research this as its own line item, not folded into a base-salary number.

When researching your own range, search under several of these title variants, and note whether a posting's total compensation includes any equity component.

## Building a range, using this path's full research toolkit

| Source type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Aggregated salary sites | Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Payscale, LinkedIn Salary |
| Government labor data | Your country's official labor statistics agency |
| Direct market signal | Live job postings in your target companies, searched under several AI-role title variants |

Build a low-to-high band from at least two independent sources, for your actual experience level and the kind of company you're targeting (startup vs. large tech company vs. traditional company adding AI features) — never a single remembered number without a source behind it.

## Answering "what are your salary expectations"

The same principle holds here as everywhere else: whoever names a number first anchors the conversation. Redirect to the posted or budgeted range where you can ("I'd love to hear what range you had in mind for this role"); if pushed, answer with your own researched range.

## Evaluating and countering a real offer

```
Thank you for the offer -- I'm genuinely excited about this role
and the team.

Based on my research for AI Engineer / Applied AI Engineer roles
at companies at a similar stage, similar positions tend to run
somewhat higher on the base, and I wanted to ask about the equity
component as well.

Is there room to move the base toward that range, or revisit the
equity grant to bridge the gap?

I'm ready to move forward once we're aligned.
```

Look at the whole package before responding: base, any equity grant and its vesting schedule and cliff, signing bonus, remote/hybrid flexibility, and the timeline to your first compensation review — not just the headline base number.
