## Turning feedback into action, not a reaction

After Lesson 24's presentation, Priya, Renata, and Dmitri will each give feedback from their own vantage point — and not all feedback is equally actionable right now. The useful move is sorting it into three buckets: **fix before calling this done** (a genuine bug or gap in the Definition of Done), **log as technical debt** (a real limitation, already named in Lesson 22, that's acceptable to defer), and **out of scope** (a good idea that belongs in a future project, not a retrofit onto this one). Treating every piece of feedback as equally urgent is how a finished capstone never actually finishes; treating all of it as optional is how real gaps get waved away.

## What "portfolio-ready" means for this platform, specifically

A portfolio-ready result from this capstone isn't just a scratch org that still exists — it's five things, several of which you built across the last 24 lessons without necessarily labeling them as portfolio pieces yet:

1. **The Git repository** (Lesson 5), with real commit history showing incremental work, not one giant commit.
2. **The ERD and data dictionary** (Lessons 3 and 21).
3. **The five ADRs** (Lesson 21), showing deliberate decision-making under real trade-offs.
4. **A recording of the demo** (Lesson 23), showing the platform actually working end to end.
5. **The retrospective** (Lesson 22), showing you can honestly evaluate your own work.

Any one of these on its own is a fragment; together, they're exactly what a hiring manager or a technical interviewer for a Platform Developer or Solution Architect role wants to see — not just that you can write Apex, but that you can design, build, test, document, and honestly assess a real system.

## Where this sits in the ladder

This capstone is **Capstone II** on the Technical Architect ladder — the programmatic-development milestone between the declarative Capstone I you may have completed earlier and the advanced architecture case studies and enterprise-scale capstone still ahead. Completing it means you've now demonstrated custom data modeling, a security model, Flow, Apex (classes, triggers, tests, async), Lightning Web Components, and a REST integration — the full technical core this ladder's later architecture coursework assumes you already have, rather than re-teaches from scratch.

## What comes next

The ladder continues from here into advanced architecture topics — integration patterns at enterprise scale, governance across multiple orgs, and deeper security architecture — building toward the enterprise transformation capstone that closes this ladder with a full Architecture Review Board defense, referenced in Lesson 24. That capstone assumes the skills from this one are solid, not something to relearn, which is exactly why this lesson's "portfolio-ready" checklist matters: it's not just evidence for an interview, it's the foundation the next course is built on.
