# Script — How This Research Lab Works

## Segment 1 (title)

Welcome to the LTV AI Research Lab, the capstone of the AI and ML Research Engineer and Alignment Engineer destination. Every course so far has taught you a specific tool and checked your work against a known answer. This one works differently: you're joining a small research lab for three connected projects, deliberately built around SQL and data systems instead of generic toy tasks, because that's this catalog's own strength.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Project 1 trains a reinforcement learning agent to pick query-plan hints against AdventureWorks2012 and compares it to SQL Server's own optimizer. Project 2 builds a small preference dataset over cleaned Northwind customer records and trains a reward model to score them. Project 3 fine-tunes a small open-weight model into "SQL Pete," a natural-language-to-SQL assistant, then runs RLHF on top of it. After that, a case study opens up SQL Pete itself to see what it's doing inside.

## Segment 3 (steps)

A graded course hands you a problem with a known answer. A research project doesn't — you decide what counts as evidence. So keep a dated research notebook from day one: what you tried, what you expected, what actually happened. Dead ends belong in there too. A documented dead end is still a finding, and it's exactly what each project's write-up asks you to summarize.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Three project write-ups and one interpretability finding are the raw material you're producing, one per chapter as you go. In the final chapter, you'll assemble all of it into a single portfolio artifact — the kind of thing you'd actually show a hiring manager — and practice presenting it out loud to a technical audience.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Hold onto that shape: three connected projects, one notebook habit running underneath all of them, one assembled portfolio at the end. Up next, lesson two: what actually makes a research question worth spending days of real work on.
