# Script — Presenting Findings to a Technical Audience

## Segment 1 (title)

A portfolio sitting in a repo is only half the job. At some point — an interview, a team meeting, a lightning talk — you'll present this same material out loud, in about ten minutes, to people who will ask hard questions. This lesson covers structuring that talk for a technical audience.

## Segment 2 (code)

Ten minutes isn't enough to present four projects in full — it's enough for one argument, well supported. Spend the first minute on the problem, the next three on just enough method to be trusted, four minutes on the actual result, and the last two on limitations and what's next. Notice how little time method gets relative to result — a technical audience forgives skipped detail, not a talk that never shows real numbers.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Design slides for engineers, not executives. One claim per slide — if a slide makes two points, it's two slides. Show the actual numbers or the actual plot, not a paraphrase. And label your axes and your baseline, because that's the first thing a technical audience looks for on any chart.

## Segment 4 (steps)

A technical crowd asks a predictable set of questions, so prepare honest answers in advance. Why this baseline. How many runs or seeds — and if it's one, say so. What broke — naming the real dead end from your notebook builds more credibility than pretending it all worked first try. And what would change your conclusion, which is just the falsifiability test from lesson two, asked out loud.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Ten minutes, one argument, real numbers on the slides, honest answers ready for the questions you already know are coming. Up next, the final lesson of the whole course: where this research could go next from here.
