## A realistic progression

Titles and exact structures vary by firm, but the underlying progression is consistent across the industry:

- **Functional Analyst** — early career, works under supervision on well-defined configuration and support tasks. This is roughly where your first 90 days (lesson 14) lands you.
- **Functional Consultant** — owns defined workstreams independently, handles client communication directly on their piece of the engagement, and is trusted with real configuration decisions.
- **Senior Consultant** — leads a module or workstream across a full engagement, mentors more junior consultants, and increasingly shapes requirements rather than just implementing them.
- **Lead / Principal / Practice roles** — owns client relationships at the engagement level, shapes overall solution design, and in some firms moves toward practice-building (growing a service line, not just delivering projects).

## Two different environments for the same skill set

- **Staffing or consulting firm.** You work across multiple clients and industries over time, which builds breadth faster and exposes you to more implementation patterns — but comes with travel, staffing changes between projects, and billable-hour pressure.
- **In-house (working for one company that runs Oracle Fusion Financials internally).** You go deep on one organization's specific configuration and processes, build long-term institutional knowledge, and generally have more schedule stability — but less variety and slower exposure to new implementation patterns.

Neither path is objectively better — they suit different priorities (breadth and pace of learning versus depth and stability), and plenty of consultants move between them over a career.

## Specializing over time

As you grow past Functional Consultant, most people specialize in some direction: a vertical industry (manufacturing, healthcare, higher education — each with its own common configuration patterns), a horizontal depth area (Subledger Accounting and reporting, for instance, which touches every other module), or a methodology/leadership track (moving toward project and practice leadership rather than deeper technical depth). There's no wrong choice here — it's worth paying attention, over your first few engagements, to which kind of work you actually enjoy more.
