# Oracle Certification Preparation Guidance

**Chapter 2 · Resume, Capstone Story and Certification · Lesson 12 of 15**

This lesson is guidance, not a guarantee. **LTV Academy does not issue Oracle certifications**, and completing this path does not automatically earn you one — a real Oracle certification requires passing Oracle's own proctored exam directly through Oracle University. What this lesson gives you is an honest map of the landscape and the habit of checking it yourself, because the landscape changes more often than people expect.

## What you'll learn

- The general tiers Oracle's certification structure uses for Cloud Applications
- Why exam names, codes, and structure change on a schedule you don't control
- Where to check the current, authoritative information before you plan or pay for anything

## The general tiers, described honestly

Oracle organizes its Cloud Applications certifications into tiers of increasing depth — a foundational tier confirming broad conceptual knowledge of a product area, and an implementation-focused tier confirming you can actually configure and implement a specific module (General Ledger, Payables, Receivables, and so on). Oracle has renamed these tiers before — what was once labeled a "Foundations Associate" credential has, across several Cloud Applications product lines, been renamed to a "Process Essentials Certified" credential — and Oracle has stated the rename doesn't invalidate certifications already earned under the old name.

## Why this drifts, and why that's not unusual

Exam codes themselves are versioned to the year — an exam might be numbered with a year suffix that gets replaced annually as content refreshes to match the current state of the cloud application (Oracle Cloud Applications update continuously, unlike older on-premise software with fixed release versions). This isn't a flaw in Oracle's program; it reflects that the underlying product itself keeps changing. It does mean that any specific exam code or name you read in a blog post, a forum, or even in this lesson, may already be stale by the time you act on it.

## Where to check before you plan anything

Go to Oracle University's own certification pages and, once credentialed, Oracle CertView, for the current, authoritative list of exams, prerequisites, and structure for the specific module you're targeting (GL, AP, AR, or whichever fits your target role). Treat any third-party blog, study-guide site, or practice-exam vendor's description of "the current exam" as a starting point to verify against Oracle's own page, not as the final word — exactly the discipline lesson 5's troubleshooting questions trained: isolate the specific, current claim, and confirm it against the real source before acting on it.

## What a realistic goal looks like

A realistic first certification target for someone finishing this path is an implementation-level credential in the module you're strongest in and most want to work in — General Ledger, Payables, or Receivables are all reasonable choices given everything this path covers. Don't chase a credential just because it sounds senior; chase the one that matches the role you're actually applying for.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Foundations / Process Essentials tier | Oracle's broader, conceptual-knowledge certification tier (name has changed over time) |
| Implementation Professional tier | The deeper, module-specific implementation certification tier |
| Oracle CertView | Oracle's own system of record for a candidate's earned certifications |
