# Building a Portfolio of Your Practice Work

**Chapter 2 · Resume, Capstone Story and Certification · Lesson 10 of 15**

A resume bullet and a good interview story go a long way, but a portfolio gives you something to actually hand someone — a concrete artifact that backs up every claim. Oracle Fusion Cloud is login-gated, licensed enterprise software, so this lesson is about what you realistically *can* put in a portfolio, and what you shouldn't.

## What you'll learn

- What you can and can't share from a licensed Oracle Fusion environment
- Four concrete portfolio artifacts you can actually build from your own practice work
- How to present a portfolio piece so it reads as evidence, not decoration

## What you can't do, and why

Oracle Fusion Cloud is licensed software, and screenshots of a live instance may be covered by terms of use you don't control, especially in a shared or employer-provided training environment. Don't assume it's fine to publish screenshots publicly just because you personally configured the screen — check the specific terms that apply to whatever environment you used, and when in doubt, leave screenshots out of anything public. This isn't unique to Oracle; it's the same caution that applies to screenshots from any licensed enterprise software.

## Four things you can actually build

1. **A written requirements and configuration workbook.** The capstone's Chapter 1 had you start a requirements document and build on it through Chapter 4 — clean that up into a shareable artifact: the company snapshot, the enterprise structure design, the chart of accounts, and your configuration decisions with the reasoning behind each one. This is text and your own original diagrams, not a screenshot, so it's entirely yours to share.
2. **An issue log in the style of the capstone's close.** Document the six root causes you diagnosed and fixed (or any subset from your own practice) the way a real consultant would log them: symptom, root cause, fix applied, verification. This single artifact demonstrates the exact skill lesson 9 covers, in writing, that an interviewer can read before you even walk in.
3. **Original SQL you wrote for reconciliation checks.** If you wrote your own queries during the SQL-for-Oracle-Financials coursework, a small collection of annotated, original SQL — the kind of reconciliation logic shown in lesson 7 — is genuinely yours to share in a public code repository, because it's your own writing, not a screenshot of licensed software.
4. **A one-page case study write-up of the capstone.** A short, narrative document — company situation, the six problems, your investigation approach, the fixes, the outcome — written entirely in your own words. This is the STAR story from lesson 9, formatted as a page you can link from a resume or LinkedIn profile instead of just describing it verbally.

## Presenting it as evidence, not decoration

Every portfolio piece should answer "what does this prove I can do?" in one sentence before someone even reads the detail. A requirements workbook proves you can gather and structure real requirements. An issue log proves you can diagnose and fix, not just configure. Lead with that sentence, then let the detail back it up.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Portfolio artifact | A concrete, shareable document that provides evidence for a skill claim |
| Terms of use | The licensing rules governing what you can share from a specific software environment — check before publishing screenshots |
