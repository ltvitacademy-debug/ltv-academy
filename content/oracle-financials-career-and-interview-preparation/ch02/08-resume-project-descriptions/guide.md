# Resume Project Descriptions

**Chapter 2 · Resume, Capstone Story and Certification · Lesson 8 of 15**

You've done real, hands-on Oracle Fusion Financials work across this entire path — configured enterprise structures, built every subledger, run transactions, and completed a full month-end close with six real problems to solve. None of that helps you if it isn't on your resume in language a recruiter and a hiring manager both understand quickly. This lesson turns that work into bullet points that hold up under questioning.

## What you'll learn

- A bullet-point formula that fits practice/training work honestly, without inflating it into real client experience
- How to describe each major area of the path (GL, AP, AR, Assets, Cash Management, SLA, the capstone close) as a resume line
- What never to put on a resume, and why it backfires in an interview

## The formula: action, system, outcome

A strong resume bullet names what you did, in which part of the system, with what result — not a vague restatement of a course title.

> **Weak:** "Learned Oracle Fusion General Ledger."
> **Strong:** "Configured a two-entity Oracle Fusion General Ledger enterprise structure (legal entities, ledgers, business units, balancing segment) supporting intercompany elimination across a US parent and Canadian subsidiary."

> **Weak:** "Studied Accounts Payable."
> **Strong:** "Built Accounts Payable 2-way/3-way matching, hold resolution, and GRNI accrual configuration; diagnosed and corrected an unreversed accrual causing an AP aging-to-GL mismatch during a simulated month-end close."

## Honest framing for training work

Be precise about what this work is: hands-on configuration and transaction practice in a structured training environment, built around a detailed fictional company scenario, not a paid client engagement. You do not need to claim otherwise, and you shouldn't — a resume bullet can say "built and reconciled a full Accounts Payable cycle, including GRNI accrual and 3-way matching, in an Oracle Fusion Financials training environment" and still read as a real, substantive skill, because it is one. Claiming client or production experience you don't have is the fastest way to lose credibility the moment an interviewer asks a natural follow-up question.

## Sample bullets by area

- **Enterprise structure & COA:** "Designed a chart of accounts and enterprise structure for a two-legal-entity manufacturer, with a balancing segment supporting clean intercompany elimination."
- **Payables:** "Configured 2-way/3-way matching, invoice holds, and GRNI accrual processing; traced and corrected an unreversed accrual during a simulated close."
- **Receivables:** "Configured AutoInvoice and receipt application; diagnosed a misapplied receipt causing conflicting AR aging results and reapplied it correctly."
- **Fixed Assets:** "Configured asset categories and depreciation methods; identified and corrected a mis-categorized asset that was overstating monthly depreciation."
- **Cash Management:** "Performed bank statement reconciliation, including an investigation of a duplicate bank transmission requiring bank-side correction."
- **Subledger Accounting:** "Configured Subledger Accounting rules; diagnosed a missing account rule mapping causing transactions to post as Incomplete, and reran Create Accounting after the fix."
- **Capstone close:** "Led a simulated month-end close across AP, AR, Assets, Cash Management, SLA, and intercompany GL for a two-entity manufacturer, resolving six interconnected reconciliation issues before closing the period."
