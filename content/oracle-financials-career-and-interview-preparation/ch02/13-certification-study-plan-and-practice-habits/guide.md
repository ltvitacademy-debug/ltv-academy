# Certification Study Plan and Practice Habits

**Chapter 2 · Resume, Capstone Story and Certification · Lesson 13 of 15**

Lesson 12 covered the landscape and why it drifts. This lesson is about the actual plan: how to study for a real Oracle certification exam using what you've already built in this path, without falling into the trap of mistaking a passed practice exam for genuine readiness.

## What you'll learn

- A realistic weekly study structure you can actually sustain
- Why your hands-on configuration work already covers more exam content than you might assume
- The three-question readiness check to run before you schedule anything, and schedule it only through Oracle's own current process

## Build the study plan around what you already did, not from zero

Before building a study plan from scratch, map your own path work against the exam's published objectives (from Oracle University's current page, per lesson 12). You spent an entire course on General Ledger, an entire course each on Payables, Receivables, Fixed Assets, Cash Management, and Subledger Accounting, plus a full capstone close. Large parts of any Financials certification's objectives are likely already familiar — your study plan should focus on the specific gaps, not re-teach everything from the beginning.

## A realistic weekly structure

- **2-3 sessions a week, 45-60 minutes each**, rather than one long weekend cram — spaced repetition beats a single marathon session for retention.
- **Alternate between objective review and hands-on rebuild.** Reading an objective is not the same as being able to configure it. For each major topic, actually rebuild the configuration in a practice environment without a guide open, the same way you did throughout this path.
- **Keep a running list of specific gaps**, not a vague feeling of "I should review GL again." A gap should be as specific as "I'm not confident on secondary ledger use cases" — specific enough to actually study.

## The three-question readiness check

Before scheduling a real exam, answer these honestly:

1. **Can I rebuild the core objectives hands-on, without a guide open, not just recognize the right multiple-choice answer?**
2. **Did my own path work (the relevant course, and the capstone where it applies) already cover a meaningful share of these objectives?** If entire sections still feel brand new, that's a signal to build more hands-on practice, not to cram harder.
3. **Am I using practice exams as a readiness check, not as the study plan itself?** A practice exam score tells you where the remaining gaps are. It doesn't fill them, and a passed practice exam is not the same thing as being ready for the real one.

## Scheduling the real thing

Once you've answered all three honestly, schedule the actual exam only through Oracle's own current registration process (Oracle University, as covered in lesson 12) — never through a third party claiming to offer a shortcut, and never by paying for leaked or "guaranteed" exam content, which violates Oracle's certification agreement and can result in your results being invalidated.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Spaced repetition | Studying in shorter, regular sessions over time rather than one long cram session |
| Readiness check | Confirming hands-on ability, not just a passed practice exam, before scheduling the real test |
