# Script — Assets

## Segment 1 (title)

Chapter two gave you the mechanics of debits and credits. Chapter three slows down and looks closely at each of the five account types, starting with the one most people already have a feel for: assets.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Formally, an asset is a resource controlled by a business, as a result of a past transaction, expected to provide future economic benefit. Controlled, not necessarily owned outright. Past transaction, not just something you expect to acquire someday. Future economic benefit - it should help generate cash going forward.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Assets split into two buckets. Current assets convert to cash or get used up within about a year - cash, receivables, inventory. Noncurrent assets provide benefit longer than a year - equipment, buildings, land, patents. This split matters enormously once we reach the balance sheet.

## Segment 4 (code)

Picture a fictional retailer, Harlow and Vance Supply Co. Cash, inventory, and receivables are current. A warehouse, delivery trucks, and a patent are noncurrent. Notice inventory counts as current even though it sits on a shelf for weeks - the test is how quickly it converts to cash, not how it looks sitting there.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Several entire Oracle Fusion modules exist to track specific categories of assets. Receivables tracks what customers owe. Cash Management tracks and reconciles cash. Fixed Assets tracks equipment and buildings, including depreciation. Up next, lesson ten: liabilities, the mirror image of what we just covered.
