# Script — Revenue

## Segment 1 (title)

Revenue is where a business's story usually starts: the money it makes from doing what it does. This lesson defines it precisely, because exactly when to record revenue is subtler than most people expect.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Revenue is the increase in equity from a business's ordinary, core operating activities - selling goods or providing services. It's recorded when it's earned - when the business has substantially done what it promised - regardless of whether cash has changed hands yet.

## Segment 3 (steps)

This is the key idea: revenue is not the same as cash received. Bill a client twenty-five hundred dollars on thirty-day credit, and revenue is earned today, even with no cash yet. Flip it around: a customer pays twelve thousand upfront for a year of subscription service. That's cash received, but only a sliver has actually been earned - the rest sits as a liability called unearned revenue until it's delivered.

## Segment 4 (code)

Picture a fictional design studio, Palisade Creative Group, completing a nine thousand dollar project on March twenty-eighth and getting paid April fifteenth. March twenty-eighth: debit accounts receivable, credit service revenue - revenue earned. April fifteenth: debit cash, credit accounts receivable - zero new revenue, just converting one asset into another.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Revenue recognition, deciding exactly when revenue counts as earned, is one of the most carefully governed areas in accounting, and Oracle Fusion Receivables exists specifically to apply consistent rules across thousands of transactions.
