# Script — Expenses

## Segment 1 (title)

Expenses close out chapter three and complete the set of five account types. Like revenue, expenses are tied to a specific idea about when they should be recorded - the matching principle, one of the quietly most powerful ideas in all of accounting.

## Segment 2 (steps)

An expense is the decrease in equity from a business's core operations - rent, salaries, utilities, supplies used up. The matching principle says expenses should be recorded in the same period as the revenue they helped generate, not necessarily the period they were paid for.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Exactly like revenue, an expense isn't the same as cash paid. A company uses electricity in March but doesn't pay the bill until April - the expense belongs in March, when the cost was incurred. Prepay a year of insurance in January, and only one twelfth is an expense that month; the rest sits as a prepaid asset.

## Segment 4 (code)

Picture a fictional landscaping company, Fernhollow Grounds Co., using six hundred dollars of electricity in March with the bill arriving in April. March thirty-first: debit utilities expense, credit utilities payable - the expense is recorded now. April fifteenth: debit utilities payable, credit cash - no new expense, just settling the obligation.

## Segment 5 (outro)

The matching principle is exactly why accrual accounting and adjusting entries exist as formal processes. Oracle Fusion's period-close process is built around making sure every expense that belongs in a period actually gets recorded there before the books close.
