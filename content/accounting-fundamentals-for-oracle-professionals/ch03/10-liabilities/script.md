# Script — Liabilities

## Segment 1 (title)

Liabilities are the mirror image of assets: instead of what a business controls, they represent what it owes. Nearly every idea from the asset lesson has a direct counterpart here.

## Segment 2 (steps)

A liability is a present obligation of the business, from a past transaction, that will require a future outflow of economic benefit to settle. It's owed now, not just maybe someday. Something already happened to create it - a purchase on credit, a loan, wages earned but not yet paid.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Liabilities split the same way assets do. Current liabilities are due within about a year - accounts payable, wages payable, taxes payable. Long-term liabilities are due beyond a year - a mortgage, long-term notes, bonds. A single loan can actually straddle both: the portion due within twelve months is current, the rest stays long-term.

## Segment 4 (code)

Picture a fictional manufacturer, Caldwell Precision Works. Accounts payable, wages payable, and sales tax owed are current. Its ten-year factory mortgage splits: the part due next month is current, the remaining nine-plus years are long-term. New students often think liabilities are only loans, but the most common one is just accounts payable - amounts owed to suppliers, no loan agreement required.
