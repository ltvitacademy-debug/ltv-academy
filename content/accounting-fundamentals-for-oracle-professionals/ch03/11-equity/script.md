# Script — Equity

## Segment 1 (title)

Equity is the third and final piece of the accounting equation, and it tends to confuse new students most - partly because it looks different depending on the business structure. This lesson gives you one consistent way to think about it.

## Segment 2 (steps)

The biggest misconception: equity is not a pot of cash sitting somewhere. Equity equals assets minus liabilities - whatever is left over for the owners once every liability is subtracted from every asset. If a business's assets are entirely financed by liabilities, equity is zero, even though the business clearly has plenty of assets.

## Segment 3 (steps)

The label changes by structure. A sole proprietorship has an owner's capital account. A partnership has a separate capital account per partner. A corporation splits equity into common stock, what shareholders paid in, and retained earnings, accumulated profits kept rather than distributed.

## Segment 4 (code)

Here's the key connection: net income, revenue minus expenses, flows into retained earnings each period, and dividends reduce it. Picture a fictional company, Thornbury Analytics, starting the year with two hundred thousand in retained earnings, earning seventy thousand in net income, and paying twenty thousand in dividends - ending at two hundred fifty thousand.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Oracle Fusion General Ledger automatically closes revenue and expense accounts into retained earnings at year end, as part of the year-end close process. Understanding equity as a residual fed by net income is exactly what makes that automated step make sense. Up next, lesson twelve: revenue.
