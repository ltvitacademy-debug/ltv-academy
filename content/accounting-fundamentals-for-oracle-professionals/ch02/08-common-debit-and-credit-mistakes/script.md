# Script — Common Debit and Credit Mistakes

## Segment 1 (title)

Every student, and more than a few professionals, makes the same handful of debit and credit mistakes on the way to fluency. This lesson closes out chapter two by naming them directly.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Mistake one: thinking debit always means increase. It doesn't - debit means left. A debit increases cash but decreases accounts payable. Always ask what type of account this is first. Mistake two: forgetting that totals must balance. A five hundred dollar debit next to a four hundred fifty dollar credit isn't close enough - it's wrong.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Mistake three: picking the wrong account even with the right side. The debits and credits can balance perfectly while still telling the wrong financial story - say, recording loan principal as pure interest expense. Mistake four: confusing a normal balance with the only balance an account can ever have. A normal balance is what to expect, not a law of physics - an overdrawn checking account can briefly show a credit balance.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Before finalizing any entry, run three checks. Do total debits equal total credits? Did I identify every account this transaction actually touches? Does each account's direction make sense given what really happened?

## Segment 5 (outro)

Oracle Fusion will absolutely stop an unbalanced entry from posting, and some validation rules can catch wrong accounts too. But it can't catch a human correctly balancing an entry that tells the wrong story. That's why the judgment this course builds still matters inside a heavily automated system.
