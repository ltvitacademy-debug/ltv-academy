# Normal Balances by Account Type

In the last lesson, we established that debit just means left, and credit just means right. Now we connect that to the five account types every transaction ultimately touches, and learn the single table that most accountants have memorized since their first week of class.

## What you'll learn

- The five fundamental account types
- Each type's "normal balance" — the side that increases it
- A memory device for keeping all five straight
- Why this table is the single most useful thing to memorize in this entire course

## The five account types

Every account in every business, no matter how complex, falls into one of five categories:

1. **Assets** — things the business owns or controls (cash, inventory, equipment, amounts owed to it)
2. **Liabilities** — things the business owes to others (loans, unpaid bills)
3. **Equity** — the owners' residual claim (common stock, retained earnings)
4. **Revenue** — increases in equity from the business's core operations (sales, fees earned)
5. **Expenses** — decreases in equity from the cost of operating (rent, salaries, utilities)

Notice that the first three are the pieces of the accounting equation from lesson 2. Revenue and Expenses are new here, but they are really just equity in disguise — revenue increases equity (the business got richer by earning it), and expenses decrease equity (the business got poorer by spending it). That link is what makes the whole system cohere.

## Normal balances: the table to memorize

Every account type has a **normal balance** — the side (debit or credit) on which *increases* to that account are recorded. Decreases are always recorded on the opposite side.

| Account Type | Normal Balance | Increases with | Decreases with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assets | Debit | Debit | Credit |
| Expenses | Debit | Debit | Credit |
| Liabilities | Credit | Credit | Debit |
| Equity | Credit | Credit | Debit |
| Revenue | Credit | Credit | Debit |

A common mnemonic for the debit-normal accounts is **"DEAD"**: **D**ividends, **E**xpenses, **A**ssets, **D**raws all normally carry debit balances. The remaining accounts — liabilities, equity, revenue — normally carry credit balances.

## Why this table works with the equation

Go back to Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Assets sit on the left of the equals sign, so they naturally increase on the left (debit) side. Liabilities and Equity sit on the right of the equals sign, so they naturally increase on the right (credit) side. Revenue increases Equity, so it behaves like Equity (credit-normal). Expenses decrease Equity, so they behave the *opposite* of Equity (debit-normal). The whole table is really just one geometric idea — which side of the equation something sits on — applied consistently.

## Worked example

A fictional company, **Ashgrove Fixtures Inc.**, receives $5,000 cash from a customer for services performed.

- Cash is an **asset**. The asset increased, so it's debited: Debit Cash $5,000.
- Service Revenue is **revenue**. Revenue increased, so it's credited: Credit Service Revenue $5,000.

Debits ($5,000) = Credits ($5,000). Balanced, and each account moved in its normal-balance direction because both increased.

Now Ashgrove Fixtures Inc. pays $1,200 rent in cash.

- Cash (asset) decreased, so it's credited: Credit Cash $1,200.
- Rent Expense (expense) increased, so it's debited: Debit Rent Expense $1,200.

## Recap

Every account is one of five types: Assets, Liabilities, Equity, Revenue, or Expenses. Assets and Expenses are debit-normal; Liabilities, Equity, and Revenue are credit-normal. This single table, grounded in the accounting equation, is the mental model behind every journal entry for the rest of this course — and every journal entry Oracle Fusion posts behind the scenes.
