# Script — Normal Balances by Account Type

## Segment 1 (title)

Last lesson, debit just meant left and credit just meant right. Now we connect that to the five account types every transaction touches, and the one table most accountants have memorized since their first week of class.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Every account falls into one of five categories: assets, liabilities, equity, revenue, and expenses. The first three are the pieces of the accounting equation. Revenue and expenses are really just equity in disguise - revenue increases equity, expenses decrease it. That link is what makes the whole system cohere.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Here's the table. Assets and expenses are debit-normal: they increase with a debit and decrease with a credit. Liabilities, equity, and revenue are credit-normal: they increase with a credit and decrease with a debit. A common mnemonic is DEAD: dividends, expenses, assets, and draws all normally carry debit balances.

## Segment 4 (code)

Picture a fictional company, Ashgrove Fixtures, receiving five thousand dollars cash for services. Cash, an asset, increased, so it's debited five thousand. Service revenue increased, so it's credited five thousand. Now it pays twelve hundred in rent: cash decreases, so it's credited; rent expense increases, so it's debited.
