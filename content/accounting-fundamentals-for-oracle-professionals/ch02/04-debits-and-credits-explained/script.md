# Script — Debits and Credits Explained

## Segment 1 (title)

This is the lesson most new accounting students dread, usually because they've heard debit means bad and credit means good. That's not true. Debits and credits are not good or bad. They are nothing more than left and right.

## Segment 2 (steps)

In modern use, debit simply means the left side of an account, and credit means the right side. There's no inherent good or bad direction. Whether a debit increases or decreases a given account's balance depends entirely on what kind of account it is, which is exactly what the next lesson covers. For now, just accept the plain mechanical fact: every entry goes left or right.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Recall the accounting equation: assets equal liabilities plus equity, and it must always stay balanced. Double-entry accounting enforces that through one simple rule: for every transaction, total debits must equal total credits. Buy a laptop with cash, and you'll debit one account and credit another, for the same amount.

## Segment 4 (code)

Visually, accountants draw an account as a capital letter T. Debit entries go on the left side, credit entries go on the right side. We'll use this picture constantly starting in lesson six, because it makes it obvious at a glance whether debits and credits balance.

## Segment 5 (outro)

This convention dates back to fifteenth century Italian merchants, and it survived five centuries because it's self-checking. Oracle Fusion General Ledger posts every transaction exactly this way, and it will not let an unbalanced entry post. Up next, lesson five: normal balances by account type.
