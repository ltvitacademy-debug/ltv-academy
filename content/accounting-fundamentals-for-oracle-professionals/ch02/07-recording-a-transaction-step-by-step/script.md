# Script — Recording a Transaction Step by Step

## Segment 1 (title)

You now have every piece needed to record a transaction from scratch. This lesson assembles those pieces into a repeatable four-step process you can apply to almost any business event.

## Segment 2 (steps)

The method has four steps. One, identify the accounts involved - what changed. Two, classify each account as asset, liability, equity, revenue, or expense. Three, determine whether each account increased or decreased. Four, apply the normal-balance rule: increases post to the normal side, decreases to the opposite side.

## Segment 3 (code)

Picture Dunmore Office Partners paying three hundred cash for supplies. Cash, an asset, decreased - credit three hundred. Supplies, also an asset, increased - debit three hundred. Now it bills a client twenty-five hundred dollars on credit. Accounts receivable increased - debit. Revenue increased - credit. No cash moved at all; that's the seed of accrual accounting.

## Segment 4 (code)

One more: Dunmore borrows fifteen thousand dollars, deposited into checking. Cash increased - debit fifteen thousand. Notes payable, the obligation to repay, increased - credit fifteen thousand.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Steps two through four are nearly mechanical once you know the normal-balance table. Step one, identifying which accounts are really involved, is where real judgment lives - and it's exactly what a correctly configured Oracle Fusion system still depends on getting right.
