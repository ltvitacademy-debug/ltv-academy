# Script — Adjusting and Reversing Entries

## Segment 1 (title)

Most journal entries record something already documented by an invoice or receipt. Adjusting entries are different: they exist purely to make the books accurate at period-end, for things that happened but haven't been formally recorded yet.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Recall the matching principle: revenue and expenses belong in the period they're earned or incurred, not when cash moves. Some events just don't generate a natural paper trail - nobody sends a bill for wages employees earned in the last few days of the month. Adjusting entries capture exactly those events, so the period's financial statements are accurate.

## Segment 3 (code)

Picture a fictional retailer, Larkspur Retail Group, whose employees earned three thousand dollars in the last days of March, with payroll not running until April fifth. March thirty-first: debit wages expense, credit wages payable, three thousand. Without this entry, March would understate expenses and overstate net income.

## Segment 4 (code)

Once April fifth arrives and payroll actually runs, a reversing entry dated April first simply flips the adjustment: debit wages payable, credit wages expense, three thousand. Now when the real payroll entry posts the full period's wages, the net effect across March and April comes out correct automatically.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Oracle Fusion General Ledger has a built-in feature to flag a journal entry to reverse automatically on a chosen date - exactly this pattern, done without a human remembering to create the second entry by hand. Up next, lesson sixteen: the general ledger and subledgers.
