# Script — The Trial Balance

## Segment 1 (title)

Every lesson so far has emphasized that debits must equal credits. The trial balance is the report that actually proves it, across every account in the ledger at once.

## Segment 2 (steps)

A trial balance lists every account in the general ledger with its ending balance, split into debit and credit columns. Its entire purpose is to confirm one thing: that total debit balances equal total credit balances across the whole ledger.

## Segment 3 (code)

Here's a simplified example. Cash, receivables, and equipment sit in the debit column. Payables, notes payable, common stock, and revenue sit in the credit column, along with two expense accounts in debits. Both columns total exactly one hundred three thousand dollars - the first good sign the period's postings were internally consistent.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Here's the crucial nuance. A balanced trial balance proves total debits equal total credits. It does not prove every transaction hit the correct accounts. Debit office supplies instead of equipment for a five thousand dollar purchase, and the trial balance still balances perfectly - it has no way of knowing you picked the wrong account.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Even inside a fully automated system like Oracle Fusion General Ledger, a trial balance report is a standard, frequently run sanity check before closing a period or building financial statements. Up next, lesson eighteen: finding errors in a trial balance, for when it doesn't balance at all.
