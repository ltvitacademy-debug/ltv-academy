# Script — Depreciation Concepts

## Segment 1 (title)

Depreciation is, at heart, just the deferred-expense pattern from last lesson applied to a specific kind of asset: equipment, vehicles, buildings, used up over years rather than months.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Recall the matching principle: expenses belong in the period that benefits from them. A forty thousand dollar delivery truck lasting eight years benefits all eight years, not just the year it was purchased. Expensing it all immediately would badly violate matching. Depreciation spreads a long-term asset's cost across the periods it actually benefits.

## Segment 3 (steps)

A few key terms. Cost is what the business paid. Salvage value is the asset's estimated worth at the end of its life. Depreciable base is cost minus salvage value - the amount actually spread out. Book value is cost minus accumulated depreciation so far.

## Segment 4 (code)

Picture a fictional company, Cobalt Courier Services, buying a forty thousand dollar truck, useful for eight years, with a four thousand dollar salvage value. Depreciable base: thirty-six thousand. Divided by eight years: forty-five hundred dollars a year. Each year: debit depreciation expense, credit accumulated depreciation, forty-five hundred. After three years, book value is forty thousand minus thirteen thousand five hundred - twenty-six thousand five hundred.
