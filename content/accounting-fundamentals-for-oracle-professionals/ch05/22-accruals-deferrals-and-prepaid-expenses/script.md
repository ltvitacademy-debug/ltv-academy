# Script — Accruals, Deferrals and Prepaid Expenses

## Segment 1 (title)

Several earlier lessons have hinted at this one: the full accrual and deferral family, laid out as a single organized map. Every "we'll come back to it" reference gets formally resolved here.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Every adjustment in this family answers two questions: revenue or expense, and has cash already moved or not. That gives four boxes. Accrued revenue: earned, not yet collected. Deferred revenue: collected, not yet earned. Accrued expense: incurred, not yet paid. Deferred, or prepaid, expense: paid, not yet incurred.

## Segment 3 (steps)

You've actually seen every one of these already. A bank earning loan interest before it's paid is accrued revenue. The subscription paid upfront is deferred revenue. Unpaid March wages are an accrued expense. A year of prepaid insurance is a deferred expense.

## Segment 4 (code)

Picture a fictional firm, Oakmere Design Studio, paying twelve thousand dollars on January first for a year of insurance. January first: debit prepaid insurance, credit cash - nothing expensed yet. January thirty-first: debit insurance expense, credit prepaid insurance, one thousand - one month used up. That same entry repeats every month for the rest of the year.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Oracle Fusion supports all four of these patterns directly - Payables and Receivables handle accruals and deferrals as normal processing, and Subledger Accounting rules can automatically defer recognition across a schedule. Up next, lesson twenty-three: depreciation concepts.
