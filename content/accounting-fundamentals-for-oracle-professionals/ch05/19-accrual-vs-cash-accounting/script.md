# Script — Accrual vs. Cash Accounting

## Segment 1 (title)

You've actually been learning accrual accounting all along - every revenue is earned, not just collected example since chapter three was accrual accounting. This lesson finally names it directly.

## Segment 2 (steps)

There are two bases for recording transactions. Cash accounting records revenue when cash is received and expenses when cash is paid. Accrual accounting records revenue when earned and expenses when incurred, regardless of when cash moves. Accrual is the method behind the matching principle and everything in chapter four.

## Segment 3 (code)

Picture a fictional consultancy, Ridgeview Advisory Partners, completing a ten thousand dollar project on December twenty-eighth, with the client paying January tenth. Under cash accounting, December shows zero revenue and January shows ten thousand. Under accrual accounting, December shows the full ten thousand, since that's when the work was actually completed.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Accrual accounting gives a far more accurate picture of performance during a specific period, because it matches revenue and expenses to when the economic activity happened, not to when cash happened to move. GAAP and most accounting standards require accrual-basis statements for any business beyond the smallest scale.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Every concept this course has built toward only makes sense under accrual accounting, and Oracle Fusion Financials is architected entirely around that model. Cash-basis reporting, where needed, is typically derived from the accrual books, not the other way around. Up next, lesson twenty: revenue recognition basics.
