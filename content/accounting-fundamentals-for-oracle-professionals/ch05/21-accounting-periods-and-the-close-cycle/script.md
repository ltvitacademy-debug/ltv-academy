# Script — Accounting Periods and the Close Cycle

## Segment 1 (title)

Everything you've learned about adjusting entries and accrual accounting only works if there's a defined boundary for this period versus the next. This lesson covers how those boundaries are set.

## Segment 2 (steps)

A fiscal year is a business's defined twelve-month accounting period, broken into smaller reporting periods. Many businesses use a calendar fiscal year, January through December, but plenty don't - a retailer might run February through January so its year-end falls after the holiday season. A fiscal calendar defines exactly which dates belong to which period.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Closing a period means locking it so no further transactions can post, once all the accounting work for it is complete. Without that, someone could quietly post into last March six months from now, changing a financial statement already shared with lenders or regulators.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Periods move through three states. Open, where transactions post freely. Closed, where nothing new can post but reopening is still possible. Permanently closed, locked for good once there's no realistic chance it'll need touching again.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Oracle Fusion General Ledger lets you open and close periods per ledger, and a huge category of real support tickets comes down to why can't I post this - which traces back to a closed period. Up next, lesson twenty-two: accruals, deferrals, and prepaid expenses.
