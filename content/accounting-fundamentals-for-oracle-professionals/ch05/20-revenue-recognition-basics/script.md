# Script — Revenue Recognition Basics

## Segment 1 (title)

Lesson twelve established that revenue is recorded when earned, not when cash arrives. This lesson goes a level deeper: what does earned actually mean, precisely?

## Segment 2 (steps)

The core principle: revenue is recognized when control of a good or service transfers to the customer, in an amount reflecting what the business expects to receive. Modern standards define this through five steps: identify the contract, identify separate performance obligations, determine the transaction price, allocate that price across obligations, and recognize revenue as each obligation is satisfied.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Here's why that matters practically. A single contract isn't necessarily all revenue at once. A software license plus a year of support bundled together gets split: the license portion recognized at delivery, the support portion spread across the year it's actually delivered.

## Segment 4 (code)

Picture a fictional software company, Thistledown Systems, selling a twelve thousand dollar package: a nine thousand dollar license and three thousand dollars of support. At delivery, recognize nine thousand of license revenue. Each of the next twelve months, recognize two hundred fifty dollars of support revenue as it's actually delivered.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Revenue recognition is one of the most heavily regulated areas of accounting, because getting it wrong misstates reported performance.
