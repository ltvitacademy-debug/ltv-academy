# Script — Foreign Currency Basics

## Segment 1 (title)

This lesson closes out chapter five by introducing one more layer of complexity that appears the moment a business operates across borders: foreign currency. It connects directly back to the ledger concept from lesson three.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Functional currency is the primary currency a business uses for its own books - usually tied to an Oracle Fusion ledger. Transaction currency is whatever currency a specific deal actually happens in, which can differ when dealing with foreign customers or suppliers.

## Segment 3 (steps)

When a transaction happens in a foreign currency, it gets converted to the functional currency using the exchange rate at that moment. If the rate moves between recording and settlement, the dollar value of that same foreign amount changes purely because of the rate movement - that difference is a foreign currency gain or loss.

## Segment 4 (code)

Picture a fictional exporter, Harrowgate Industrial Supply, selling ten thousand euros of goods on June first at a rate of one ten, recording eleven thousand dollars. The customer pays July first at a rate of one oh eight - only ten thousand eight hundred dollars arrives. The two hundred dollar gap is a foreign currency loss, nothing to do with how the sale itself performed.

## Segment 5 (outro)

This is exactly why an Oracle Fusion ledger's currency matters, and why multi-currency businesses often need multiple ledgers. Oracle Fusion automatically handles currency conversion and calculates these gains and losses as transactions are recorded and settled. That completes chapter five.
