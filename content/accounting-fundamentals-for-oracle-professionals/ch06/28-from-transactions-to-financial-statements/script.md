# Script — From Transactions to Financial Statements

## Segment 1 (title)

This lesson doesn't introduce a single new concept. It walks the entire path, start to finish, from a real business event all the way to its appearance on a financial statement - the complete accounting cycle this whole course has built toward.

## Segment 2 (steps)

The cycle runs in order. A transaction occurs. A journal entry gets recorded and posted to accounts or subledgers. Adjusting entries capture anything with no natural paper trail at period-end. A trial balance confirms debits equal credits. Financial statements get built from those verified balances. The period closes, and reversing entries clean things up as the next period begins.

## Segment 3 (code)

Picture a fictional company, Everhart Manufacturing, completing fifteen thousand dollars of work on March twentieth, billed on credit. Debit accounts receivable, credit service revenue, fifteen thousand - posted to the receivables subledger, feeding the general ledger. No adjustment needed; it's already fully recorded.

## Segment 4 (steps)

That fifteen thousand flows straight through: into the trial balance, into revenue on the income statement, into accounts receivable on the balance sheet. Each lesson in this course taught one link in this chain well, but the real job is understanding how the whole chain connects - because when a number looks wrong, the cause could be at any single link.
