# Script — The Balance Sheet

## Segment 1 (title)

If the income statement is a video, the balance sheet is a photograph: a snapshot of exactly what a business owns and owes at one specific instant. It's literally the accounting equation, formatted as a report.

## Segment 2 (steps)

The balance sheet reports assets, liabilities, and equity as of a single specific date, not over a period. That's why it's dated as of a date, while the income statement covers a period ended on a date. They answer different questions: how did the business perform, versus what does it own and owe right now.

## Segment 3 (code)

Here's the structure. Current and noncurrent assets total four hundred three thousand. Current and noncurrent liabilities total one hundred one thousand. Equity, common stock plus retained earnings, totals three hundred two thousand. And total liabilities plus equity also equals four hundred three thousand.

## Segment 4 (steps)

That's not a coincidence - it's the accounting equation itself. If a balance sheet doesn't balance, something upstream is definitively wrong. That retained earnings figure of two hundred two thousand isn't invented either - it's the exact roll-forward calculation from earlier lessons: beginning balance, plus net income, minus dividends.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Oracle Fusion General Ledger generates the balance sheet directly from current account balances as of the report date. Lenders and investors read this report constantly to judge a company's financial position. Up next, lesson twenty-seven: the cash flow statement.
