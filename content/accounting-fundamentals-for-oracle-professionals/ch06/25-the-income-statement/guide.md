# The Income Statement

Everything in this course has been building toward three outputs: the financial statements that everyone outside the accounting department actually reads. This lesson covers the first and most intuitive of the three: the income statement.

## What you'll learn

- What the income statement reports, and over what time frame
- Its basic structure, from revenue down to net income
- How it connects directly to Retained Earnings (lesson 11)
- Why this is the statement most people mean when they ask "is the company profitable?"

## What the income statement reports

The **income statement** (also called the Profit & Loss statement, or P&L) reports a business's revenues and expenses **over a period of time** — a month, a quarter, a year — and the resulting net income or net loss. This is a crucial distinction from the balance sheet (next lesson): the income statement is a *video*, covering a stretch of time, not a *snapshot* of a single moment.

## Basic structure

```
                    [Company Name]
              Income Statement
         For the Year Ended December 31, 2026

Revenue
  Sales Revenue                        $500,000
Total Revenue                                       $500,000

Expenses
  Cost of Goods Sold                   $220,000
  Salaries Expense                     $120,000
  Rent Expense                          $36,000
  Utilities Expense                     $14,000
  Depreciation Expense                  $18,000
Total Expenses                                       $408,000

Net Income                                            $92,000
```

Revenue minus Total Expenses equals Net Income (or Net Loss, if expenses exceed revenue). Every account type from Chapter 3 that's revenue or expense ends up somewhere on this statement.

## Worked example, connecting back to equity

A fictional retailer, **Juniper Hollow Trading Co.**, generates the income statement above: $500,000 revenue, $408,000 expenses, $92,000 net income. Recall from lesson 11 that net income flows directly into Retained Earnings:

```
Beginning Retained Earnings:  $340,000
+ Net Income:                  $92,000
- Dividends Paid:              $30,000
= Ending Retained Earnings:   $402,000
```

This is the literal mechanical link between the income statement and the balance sheet's equity section — the income statement doesn't just sit there in isolation; its bottom line feeds directly into the next statement.

## Why this is the statement everyone asks about first

When someone asks "is this company profitable?" or "how's the business doing?", they are almost always asking about the income statement. It's the most immediately intuitive of the three core financial statements, which is exactly why it's covered first in this chapter.

## Why this matters for Oracle Fusion

Oracle Fusion General Ledger generates income statement reports directly from the summarized revenue and expense balances flowing in from subledgers (Chapter 4) and accrual-based adjusting entries (Chapter 5). Every dollar on this report traces back to a specific journal entry, built from the specific accounts you now understand.

## Recap

The income statement reports revenue and expenses over a period of time, netting down to net income or net loss, which flows directly into Retained Earnings. Next up, lesson 26: the balance sheet, a snapshot statement rather than a period statement.
