# Script — The Income Statement

## Segment 1 (title)

Everything in this course has been building toward three outputs: the financial statements everyone outside accounting actually reads. This lesson covers the first and most intuitive: the income statement.

## Segment 2 (steps)

The income statement, also called the P&L, reports a business's revenues and expenses over a period of time - a month, a quarter, a year - and the resulting net income or loss. That's a crucial distinction from the balance sheet: the income statement is a video covering a stretch of time, not a snapshot of one moment.

## Segment 3 (code)

Here's the basic structure. Total revenue, five hundred thousand. Total expenses, four hundred eight thousand, covering cost of goods sold, salaries, rent, utilities, and depreciation. Revenue minus expenses leaves net income of ninety-two thousand dollars.

## Segment 4 (code)

Picture a fictional retailer, Juniper Hollow Trading Co., with that exact result. Recall that net income flows directly into retained earnings: beginning balance three hundred forty thousand, plus ninety-two thousand net income, minus thirty thousand in dividends, equals four hundred two thousand ending. The income statement's bottom line literally feeds the next statement.
