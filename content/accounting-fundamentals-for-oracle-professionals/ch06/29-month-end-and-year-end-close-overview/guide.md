# Month-End and Year-End Close Overview

This is the final lesson of Accounting Fundamentals for Oracle Professionals. It zooms into step 7 of the accounting cycle from lesson 28 — closing the books — and separates the routine monthly version from the more involved annual version, closing out everything this course has taught.

## What you'll learn

- The additional steps unique to a year-end close

- Where this path goes next, now that the accounting foundation is complete

## A month-end close checklist

A typical month-end close works through these steps, in roughly this order:

1. **Cut off transaction entry** for the period — stop new postings to the period being closed, so its numbers hold still while being reviewed.
2. **Complete subledger close** — ensure Payables, Receivables, and other subledgers have processed all their activity and summarized it to the GL (lesson 16).
3. **Record adjusting entries** — accruals, deferrals, depreciation (lessons 15, 22, 23) for anything without a natural paper trail yet.
4. **Reconcile key accounts** — confirm account balances (especially Cash) match external sources like bank statements.
5. **Review the trial balance** — confirm debits equal credits, and that balances look reasonable given what you know about the business (lessons 17-18).
6. **Generate financial statements** — the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement (lessons 25-27).
7. **Management review** — someone reviews the statements for anything that looks wrong before they're finalized.
8. **Close the period** — lock it against further postings (lesson 21).
9. **Post reversing entries** — for the new period, where applicable (lesson 15).

## What's different (and additional) at year-end

Year-end close includes everything above, plus:

- **Closing temporary accounts**: revenue and expense accounts are "closed" — zeroed out — with their net effect rolled into Retained Earnings (lesson 11), so each new fiscal year's income statement starts fresh from zero.
- **Annual reconciliations and reviews** that are more thorough than monthly ones, often including external audit involvement.
- **Permanently closing** older periods that are unlikely to ever need adjustment again (lesson 21).
- **Preparing annual financial statements and disclosures**, often to a more rigorous standard than monthly internal reporting.

## Why close speed and accuracy matter so much

A "fast close" — completing this entire checklist accurately within just a few business days of period-end — is one of the most visible signs of a well-run accounting department and a well-configured accounting system. A slow, error-prone close usually means something upstream in the cycle (transactions coded incorrectly, subledgers not reconciling, manual processes that should be automated) needs attention. This is precisely the kind of problem Oracle Fusion Financials implementations are brought in to solve: automating subledger-to-GL summarization, automating reversing entries, standardizing reconciliation reporting, and giving a close team visibility into exactly where they are in this checklist at any moment.
