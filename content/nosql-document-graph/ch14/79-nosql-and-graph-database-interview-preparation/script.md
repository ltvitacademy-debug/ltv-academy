# Script — NoSQL & Graph Database Interview Preparation

## Segment 1 (title)

This course has spent 78 lessons building real skill across MongoDB, Cosmos DB, and Neo4j. This lesson turns that knowledge into answers that hold up in an actual interview, including the follow-up questions a good interviewer asks when your first answer sounds memorized.

## Segment 2 (code: CAP theorem)

Take the CAP theorem question. The weak answer just recites the acronym. The strong answer is precise: during a network partition, a distributed system chooses consistency or availability, not both. MongoDB tunes this through read and write concerns; Cosmos DB makes it explicit through five consistency levels — Meridian's cart used session consistency specifically because strict global consistency wasn't worth its latency cost.

## Segment 3 (steps: talking through Meridian)

When an interviewer asks you to walk through a real architecture, don't recite product IDs — walk through the reasoning. State the business problem first, not the database. Name the platform and why, in one sentence. And volunteer the operational cost before you're asked for it — that's the DBA judgment this whole path has been building.

## Segment 4 (code: scope honesty)

Be precise about what this course actually qualifies you to claim. You genuinely know how these three platforms model, query, secure, and back up data. You haven't run a multi-region outage or a sharded cluster under a live traffic spike. Saying "here's how I understand it, and what I'd verify" is more credible than overclaiming — and credibility matters more in a DBA interview than almost anywhere else.
