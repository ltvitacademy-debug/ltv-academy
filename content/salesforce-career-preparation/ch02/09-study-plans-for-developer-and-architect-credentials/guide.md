## The Developer track, by its real current names

Trailhead's live catalog lists **Salesforce Certified Platform Developer**, which assesses how to develop and deploy custom business logic and custom interfaces using Apex and Visualforce, extending the Lightning Platform programmatically. Above it sits **Salesforce Certified Platform Developer II (PDII)**, which Trailhead's own catalog lists with Platform Developer as a required prerequisite — PDII goes further into advanced programmatic capabilities and data modeling for complex business logic.

**Study plan:** This track builds directly on the declarative foundation from Lesson 8, not past it. Practice writing and testing real Apex — triggers, classes, and unit tests — inside a scratch org, using the `sf` CLI workflow from Lesson 5 to push and pull that work in a way you can also commit to GitHub. Follow Trailhead's own curated preparation path for each credential, and treat your own Apex test coverage and code review habits as part of the study, not an afterthought once the "real" studying is done.

## The Architect domain credentials, by their real current names

Above the Developer track, Trailhead's live catalog lists a set of focused **Architect domain credentials**, each covering one specific area of enterprise Salesforce architecture: Data Architect, Sharing and Visibility Architect, Integration Architect, and Identity and Access Management Architect are confirmed, currently live credentials. This course's own roadmap groups several of these into two broader milestones — Application Architect and System Architect — on the way to Technical Architect (CTA).

**Study plan:** Architect-level preparation looks different from the two tiers below it. Scenario-based study — working through real, messy, multi-system design problems rather than single-feature configuration — matters more here than any trail alone can teach. This same course covers exactly that kind of practice later, in Architecture Whiteboarding and Explaining Architecture Decisions. Treat those chapters as a direct continuation of this lesson's study plan, not a separate topic.

## Why experience matters more at this tier

A Trailhead trail can teach you what a sharing rule does. It can't substitute for having actually designed one under real constraints — a client with contradictory requirements, a legacy system you can't change, a deadline that doesn't move. That's exactly why Architect-level credentials, and especially Technical Architect (CTA), expect real production experience behind the study, not just completed modules. Treat the Architect domain credentials as a long-term target you build toward over years in the field, not a checklist to clear in the weeks right after this course.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Salesforce Certified Platform Developer | The entry-level programmatic credential: Apex, Visualforce, extending the platform with code |
| Platform Developer II (PDII) | The advanced programmatic credential, requiring Platform Developer as a prerequisite |
| Architect domain credential | A focused credential covering one architecture area: data, sharing and visibility, integration, or identity and access management |
