# Lesson 7 — Certification Strategy

## Why order matters

Each Salesforce credential assumes the skills of the ones that typically come before it. The Platform App Builder exam assumes Administrator-level comfort with objects, fields, and security. The Architect domain credentials assume real Developer and Administrator depth. Jumping straight to an advanced exam without that foundation usually ends one of two ways: you fail it, or you pass it without actually being able to do the job it's supposed to certify — which catches up with you fast in an interview or on the job.

## A realistic sequence

The two destinations after this course call for different certification paths:

- **Foundation, shared by both destinations.** Administrator-level credential, then Platform App Builder. This is what the LTV Customer Management System capstone was built to prepare you for, and what Lesson 8 covers in depth.
- **Toward Salesforce Technical Architect.** From the foundation, the realistic next step is a Developer-level credential, then the Architect domain credentials (data, sharing and visibility, integration, identity and access management, and development lifecycle and deployment are the named domains on Trailhead today), building toward the Application Architect and System Architect milestones, and — eventually, after real production experience — Certified Technical Architect. Lesson 9 covers study plans for this track.
- **Toward Salesforce Data Analyst.** From the same foundation, the path shifts toward SQL, SOQL, and analytics tooling rather than stacking more Salesforce platform credentials — this course's companion path covers that analytics-side progression separately.

## Deciding when you're actually ready

A study plan should end with a real hands-on check, not just a passed practice exam:

1. **Can you rebuild the core objectives from memory, hands-on, in a Trailhead Playground?** Not "recognize the right answer on a quiz" — actually configure the thing, without a guide open next to you.
2. **Did your capstone work already cover a meaningful share of the objectives?** If large parts of the exam are still unfamiliar after building the LTV Customer Management System, that's a sign to go build more hands-on practice before scheduling, not to cram harder.
3. **Are you treating practice exams as a readiness check, not the study plan itself?** A practice exam tells you where the gaps are. It doesn't fill them.

## Why this lesson doesn't hand you a fixed cert map to memorize

Salesforce updates certification names, exam structure, and prerequisites on its own schedule — the certification this catalog's own path roadmap lists as "Salesforce Certified Administrator," for example, is listed on Trailhead's live site today as "Salesforce Certified Platform Administrator." That's not a typo in either place — it's exactly the kind of drift that happens over time, and it's why Lesson 10 is dedicated entirely to checking the current, authoritative source yourself before you schedule anything. Treat this lesson as the sequencing logic, and Lesson 10 as the habit that keeps the specifics accurate.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Foundation credentials | The Administrator and Platform App Builder credentials every Salesforce career builds on |
| Architect domain credential | One of several specialized architecture credentials (data, sharing and visibility, integration, identity and access management, development lifecycle and deployment) |
| Readiness check | Confirming you can rebuild exam objectives hands-on, not just pass a practice quiz |
