## The action + artifact + tool pattern

A strong Salesforce admin bullet answers three questions in one line: what did you do, what did you actually configure or build, and what tool or feature did you use to do it. "Worked on Salesforce" or "helped with CRM stuff" tells a reviewer nothing they can follow up on — and nothing they can ask you to walk through in an interview.

**Before:** "Worked on automating our sales process."

**After:** "Built a 5-step Record-Triggered Flow that auto-assigns new Leads by territory and SLA, replacing a manual queue-triage process."

**Before:** "Did security stuff for the org."

**After:** "Designed a role hierarchy and sharing rule model enforcing least-privilege access across Sales and Service profiles, with field-level security applied per profile."

**Before:** "Helped with reporting."

**After:** "Built 6 reports and 2 dashboards giving sales leadership real-time pipeline visibility by stage, owner, and close date."

**Before:** "Worked on the data model."

**After:** "Designed 3 custom objects with lookup and master-detail relationships to track [your real domain], with validation rules enforcing required fields on save."

Notice what each "after" version does: it names a real number (5 steps, 6 reports, 3 objects), a real deliverable (a Flow, a sharing model, a dashboard, an object schema), and the real Salesforce tool or feature used to build it (Flow Builder, role hierarchy and sharing rules, report/dashboard builder, Schema Builder, validation rules). That's the pattern — repeat it for every bullet, and build it straight from your LTV Customer Management System capstone.

## Structure of a one-page Salesforce admin resume

At this career stage, one page is the right length. A common order:

1. **Header.** Name, city, email, LinkedIn URL, and your Trailhead profile link (covered next lesson).
2. **Summary (optional).** Two lines naming your target role and strongest Salesforce skills.
3. **Capstone / projects.** Your LTV Customer Management System build, broken into 3-4 deliverables, each with two to three bullets written in the action + artifact + tool pattern.
4. **Skills, grouped.** Declarative configuration (objects, fields, page layouts), security (profiles, roles, sharing rules), automation (Flow Builder), data management (import/export, deduplication), reporting (reports, dashboards).
5. **Experience.** Earlier roles, with bullets reframed toward process, systems, or customer-facing work where honestly possible.
6. **Education and certifications.** List only certifications you have actually earned — a certification you're studying for belongs in a cover letter or interview conversation, not a bulleted certifications line.

Draw your project bullets from across the whole capstone: the objects and relationships you designed, the security model you built, the Flow automations you configured, the validation rules you wrote, and the reports and dashboards you shipped. Pick the ones closest to the job posting in front of you, and only list a tool or feature you could discuss out loud for five minutes.

## Tailoring and keywords

Read the job posting and note the terms it repeats — "Flow," "role hierarchy," "validation rules," "AppExchange," "data management." Where they truthfully describe your work, reuse that exact wording; applicant tracking systems and human reviewers both scan for it. Never paste in a term you can't back up in conversation.

## Common mistakes

- **Vague verbs instead of named deliverables.** "Assisted with Salesforce" says nothing; "built," "configured," "automated," "designed" each point at something real.
- **Listing a certification you haven't actually earned yet.** Be precise about in-progress versus passed — this is the first thing a careful interviewer checks.
- **Blurring who decided versus who implemented.** If a bullet describes a decision a stakeholder or consultant made and you only executed it, say so honestly rather than implying you owned the design.
- **Inconsistent numbers across documents.** If your resume says "6 reports" and your portfolio page says "8," that gap is the first thing a careful reviewer will notice.
