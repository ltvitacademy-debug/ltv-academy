# Lesson 3 — Your Trailhead Profile

**Chapter 1 · Building Your Presence · Lesson 3 of 19**

## What you'll learn

- What a Trailhead profile actually shows a hiring manager, and why it works as a second resume
- How ranks, badges, trails, and Superbadges relate to each other
- Why a Superbadge carries more weight than a stack of quick badges
- How to set your profile to Public, and what that does and doesn't prove

## Why a Trailhead profile matters

A resume is a claim. A Trailhead profile is closer to a record — ranks, badges, trails, Superbadges, and earned certifications, all tied to an account a hiring manager can look at directly. Setting your profile to **Public** makes your certifications, community activity, badges, points, avatar, and about-me information visible to anyone with the link, the same way a well-prepared candidate links their Trailhead profile right next to their resume and LinkedIn.

Note what this does and doesn't prove, though: a public profile makes your *learning activity* checkable, not your on-the-job experience. Points and badge counts alone describe what a module you clicked through; they don't by themselves prove you can do the job. What a reviewer is actually scanning for is which badges and trails are relevant to the role, whether you've completed any Superbadges, and whether your real, earned certifications show up in the Certifications section.

## Ranks: what the numbers actually mean

Every badge you complete earns points that count toward a Trailblazer Rank. The real rank ladder, from Trailhead's own current site, runs:

| Rank | Badges | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Scout | 0 | 0 |
| Hiker | 1 | 200 |
| Explorer | 5 | 3,000 |
| Adventurer | 10 | 9,000 |
| Mountaineer | 25 | 18,000 |
| Expeditioner | 50 | 35,000 |
| Ranger | 100 | 50,000 |
| Double Star Ranger | 200 | 100,000+ |

A high rank is a volume signal, not a relevance signal. A Double Star Ranger who has never touched a Flow-automation trail isn't more hireable for an admin role than a Hiker who completed exactly the right trail and a relevant Superbadge. Don't chase rank for its own sake — chase the badges and trails that actually match the job you want.

## Superbadges: the credential that's hardest to fake

A badge usually certifies that you clicked through a module and answered some multiple-choice questions. A **Superbadge** is different: it's a longer, hands-on, scenario-based challenge where you're given a business problem and have to actually build the solution in a real Salesforce org, with the platform checking your work. Because a Superbadge can't be passed by memorizing answers, it's the single strongest signal on a Trailhead profile short of an earned certification — and it's exactly the kind of evidence worth naming by title on both your resume and your profile's About Me section.

## Trails: your learning path, visible

A **Trail** is a guided sequence of badges built around a specific role or skill — the Administrator and Platform App Builder trails you've likely already followed through this path are the clearest example. A completed trail tells a reviewer you followed a structured, Salesforce-authored curriculum for the exact role you're applying for, not a random scatter of unrelated badges.

## Setting up a credible profile

1. Go to your profile settings and confirm Public visibility is turned on — a private profile is invisible to a recruiter, which defeats the purpose entirely.
2. Fill in About Me with a short, honest line about your focus (for example: Salesforce Administrator, Flow automation, data security).
3. Link it in your resume header and LinkedIn, the same way you'd link a GitHub profile (next lesson covers that).
4. Keep it current — an out-of-date profile with no recent activity reads as a stale search just as easily as no profile at all.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Trailblazer Rank | A tier (Scout through Double Star Ranger and beyond) based on badges and points earned |
| Badge | Proof of completing one Trailhead module, trail step, or challenge |
| Superbadge | A longer, hands-on, scenario-based challenge graded against a real Salesforce org |
| Trail | A guided, ordered sequence of badges built around one role or skill |
| Public profile | A visibility setting that shows your certifications, badges, points, and activity to anyone with the link |
