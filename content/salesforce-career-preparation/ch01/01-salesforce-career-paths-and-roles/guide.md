## The five roles, and how they connect

Salesforce job postings use a lot of different titles, but most of them map onto five real roles:

- **Administrator.** Configures the platform declaratively: objects, fields, security, Flow automation, reports and dashboards. The most common entry point into a Salesforce career, and the foundation every other role sits on.
- **Platform App Builder.** Extends the Administrator skill set into designing and deploying full custom applications using the platform's declarative tools, without writing code.
- **Developer.** Extends the platform programmatically with Apex and Lightning Web Components, where declarative tools like Flow and App Builder reach their limit.
- **Consultant.** Works with a client or business stakeholders to turn a business problem into a Salesforce solution, then hands it to admins and developers to build.
- **Architect.** Designs how an entire org — or a specific domain of it, like data, sharing, integration, or identity — holds together at scale, across multiple clouds and systems. The most senior, most experience-dependent role in the ecosystem.

These aren't five unrelated jobs. Each one builds on the skills below it: a strong Architect understands administration and development deeply; a strong Developer understands declarative tools deeply; and so on. In this ecosystem, you don't skip layers.

## Two destinations from here

Once this course is complete, the Salesforce Administrator path branches into two long destinations:

- **Salesforce Data Analyst / CRM Analytics.** Adds SQL, SOQL and native Salesforce reporting, Tableau, Excel, Python and statistics, then Salesforce data architecture and governance, before going deep on CRM Analytics, Tableau Next, Snowflake, dbt, and the enterprise data stack.
- **Salesforce Technical Architect.** Adds Platform Developer I, Lightning Web Components, data architecture, security, integrations, and DevOps, working through the Architect domain credentials toward Application Architect, System Architect, and — eventually, with real production experience behind you — Certified Technical Architect (CTA).

Neither destination is a first job on its own. The realistic sequence is: get hired as a Salesforce Administrator, or a related role, using what this chapter and the next three lessons help you build — then keep progressing through whichever destination matches the work you actually want to do.
