# Lesson 13 — Consultant Interviews

**Chapter 3 · Interviews · Lesson 13 of 19**

## What you'll learn

- How a Consultant interview differs from both the Administrator and Developer interviews
- A structured way to answer "how do you gather requirements?"

## A role judged on client conversations

Administrator interviews test configuration judgment; Developer interviews test code. Consultant interviews test something else again: can you run a conversation with a client, turn what they say into a workable Salesforce solution, and manage their expectations along the way. Technical depth still matters, but it's rarely the main event — communication and judgment are.

## "How do you gather requirements?" — a structured answer

A strong answer to this question walks through a repeatable process, not a vague "I ask good questions":

1. **Identify stakeholders** across the departments actually affected — not just the person who called the meeting.
2. **Run workshops or discovery sessions** to surface current processes, pain points, and goals, before proposing anything.
3. **Document functional and non-functional requirements** in detail, so nothing is relying on memory later.
4. **Prioritize** by business impact, resources, and timeline — not just by whoever asked loudest.
5. **Validate** that what's documented actually matches business goals and the agreed scope, before build work starts.

## Handling ambiguous or non-technical stakeholders

Two situations interviewers like to probe:

- **The stakeholder who isn't sure what they need.** The test is whether you ask good clarifying questions and guide them toward clarity, rather than either guessing or asking them to "just write it down."
- **The stakeholder who isn't technical.** The right move is to stay focused on understanding their business process first, and translate that into Salesforce concepts yourself — not force them to think in objects and fields before you've understood what they're actually trying to accomplish.

The theme underneath both: discovery is about understanding the *why* behind a request, not just collecting a list of *whats*.

## When a client asks for something that breaks best practice

This is one of the most common Consultant scenario questions, and it's really a judgment test, not a technical one. A strong answer acknowledges the request, explains the specific downside in terms the client cares about (cost, risk, maintainability, future flexibility), and offers an alternative that gets them most of what they actually need. Simply saying "that's against best practice" without the translation step reads as rigid, not expert.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Discovery | The iterative process of uncovering both stated needs and the underlying business reasons behind them |
| Functional requirement | A specific capability the solution must deliver |
| Non-functional requirement | A constraint the solution must meet (performance, security, scalability) |
| Scope | The agreed boundary of what will and won't be built |
