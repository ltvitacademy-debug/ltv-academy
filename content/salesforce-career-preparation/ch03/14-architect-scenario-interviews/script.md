# Lesson 14 — Architect Scenario Interviews · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Architect interviews are built around open-ended scenarios with no single right answer. This lesson covers how they're structured and how to approach one.

## S2 · STEPS — A different interview structure

Expect a manager round, often a round with their manager, and a panel round built around a mock customer scenario, where you present an approach and then field follow-up questions from the panel role-playing as the client -- sometimes with a demo or live sketch. Some companies even assign a coach to help candidates prepare for this round specifically.

## S3 · STEPS — Approaching an open-ended scenario

Ask clarifying questions first -- real prompts are incomplete on purpose. State your assumptions out loud when something isn't fully answered. Propose a direction with tradeoffs named explicitly. And address security and scale even if nobody asked directly, since both are assumed baseline knowledge at this level.

## S4 · STEPS — Example scenario one (fictional)

This company and scenario are entirely fictional, for practice only. "Cascadia Outdoor Supply" runs three Salesforce orgs from past acquisitions plus a legacy ERP, and wants a single customer view with no processing downtime during a transition. A strong answer asks what "single view" actually means -- reporting or a true merge -- before proposing anything.

## S5 · STEPS — Example scenario two (fictional)

Also entirely fictional. "Northbridge Insurance Group" wants external agents to work certain records without seeing other agents' data or internal-only records. This is a sharing-model and Experience Cloud scenario -- default to the most restrictive access and open it up deliberately, not the reverse.

## S6 · STEPS — What separates a strong answer

A weak answer jumps to a feature name before understanding the constraint. A strong answer names the tradeoff, states the assumption, and can defend the reasoning under follow-up questions -- because that's exactly what the panel is testing.
