## The full journey, not just the next step

You've already covered the foundation: Salesforce Certified Administrator, then Salesforce Certified Platform App Builder. From here, the realistic path toward the Technical Architect destination keeps building in layers:

1. **Developer.** Salesforce Certified Platform Developer I.
2. **Architect domain credentials.** Specialized credentials covering data architecture, sharing and visibility, integration, identity and access management, and development lifecycle and deployment.
3. **Architect milestones.** Application Architect and System Architect — each one a bundle of the domain credentials above, recognized as its own milestone.
4. **The destination.** Salesforce Certified Technical Architect (CTA) — earned through a review-board process that tests real architectural judgment directly, not just exam questions.

## Why this is described as a destination, not a fast track

A student does not finish a curriculum and instantly become a Technical Architect. The credentials above describe a body of knowledge; CTA additionally requires the kind of judgment that only comes from real production experience — the sort of experience you start building on day one of your first Salesforce job, not in a course. Expect this to be a multi-year journey, built on real work, not a checklist to clear quickly. That framing isn't meant to discourage you — it's meant to keep your expectations accurate, which is exactly what Lesson 10 asked you to do with certification requirements generally: check the real, current picture rather than assume a shortcut exists.

## You've just finished the entire Salesforce Administrator path

This isn't only the last lesson of this course — it's the last lesson of the full Salesforce Administrator path: Salesforce Foundations, Salesforce Administration, Declarative Development, your capstone, and this career-preparation course. That's a real, complete body of work, worth recognizing on its own before looking at what's next.

## Where the path forks from here

The catalog's own path map shows one destination opening up from this point: **Salesforce Architect.** It isn't two separate destinations to choose between — it's a single destination that forks into two converging ladders, both building on the foundation you just finished:

- **Data & Analytics Architect ladder.** This ladder shifts toward SQL, native and advanced reporting, Tableau and analytics tooling, and Salesforce's own data architecture and governance — eventually reaching CRM Analytics, Tableau Next, and the broader enterprise data stack.
- **Technical Architect ladder.** This ladder continues the path described above — Developer, then the Architect domain credentials, then the Architect milestones, working toward CTA.

## How to think about which ladder fits you — not which one is "better"

Neither ladder is the "real" or "more advanced" one; they lead toward different kinds of work on the same destination:

- If the parts of this course and the capstone you enjoyed most were building reports and dashboards, answering "what does the data actually tell us," and you'd rather go deeper into analytics tooling than deeper into Apex and integration architecture — the Data & Analytics Architect ladder plays to that.
- If the parts you enjoyed most were the data model and security-model decisions, the automation design choices, and the kind of scenario and whiteboarding thinking from Chapter 3 of this course — the Technical Architect ladder plays to that.
- It's also fine to not be sure yet. Both ladders share enough of a starting point that the choice doesn't have to be permanent or made perfectly today — pick the one that matches what energized you most in this path, and check the catalog's current Salesforce Architect page for the specific course sequence in each ladder before you commit your study time.
