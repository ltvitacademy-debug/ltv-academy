## The shape of this chapter

Chapter 2 had one job: take everything already taught across the
System Design chapter and the Fabric & Real-Time Analytics and
Databricks & Delta Lake courses, and drill it from the DP-700 exam's
specific angle. Nothing here was new technology — every lesson pointed
back to a lesson you'd already sat through.

## Lessons 26–30: the exam itself

Lesson 26 introduced what DP-700 certifies and who it's for. Lesson 27
gave you the three domains and their near-even weighting — no domain is
safe to skip. Lessons 28, 29, and 30 drilled each domain directly:
implementing and managing an analytics solution, ingesting and
transforming data, and monitoring and optimizing a solution.

## Lessons 31–36: six practice-question sets

OneLake and Lakehouses, Data Warehouses, Eventstreams and KQL, Notebooks
and Spark, Security and Governance, and Monitoring and Deployment — six
sets, each built as scenario questions with real wrong answers, not
trivia. The recurring lesson across all six: DP-700 rewards the
narrowest correct control or tool, and punishes picking a broader one
that would also technically work.

## Lesson 37: the traps, named

Five patterns that repeat everywhere — lookalike item types,
overcomplicated external-service answers, absolute wording, buried
qualifiers, and over-broad controls. Every wrong answer in Lessons
31–36 and 40–41 was an instance of one of these five.

## Lessons 38–39: the plan and the pacing

Lesson 38 turned the domain map into an actual 4-week schedule built
around re-reading, not re-learning. Lesson 39 gave you the mechanics for
exam day itself: a time budget under two minutes a question, flag-and-
return instead of fighting a stuck question, and process of elimination
as a default habit.

## Lessons 40–41: the mock exam

Eighteen questions, mixed across all three domains the way the real
exam mixes them, split into two timed sittings. The real value wasn't
any single question — it was practicing the pacing and elimination
habits from Lesson 39 under actual time pressure, and building a
combined miss list to focus whatever study time is left.
