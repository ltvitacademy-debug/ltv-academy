## Time budgeting, roughly

Microsoft certification exams generally run around two hours for
roughly fifty to sixty questions, which works out to a little under two
minutes per question on average. That average matters more than any
single question — a handful of scenario questions will legitimately
take three or four minutes to read and reason through, and that's fine,
*if* you've banked extra time on the quick ones.

```
Rough budget:
~110-120 minutes total / ~50-60 questions ~= under 2 min/question avg
Short, factual questions: aim for well under a minute
Long scenario questions: budget the extra time you banked
```

The practical habit: after the first 15-20 questions, do a quick mental
check — are you roughly on pace? If you're already behind, that's your
signal to move faster on the easy questions, not to panic on the hard
ones.

## Flag and return, don't fight

If a question isn't resolving after a genuine attempt — you've read the
full stem, eliminated at least one option, and you're still stuck — flag
it and move on. Fighting a single question for five minutes when the
whole exam only allows two minutes per question on average is a losing
trade: that time comes directly out of questions you'd have answered
correctly with no trouble. Many Microsoft exams include a review screen
at the end that lets you revisit flagged questions — use it as designed,
not as an afterthought.

Some Microsoft certification formats also include a separate case-study
or scenario section that, once you move past it, can't be revisited —
if the real exam interface tells you this on exam day, budget extra time
for that section specifically, since there's no second pass available.

## Process of elimination as a default, not a fallback

Lesson 37's traps lesson gave you concrete patterns for spotting wrong
answers: lookalike item types, overcomplicated external-service options,
absolute wording, over-broad controls. Apply those *before* you try to
identify the right answer directly. Eliminating two obviously-wrong
options turns a four-way guess into a coin flip, and often the process
of elimination itself surfaces the right answer by removing the noise
around it.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Time budget | The rough per-question time allowance based on total exam time and question count |
| Flag and return | Marking a stuck question and moving on, rather than losing minutes on one item |
| Process of elimination | Using Lesson 37's trap patterns to remove wrong options before hunting for the right one |
