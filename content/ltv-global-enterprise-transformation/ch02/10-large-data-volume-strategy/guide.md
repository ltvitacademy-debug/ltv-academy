## Parts Order is where LDV becomes real

Lesson 9 called Parts Order the highest-volume object in LTV Global's data model, generated by tens of thousands of dealer orders a day across the Parts & Aftermarket Distribution business unit. At that rate, Parts Order crosses into Large Data Volume territory — the behavioral threshold, not a fixed number, where data scale starts changing how the platform has to perform — well within the first year or two of go-live, which means its LDV strategy has to be designed up front rather than patched in after the first production incident.

## Resolving Risk #2: avoiding ownership and data skew

Lesson 6's risk register flagged a specific danger: if a single integration user is set as the owner of every Parts Order record synced from Meridian's order confirmation, that one user becomes an extreme case of **ownership skew** — tens of millions of records owned by one user, which degrades sharing performance and makes role-hierarchy-based visibility calculations dramatically more expensive for everyone. LTV Global's mitigation is deliberate: Parts Order records are owned by a **queue** structured per region and business unit (for example, a "NA Parts Orders" queue, an "EMEA Parts Orders" queue), not by the Meridian integration user directly. Queue ownership distributes the records across a structure Salesforce's sharing engine already handles well, and gives regional parts teams a natural, pre-filtered work queue as a side benefit, rather than a performance mitigation being the only reason for the choice.

## Keeping queries selective as volume grows

Equipment Asset carries a `Serial_Number__c` field marked as an **external ID** and indexed, because the single most common query pattern in this design — "find the equipment record for this serial number," run constantly by Field Service, by the dealer portal, and by the nightly Meridian sync — has to stay fast as the object grows past millions of rows. A query filtered on an indexed, external-ID field is **selective**: it narrows the candidate rows down efficiently before Salesforce has to do any further work, which is the difference between a query that stays fast at ten million rows and one that silently degrades the moment it crosses an internal non-selectivity threshold. Any batch job or integration touching Equipment Asset at volume is designed to filter on this field first, not as an afterthought.

## Archiving: not letting Parts Order grow forever

Even with good ownership and indexing, letting Parts Order grow without bound is its own risk — every read- and write-path problem gets worse the more total data there is to search through, regardless of how well-indexed it is. LTV Global's strategy moves Parts Order records older than 24 months out of the standard object and into a **Big Object**, `Parts_Order_Archive__b`, indexed on Account and order date — the same pattern this catalog's data-architecture courses describe for write-once, rarely-queried historical data. Dealers and service reps only need recent order history in day-to-day work; the rare case of an auditor or a dealer needing a specific order from three years ago is still served, just through the archive rather than the live object, keeping the live Parts Order object's working size manageable indefinitely.

## Why this resolves, rather than just restates, the risk

This isn't a new risk discovery — it's Lesson 6's Risk #2, given an actual mitigation with a name (queue ownership) and a mechanism (regional/BU-based queue structure) instead of a placeholder. The same pattern repeats through this course: a risk gets named early, and a specific chapter gives it teeth.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ownership skew | Too many records owned by a single user, degrading sharing performance at scale |
| Queue ownership | Assigning record ownership to a queue instead of an individual user, distributing sharing load and enabling team-based work assignment |
| Selective query | A query that uses an indexed field to narrow candidate rows efficiently before further processing |
| External ID | A field marked to uniquely identify a record for integration lookups, commonly indexed for query performance |
| Big Object | A metadata-defined object for very large record volumes at consistent performance cost, queried mainly through its defined index |
