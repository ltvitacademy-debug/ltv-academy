# What Is Salesforce & CRM?

This course is not a Salesforce Administrator mega-course, and it doesn't try to be. It
teaches exactly what a data analyst needs: what Salesforce data actually means, before you
ever try to analyze it. Salesforce reporting shows up in a huge share of analyst job
postings, and understanding the platform underneath the reports is what separates someone
who can write a query from someone who can be trusted with the sales team's real numbers.

## What you'll learn

- What a CRM actually is, and the real business problem it solves
- Where Salesforce fits in the CRM market, and why it matters for your career
- Why this course teaches the platform before it teaches SOQL

## CRM: a system of record for relationships, not just contacts

**CRM (Customer Relationship Management)** software exists to solve a specific business
problem: a growing company's information about its customers, prospects, and deals stops
fitting in one person's head or one spreadsheet. Before CRM, a salesperson's pipeline lived
in their own notes; when they left the company, that knowledge left with them. A CRM makes
customer and deal data a shared, structured, queryable asset that survives any one person
leaving — every lead, every deal, every support case, tracked in one system the whole
company can see and report on.

## Salesforce's real position in the market

Salesforce is the dominant CRM platform by market share, and has been for years — genuinely
the default choice for mid-size and large companies buying CRM software. That matters
directly for your career: an analyst who understands Salesforce data isn't learning a niche
skill, they're learning the platform underneath an enormous share of real analyst job
postings. Salesforce is built around a core relational data model (Sales Cloud, Service
Cloud, and other "Clouds" all sit on top of it), plus a genuinely enormous ecosystem of
customization, integrations, and add-on products — CRM Analytics and Tableau among them,
which this path builds toward.

## Why platform before SOQL

It's tempting to skip straight to "just teach me the query language." That's a mistake
this course deliberately avoids. SOQL (Salesforce's query language, covered in the next
course in this path) lets you ask Salesforce questions — but a query is only as good as
your understanding of what you're actually querying. Writing `SELECT Amount FROM
Opportunity` without knowing what an Opportunity record actually represents, how it relates
to Accounts and Contacts, or what "Amount" means in your specific org's sales process, is
how an analyst produces a technically-correct number that's practically meaningless. This
course builds that platform understanding first, on purpose.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CRM | Customer Relationship Management — software making customer/deal data a shared, structured asset |
| Salesforce | The dominant CRM platform by market share; the subject of this course |
| Cloud (Salesforce) | A product line built on Salesforce's core platform (Sales Cloud, Service Cloud, etc.) |
| SOQL | Salesforce Object Query Language — covered in the next course in this path |
