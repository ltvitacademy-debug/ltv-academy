# Compensation, Offers & Choosing a Firm

The last lesson in this capstone covers the decision that actually ends the job search: evaluating an offer and choosing where to go. Quant compensation structures vary more than almost any other technical field, and the best firm on paper isn't always the best firm for you.

## What you'll learn

- Why this lesson doesn't quote a specific salary figure, and what to use instead
- How quant compensation structures differ by firm type, and why that changes how you evaluate an offer
- How to build your own researched range before a number ever comes up
- A short framework for choosing between competing offers that isn't just "which number is bigger"

## Why no specific number appears here

Compensation figures go stale within months, vary enormously by firm type, strategy, city, and market conditions, and this catalog has no reliable way to verify a precise figure as current for your specific situation. Quoting an invented or outdated number would do more harm than good. Instead, this lesson teaches the research process — the same discipline SR-5 itself was built on: build your own evidence-based estimate rather than trusting a single unverified figure.

## Why quant compensation structures vary so much by firm type

Unlike most technical roles, a quant's total compensation structure depends heavily on firm type, not just seniority:

| Firm type | Common structure |
|---|---|
| Systematic hedge funds | Base salary plus a bonus often tied to fund or team performance, sometimes a share of PnL at senior levels |
| Prop trading firms | Often a lower base with a bonus or profit-share heavily tied to individual or desk trading performance |
| Bank quant desks (sell-side) | Base plus a bonus more tied to overall bank and division performance, generally less individual-PnL-linked than a prop shop |
| Quant-adjacent tech/fintech roles | Often closer to standard tech compensation: base, bonus, equity |

The practical consequence: two offers with the same headline base salary can have very different expected total compensation and very different variance, because a profit-share-heavy structure can pay far more in a good year and far less in a bad one than a steadier, more bank-like structure. Understand which structure you're being offered before comparing numbers across firms.

## Building a researched range before a number comes up

Use the same research habit worth applying to any job offer, with quant-specific sources added:

- **Aggregated salary sites** — Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Payscale — read skeptically, since quant-specific roles are less reliably represented on general salary sites than standard tech roles.
- **Direct market signal** — live postings for comparable roles at comparable firm types in your target city, since quant pay is unusually city- and firm-type-sensitive.
- **Your own network** — alumni from your program or people in similar roles are often more candid about structure (base vs. bonus split, typical bonus range) than any aggregated site can be.

Build a range from at least two independent sources, specific to the firm type and city you're evaluating — a systematic-fund number and a prop-shop number for the same seniority level can look completely different, and averaging them produces a meaningless middle.

## Evaluating and countering an offer

Once a real offer exists, evaluate the whole package, not just the base: bonus structure and how it's determined (team performance vs. individual PnL vs. discretionary), vesting schedule on any equity or deferred compensation, and the realistic timeline to meaningful bonus eligibility. A respectful counter follows the same shape that works in any field — thank them for the offer, state your researched range with a specific reason behind it, and stay collaborative rather than issuing an ultimatum.

## Choosing between offers: beyond the number

When comparing two real offers, weigh these alongside compensation, since they often matter more to how the next few years actually feel:

- **Strategy and risk culture fit.** Does the firm's approach to risk and research rigor match what you valued building SR-5 — honest cost modeling, real out-of-sample validation — or does the culture reward overselling results?
- **Mentorship and learning curve.** Will you work alongside people who'll actively teach you, or be left to figure things out alone?
- **Stability of the structure.** A profit-share-heavy role can be lucrative in good years and thin in bad ones — know your own tolerance for that variance before accepting it.
- **What the role actually does day to day.** Research-heavy, trading-heavy, and infrastructure-heavy roles can carry the same title at different firms; confirm which one you're actually signing up for.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Profit share | A bonus structure tying pay directly to the PnL generated by an individual, desk, or fund |
| Total compensation | The full value of an offer — base, bonus, equity, and benefits — not just the base salary figure |
| Risk culture | A firm's actual, practiced attitude toward risk-taking and research honesty, as distinct from what it states in recruiting materials |

## Recap

Quant compensation structures vary by firm type more than title or seniority alone would suggest — systematic funds, prop shops, bank desks, and fintech roles each weight base, bonus, and profit-share differently, so build a researched range specific to the firm type and city in front of you rather than trusting one borrowed number. Evaluate the whole package, and weigh strategy fit, mentorship, and risk culture alongside the number itself.
