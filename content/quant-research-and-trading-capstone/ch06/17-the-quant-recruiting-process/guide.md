# The Quant Recruiting Process

Quant hiring doesn't follow one script — a systematic hedge fund, a market-making prop shop, and a bank's quant desk each run a noticeably different process. This lesson maps the common stages so you can recognize where you are in any of them, and previews which later lesson in this chapter prepares you for each one.

## What you'll learn

- The common stages of quant recruiting, roughly in order
- How the process differs across fund types: systematic funds, prop trading shops, and bank quant desks
- What a recruiter screen is actually evaluating, and how to handle it
- How long a realistic timeline takes, and how to manage multiple processes at once

## The common stages

Most quant processes include some version of these stages, though the order and number of rounds vary by firm:

1. **Resume/portfolio screen.** Often the highest-attrition stage — this is what Lesson 16's resume and portfolio are built to survive.
2. **Recruiter or HR screen (15-30 minutes).** Fit, logistics, visa status, timeline. Rarely technical, but you can be filtered out here.
3. **Math/probability screen.** A phone or online round of brainteasers and probability puzzles (Lesson 18).
4. **Coding/technical screen.** Live coding, often in Python or C++ depending on the role (Lesson 19).
5. **Statistics/ML round.** For research-flavored roles — model evaluation, overfitting, time-series pitfalls (Lesson 20).
6. **Market-knowledge or trading round.** For trading-adjacent roles — P&L intuition, market microstructure, risk scenarios (Lesson 21).
7. **Case study or research presentation.** Present a past project — your SR-5 capstone is built exactly for this (Lesson 22).
8. **Final/onsite loop.** Several back-to-back rounds, sometimes including senior researchers or traders, often in one day.
9. **Offer and negotiation** (Lesson 23).

## How the process differs by firm type

| Firm type | What gets emphasized |
|---|---|
| Systematic hedge funds | Statistics, ML, research methodology, backtesting rigor — closest to the SR-5 project itself |
| Prop trading firms | Math/probability speed, market intuition, real-time decision-making under uncertainty |
| Bank quant desks (sell-side) | Stochastic calculus, derivatives pricing, C++, sometimes less emphasis on live trading intuition |

Read the job description for which flavor you're in, since the prep this chapter covers (math, coding, stats, market knowledge) applies unevenly across these three — a systematic-fund interview will spend far more time on your SR-5-style research than a market-making interview will.

## What the recruiter screen actually evaluates

The recruiter screen isn't technical, but it isn't nothing either. Prepare a 60-90 second answer to "tell me about yourself" that moves through background, the SR-5 capstone in one sentence (including its net result and that it's explicitly research, not a live strategy), and what you're looking for. Have specific questions ready about the team, the strategies they run, and how research turns into live trading — generic questions ("what's the culture like?") read as under-prepared for a quant-specific process.

## Timeline and running multiple processes

A full quant process, from first screen to offer, commonly takes 4-10 weeks, and competitive candidates are usually running several processes in parallel rather than one at a time. That's normal, not deceptive — be honest if a firm asks about your timeline or whether you have other offers in motion, since quant hiring managers expect candidates to be interviewing in parallel and plan their own timelines around it.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Recruiter screen | A non-technical early call evaluating fit, logistics, and basic communication |
| Onsite loop | A condensed sequence of back-to-back interview rounds, often in a single day |
| Sell-side / buy-side | Sell-side: banks serving clients (often derivatives/pricing-heavy); buy-side: funds managing their own or client capital (often research/trading-heavy) |

## Recap

Quant recruiting runs through a recognizable sequence — resume screen, recruiter call, math/probability, coding, statistics/ML, market knowledge, a case study or presentation, an onsite loop, then an offer — but the emphasis shifts meaningfully between systematic funds, prop shops, and bank desks. Expect the full process to take 4-10 weeks and to run in parallel with others. Next, Lesson 18 starts the technical prep with math and probability.
