# Script — Receivables Work Areas and the Transaction Lifecycle

## Segment 1 (title)

Now let's get oriented inside the application itself. Oracle Fusion organizes Receivables into a handful of work areas, and every transaction you create moves through a predictable sequence of statuses from entry to fully paid.

## Segment 2 (steps)

There are four main work areas. Billing is where you create and complete invoices, debit memos and credit memos. Receipts is where you enter, apply and reverse customer payments. Customers is where you maintain parties, accounts, sites and credit limits. And Collections is where overdue balances get dunning letters and follow-up. Setup for all of this lives in its own area under Setup and Maintenance.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Every transaction actually carries two separate statuses, and mixing them up is a classic beginner mistake. Completion status is either incomplete or complete, and it just means: is the transaction finished being entered. Balance status is open or closed, and it means: how much is still owed. A transaction can be complete and still open for months if the customer hasn't paid.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Here's the full lifecycle of a standard invoice. Entry, while it's still incomplete. Completion, once all required fields are present. Accounting, when Create Accounting derives the journal entry. Open, while it sits on the customer's account waiting for payment. Application, when a receipt comes in and reduces the balance. And finally closed, once the balance hits zero.

## Segment 5 (outro)

So: four work areas for the day-to-day work, two independent statuses on every transaction, and one lifecycle that every invoice follows. Up next, lesson three: a review of everything Receivables setup needs before you can enter a single transaction.
