# Script — Write-Offs and Approval Limits

## Segment 1 (title)

An adjustment corrects a balance because it's wrong. A write-off is different: the balance is correct, the company just isn't going to collect it, and formally removes it from the books.

## Segment 2 (steps)

A receipt write-off handles a small leftover balance - a minor underpayment that isn't worth chasing - cleaned up right at the point of applying the receipt. System options define a limit range for what can be written off this way.

## Segment 3 (steps)

A transaction write-off deals with a larger, standalone balance the company has given up on collecting, usually after collections efforts have run their course. The invoice stays in history for audit purposes, but the balance is zeroed out as a bad debt.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Write-offs are governed by approval limits, just like adjustments - assigned per user, by currency, with a minimum and maximum amount a person can write off without escalating to a credit manager or controller.

## Segment 5 (outro)

A write-off abandons collection of a legitimate balance, not a correction of a wrong one. Up next, lesson 32: collections and dunning, the process that usually precedes a transaction write-off.
