# Script — Customer Statements

## Segment 1 (title)

A dunning letter is a pointed message about a specific overdue amount. A customer statement is the broader, routine document - a periodic summary of everything on a customer's account, like a monthly credit card statement.

## Segment 2 (steps)

A statement typically includes every open transaction, payment activity for the period, a running account balance, and aging information. The key difference from dunning: a statement covers the whole account, overdue or not.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Statements run on a recurring cycle, usually monthly, configurable per customer or site. Not every customer automatically gets one - a profile flag controls it, and some companies only send statements to accounts carrying an open balance.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Statement messages can vary with how overdue the account is - a plain thank you for a current customer, a firmer note appended for one that's sixty days past due, without the full escalation structure of a dunning letter.

## Segment 5 (outro)

A statement is routine and informational; dunning is a targeted escalation. Up next, lesson 34: aging reports, which quantify exactly how overdue every balance is.
