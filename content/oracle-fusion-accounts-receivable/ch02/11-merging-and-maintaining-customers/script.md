# Script — Merging and Maintaining Customers

## Segment 1 (title)

Customer data doesn't stay clean by itself. Sales reps skip the search, companies restructure, duplicates creep in. Let's close out chapter two by covering how to fix that without losing transaction history.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Even with a shared party registry, duplicates still happen. A rep types a slightly different name instead of searching first. Two business units enter the same customer independently. A company renames and a new party gets created instead of renaming the old one. Left alone, that splits one customer's transaction history across two records, and quietly breaks aging, statements, and credit checks.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Here's what a merge actually does. It moves open and historical transactions from the duplicate into the surviving account you designate. It moves receipts and their applications along with them, preserving history. And it inactivates the duplicate rather than deleting it, so the old record stays for audit purposes, it just can't be transacted against anymore.

## Segment 4 (outro)

Picture two Northwind Fixtures Co business units, each entering Cascade Outdoor Supply independently months apart, one spelled slightly differently. The AR team spots it, designates the one with more history as the survivor, and merges the duplicate into it. All the duplicate's open invoices now show under the surviving account's aging, and the duplicate gets marked inactive, not deleted. That closes chapter two.
