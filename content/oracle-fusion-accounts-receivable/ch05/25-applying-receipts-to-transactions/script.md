# Script — Applying Receipts to Transactions

## Segment 1 (title)

Real customer payments are rarely clean. A customer pays two invoices with one check, pays less than they owe, or pays more. This lesson covers how Receivables handles each of those situations.

## Segment 2 (steps)

A receipt amount doesn't have to match a single invoice. If one check covers three open invoices, the clerk applies against all three in the same action, and Receivables tracks the applied amount per transaction.

## Segment 3 (steps)

When a customer underpays, Receivables applies the available amount and leaves the rest open, or a small residual can be written off depending on policy. When a customer overpays, the excess can be left unapplied, put on-account, or refunded.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Applications normally happen at the transaction level, not the individual line. And when a receipt gets applied to the wrong invoice, it can be unapplied - reversing the application - and then reapplied correctly. It's a normal workflow step, not an exception.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Payments rarely match exactly, and Receivables has a path for every variation. Up next, lesson 26: unapplied and on-account cash in more depth.
