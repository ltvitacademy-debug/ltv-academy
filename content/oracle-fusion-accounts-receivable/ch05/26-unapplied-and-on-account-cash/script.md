# Script — Unapplied and On-Account Cash

## Segment 1 (title)

Overpaid or unmatched cash can end up unapplied or on-account. The words sound similar, but Receivables treats them as genuinely different states, and this lesson draws the line between them.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Unapplied cash is money from a known customer that hasn't been matched to a specific transaction yet. It still belongs to the customer, but formally every open invoice still shows its full balance due until the cash is applied.

## Segment 3 (steps)

On-account cash is a step further removed. It's formally a credit on the customer's account, independent of any invoice - often from a deliberate prepayment or a decision to just leave an overpayment as a standing credit.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Neither one closes an open invoice by itself. That creates a real risk: a collections team working off the aging report alone could chase a customer who's actually sitting on a credit that covers it. Clearing these balances is routine receivables hygiene.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Unapplied cash waits to be matched; on-account cash is a standing credit with no invoice in mind. Up next, lesson 27: automatic receipts and remittances.
