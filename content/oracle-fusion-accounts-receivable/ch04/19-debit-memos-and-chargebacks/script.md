# Script — Debit Memos and Chargebacks

## Segment 1 (title)

Both debit memos and chargebacks increase what a customer owes, and students often mix them up. They solve different problems. A debit memo bills for something new. A chargeback closes an old invoice and recreates the still-owed part as a new item. Let's separate the two clearly.

## Segment 2 (steps)

A debit memo bills for something that wasn't on the original invoice at all, a fee, a restocking charge, a correction. It's usually not linked to any specific original invoice, it's just a new amount owed, with its own number, its own due date, aging independently.

## Segment 3 (steps)

A chargeback responds specifically to an underpayment or unauthorized deduction on an existing invoice. Say a ten thousand dollar invoice gets paid at nine thousand two hundred, with an eight hundred dollar deduction the business disputes. A chargeback closes the original invoice as fully applied, and creates a brand new eight hundred dollar debit item automatically. The key difference from an adjustment: an adjustment would write that eight hundred off. A chargeback recreates it as a new, fully collectible balance.

## Segment 4 (outro)

Picture Harborline Retail Group paying nine thousand two hundred against Northwind Fixtures Co's ten thousand dollar invoice, deducting eight hundred for a damage claim Northwind's logistics team already rejected. Northwind creates a chargeback: the original invoice closes fully applied, and a new eight hundred dollar chargeback transaction gets created, due under Harborline's standard terms, and Northwind keeps pursuing it as a legitimate balance, not writing it off.
