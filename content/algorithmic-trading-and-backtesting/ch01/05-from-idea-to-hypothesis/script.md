# Script — From Idea to Hypothesis

## Segment 1 (title)

This lesson closes out chapter one with a discipline rather than a technique: turning a vague idea into a hypothesis specific enough to actually fail. If you can't say in advance what result would prove you wrong, you're not testing the idea — you're just confirming it.

## Segment 2 (steps)

"Momentum works" isn't falsifiable — it's vague enough that almost any result can be waved in as support. A real hypothesis names the exact signal, the exact universe and period, and critically, a kill threshold: the specific result that would make you discard the idea rather than keep tuning it until it looks better.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Before you touch the data, pin down five things: the universe and period, including data you're holding out untouched for later; the exact signal formula, with no parameters you plan to tune after seeing results; the direction and rough size of the effect you expect; the one success metric you'll judge it by; and your kill criteria.

## Segment 4 (code)

Write all of that down before any backtest code runs — something as simple as a logged dictionary works. The reason isn't bureaucratic, it's that your own brain is a very good after-the-fact rationalizer. Once you've seen a result, it's easy to convince yourself a particular cutoff "obviously" made sense all along, when really you picked it because it produced a better number.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Skip this discipline and you get the seed of chapter four's central problem: tuning a rule until it matches the one history you have, which is overfitting, not an edge. Up next, lesson six starts chapter two by turning a hypothesis into a real, computable trading signal.
