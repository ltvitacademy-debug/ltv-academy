# Script — Research Engineer vs. Research Scientist

## Segment 1 (title)

Welcome to AI Research Engineering, the fifth course in the AI and ML Research Engineer and Alignment Engineer path. Every course so far taught you a subfield. This one teaches the job itself, starting with the question every new hire asks: what's the real difference between a research engineer and a research scientist?

## Segment 2 (steps)

Both titles point at the same mission from different angles. The research scientist is more often asking what to try next and why. The research engineer is more often asking how to build something that lets the team try it, at the speed and scale they need. Labs like Anthropic, OpenAI, and DeepMind post both titles on the same teams, often with the same interview loop.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Where they tend to diverge: research scientists are evaluated more on novel ideas and publications, while research engineers are evaluated more on whether experiments actually run. A scientist stuck for a week might be stuck on whether an idea is right. An engineer stuck for a week might be stuck on why a training run silently diverges at sixty-four GPUs. Engineers also disproportionately own shared infrastructure, and their output is more often a working system than a paper.

## Segment 4 (code)

Take one question, asked two ways. Does sparsity hurt accuracy past fifty percent — that's the scientist's framing: design an ablation, interpret the curve. Can I even run that sweep overnight without it silently running out of memory — that's the engineer's framing: build the harness, make it finish. Both have to get answered before the research exists at all.

## Segment 5 (outro)

That's the shape to hold onto: a spectrum of emphasis, not a wall, and most people do some of both. Up next, lesson two: research engineer versus product engineer.
