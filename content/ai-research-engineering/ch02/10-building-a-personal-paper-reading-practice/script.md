# Script — Building a Personal Paper-Reading Practice

## Segment 1 (title)

Everything in this chapter only compounds if you keep doing it. The field moves fast enough that a single burst of reading effort fades in relevance within months — this closing lesson is about the habits that sustain the practice for years, not a skill you deploy once on a project and then quietly drop.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Keep a reading log: for every paper that clears the skim, write down what the load-bearing claim was and your own judgment of the evidence, in whatever tool you already use day to day. Revisit papers that turned out to matter on a spaced schedule — new context from everything you've read since often reveals things you missed the first time around.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Reading groups add two things solo reading can't: a real deadline that gets you to actually finish the paper, and other people's objections to claims you might otherwise have accepted too easily on your own. Explaining a paper out loud to colleagues who ask "how do you know that's actually the cause" is one of the fastest ways to discover you missed the load-bearing claim entirely.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Reading everything posted to arXiv every day is impossible, so follow a small, deliberately chosen set of labs, researchers, and digests instead, checked on a fixed cadence rather than continuously. Every incoming paper gets the five-to-ten-minute skim and nothing more unless it earns a deeper read on its own merits — and "I didn't read that one" has to be a normal, expected outcome, not a failure.

## Segment 5 (outro)

With a sustainable reading practice in place, Chapter 3 turns from reading other people's work to structuring your own: building a research codebase that holds up under real experimentation.
