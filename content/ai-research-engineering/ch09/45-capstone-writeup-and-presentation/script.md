# Script — Capstone: Write-Up & Presentation

## Segment 1 (title)

This is the last lesson of AI Research Engineering. You've picked a paper, reproduced its core result, and run a small extension. What's left is writing it up the way chapter seven taught and presenting it clearly — a result nobody can read or evaluate is, for practical purposes, a result that doesn't exist yet.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Reuse lesson thirty-three's report structure directly. Method: the claim, in your own words, and the number you targeted. Reproduction: your number against the reported one, and which tier you're claiming. Extension: the question you asked, the experiment you ran, and the result, with the same rigor as the reproduction. Limitations: your compute, your seed count, and anything that doesn't generalize, stated plainly rather than hidden.

## Segment 3 (code)

Make the reproduction a demonstrated claim, not an assertion. A short table showing the reported number against yours, the tier you're claiming, and a direct link to the actual tracked run and its logged config turns "I reproduced it" into something a reader can go verify themselves, rather than something they have to take on faith.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Structure the presentation the same way as the report. Open with the comparison table, because the audience wants the evidence before the process that produced it. Report the extension honestly, whether it showed a real effect, a partial one, or none at all. And close with what you'd do next given more compute, more seeds, or more time.

## Segment 5 (outro)

You started this course learning what separates a research engineer's job from a scientist's or a product engineer's, and you're finishing it having reproduced and extended a real published result using every skill in between. That's the actual job — pick it up again on the next real paper. Congratulations on completing AI Research Engineering.
