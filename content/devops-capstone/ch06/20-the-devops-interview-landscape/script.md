# Script — The DevOps Interview Landscape

## Segment 1 (title)

Before diving into specific questions, it helps to see the whole process laid out, because most candidates lose points not from lacking knowledge but from preparing for the wrong stage at the wrong time, in the wrong order.

## Segment 2 (steps)

A typical pipeline runs through a recruiter screen checking fit and logistics, a technical screen testing real depth in Linux, networking, Kubernetes, and Terraform, a system design conversation testing reasoning under ambiguity, and then behavioral questions and sometimes a take-home exercise or a live pairing session with an engineer. Have a sixty-second capstone summary ready, because "tell me about a recent project" comes up almost every single time, at almost every stage.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Each stage is really testing something different underneath the surface question it asks. The technical screen is checking whether you actually did the work your resume claims. System design is checking trade-off awareness, not memorized facts. The behavioral interview is checking how you work under pressure and with other people. And the final round usually weighs culture fit and the quality of the questions you ask them more than raw technical depth at that point.

## Segment 4 (code)

For system design specifically, lean on real trade-offs from the capstone instead of inventing an answer cold under pressure. Checkout needed a wider autoscaling range than product-catalog because of flash-sale spikes — that's a real reasoning chain you can extend to whatever system an interviewer puts in front of you, which sounds far more credible to a listener than guessing from scratch on the spot.
