# Script — Resume & LinkedIn for DevOps Engineers

## Segment 1 (title)

You just spent seventeen lessons building a real system end to end — infrastructure, a pipeline, monitoring, security, an incident drill, a postmortem. That is genuinely strong material, but a hiring manager will never see any of it unless your resume and LinkedIn profile translate it into language they can scan in seconds. This lesson is about that translation.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Every strong resume bullet has the same shape: an action verb, what you actually built, and a quantified outcome. "Worked on CI/CD" is a tutorial sentence — it describes a topic, not a result. Naming the real pipeline mechanism, the real tool, and a real number is a sentence a hiring manager has to take seriously, because it sounds like something that actually exists and still runs.

## Segment 3 (code)

Pull straight from the capstone instead of inventing anything. You built a GitHub Actions pipeline with Trivy and gitleaks scanning promoting across three environments behind a manual approval gate. You configured different autoscaling targets for two services with different load profiles — checkout wider, product-catalog narrower. And you traced a real latency spike to a saturated connection pool using Prometheus and the USE method, then wrote a postmortem. Every one of those is a resume line with a real number already built into it.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Your LinkedIn headline should name your target role plus three or four real tools, not your current job title, especially if you're changing careers. Your About section gets three short paragraphs describing what you do, the capstone project by name with real architecture details, and what you're looking for next. And your Featured section should pin the storefront repository right under your headline, so it's the very first thing anyone clicks before they even scroll.
