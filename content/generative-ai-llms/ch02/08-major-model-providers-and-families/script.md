# Lesson 8 — Major Model Providers & Families · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2.5 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Chapter One covered how any LLM works under the hood. Chapter Two is about the actual landscape —
who makes these models, and what they're each called right now. Checked directly against every
provider's own documentation, because this list moves fast.

## S2 · CODE CARD (provider -> family -> current flagship)

Five names to know. Anthropic's Claude: Haiku, Sonnet, Opus, and Fable tiers, Fable currently the
flagship. OpenAI's GPT: the six-point-x Astra family at the top, with Sol and Luna nearby. Google's
Gemini: the three-point-eight Flash generation, plus a Pro Preview tier. xAI's Grok: four-point-
seven, five hundred thousand tokens of context. And Meta's Llama: four, open-weighted instead of
API-only — Lesson 9 explains exactly what that distinction means.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (how to read a model name)

Three pieces, every name. The family — Claude, GPT, Gemini — identifies the lineage. The
generation number marks a major version bump. And the tier name — Haiku, Sonnet, Opus, Fable, or
Flash, Pro — marks where it sits on the speed-versus-capability trade-off from last lesson. Parse
it that way, and a release you've never seen still tells you roughly what it's for.

## S4 · CODE CARD (same tier pattern, different positioning)

The same four-ish-tier pattern repeats at every provider — cheap and fast, balanced, flagship —
even though the names differ completely. What actually differs more is positioning: Anthropic
leans into coding and agentic work, OpenAI has the broadest consumer ecosystem, Google leans on
its own cloud and multimodal strengths, xAI positions itself as lean and cost-conscious.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

Five families, four-ish tiers each, and a naming pattern that holds up across all of them. Next
lesson: the other major split in this landscape — open-weight models you can download, versus
closed models you can only call through an API.
