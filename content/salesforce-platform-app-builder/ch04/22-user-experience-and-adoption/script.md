# Script — User Experience and Adoption

## Segment 1 (title)

A deployed application that nobody actually uses has failed, no matter how clean its data model or how airtight its security. This lesson is about the gap between "it works" and "people choose to use it every day" — and the declarative tools built specifically to close that gap.

## Segment 2 (steps: fewer clicks)

The biggest lever you have is reducing actions between needing to do something and having it done. Dynamic Forms show only the fields relevant to the current record state. Dynamic Actions do the same for buttons — a quick action only appears when it's actually eligible. Compact layouts control what shows in the highlights panel without opening the full record. Every field someone scrolls past is friction, and friction is why people route around your app with a spreadsheet.

## Segment 3 (code: in-app guidance)

Three tools for teaching in the moment. In-App Guidance, or Prompts, attach a message to a specific page — the standard way to announce a change without an email nobody reads. Field-level help text is the small info icon next to a label — cheap to write, constantly skipped, and the first place a confused user actually looks. And a guided screen Flow beats a wiki page for any genuinely multi-step process.

## Segment 4 (steps: measuring adoption)

"We shipped it" isn't the same claim as "people use it." Salesforce's Optimizer app surfaces unused fields and inactive rules — signs of a build that landed but didn't stick. Login frequency and feature-usage reports, built the same way as lesson nineteen's reports, tell you the real story. And simply asking actual users in the first two weeks works better than either.

## Segment 5 (code: rollout)

Roll out by permission set, not by flipping a switch for everyone — pilot group first, fix what's actually broken, then expand. Train on the workflow, not the field list: "here's how you log a visit" beats "here's every field on this object." And expect a transition period where old habits compete with the new app — plan for it instead of being surprised by it.

## Segment 6 (outro)

Reports to see it, security to trust it, deployment to ship it, adoption to make it matter — that's this chapter's pipeline, complete. Next: a case study that walks through an entire app, start to finish.
