# Hypercare

Lesson 22 ended with Brightfield's command center handling day-one issues. That elevated level of support doesn't stop after day one — it continues, deliberately, for a defined stretch of time called hypercare. This lesson covers what hypercare actually is, how it's staffed, and how a project knows when it's over.

## What you'll learn

- What hypercare is and how it differs from steady-state production support
- Typical hypercare duration and why it varies by project complexity
- What exit criteria end hypercare and hand off to steady-state support
- How Brightfield structured and closed its hypercare period

## What hypercare actually is

**Hypercare** is the intensive, stabilization-focused support period immediately following go-live. The implementation team — the same Functional and Technical Consultants who built the system — stays available with elevated responsiveness, often with daily stand-up meetings reviewing every open issue, specifically because the business is still discovering how the new system behaves under real daily use. This is different from standard production support (Lesson 24) both in who provides it (the implementation team, not a steady-state help desk) and in how fast it responds (hours or same-day, not the longer response windows typical of routine support).

## How long hypercare lasts

Hypercare commonly runs a few weeks of intensive support, though more complex ERP go-lives sometimes extend it to a couple of months, particularly if the implementation needs to get through at least one full month-end close under live conditions before confidence is high enough to step down support. There's no single correct number — what matters is that hypercare has a **defined scope, staffing model, and exit criteria** going in, rather than an open-ended "we'll figure out when we're done" arrangement that tends to drag on indefinitely.

## Exit criteria: knowing when hypercare is over

Hypercare typically ends against explicit exit criteria agreed before go-live: a declining trend in new issues, no outstanding Severity 1 or 2 defects, successful completion of at least one full business cycle (often month-end close) under live conditions, and a confirmed handoff of outstanding items to whoever owns steady-state support (Lesson 24). Ending hypercare on a calendar date alone, regardless of whether these conditions are met, risks dropping the business right when they need support most.

## Brightfield Industrial Group: hypercare in practice

Brightfield's hypercare runs for four weeks, with daily stand-ups for the first two weeks dropping to three times a week for the second two as issue volume declines. The team deliberately keeps hypercare open through Brightfield's first full month-end close in the new system — the real test of whether GL, AP, AR, and Cash Management close cleanly together — before declaring exit criteria met and formally handing remaining low-priority items to the internal support team covered in Lesson 24.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hypercare | Intensive, elevated support immediately following go-live |
| Exit criteria | The conditions that must be met before hypercare formally ends |
| Steady-state support | The standard, ongoing support model hypercare eventually hands off to |

## Recap

Hypercare is a deliberately scoped, elevated support period run by the implementation team itself, ending against explicit exit criteria — not an arbitrary calendar date — often extending through a first full business cycle like month-end close. Brightfield's four-week hypercare, tapering as issues declined, closed out cleanly once that first close succeeded.
