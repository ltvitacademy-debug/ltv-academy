# Script — Hypercare

## Segment 1 (title)

Lesson twenty-two ended with Brightfield's command center handling day-one issues. That elevated support doesn't stop after day one — it continues, deliberately, for a defined stretch of time called hypercare. This lesson covers what hypercare actually is and how a project knows when it's over.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Hypercare is the intensive, stabilization-focused support period right after go-live. The same implementation team that built the system stays available with elevated responsiveness, often with daily stand-ups, because the business is still discovering how the new system behaves under real daily use. It differs from standard support both in who provides it and how fast it responds.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Hypercare commonly runs a few weeks, sometimes extending to a couple of months for complex go-lives, particularly to get through at least one full month-end close under live conditions. There's no single correct number — what matters is a defined scope, staffing model, and exit criteria going in, not an open-ended arrangement.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Hypercare typically ends against explicit exit criteria: a declining trend in new issues, no outstanding severity 1 or 2 defects, a successful full business cycle under live conditions, and a confirmed handoff to steady-state support. Ending on a calendar date alone, regardless of whether conditions are met, risks dropping the business right when it needs support most.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Brightfield's hypercare runs four weeks, tapering from daily stand-ups to three times a week as issues decline, deliberately staying open through the first full month-end close before declaring exit criteria met. Up next, lesson twenty-four: what steady-state production support looks like once hypercare ends.
