# Script — Training, Documentation and Knowledge Transfer

## Segment 1 (title)

This final lesson closes the loop on two things referenced throughout the course: how business users actually learn the new system before go-live, and how the internal support team became capable of handling issues on their own. Both are forms of the same discipline: making sure knowledge doesn't live only inside the implementation team's heads.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Direct training has the implementation team teach every end user personally, practical for a small population but slow to scale. Train-the-trainer trains a smaller group of super users deeply, who then train their own departments, scaling faster and building exactly the depth those super users need for Tier 1 support later.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Training is scoped to what a specific role actually needs to do, not a general tour of every screen. An AP clerk needs deep training on invoice entry and matching, not Cash Management's reconciliation rules. Documentation persists after training ends: user guides, quick reference job aids, and the project's own configuration workbook and solution design documents as a permanent record.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Knowledge transfer is distinct from end-user training. It's the deliberate process of making the client's own internal staff capable of supporting the system without the implementation partner: how to troubleshoot configuration, read a defect log, file a Service Request. It typically happens through shadowing, then reverse shadowing, before the implementation team steps back.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Brightfield trains its super users directly, who then train their own departments with role-based job aids, while two internal staff shadow and reverse-shadow the functional consultants before handling support unassisted. This completes Oracle Fusion Implementation Lifecycle. The next course in the path, Troubleshooting Oracle Financials, picks up exactly where production support left off.
