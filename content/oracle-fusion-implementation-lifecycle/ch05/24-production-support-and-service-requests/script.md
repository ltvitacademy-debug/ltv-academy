# Script — Production Support and Service Requests

## Segment 1 (title)

Hypercare ended. Brightfield's implementation team steps back, and steady-state support takes over for the rest of the system's life. This lesson covers that ongoing model, including the one escalation path unique to Oracle Cloud: the Service Request.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Steady-state support runs in tiers. Tier one is an internal help desk or super users, handling how-to questions. Tier two is internal functional or technical staff who can resolve configuration-level issues. Tier three is Oracle itself, reached through a Service Request when an issue looks like a genuine product defect.

## Segment 3 (steps)

A Service Request is appropriate once tier one and two have ruled out configuration or user error and the issue looks like a product defect or something only Oracle can fix. Opening one too early wastes everyone's time; opening one too late after days of fruitless internal effort delays the business unnecessarily.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Oracle severity one means production is stopped or so impacted work can't continue, worked 24 by 7 with a response target in minutes. Severity two is a severe loss with no workaround. Severity three is a minor loss, an inconvenience that may need a workaround. Matching the right severity actually matters for getting the right response speed.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Brightfield staffs tier one with super users and tier two with internally trained staff, escalating to Oracle only when needed. A rounding discrepancy three months post-go-live becomes a Severity 3 SR, resolved in the following quarterly update. Up next, the final lesson: training, documentation, and the knowledge transfer that made this handoff possible.
