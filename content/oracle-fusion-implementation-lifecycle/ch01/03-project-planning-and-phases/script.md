# Script — Project Planning and Phases

## Segment 1 (title)

You know the TCM phases and the roles that staff a project. This lesson covers the planning artifacts that turn those phases into an actual schedule, and the governance that keeps a project honest about its progress.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Every implementation needs a few core documents. A project charter states the business case and scope. A scope statement nails down which modules, legal entities, and countries are actually in scope. A work breakdown structure splits the project into workstreams and tasks with owners. And a RAID log tracks risks, assumptions, issues, and dependencies as a running record, not a one-time exercise.

## Segment 3 (steps)

TCM phases aren't just labels on a calendar. Each one has entry and exit criteria tracked inside Oracle Cloud Success Navigator. A project typically can't move from Design into Configure until solution design is signed off, and can't move into Transition until UAT has formally passed. Those checkpoints are phase gates, giving the sponsor a real go or no-go decision instead of a surprise near go-live.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Timelines vary enormously by scope: modules, legal entities, countries, data volume, and integrations. Rather than memorizing a number, focus on the discipline itself: a realistic work breakdown structure, an honestly maintained RAID log, and milestone reviews that are actually enforced.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Brightfield scopes its Phase 1 to GL, AP, AR, and Cash Management for US operations only, deferring Fixed Assets and its Canadian subsidiary. Its RAID log already flags a key risk: the legacy export tool has never been tested against the new chart of accounts. Up next, lesson four: gathering the requirements that drive that plan.
