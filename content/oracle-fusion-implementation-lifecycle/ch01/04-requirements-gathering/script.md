# Script — Requirements Gathering

## Segment 1 (title)

With a plan in place, the first real work of the Design phase begins: figuring out exactly what the business needs. Requirements gathering turns a vague statement like "we need better cash visibility" into something a project can actually design, configure, and test against.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Requirements come in three layers. A business requirement states the outcome in business language. A functional requirement states what the system must do to satisfy it. A technical requirement states what has to be built or configured to support that functional requirement. A functional consultant usually owns that first translation for their module.

## Segment 3 (steps)

The team documents current-state process first: how it works today, including every manual workaround. Then future-state mapping starts not from a blank page, but from Oracle Modern Best Practice, Oracle's own reference flows. The gap between current-state and that future-state reference is exactly what fit-gap analysis formalizes next chapter.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Requirements get a unique ID in a business requirements document, and that ID flows into a Requirements Traceability Matrix, linking the requirement to its design decision, its configuration, and the test script that proves it works. A requirement with no test script by the time testing starts is a red flag.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Brightfield's requirement BR-CM-014 asks for same-day cash visibility across US bank accounts. It becomes a functional requirement for automatic statement import and Cash Positioning configuration, traced all the way to a UAT test script. Up next, lesson five: the interviews and workshops where this information gets collected.
