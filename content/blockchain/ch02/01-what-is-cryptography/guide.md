## A working definition

Cryptography is **the science of protecting information so that only the
right people can read or verify it.** In simple terms: it's like locking
your message in a secret box that only the right person has the key to
open — or sealing it with a stamp only you have, so others know it's really
from you.

## What cryptography actually solves

For blockchain specifically, cryptography's job comes down to three real
things: **trust, security, and verification.** Its role is to make
**trustless systems** possible — systems where participants don't need to
personally trust each other, because the math itself guarantees the rules
are being followed.

## You already use this every day

Cryptography isn't some exotic blockchain-only concept — it's already
everywhere:

- **Passwords** — proving you're the right person without revealing the
  password itself to everyone who checks it.
- **Messaging apps** — WhatsApp and Signal use end-to-end encryption so only
  the sender and recipient can actually read a message.
- **ATMs** — verifying your card and PIN without exposing your account
  details to anyone intercepting the transaction.
