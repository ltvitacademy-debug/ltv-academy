## The real question a student actually asked

A student in this class asked something worth sitting with: **how could
blockchain actually be used in healthcare** — specifically, as a
replacement for insurance for people who can't afford it or fall through
the cracks? Rather than guess, the instructor pulled up ChatGPT live, in
front of the class, and asked it directly.

![ChatGPT's live answer on screen: the problem with traditional health insurance, and a blockchain-based decentralized healthcare coverage model.](/courses/blockchain/ch01/05-blockchain-in-the-real-world/shot_chatgpt1.png)
*The real problem stated plainly: people can't afford traditional insurance, insurance companies control coverage decisions, and payments to providers are often delayed.*

## Don't just port Bitcoin onto a new industry

The first, important lesson here isn't the healthcare idea itself — it's a
warning against a common mistake: **you wouldn't want to just take the idea
of Bitcoin and put it on healthcare.** You'd want to design a system
actually suited to the industry's real problem, using blockchain's
underlying properties rather than copying Bitcoin's specific model.

## The actual proposal

The class's live ChatGPT answer laid out a genuinely coherent model:

- **Smart contracts for automatic coverage** — a contract on the blockchain
  automatically releases payment when certain conditions are met, like a
  diagnosis or a treatment record. This removes the need for an insurance
  company acting as a middleman.
- **Tokenized mutual aid pools** — people contribute tokens regularly into
  a shared pool; when someone gets sick, the system automatically pays out
  of that pool, like a digital co-op.
- **Transparent, patient-controlled medical records** — encrypted health
  records the patient actually controls and shares, with smart contracts
  reading them to trigger coverage based on diagnosis codes.

*A worked example: a woman named Rosa, in a rural area with no insurance, joins a blockchain-based community health fund. A doctor logs her diagnosis on-chain; a smart contract verifies it and instantly releases funds from the pool to the hospital.*

## Why this example matters for the rest of the course

Every idea in this proposal traces directly back to what Chapter 1 already
covered:

- **Decentralization** (Lesson 1) means no single insurance company
  controls the coverage decision.
- **Smart contracts** — introduced briefly here, covered in real depth
  starting in Chapter 4 — are what actually automates the payout.
- **Transparency and immutability** (Lesson 1) are what make the whole
  system auditable: "all actions are transparent and immutable."

This is genuinely the point of learning the foundations first: once you
understand decentralization, consensus, and smart contracts as real
building blocks, you can combine them to solve problems that have nothing
to do with cryptocurrency trading at all.
