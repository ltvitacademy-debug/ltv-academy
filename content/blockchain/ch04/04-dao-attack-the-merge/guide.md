## The DAO attack of 2016

A **DAO (decentralized autonomous organization)** is an organization run by
code instead of a board of directors — decisions are made by token-holder
vote, and the rules are enforced automatically by smart contracts.

![The real CryptoPotato article, continued: the actual paragraph on the 2016 DAO attack, read live in class.](/courses/blockchain/ch04/04-dao-attack-the-merge/shot_dao_attack.png)
*"The DAO Attack of 2016" — the real article text shown on screen, describing exactly what happened.*

In April 2016, a DAO called simply **"The DAO"** raised **150 million
worth of Ether from over 11,000 investors** — one of the biggest projects
on Ethereum at the time. Barely three months later, an attacker exploited
a flaw in its code and drained about **3.6 million ETH (roughly $60
million at the time)**.

## The hard fork: how we got two Ethereums

The community first tried a **soft fork** to contain the damage, but it
didn't work. Ethereum's developers then implemented a **hard fork** — a
permanent split from the blockchain's history — that reverted the chain
back to before the attack and returned the stolen funds to investors.

This is the real, concrete difference between the two resulting chains:

- **Ethereum** — the new chain, with the hack reverted and funds restored.
- **Ethereum Classic** — the original chain, left exactly as it was,
  hack included, kept alive by those who believed "code is law" and the
  chain's history shouldn't be altered even to undo a theft.

## The scalability trilemma

By late 2017, Ethereum had become the leading smart contract platform —
and its own popularity (accelerated by the CryptoKitties craze) caused
real network congestion and high gas fees. This exposed what Buterin
himself named the **blockchain trilemma**: a network can't simultaneously
maximize **security, decentralization, and scalability** — improving one
usually means compromising another.

## 2022: the Merge

Ethereum spent years preparing a proof-of-stake chain, called the
**Beacon Chain**, running in parallel to its original proof-of-work
mainnet. On **September 15, 2022**, an upgrade called **Paris** joined the
two — Ethereum's existing execution layer merged with the new
proof-of-stake consensus layer. At the time of the upgrade, over **13.4
million ETH** had already been staked in preparation.

## What's next: sharding and ETFs

- **Sharding** is a planned upgrade that would split the Ethereum network
  into independent partitions ("shards"), aiming to improve throughput by
  up to 1,000x while working alongside Layer 2 rollups.
- In **July 2024**, the first **Ethereum ETFs** launched, letting
  traditional investors gain exposure to Ethereum's price without holding
  the cryptocurrency directly — a real, concrete sign of Ethereum's move
  into mainstream finance.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| DAO | An organization governed by code and token-holder votes instead of a board |
| Hard fork | A permanent split from a blockchain's prior history |
| Ethereum vs. Ethereum Classic | The reverted (funds-restored) chain vs. the original, unaltered chain |
| The Merge | The 2022 joining of Ethereum's execution layer with its proof-of-stake consensus layer |
