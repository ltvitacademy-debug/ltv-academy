## S1 · TITLE CARD (SVG: lesson title, LTV brand)

Five lessons in, and you've already touched more of Power BI than you might
realize. Let's zoom all the way out and name the pattern underneath
everything you've done — because it's the same pattern for the rest of this
course.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (SVG: Get → Transform → Model → Visualize → Publish)

Here it is: Get, Transform, Model, Visualize, Publish. Five words, and every
single thing you will ever do in Power BI is one of them. Not most things —
everything. Let's walk through each one, and I'll tell you exactly which
chapter of this course picks it up.

## S3 · IMAGE: qs-connect-data_02.png (Get data button)

Step one, Get. You connect to wherever your data lives. You did this in the
last lesson with an Excel file. Chapter 2 opens this up fully — CSV files,
SQL databases, web pages, REST APIs, and the choice between importing data or
querying it live.

## S4 · IMAGE: query-overview-with-data-connection.png (Power Query Editor)

Step two, Transform. Real data is rarely clean on arrival. Power Query Editor
is where you fix data types, remove what you don't need, split and combine
columns — and every step you take gets recorded, so it replays automatically
the next time your data refreshes. Chapter 3 is entirely this.

## S5 · IMAGE: modeling-view-07.png (Model view relationship diagram)

Step three, Model. Once your data is clean, you tell Power BI how your tables
relate to each other — this is the exact screen from Lesson 3's interface
tour. Chapter 4 covers the star schema and relationships properly; Chapters 5
and 6 build DAX formulas and time intelligence on top of that same model.

## S6 · IMAGE: change-visual.png (Visualizations pane, changing chart type)

Step four, Visualize. This is where data turns into something a person can
actually read — you built your first one last lesson. Chapter 7 covers the
full range of visuals and formatting; Chapter 8 goes further, into designing
a whole dashboard that reads clearly at a glance.

## S7 · IMAGE: pbid_publish_publishbutton.png (Publish button on ribbon)

And step five, Publish. One button turns your file into something your team
can open and share. Chapter 9 covers the Power BI service itself —
workspaces, sharing, and Fabric. Chapters 10 and 11 round it out: keeping data
refreshed on a schedule, and controlling exactly who can see which rows.

## S8 · OUTRO CARD (SVG: next lesson, LTV seal)

Get, Transform, Model, Visualize, Publish. That's the whole course, in five
words, and Chapter 1 is done. Next up, Chapter 2: every way to get data into
Power BI. See you there.
