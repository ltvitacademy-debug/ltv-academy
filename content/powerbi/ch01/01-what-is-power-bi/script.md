# Lesson 1 — What Is Power BI? · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Each segment is one TTS call so slide timing follows

## S1 · TITLE CARD (SVG: lesson title, LTV brand)

Welcome to the Lifting the Veil Power BI course. I'm glad you're here. In this
first lesson we answer the most basic question of all: what exactly is Power BI,
and why do companies everywhere pay good money for people who know it?

## S2 · IMAGE: overview-blocks.png (Desktop → Service → Mobile diagram)

Power BI is Microsoft's business analytics platform. Its job is to turn raw
data — spreadsheets, databases, cloud services — into interactive reports and
dashboards that people actually use to make decisions. It isn't one program;
it's three parts working together. Power BI Desktop, a free Windows application
where you build reports. The Power BI service, the cloud, where you publish and
share them. And Power BI Mobile, so the people you share with can read those
reports on a phone or tablet. Build on Desktop, publish to the service, consume
anywhere. That flow is the heart of everything in this course.

## S3 · IMAGE: desktop-splash.png (Desktop opening screen)

Power BI Desktop is where the real work happens, and it costs nothing. You
download it, install it on Windows, and you have the same tool the
professionals use — there is no paid version of Desktop with more buttons.
Everything you'll build in this course, you'll build here first.

## S4 · IMAGE: desktop-report-view.png (numbered interface)

Here's the workspace you'll live in. Across the top, a ribbon, just like Excel.
In the middle, the report canvas where visuals go. On the right, three panes:
Filters, Visualizations, and your data Fields. Down the left edge you can switch
between the report, the raw data tables, and the model view. Don't memorize it
now — the next lessons walk through every piece.

## S5 · IMAGE: viz-types.png (visualizations gallery)

Building a report is drag and drop. Pick a visual from this gallery — bar
charts, line charts, maps, cards, tables — drop your fields onto it, and Power
BI draws it. More than thirty visuals ship in the box, and the marketplace adds
hundreds more.

## S6 · IMAGE: service-dashboard.png (dashboard in the service)

When a report is ready, you publish it to the Power BI service in the cloud.
That's where your work meets its audience: dashboards that update as the data
changes, apps that bundle reports for a whole department, alerts, subscriptions,
and sharing controlled down to the row of data a person is allowed to see.

## S7 · IMAGE: service-content.png (reports, models, dashboards)

Three building blocks to remember. A semantic model is your prepared data — the
single version of the truth. A report is a set of interactive pages built on a
model. A dashboard pins the most important visuals from one or many reports onto
a single screen. Model, report, dashboard. You'll hear those words in every
lesson from here on.

## S8 · WORKFLOW CARD (SVG: Get → Transform → Model → Visualize → Publish)

And here is the whole job in five words. Get data from wherever it lives.
Transform it until it's clean. Model it so the pieces relate. Visualize it so
people can see the story. Publish it so the business can act. Every chapter in
this course teaches one link in that chain.

## S9 · OUTRO CARD (SVG: next lesson, LTV seal)

That's Power BI in one lesson: a free desktop tool to build, a cloud service to
share, and a workflow you're about to master. In the next lesson we install
Power BI Desktop together and take the interface apart piece by piece. See you
there.
