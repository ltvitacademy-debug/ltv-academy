# Lesson 3 — Power BI Desktop Interface Tour · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Each segment is one TTS call so slide timing follows
the audio. Target: ~3-4 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD (SVG: lesson title, LTV brand)

Power BI Desktop is installed and open. Before we touch a single button, let's
take the whole interface apart, piece by piece, so nothing in the rest of this
course catches you by surprise.

## S2 · IMAGE: report-view-blank-canvas.png (full workspace, blank canvas)

Here's the whole window. Across the top, the ribbon — Home, Insert, Modeling,
View. In the middle, the canvas, where every visual you build will live. And on
the right, three panes: Filters, Visualizations, and Fields. Same layout, every
single time you open Power BI Desktop.

## S3 · IMAGE: pbi_reportviewinpbidesigner_changeview.png (view switcher icons)

Down the far left edge are three small icons, and they switch your entire
view. Report view — the bar chart icon — is where you'll live almost all of
the time; it's what you're looking at right now. Data view shows you the raw
rows in a table. Model view shows how your tables connect to each other. We'll
look at all three.

## S4 · IMAGE: add-visual.png (Fields pane building a visual)

Start with the Fields pane, on the far right. Once you've loaded data, it
lists every table and column you have. Check a box, or drag a field straight
onto the canvas, and Power BI builds a visual for you automatically — no
chart-drawing required.

## S5 · IMAGE: change-visual.png (Visualizations pane changing chart type)

That visual isn't locked in. Select it, then look at the Visualizations pane
just above Fields: click a different icon, and the exact same data redraws as
a completely different chart type. Line to bar, bar to map — your field
choices carry over every time.

## S6 · IMAGE: dataview_fullscreen.png (Data view with numbered elements)

Switch to Data view, and you're looking at the actual table sitting behind
your report — every row, every column, exactly as loaded. This is also where
you'll write DAX formulas for measures and calculated columns, starting in
Chapter 5, and where you'll come back to sanity-check that a formula is
producing the numbers you expect.

## S7 · IMAGE: modeling-view-07.png (Model view relationship diagram)

And this is Model view: every table in your model, drawn as a box, connected
by a line wherever a relationship exists. Don't worry about why these
particular tables connect this way yet — that entire question is Chapter 4.
For now, just recognize this screen, because you'll be living in it soon.

## S8 · OUTRO CARD (SVG: next lesson, LTV seal)

Ribbon, canvas, three panes, three views — that's the whole interface. Nothing
left to guess at. In the next lesson, we finally load real data and build your
first report from scratch. See you there.
