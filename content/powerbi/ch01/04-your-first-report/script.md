# Lesson 4 — Your First Power BI Report · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Each segment is one TTS call so slide timing follows
the audio. Target: ~3-4 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD (SVG: lesson title, LTV brand)

You know the interface now. Time to actually build something. This lesson,
we connect to real data and get two visuals on the canvas — start to finish.

## S2 · IMAGE: power-bi-report-by-numbers.png (finished report preview)

Here's where this is all headed eventually: a title, a few charts, a map,
arranged on one page that tells a story at a glance. We're not building all of
that today — formatting, DAX, and slicers each get their own lessons later.
Today, just two pieces: getting data in, and getting a visual out.

## S3 · IMAGE: qs-connect-data_02.png (Home ribbon, Get data highlighted)

Every report starts the same way. On the Home ribbon, select Get data. This
one button is the front door to dozens of connectors — Excel, SQL databases,
web pages, and more. Chapter 2 covers all of them. Today, we're using Excel.

## S4 · IMAGE: qs-connect-data_03.png (Get Data dialog, Excel highlighted)

Choose Excel from the list, connect, and point Power BI at your workbook.

## S5 · IMAGE: qs-connect-data_05.png (Navigator, table selected, Load button)

Power BI shows you every table it found inside the file, in a window called
Navigator. Check the ones you want, and select Load. Once it's loaded, look at
the Fields pane — your table and its columns are sitting right there, ready
to use.

## S6 · IMAGE: power-bi-column-chart-date.png (column chart built from two fields)

Now the fun part. Drag a numeric field — profit, sales, anything with numbers
— onto a blank spot on the canvas. Power BI draws a chart automatically. Drag
a second field, like a date, onto that same visual, and it redraws to break
your numbers out across time. No chart-drawing, no formulas — just fields,
dragged.

## S7 · IMAGE: power-bi-map-visual.png (bubble map by country)

Try it again with a different kind of field — a country or region — and Power
BI might hand you a map instead of a bar chart, sized by whatever number you
add next. Same exact mechanic every time: drag fields, let Power BI guess,
then pick the visual type you actually want from the Visualizations pane.

## S8 · OUTRO CARD (SVG: next lesson, LTV seal)

Data in, two visuals out, and saved with Control-S. That's a real Power BI
report, built by you. In the next lesson, we zoom back out and walk through
the entire five-step workflow this whole course is built around. See you
there.
