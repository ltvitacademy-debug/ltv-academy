## S1 · TITLE CARD (SVG: lesson title, LTV brand)

A report page should make one point clearly, not show off every chart
type you know. Here's how to keep it honest while you're at it.

## S2 · CODE: 3D charts. Truncated axes. Pie charts past 8 slices. Mismatched scales.

A handful of choices genuinely distort a comparison: 3D effects and
axes that don't start at zero both exaggerate differences. Pie charts
lose their point past a small number of slices — humans compare bar
lengths far better than angles. And mixing a measure in the millions
with one in the thousands, on the same axis, flattens the smaller one
completely.

## S3 · CODE: "3,400,000" vs "3.4 million"  ->  fewer digits, faster to read

Keep displayed numbers to three or four significant digits. And sorting
isn't just tidiness — sort by measure to highlight extremes, sort by
category to help someone find a specific item. Same chart, same data,
different story depending on which you choose.

Look again at this dashboard: consistent color, consistent scale, and
a treemap where size itself does the sorting. Every one of these is a
small, deliberate storytelling decision — not a default left untouched.
