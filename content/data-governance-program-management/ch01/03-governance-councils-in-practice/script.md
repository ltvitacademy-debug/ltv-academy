# Lesson 3 — Governance Councils in Practice · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Foundations explained why a council and committee structure exists. This lesson is about what
that looks like on a Tuesday morning — who's in the room, what's on the agenda, and how a decision
actually gets made.

## S2 · STEPS — Selecting membership deliberately

A council assembled by "whoever's available" either rubber-stamps everything or gets stuck in
constant disagreement. A working council needs enough functional range to resolve real disputes,
members with real decision authority who can commit their function, and a manageable size —
usually six to ten voting members.

## S3 · STEPS — Meeting cadence and agenda

The council — strategic layer — typically meets quarterly; the committee, monthly or every two
weeks; working groups as often as active drafting requires. A good agenda has four sections every
time: standing items, decisions needed, escalations, and new business. If there's no decision
attached, it doesn't belong on the agenda.

## S4 · STEPS — Making and recording decisions

Some councils vote formally; many run by consensus with the chair breaking ties. Either way, two
things matter: the decision gets recorded — who decided what, and when — and it's traceable back
to the charter's scope. A decision nobody wrote down gets re-argued from scratch next time.
