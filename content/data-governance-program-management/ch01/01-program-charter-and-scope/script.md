# Lesson 1 — Program Charter and Scope · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Welcome to Data Governance Program Management. Foundations covered what governance is — this
course covers how you actually launch and run a program. We start with the one document that
makes a governance effort real: the program charter.

## S2 · STEPS — What a charter actually is

A program charter is the formal document that authorizes a governance program to exist. It names
the purpose, the scope, the sponsor, and the success criteria — so when someone asks "who said we
could do this," there's a real answer. Without one, governance is just good intentions that any
reorg can quietly cancel.

## S3 · STEPS — Why scope is where programs fail

The most common governance failure isn't bad policy — it's scope that tries to cover everything at
once. A charter that says "govern all enterprise data" gives you no way to ever say you succeeded.
The fix is a phased scope: name two or three high-value domains for phase one, and write down,
explicitly, what's out of scope for now.

## S4 · STEPS — Who drafts and approves it

The executive sponsor and program lead draft the charter together — sponsor brings authority,
program lead brings detail. The governance council or committee formally approves it. A charter
nobody with real authority signed off on carries no more weight than an email.
