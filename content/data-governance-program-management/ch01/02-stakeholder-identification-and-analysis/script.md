# Lesson 2 — Stakeholder Identification and Analysis · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Last lesson's charter named a scope. That scope creates a whole group of people with something at
stake — and finding out who they are informally, by who shows up to meetings, is how programs get
blindsided by objections after a policy is already written.

## S2 · STEPS — Identifying stakeholders

A thorough pass looks in four directions: data owners and stewards who have direct authority; the
business units that consume the data even if they don't own it; IT, security, legal, and
compliance, who weigh in on feasibility and risk; and leadership — the sponsor and council — plus
any external regulators. The output is a stakeholder register.

## S3 · STEPS — The power/interest grid

Not every stakeholder needs the same attention. The power/interest grid plots each one by how much
influence they have and how much they care. High power, high interest — manage closely. High
power, low interest — keep satisfied. Low power, high interest — keep informed. Low power, low
interest — just monitor.

## S4 · STEPS — Turning it into a strategy

The grid only matters if it changes what you do. For each stakeholder, decide how often they hear
from the program, through what channel, and what you actually need from them — approval, input, or
just awareness. A VP getting a generic monthly email when they could block the program is
under-engaged; an analyst pulled into every steering meeting is over-engaged.
