# Lesson 4 — Stewardship Programs · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Foundations defined the steward role. A stewardship program is the operational machinery that
makes that role actually function — and most stalled governance programs failed here, not at the
role definition.

## S2 · STEPS — Recruiting stewards

A steward almost always already has a job and takes this on in addition. Recruitment needs three
things: the right person with deep domain knowledge, an explicit time allocation — commonly ten to
twenty percent of their week, written down — and real buy-in from their manager that it won't get
quietly absorbed by their primary job.

## S3 · STEPS — A community of practice

Stewards across domains face strikingly similar problems. A recurring peer forum — a community of
practice — shares solutions so nobody solves the same problem alone, builds consistency across
domains that would otherwise drift apart, and gives stewards a network that makes the role feel
less isolated.

## S4 · STEPS — Tracking workload

A program that never measures steward workload can't tell "stewardship is working" from "stewards
are quietly falling behind." Track open issues per steward, time to resolution, and whether the
negotiated time allocation still matches reality. When it doesn't, that's a signal to add stewards
or time — not to expect them to absorb it.

## S5 · OUTRO

Next lesson: once stewards and owners are in place, the full picture — roles, responsibilities,
and how you actually onboard people into them.
