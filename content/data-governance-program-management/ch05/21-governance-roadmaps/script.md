# Lesson 21 — Governance Roadmaps · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Data Governance Foundations covered the roadmap a brand-new program
needs to get off the ground — discovery, foundation, pilot, expand,
scale, over about a year. This lesson picks up after that: the program
is already running, so what does year two's roadmap actually look
like?

## S2 · STEPS CARD (from sequence to backlog)

A first-program roadmap is mostly linear, because foundation has to
come before pilot. A sustaining roadmap is different — it's a backlog
of competing initiatives, all wanting the same limited stewardship and
council time, re-sorted on a regular cadence instead of following one
fixed calendar.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (now / next / later)

Mature programs sort that backlog into three horizons. Now: committed
work this quarter, with a named owner and milestone. Next: likely work
for the following one to two quarters, scoped but not yet committed.
Later: real candidates, described loosely on purpose, so the roadmap
never pretends to know things it doesn't know yet.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (what forces re-sequencing)

Four things legitimately reshuffle a mature roadmap: a maturity
assessment exposing a weak dimension, a new domain or regulation
forcing urgent work to the front, a funding change that forces
re-ranking by value, and stakeholder turnover stalling work that
depended on one person's momentum.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

Keep it honest the same way Foundations taught for a first roadmap,
just at a longer time scale: revisit every horizon on a fixed cadence,
retire items the evidence no longer supports, and show what actually
moved to done last quarter — not just what's planned next. Next:
funding and business cases for a program that already exists.
