# Lesson 25 — Program Management Practice Lab

**Chapter 5 · Sustaining Governance · Lesson 25 of 25**

## What you'll learn

- How to pull every chapter of this course into one working artifact
  for a program you'd actually run
- A nine-step practice lab, one step per major course concept, that
  produces a real one-to-two-page program packet by the end
- How to tell, using your own packet, whether a program is ready to
  launch, already running, or already due for a sustaining-phase
  check
- Where the Data Governance career path goes next

## Why this lesson is a lab, not a lecture

Twenty-four lessons have each taught one piece of running a governance
program: a charter, a council, stewardship, policy, workflow,
measurement, adoption, and — in this chapter — the roadmap, funding,
and maturity discipline that keeps all of it alive past year one. The
closing lesson of this course isn't a twenty-fifth concept. It's the
exercise that makes you actually assemble the other twenty-four into
one packet you could hand to a real governance council.

Work through all nine steps below for one real or representative
domain — your own organization's, or one you've used throughout this
course. Each step is short on purpose; the discipline this course
teaches is being specific in a small space, not writing a long
document.

## The nine-step program packet

1. **Charter and scope (Chapter 1).** One sentence naming the domain
   and one sentence naming what's explicitly out of scope for now.
2. **Stakeholders and council (Chapter 1).** List three stakeholders
   who are affected by this domain's data, and name who chairs the
   council that would make cross-team decisions about it.
3. **Stewardship (Chapter 1).** Name one steward for this domain and
   one specific responsibility they'd own that nobody currently owns.
4. **One policy (Chapter 2).** Write one real rule this domain needs —
   a trusted source, a naming standard, an approval requirement — in
   one or two sentences, plus who has the decision rights to change
   it.
5. **One workflow and escalation path (Chapter 2).** Describe, in
   three steps, what happens when someone disputes a number or
   requests an exception, ending in a named escalation contact.
6. **Two KPIs (Chapter 3).** Name two measurable things tied directly
   to the original pain this program solves — not a long vanity list,
   exactly two, the way Brightfield Agritech did in Lesson 24.
7. **One adoption risk and its fix (Chapter 4).** Name the group most
   likely to resist this program and one specific communication or
   training tactic aimed at them, not at the organization generally.
8. **Roadmap horizon placement (Lesson 21).** Sort three next steps
   for this program into Now, Next, and Later.
9. **Funding and maturity snapshot (Lessons 22-23).** Write one
   sentence on how you'd defend this program's budget to whichever
   executive controls it, and name the single dimension (stewardship,
   policy, measurement, adoption, or tooling) you'd score lowest today.

## Reading your own packet

Once the nine steps are filled in, read the packet back as a whole,
not step by step:

- If steps 1-3 are solid but 4-5 are vague, the program isn't ready to
  launch yet — it needs Chapter 2's policy work before it needs
  anything from this chapter.
- If steps 1-7 are solid and step 8 is the hard one, the program is
  genuinely in the sustaining phase this chapter is about — the
  concepts from Chapters 1-4 are working, and the open question is
  sequencing and funding, not basics.
- If step 9's weakest dimension keeps showing up as the same answer
  every time you revisit the packet, that's not a one-time finding —
  it's a standing item that belongs on the roadmap every cycle until
  it moves.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Program packet | A short, concrete artifact pulling charter, stewardship, policy, measurement, adoption, and sustaining-phase decisions for one domain into a single reference |
| Launch-ready | A program whose charter, stakeholders, and stewardship are solid but whose policy and workflow are still undefined |
| Sustaining-phase | A program where the launch fundamentals already work and the open questions are roadmap sequencing, funding, and maturity |

## Lab

Complete all nine steps above for one domain, in writing, in one
sitting if possible — the goal is a single packet, not nine separate
essays. Keep every step to the length specified; a packet that fits on
two pages is more useful in an actual council meeting than one that
doesn't. When you're done, write one closing sentence: what's the
single next action you'd take this week if this were a real program.

## Check yourself

Can you produce a complete nine-step program packet for a domain you
know, without needing to re-read earlier lessons for any single step,
and explain in one sentence each why steps 1-7 belong to earlier
chapters while steps 8-9 belong specifically to this one?
