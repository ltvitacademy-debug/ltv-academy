# Lesson 22 — Funding and Business Cases · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Foundations covered the one-page business case that gets a pilot
funded. That document's whole job is to win a first yes. Once the
pilot proves itself and the program scales, that one-time ask is gone
— something else has to keep the money flowing every budget cycle
after that.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (project budget to run-rate)

Many programs start as a funded project with a start date, an end
date, and a personal champion. That works for a pilot, but it doesn't
survive year two unless the program converts to a run-rate budget — an
ongoing operating line renewed like payroll, not re-pitched from
scratch every twelve months.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (value realization report)

Defending that budget takes a value realization report, built from
the same KPIs covered in Chapter 3: value actually delivered against
the original promise, the cost of not having the program framed for
the specific audience, and what's different this cycle. Proving the
existing ask already paid off is the argument — new asks get their own
scoped case.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (cuts and champion turnover)

When a budget gets cut anyway, the roadmap's now, next, later
structure makes the conversation survivable — naming exactly what
stays, what slips, and what waits. And when a sponsor leaves, spreading
sponsorship across the council before it's tested is what keeps the
renewal from depending on one person's memory.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

A sustaining program defends the budget it already has with evidence,
not a fresh pitch — and protects itself from both a bad quarter and a
departed champion by spreading the case and the sponsorship across
more than one person. Next: assessing how mature the program actually
is.
