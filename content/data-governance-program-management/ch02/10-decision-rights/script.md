# Lesson 10 — Decision Rights · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

RACI is built for recurring activities. Some governance moments are different — genuinely
contested, one-off decisions that need absolute clarity on who has the final say, named before
the disagreement happens.

## S2 · STEPS — Two real frameworks

DACI — Driver, Approver, Contributors, Informed — is lightweight, popularized by Atlassian, for
everyday decisions. RAPID — Recommend, Agree, Perform, Input, Decide — from Bain and Company, is
heavier, for bigger stakes. Both isolate one thing: exactly one person holds final say.

## S3 · STEPS — Where this actually matters

A policy exception needs a named Approver so it's not a fresh negotiation every time. A
cross-domain dispute lands with the committee, but decision rights say who inside the committee
breaks the tie. And when there's more work than capacity, someone has to Decide what gets
attention first — with input, but without a veto from everyone else.

## S4 · STEPS — Why naming it up front helps

It feels more democratic to leave "who decides" vague until the moment arrives. In practice,
that's when it turns personal — the fight becomes about who gets to decide, on top of the actual
question. Naming it in advance keeps the real disagreement in focus.

## S5 · OUTRO

That closes chapter two — policies, standards, workflows, issues, and decision rights. Chapter
three turns to measuring whether all of this is actually working: governance KPIs.
