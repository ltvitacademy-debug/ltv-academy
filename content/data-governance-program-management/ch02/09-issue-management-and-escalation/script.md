# Lesson 9 — Issue Management and Escalation · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

At any moment, a governance program has multiple issues open at once. An issue log is the single
record of all of them — without one, "how are we actually doing" can only be answered by asking
around.

## S2 · STEPS — The issue log and severity

An issue log tracks ID, description, domain, severity, status, and dates. Severity only matters if
it changes behavior: critical gets same-day attention, high gets days not weeks, medium follows
the normal backlog, low gets addressed when there's capacity. If critical and medium get the same
response time, the label is decorative.

## S3 · STEPS — The escalation ladder

Escalation follows Chapter one's roles in order. Steward resolves what's within their domain. The
data owner steps in when it crosses what the steward alone can decide. The governance committee
handles cross-domain disputes. Sponsor or council is the last resort for organization-wide stakes.

## S4 · STEPS — Why escalating everything fails

An escalation path only works if it's used selectively. A program that pushes every minor issue to
the sponsor trains the organization to ignore escalations — the real signal gets buried in noise.
Escalation is for issues genuinely stuck at the current level, not a way to avoid owning a
decision.

## S5 · OUTRO

Next lesson closes the chapter: once an issue does land somewhere, who actually has the final say
— decision rights.
