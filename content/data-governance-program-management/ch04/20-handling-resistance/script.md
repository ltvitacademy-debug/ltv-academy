# Lesson 20 — Handling Resistance · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

This lesson closes chapter four: what to do with the resistance that shows up no matter how well you've communicated and trained.

## S2 · STEPS — Resistance is normal, not a failure

Even a rollout with a good stakeholder map, a thoughtful communication plan, and solid training will still run into resistance. That's not a sign something went wrong upstream — some portion of any group sits in "aware but resistant" for a while no matter what.

## S3 · STEPS — What's actually behind it

"They just don't get it" is the laziest explanation, and it's rarely true. More often: extra work with no visible benefit to them, a past bad experience with a different initiative, a genuine gap in the new process, or the loss of informal control they used to have.

## S4 · STEPS — Working through it instead of overriding it

Ask before assuming — a direct conversation surfaces the real cause faster than guessing. Separate the legitimate complaint from the delivery. Fix what's actually fixable through the governance workflow. And use a visible early adopter instead of another mandate where you can.

## S5 · STEPS — When escalation is the right call

Working through resistance has limits. If a real process gap's been fixed and communication and training were adequate, and someone still won't comply, that's the point to escalate through the council's decision rights — not keep negotiating indefinitely. The judgment call is telling "won't" from "can't yet."

## S6 · OUTRO

That closes chapter four. Chapter five moves into sustaining governance long-term — starting with governance roadmaps, the plan for where the program goes from here.
