# Lesson 19 — Training and Data Literacy · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

This lesson is about the gap communication alone can't close: knowing a process matters versus actually knowing how to do it.

## S2 · STEPS — Awareness isn't the same as ability

Someone can fully agree a new workflow matters and still not know how to submit a request through it. Training closes that specific gap — not convincing people the process matters, but teaching them exactly how to do it.

## S3 · STEPS — Process training vs. data literacy

Process training is narrow — how to use this specific tool, this specific workflow. Data literacy is broader and more durable — understanding what a data domain is, how to read a scorecard. A program that skips data literacy re-teaches the same basics from scratch every rollout.

## S4 · STEPS — Designing training people retain

A single one-hour session delivered once is one of the weakest ways to produce lasting change. Better: short task-specific materials available on demand, hands-on practice instead of a lecture, built into onboarding, and reinforced right at the moment someone needs it.

## S5 · STEPS — Why data literacy pays off beyond compliance

Baseline data literacy is useful to people whether or not they ever touch the formal governance process directly. That's part of why it's easier to justify to a skeptical stakeholder than process training alone — it's a durable capability, not just compliance overhead.
