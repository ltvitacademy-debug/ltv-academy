# Lesson 16 — Governance Adoption and Change Management · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Welcome to chapter four. We've measured governance — now we tackle the harder problem: getting people to actually change what they do.

## S2 · STEPS — A change problem wearing a technology costume

A program can have a perfect operating model, a well-written policy, and an accurate dashboard, and still fail — because adoption isn't a property of the program design, it's a property of whether people change their daily habits. That makes governance fundamentally a change-management problem.

## S3 · STEPS — The stages people move through

Most change arcs describe some version of the same four stages: unaware the process exists, aware but resistant, compliant because it's mandatory, and engaged — where people have actually internalized why it matters. Most programs get stuck between resistant and compliant.

## S4 · STEPS — Why mandate alone isn't enough

A council can mandate a process. It can't mandate belief, and belief is what separates compliant from engaged. Mandate alone produces exactly enough behavior to avoid consequences, performed under observation, and abandoned the moment attention moves elsewhere.

## S5 · STEPS — Where this chapter goes

Four lessons ahead: identifying the stakeholders actually affected, building a communication plan that really reaches people, training so people have the skills to comply, and handling the resistance that shows up no matter how well you do the first three.

## S6 · OUTRO

Next lesson: stakeholder management — figuring out exactly who's affected by a governance change before you try to change their behavior.
