# Lesson 13 — Governance Dashboards · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

This lesson is about where the KPIs from the last two lessons actually get seen — governance dashboards, with a real example from Power BI.

## S2 · STEPS — What a governance dashboard needs to show

A dashboard turns the metric set into something a steering committee can read in under a minute. It needs to answer four questions: is the program on track overall, where specifically is it off, is it trending the right direction, and who owns each item that needs attention.

## S3 · SCREENSHOT — Scorecards hub

Here's Power BI's own Scorecards hub — not built for governance specifically, but the shape is exactly right: value over target, a trend sparkline, and a status pill for each tracked item, with a list of named scorecards underneath.

## S4 · SCREENSHOT — One goal's fields

Opening a single goal shows the fields underneath: owners, current value, final target, status, and dates. These are exactly the fields a governance KPI needs too.

## S5 · SCREENSHOT — Organizing many metrics

And here's a list of multiple scorecards, each with a clear owner column — the piece that turns a red status into an actual next step instead of a dead end.

## S6 · STEPS — Design habits that keep it useful

Keep the headline level small — five to ten metrics, not every KPI you track. Match your status thresholds to what the escalation process actually uses. And never ship a metric without an owner attached.
