# Lesson 14 — Measuring Governance Success · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

This lesson pulls the last three lessons together into one question: how do you actually know if a governance program is succeeding?

## S2 · STEPS — Success means something different to each audience

A steward calls it success when data quality improves. A business sponsor calls it success when governance stops being a blocker. An executive calls it success when they can trust the numbers without double-checking. None of these views is wrong, and none alone is complete.

## S3 · STEPS — Three honest questions

Turning your metrics into a verdict means asking three things on a regular cadence: did the headline metrics actually trend up since baseline, did adoption increase and not just quality, and are issues actually getting resolved faster — not just logged faster.

## S4 · STEPS — Compliance vs. outcomes

It's much easier to measure compliance — did people do the thing — than outcomes — did it actually help. A 95% policy acknowledgment rate tells you people clicked "I agree." It tells you nothing about whether behavior actually changed.

## S5 · STEPS — Hitting targets isn't the same as succeeding

A program can hit every target and still be failing, if the targets were set too low or a team learned to game a metric. And a program can miss early targets while still being on a legitimate path — real change takes longer than one quarter to show up.

Next lesson: reporting to executives — taking this verdict and presenting it to the audience that has the least patience for instrumentation and the most power to fund or cut the program.
