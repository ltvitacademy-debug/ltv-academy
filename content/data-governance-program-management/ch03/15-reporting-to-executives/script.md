# Lesson 15 — Reporting to Executives · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

This lesson closes out chapter three: taking everything you've measured and reporting it to the audience that decides whether the program keeps getting funded.

## S2 · STEPS — Why executives need a different report

The dashboard from lesson thirteen is built for someone who wants to explore. An executive update is the opposite — a fixed, short artifact that answers "should I still be funding this" without making the reader click anything. A link to a live dashboard is not a report.

## S3 · STEPS — A structure that works

Four parts, in order: the one-line verdict — on track, at risk, or behind — stated first. The two or three numbers that justify it, with trend. What changed since the last update. And what you need from this group — or nothing, if that's genuinely true.

## S4 · STEPS — Presenting bad news without losing the room

State the honest verdict first. Pair it immediately with why — a staffing gap, a slipped dependency. Bring a specific ask, not just a complaint. And don't over-promise a recovery date you're not confident in.

## S5 · STEPS — Cadence matters as much as content

An update that arrives irregularly trains its audience to stop expecting it. A predictable cadence — monthly for a steering committee, quarterly for a board — keeps governance visible as a standing item instead of something only noticed during a crisis.

## S6 · OUTRO

Chapter four is next: adoption and change management — because measuring the program is only half the job. Getting the organization to actually change its behavior is the other half.
