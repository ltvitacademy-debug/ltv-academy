# Script — Capstone: Wrap-Up & Portfolio Presentation

## Segment 1 (title)

A working dashboard with no story around it is just a screenshot. The same dashboard with a clear write-up and a two-minute explanation of your decisions is a portfolio piece an interviewer remembers. This lesson is about packaging the work you just built.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Keep the write-up to about one page. What the service does, in a sentence or two — the interviewer doesn't need the full product spec. What you instrumented and why, tied to its golden signals. One real decision you made and its trade-off — that's the part that actually shows judgment, not just checklist-following. And an honest note on what you'd still add, which is a sign of maturity, not a weakness to hide.

## Segment 3 (code)

A simple block diagram does more work than three paragraphs. Your service, its one dependency, where metrics, logs, and traces flow, and where the dashboard and alerts sit. Lead with this picture in an interview, then explain the boxes.

## Segment 4 (steps)

When you present it, structure your answer like a postmortem: what it does, what you measured and why, one trade-off, what's next. Expect follow-ups — how you picked that threshold, what happens if an alert fires as a false positive, what you'd change if this had to handle ten times the traffic. You don't need perfect answers, just reasoning that shows you understand the trade-offs the same way you learned to analyze them throughout the course.

## Segment 5 (outro)

This capstone pulled together golden signals, the tools, the logging and tracing, and the incident discipline from every chapter before it — applied to something you built yourself, not a script you followed or a replay of Northbridge's incident. That's the whole course, start to finish. Well done.
