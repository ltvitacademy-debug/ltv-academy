# Script — Customer/Service Analytics

## Segment 1 (title)

Chapter 6 was about revenue coming in. Chapter 7 covers what happens after the customer signs, starting with service. Let's frame the questions and map them to native reports.

## Segment 2 (steps: four service questions)

Service leaders ask four kinds of question. Volume: how many cases arrive. Backlog: how many are open right now. Speed: how long they take. And customer impact: who is affected most. All four are measures on the Case object. The next lesson builds each of them as a real report.

## Segment 3 (code: the customer lens)

Queues are an internal view, but accounts are how the business thinks about customers. Group cases by account name, then filter to high priority or escalated cases to find customers under strain. Compare counts with customer size before drawing conclusions. A large customer with many cases can be healthier than a small one with a few escalations.

## Segment 4 (steps: metrics that need setup)

Some popular metrics don't exist out of the box. Satisfaction scores come from surveys or custom fields. Response time and SLA compliance need entitlements and milestones or custom timestamps. First contact resolution usually needs a field someone maintains. Before you promise one of these to leadership, check whether the data is captured at all. If it isn't, that's a data model conversation, not a reporting trick.

## Segment 5 (steps: keep numbers fair)

Keep the numbers fair. Use an explicit date range and say whether you count cases opened or closed. Exclude test or spam cases, separate case types, and watch for reopened or merged cases inflating volume. These small choices decide whether people trust the dashboard.
