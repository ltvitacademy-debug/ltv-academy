# Script — ROI Reporting

## Segment 1 (title)

Every marketing leader eventually asks: what did we get for that money? Let's turn campaign data into return on investment, and be clear about where native attribution runs out.

## Segment 2 (screenshot: campaigns with opportunities and revenue)

This is the standard Campaigns with Opportunities and Revenue report from Trailhead. Each opportunity is listed under the campaign that sourced it, with its amount and a total at the top. It's the raw material for ROI. Also check that actual cost was entered. A blank cost means ROI is undefined, not zero.

## Segment 3 (code: ROI formula)

ROI is value won minus actual cost, divided by actual cost. Example: a webinar costing ten thousand dollars, linked to forty-five thousand dollars of won deals, has an ROI of three point five, or three hundred fifty percent. Use a summary formula on the Campaigns report type, and show cost and revenue beside it. Note that value won is revenue, not profit, so this is closer to return on spend.

## Segment 4 (steps: efficiency and lag)

Also report cost per lead and cost per won deal, since cheap leads that never close can be worse than expensive ones that do. And allow for time lag. Campaigns newer than your sales cycle will understate revenue, so show them as pipeline instead. Compare campaigns older than a full sales cycle, using the cycle length from lesson 27.

## Segment 5 (steps: where attribution ends)

Native credit is single-source, one campaign per opportunity. Campaign Influence, depending on edition and setup, can split credit across touches, but you must know which model is on. Anything outside Salesforce is invisible. Treat ROI as a comparable estimate, not a full accounting. Say so plainly to stakeholders, and you'll keep their trust.
