# Script — Mobile Dashboards

## Segment 1 (title)

Sales leaders check dashboards in taxis and hallways, mostly on phones. The Salesforce mobile app can show your Lightning dashboards, but a small screen changes what makes them readable.

## Segment 2 (steps: what changes)

On a desktop you get a wide grid. On a phone, widgets are generally shown in a single scrolling column, following your reading order. So order becomes the story. Wide charts get narrower, tables lose width, and tapping a widget generally opens it larger, with a path to the source report. The exact experience varies by release and configuration, so confirm on your own device.

## Segment 3 (code: reading order)

Here's the idea in a sketch. The desktop has row one, filters. Row two, metrics. Row three, funnel beside a trend. Row four, table. On mobile that becomes one column: filters, then each metric, then funnel, then trend, then table. Whatever is first in reading order arrives first on a phone.

## Segment 4 (steps: design habits)

You don't need a separate dashboard. Lead with metrics, the most phone-friendly widget there is. Limit categories to a handful. Prefer bars, donuts, and funnels over scatter charts and dense lines. And write short titles that fit on one line.

## Segment 5 (code: test checklist)

Then test as a viewer, on a real phone, with an account that has your audience's access. Does the headline show without scrolling? Is a tapped chart legible? Is the as-of time clear? If not, fix it on the desktop, which is where building and editing generally belong.

## Segment 6 (outro)

That wraps up dashboards. Next chapter turns to sales analytics, starting with pipeline analysis.
