# Script — Scheduling & Subscriptions

## Segment 1 (title)

A dashboard nobody opens does not help anyone. Scheduling and subscriptions bring your work to the people who need it, instead of waiting for them to visit.

## Segment 2 (screenshot: refresh and subscribe)

Look at the header of this real Lightning dashboard. The as-of timestamp says when the data was last refreshed, because a dashboard does not update itself in real time. Beside it are Refresh, Edit, and Subscribe. An old timestamp is your cue to refresh, but manual refresh does not scale to a whole team.

## Segment 3 (steps: subscribing)

Click Subscribe, and a small dialog opens. In most orgs you choose a frequency, such as daily, weekly, or monthly. Then the days and time, for example weekdays at seven a.m. Then the recipients: yourself, and, if your permissions allow, other users or groups. Salesforce refreshes the dashboard and emails a snapshot.

## Segment 4 (code: conditional report subscription)

Reports can be subscribed to as well, often with a condition. Send the email only when total amount is above a threshold, or when the record count is above zero. That's light alerting. A stalled-deals report that arrives only when there is a stalled deal beats one that reads zero records every Monday.

## Segment 5 (steps: limits)

Check these in your own org. Editions limit the number of scheduled refreshes and subscriptions, so plan for a handful. Avoid scheduling many at the same moment. And remember subscribers see what the running user sees, and dynamic dashboards generally can't be scheduled.

## Segment 6 (outro)

Automation only helps if what arrives is worth opening, so keep each scheduled dashboard focused on one audience and one decision. Next lesson: layout best practices, so what you deliver is worth opening.
