# Script — Capstone: Wrap-Up & Portfolio Presentation

## Segment 1 (title)

You've built two dashboards. The last step is the one many analysts skip: turning them into a story a VP can act on, and into a portfolio piece that shows your thinking, not just your clicks.

## Segment 2 (code: one-page result)

Start with the one-page result. Sales: 3.9 million won, 65 percent of quota, 4.2 million open pipeline at 2.0 times coverage, 31 percent win rate. Service: 1,840 opened, 1,725 closed, backlog up 115 to 412, 68 percent within target. All illustrative, but the pattern is real: a handful of numbers with a verdict.

## Segment 3 (screenshot: finished dashboard)

Before presenting, look at what viewers see. The dashboard header shows when data was last refreshed and who it's viewed as. Be ready to explain both, because a VP's first question is usually whether the numbers are current.

## Segment 4 (steps: presentation)

Structure ten minutes in five parts. Headline: the one-sentence verdict. Evidence: two or three components. Risk: what could go wrong. Ask: what you need the VP to decide. Next: what you'll do this week.

## Segment 5 (code: talking points)

Here's how sales sounds. We're at 65 percent of quota with 4.2 million open, 2.0 times coverage. At the 31 percent win rate, a rough count-based rule of thumb, that converts to about 1.3 million, landing near 5.2 million, roughly 0.8 million short. The ask: 23 stalled deals worth 1.1 million need contact this week. Service is similar: the backlog grew 115 and 38 high-priority cases are over five days old, so the ask is coverage for them. Notice every sentence carries a number, a check, and an action.

## Segment 6 (steps: portfolio)

Your portfolio write-up mirrors it: the problem and audience, the data and report types, key design decisions, results with screenshots of your own dashboards, and limits with next steps. Use developer org or sample data, never confidential employer data, and blur or replace any real names.

## Segment 7 (code: limits)

Finish with honesty. The quota is hard-coded, there's no daily backlog trend, and ERP revenue can't blend in. By Chapter 8's framework, those are evidence for exploring CRM Analytics or Tableau, while the native dashboards fully answer Monday's questions.

## Segment 8 (outro)

Congratulations, you've finished Salesforce Reports and Dashboards. Next up: Salesforce CRM Analytics and Tableau Next.
