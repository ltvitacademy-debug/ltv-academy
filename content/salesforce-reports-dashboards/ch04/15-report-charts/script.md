# Script — Report Charts

## Segment 1 (title)

Grouping and formulas gave your report real answers. A report chart turns those grouped numbers into a picture that sits right on the report. It's also the building block that dashboards reuse in the next chapter.

## Segment 2 (screenshot: disabled)

Look at this real tabular report, Direct Customer Accounts. In the header, the Add Chart button is greyed out. A plain list of rows has nothing to aggregate, so there is nothing to plot. The everyday rule is no groupings, no chart.

## Segment 3 (screenshot: enabled)

Now a grouped summary report, Leads by Lead Source. Here Add Chart is enabled. The groupings supply the categories, and the summarized values, even just the record count, supply the measure. That's why analysts build the grouped report first and the chart second. A matrix report, with two groupings, gives you the most flexibility.

## Segment 4 (steps)

To add one, click Add Chart, choose a type, and open the chart properties. The X-axis or slices come from a grouping. The Y-axis or slice size comes from a summarized value. A second grouping, as in a matrix, unlocks stacked and grouped series. Then save, because the chart is stored with the report.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Remember the limits. A report holds one chart. Too many categories make a chart unreadable, so filter or sort to the top values. And native styling is modest compared with Tableau. Later, a dashboard component can reuse the chart you save here through the Use chart settings from report option. Next up: the chart types Salesforce offers.
