# Lesson 4 — AI Governance Roles · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Someone has to be accountable for each of the risks from the last lesson. This lesson covers the roles that actually do that work.

## S2 · STEPS — Roles that extend familiar ones

If you know data governance roles already, these are close relatives, one layer up. Model owner — business-side accountability for a model's outcomes. Model builder — the data scientist or ML engineer who develops it. Independent validator — reviews it before it's trusted with real decisions.

## S3 · STEPS — The council and legal

An AI governance council — often legal, compliance, risk, and senior business leaders — approves new use cases and reviews incidents, the same role an existing data governance council already plays. Legal and compliance flag applicable rules before deployment, not after.

## S4 · STEPS — Why independence matters

Because a model's logic isn't written down as readable rules, the people who built it share the same blind spots that shaped the model in the first place. An independent validator exists not because builders are untrustworthy, but because nobody can fully audit their own work on something this opaque.

## S5 · STEPS — Where accountability sits

A simple rule of thumb. The model owner owns the business outcome. The model builder owns technical quality. The council owns whether the use case should have been approved at all. Leaving any of the three unassigned is one of the most common gaps found in an audit.
