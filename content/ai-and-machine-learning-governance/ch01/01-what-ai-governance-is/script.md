## S1 · TITLE

Welcome to AI and Machine Learning Governance. This first lesson answers the question everyone assumes they already know the answer to: what actually is AI governance?

## S2 · STEPS — A working definition

AI governance is the system of decision rights, accountability, and controls over how an organization sources data for AI, builds and validates models, and takes responsibility for the decisions those models make. It's the same core idea as data governance — agreements, not just tools — applied to systems that learn patterns and act on them automatically, at scale.

## S3 · STEPS — Three things it covers

It breaks into three buckets. The data going in — what trained the model and whether that was allowed. The model itself — how it was built, tested, and approved. And the decisions coming out — what it's trusted to decide on its own, and what happens when it's wrong.

## S4 · STEPS — Not a brand-new discipline

If your organization already runs data governance — owners, stewards, a review council — you're not starting from zero. AI governance extends that same structure to cover two things data governance alone doesn't fully reach: a trained model's behavior, and decisions made continuously without a human reviewing each one.

Chapter one stays conceptual. Chapter two covers governing the data that trains models. Chapter three covers governing models themselves. Chapter four covers security and production monitoring. Chapter five covers responsible AI and named frameworks. Chapter six applies it all to real case studies.
