# Lesson 1 — What IT Service Management Actually Is

**Chapter 1 · IT Service Management Foundations · Lesson 1 of 28**

## What you'll learn

- A working definition of IT Service Management (ITSM) that goes beyond "the help desk"
- Why ITSM is a management discipline, not a piece of software or a single team
- The real distinction between "the technology" and "a service"
- How a reactive, firefighting IT operation differs from a managed, service-oriented one
- Where ITIL fits as the best-known framework for practicing ITSM

## ITSM is a discipline, not a department

IT Service Management is the set of policies, roles, processes, and practices an
organization uses to plan, deliver, support, and continually improve the IT services
it provides — to employees, customers, or both. It is not the help desk, not a ticketing
tool, and not any single team. The help desk and the ticketing tool are *implementations*
of ITSM thinking; ITSM itself is the discipline that decides what those teams and tools
should actually be doing and why. An organization can own every enterprise ITSM tool on
the market and still have no real ITSM, if nobody has defined what a service is, who owns
it, or what "working" means for it.

## The shift from firefighting to managing

Before ITSM takes hold, IT support in most organizations looks the same: something
breaks, someone notices, someone technical fixes it, and everyone moves on until the
next thing breaks. There's no record of how often it breaks, no agreed time limit for
fixing it, and no plan beyond "call whoever knows that system." This reactive model
scales only as far as institutional memory does — the moment the one person who knows
the fix goes on vacation or leaves the company, the organization is exposed.

ITSM replaces that with a *managed* model: IT capability is organized around defined
services with known owners, agreed performance targets, documented procedures for
common requests and failures, and a feedback loop that uses what went wrong last time
to prevent it next time. The work doesn't disappear — incidents still happen — but it
stops depending entirely on memory and heroics.

```
Reactive IT:                         Managed IT (ITSM):
- fix it when someone notices        - monitored against defined targets
- tribal knowledge, undocumented     - documented procedures, owned by a role
- no record of recurrence            - incidents and problems tracked over time
- "whoever's free handles it"        - defined escalation path and ownership
```

## A service is not the same thing as the technology

This is the distinction that trips people up most: the email server is technology; "the
corporate email service" is a service. The technology is hardware, software, and
configuration. The service is everything wrapped around that technology that lets a
user actually get value from it without having to own the risk or cost of running it
themselves — an agreed level of availability, a support path when something goes wrong,
onboarding when someone joins the company, and a security policy that protects them
without them having to think about it.

ITIL 4 formalizes this: a **service** is a means of enabling value co-creation by
facilitating outcomes that customers want to achieve, without the customer having to
manage specific costs and risks. Notice what that definition does *not* mention —
servers, software versions, or network diagrams. It's defined entirely by the outcome
the customer gets and the costs and risks they're relieved of. Two organizations can run
identical technology and have very different services, depending on what support,
availability, and risk-transfer is actually built around it.

## Where ITIL fits

ITSM is the broad discipline; ITIL is the most widely adopted *framework* for actually
practicing it — a published, structured body of guidance (currently in its fourth major
version, ITIL 4) that organizations adopt, adapt, and certify their staff against. ITIL
isn't the only ITSM framework, but it's the one this course — and most ITSM job
postings — will assume as the shared vocabulary going forward.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| IT Service Management (ITSM) | The discipline of planning, delivering, supporting, and improving IT services as a managed practice, not ad hoc firefighting |
| Service | A means of enabling value co-creation by facilitating outcomes the customer wants, without the customer managing specific costs and risks |
| Reactive support | Fixing problems only as they're noticed, with no defined targets, ownership, or recurrence tracking |
| Service owner | The named role accountable for a specific service's performance and improvement |
| ITIL | The most widely adopted published framework for practicing ITSM |
