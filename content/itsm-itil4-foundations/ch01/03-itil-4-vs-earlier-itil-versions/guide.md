# Lesson 3 — ITIL 4 vs. Earlier ITIL Versions

**Chapter 1 · IT Service Management Foundations · Lesson 3 of 28**

## What you'll learn

- How ITIL evolved from ITIL v2's process books to ITIL v3's Service Lifecycle to ITIL 4
- Why ITIL v3 organized itself around five lifecycle stages, and what limited that model
- Why ITIL 4 replaced "processes" with 34 "practices," and why that word choice matters
- What the four dimensions model adds that a pure process view didn't capture
- Why ITIL 4 explicitly folds in Agile, DevOps, and Lean thinking that earlier versions didn't address

## From process books to a lifecycle

ITIL's early versions (ITIL v1 in the late 1980s and early 1990s, then ITIL v2 around
2000) were published as a set of separate books, each covering a cluster of processes —
most famously Service Support and Service Delivery. ITIL v3, released in 2007 and
refreshed in 2011, reorganized that guidance around a **Service Lifecycle**: five
stages — Service Strategy, Service Design, Service Transition, Service Operation, and
Continual Service Improvement (CSI) — each containing a defined set of **processes**
(26 in total across the lifecycle). This was a real improvement: it gave ITIL a
structure that mapped to how a service actually moves from idea to retirement, not just
a loose pile of best practices.

## Where the v3 lifecycle model ran into limits

The v3 lifecycle was still fundamentally rigid: it modeled IT service delivery as a
largely linear, sequential flow — strategize, then design, then transition into
production, then operate, then look back and improve — with processes as the primary
unit of work. By the time ITIL 4 was being developed, IT organizations were routinely
practicing Agile and DevOps: continuous delivery, cross-functional teams, and rapid
iteration that didn't map cleanly onto "finish design before you transition." ITIL v3's
vocabulary and structure had no real vocabulary for that — it treated "process" as
the whole unit of capability, when in practice a team's ability to actually deliver a
service also depends on skills, organizational structure, information, and
partnerships, not just the steps in a workflow.

## What ITIL 4 (2019) actually changed

ITIL 4 replaced the five-stage lifecycle with the broader **Service Value System
(SVS)** — covered in the next lesson — and replaced the word "process" with
**practice**. That's not a cosmetic rename: a practice is defined as a set of
organizational resources designed for performing work or accomplishing an objective,
which deliberately includes the people, skills, and tools involved, not just the
sequence of steps. ITIL 4 defines 34 practices grouped into three categories — general
management, service management, and technical management — replacing v3's 26
processes with a broader and more flexible set.

ITIL 4 also introduced the **four dimensions model**, which requires considering any
practice or service from four angles at once: organizations and people, information
and technology, partners and suppliers, and value streams and processes. A process
view alone can tell you the steps; the four dimensions model forces you to also ask who
does it, what information and tools they need, who else is involved outside the
organization, and how the work actually flows end to end.

Critically, ITIL 4 explicitly incorporates **Agile, DevOps, and Lean** thinking into its
guidance — something ITIL v3 never did, because those practices weren't yet standard
when v3 was written. ITIL 4 treats continuous, iterative delivery as a normal way of
working, not an exception to a sequential lifecycle.

```
ITIL v3 (2007/2011):                    ITIL 4 (2019):
Service Lifecycle — 5 linear stages     Service Value System (SVS)
26 "processes"                          34 "practices" (3 categories)
No formal four dimensions model         Four dimensions model
No explicit Agile/DevOps/Lean guidance  Explicitly incorporates Agile/DevOps/Lean
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Service Lifecycle | ITIL v3's five-stage model: Strategy, Design, Transition, Operation, CSI |
| Process (ITIL v3 term) | A defined sequence of activities producing a specific outcome |
| Practice (ITIL 4 term) | A set of organizational resources — people, skills, information, tools — designed for performing work, replacing "process" as the unit of capability |
| Four dimensions model | ITIL 4's requirement to view any practice through organizations/people, information/technology, partners/suppliers, and value streams/processes |
