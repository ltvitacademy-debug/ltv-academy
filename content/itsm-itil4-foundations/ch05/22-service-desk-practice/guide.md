## The purpose: capture demand, be the single point of contact

ITIL 4 defines the purpose of the service desk practice as capturing
demand for incident resolution and service requests. It also
describes the service desk as the entry point and single point of
contact for the service provider with all of its users. That second
part is doing a lot of work: the service desk isn't defined by having
a phone queue, or a ticketing tool, or an office with a help counter —
it's defined by being the one place users go, regardless of how they
reach it. Everything else in this chapter — service requests,
knowledge articles, SLA performance, incidents from chapter four —
flows through this single point of contact in one direction or
another.

## Not one channel — many, by design

ITIL 4 deliberately broadens the service desk beyond the image of a
phone bank. A modern service desk may take contact through a
self-service portal where users submit a ticket or pick from the
catalog, live chat, email, a dedicated phone line, walk-up support,
and increasingly, AI-driven chatbots that triage a request before a
human ever sees it. None of these channels is "the real" service
desk; they're all valid front doors to the same single point of
contact. What matters is that wherever a user starts, their request
or incident ends up logged, categorized, and routed the same way,
with nothing falling through the cracks between channels.

## The value is empathy and triage, not the ticket count

ITIL 4 is explicit that a service desk's value isn't really about how
many tickets it closes per hour. It's about empathy — genuinely
understanding how disruptive a problem is for the person reporting it
— paired with clear communication and fast, accurate triage: is this
actually an incident or a service request, how urgent is it, and who
is the right team to route it to. A service desk agent who listens
well, sets an honest expectation about timing, and routes correctly
the first time is doing the job well, even if the resolution itself
happens somewhere else entirely. A technically correct answer
delivered with no empathy, or a slow and confusing handoff, undermines
trust in the whole IT organization regardless of how good the
underlying fix turns out to be.

## The hub this chapter's practices run through

The service desk is where incident and service request intake
actually happens day to day — it's the first classification point
that decides which of chapter four's or this chapter's practices picks
up the work. It's also a major source of the recurring questions that
feed knowledge management, and a place where SLA breaches first
become visible to a frustrated user before they show up in a report.
Every practice covered so far connects back through this one point of
contact.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Service desk | The entry point and single point of contact between the service provider and its users |
| Single point of contact | The principle that users have one place to go, regardless of which channel they use to get there |
| Triage | Quickly and accurately sorting an incoming contact by type and urgency, and routing it correctly |
| Omnichannel support | Offering multiple contact channels (portal, chat, email, phone, chatbot) that all feed the same intake process |
