# Lesson 12 — Obtain/Build, Deliver & Support Activities

## Obtain/build: getting the components, built or bought

The Obtain/build activity's purpose is to ensure that service components are available when and where needed, and that they meet agreed specifications. "Components" is broad on purpose: a software license, a cloud compute resource, a piece of custom code, a vendor-supported hardware appliance, a third-party integration. For every component, the organization makes an explicit or implicit make-or-buy decision — develop it in-house, or acquire it from a supplier — and Obtain/build is where that decision gets executed against whatever Design & transition specified.

This activity is where Engage's supplier-facing relationship work becomes concrete: a contract negotiated with a vendor, a service level agreed with a supplier, an escalation path if a component doesn't meet spec. Obtain/build doesn't decide strategy — that's Plan's job — but it's accountable for the components actually showing up, on time, meeting the agreed specification, whichever source they came from.

## Deliver & support: the day-to-day front line

The Deliver & support activity's purpose is to ensure services are delivered and supported according to agreed specifications and stakeholder expectations. This is the activity most people associate with "IT" without ever learning its name: the service desk taking a call, an incident getting logged and resolved, a routine access request getting fulfilled. It's continuous, operational, and user-facing in a way the other five activities mostly aren't.

Several of the practices you'll study in Chapters 4 and 5 live mainly inside Deliver & support: **Service desk**, **Incident management**, **Service request management**, and **Service level management**, which defines what "according to agreed specifications" actually means in measurable terms for a given service. This lesson introduces where those practices sit in the bigger value chain picture; the chapters ahead cover each one in full.

## How Obtain/build and Deliver & support feed each other

It's tempting to picture a one-way flow: Obtain/build sources a component, hands it to Deliver & support, done. In practice the relationship runs both directions constantly. Deliver & support, running day after day, is often the first to notice a component isn't holding up — a supplier's response time slipping, a license limit being hit, hardware approaching end of support. That observation becomes new demand on Obtain/build (renegotiate, re-source, replace), without necessarily routing through a fresh Design & transition cycle first.

## Why this distinction matters for the exam and for real work

ITIL 4 Foundation exam questions frequently test whether you can tell Obtain/build and Deliver & support apart from Design & transition: Design & transition is about getting something *ready*; Obtain/build is about *sourcing the pieces* to agreed spec; Deliver & support is about *running it*, continuously, once it's live. All three can be touching the same service at the same time, for different reasons.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Obtain/build | The SVC activity ensuring service components are available, when and where needed, to agreed specification |
| Make-or-buy decision | Whether a component is developed in-house or acquired from a supplier |
| Deliver & support | The SVC activity ensuring services are delivered and supported day to day, per agreed specifications |
| Service level management | The practice defining what "agreed specifications" means in measurable terms (full coverage in Chapter 5) |
| Service desk | The practice usually acting as the visible entry point for Deliver & support (full coverage in Chapter 5) |
