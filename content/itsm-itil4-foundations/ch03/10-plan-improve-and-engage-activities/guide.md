## The Service Value Chain: six activities, not six steps

Lesson 4 introduced the Service Value System as the whole picture: governance, guiding principles, practices, continual improvement, and the Service Value Chain (SVC) that turns demand into value. The SVC is the operating model inside that system — six activities that every service provider performs, regardless of size or industry: **Plan**, **Improve**, **Engage**, **Design & transition**, **Obtain/build**, and **Deliver & support**.

The easiest mistake to make here is picturing them as stations on an assembly line — Plan happens, then Engage, then Design & transition, and so on, in that order. ITIL 4 is explicit that this is wrong. All six activities are active, in some form, in every organization at every moment. They're connected by flows of inputs and outputs, not by a sequence number: Engage might hand a requirement straight to Design & transition without Plan being involved at all, if the work is small and routine. This lesson covers the three activities most concerned with direction and relationships — Plan, Improve, and Engage. Lesson 11 covers Design & transition, Lesson 12 covers Obtain/build and Deliver & support, and Lesson 13 walks a real request through all six.

## Plan: shared understanding and direction

The Plan activity's purpose is to ensure a shared understanding of the vision, current status, and improvement direction for all four dimensions of service management (organizations and people, information and technology, partners and suppliers, and value streams and processes) and for all products and services across the organization.

In practice, Plan is where strategic, tactical, and operational plans get made: portfolio decisions about which services to invest in, architectural direction, policy, and the backlog priorities that other activities draw from. Plan doesn't invent what the organization should do out of nowhere — it depends on Engage to surface what stakeholders actually need, and on Improve to surface where current plans and products are falling short. What Plan produces — direction, priority, architecture — becomes an input other activities consult constantly, not a document written once and filed away.

## Improve: continual improvement, not a project

The Improve activity's purpose is to ensure the continual improvement of products, services, and practices across all value chain activities and the four dimensions of service management. That wording matters: Improve isn't a single improvement initiative with a start and end date. It's a standing activity, active at all times, inside every other activity.

A recurring incident pattern noticed during Deliver & support, a slow handoff noticed during Design & transition, a supplier process noticed during Obtain/build, feedback captured during Engage — all of these are raw material for Improve. ITIL 4 gives this activity a dedicated practice, Continual Improvement, which you'll study in full in Chapter 6; this lesson introduces the value-chain activity that practice supports. Improve takes what it finds and either acts on it directly or feeds it to Plan, so improvement opportunities compete for priority the same way any other planned work does.

## Engage: the organization's outward-facing activity

The Engage activity's purpose is to provide a good understanding of stakeholder needs, transparency, continual engagement, and good relationships with all stakeholders. "Stakeholders" here is broader than "customers": it includes users, customers, suppliers, partners, and internal colleagues in other parts of the business.

Engage is active at both ends of a relationship — understanding what a customer needs before anything is built, and staying in communication with users while they're actively consuming a service. It's also the activity that manages the organization's side of supplier and partner relationships: contract performance, escalation, and shared planning. What Engage produces — a captured need, a piece of feedback, a reported issue — is only useful once it reaches an activity that can act on it, usually Plan or Design & transition.

## How these three work together before anything is built

Picture a department asking for a new report their current system can't produce. Engage is what captures that request and makes sure the requester's actual need — not just their literal wording — gets understood and relayed accurately. Plan is what decides whether this fits current priorities, how it compares to other pending work, and what resourcing it needs. Improve is running the entire time in the background, watching the request-to-decision process itself: if this exact kind of request keeps arriving and keeps taking too long to triage, Improve is what turns that pattern into a proposal to fix the process, not just this one request. Lesson 13 follows a full request like this through all six activities.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Service Value Chain (SVC) | The six-activity operating model inside the Service Value System that converts demand into value |
| Plan | The SVC activity ensuring shared understanding of vision, status, and improvement direction |
| Improve | The SVC activity ensuring continual improvement of products, services, and practices, active across all other activities |
| Engage | The SVC activity responsible for stakeholder relationships, transparency, and understanding needs |
| Four dimensions of service management | Organizations & people, information & technology, partners & suppliers, value streams & processes — what Plan maintains shared understanding of |
