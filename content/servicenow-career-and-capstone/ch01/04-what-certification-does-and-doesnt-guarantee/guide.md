# Lesson 4 — What Certification Does & Doesn't Guarantee

## What certification actually demonstrates

A ServiceNow certification is real evidence of something specific: it
demonstrates verified baseline knowledge against a published, standardized
exam blueprint. That's genuinely useful — to a recruiter or hiring
manager scanning a stack of resumes, a certification is a credible,
third-party-verified signal that you cleared a real bar, not just a
self-reported claim. It can help your resume clear an initial screen,
and it gives you something concrete and specific to put on a resume
and LinkedIn profile.

That's a real, worth-having benefit. It's also the complete list of
what it reliably does.

## What it does not guarantee

Be honest with yourself about the limits, because overestimating them
leads to disappointment and underprepared interviews:

- **It does not guarantee a job.** Plenty of certified candidates don't
  get hired for a given role, because a certification is one input among
  many — relevant experience, interview performance, and fit for the
  specific role all matter at least as much, often more.
- **It does not guarantee a specific salary.** There's no verified,
  universal number a certification entitles you to. Compensation depends
  on role, location, employer, and your actual demonstrated ability —
  not on the certificate alone.
- **It does not substitute for real hands-on project experience.** This
  is the most important limit. A certification proves you can answer
  standardized questions about a module correctly. It does not prove you
  can walk into a messy, real organization's actual requirements,
  translate them into a working implementation, and defend the choices
  you made under real questioning.

## Why employers increasingly test the skill directly

A noticeable pattern across ServiceNow hiring: employers increasingly
don't just take a certification at face value. They ask certified
candidates to demonstrate the underlying skill directly — a technical
screen where you configure something live, walk through a past project
in detail, or solve a scenario on the spot. The certification got your
resume a look. The technical screen is where the actual skill gets
verified. If the certification was the only thing behind your resume —
with no real project to describe when asked "walk me through something
you actually built" — that's exactly where the gap shows up.

## Why this course builds a real capstone

This is precisely why Chapters 4 through 6 of this course exist: a
cumulative capstone project, built across a realistic 5,000-employee
organization scenario, that gives you something real to describe in
that technical screen. It isn't a certificate — it's a project with
real requirements, a real scope, real build work, and a real
presentation and defense of your architectural decisions. Certification
can get your resume read. The capstone is what you'll actually be
able to talk about once someone starts asking real questions.
