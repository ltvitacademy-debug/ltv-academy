## The major CIS specializations, honestly mapped

CIS credentials are module-specific. Four come up often enough to be
worth naming directly, and this path gave you very different amounts of
real exposure to each:

- **CIS-ITSM** — Implementation Specialist for IT Service Management
  (incident, problem, change, request). This path covered ITSM twice,
  in real depth: Course 1 (ITSM & ITIL 4 Foundations) built the
  conceptual ITIL foundation, and Course 8 (ServiceNow ITSM
  Implementation) had you actually configure ITSM workflows on the
  platform. If you're choosing based on where this path gave you the
  most real practice, CIS-ITSM is the honest front-runner.
- **CIS-Discovery** — Implementation Specialist for Discovery and CMDB
  population. Course 9 (ServiceNow CMDB, CSDM & Discovery) covered this
  directly and in depth: CMDB data modeling, the CSDM framework, and
  Discovery configuration. This is the second specialization this path
  genuinely prepared you for.
- **CIS-CSM** — Implementation Specialist for Customer Service
  Management. Be honest with yourself here: this path does not cover
  CSM in depth anywhere in its 13 courses. If CSM genuinely interests
  you, that's a real and valid direction — but it means starting
  additional study closer to the beginning, not leaning on hands-on
  work you already did here.
- **CIS-HR** — Implementation Specialist for HR Service Delivery.
  Same honest caveat as CSM: this path doesn't build real depth in HR
  Service Delivery. Pursuing it means the same — real, but starting
  mostly from scratch on the module-specific content.

## Why not choose based on perceived salary?

It's tempting to pick a specialization by guessing which one pays more.
Resist that instinct for two reasons. First, you don't actually have
reliable, verified data on module-by-module salary differences — any
number you've seen quoted online is likely anecdotal, regional, or
outdated, and this course won't pretend otherwise by inventing a figure
for you. Second, and more important: a CIS credential only has value
once you've actually implemented that module competently, and
competence comes from real hands-on practice, not from the certificate
itself. Choosing a specialization you have no real depth in — purely
because of a salary rumor — sets you up to study module mechanics you've
never touched, which is both harder and less durable than reinforcing
something you've already built.

## A practical method: start from what you actually did

Rather than guessing, look back honestly at your own work across
Courses 1–13:

1. Which module did you spend the most real, hands-on time configuring
   — not just reading about?
2. Could you walk a technical interviewer through a real example of
   what you configured, why, and what broke along the way?
3. Does the specialization's scope match work you'd actually want to do
   day to day — implementation consulting on that module, specifically?

For most students finishing this path, that process points to
CIS-ITSM or CIS-Discovery, because those are the two modules this path
gave you genuine, repeated hands-on exposure to. If your honest answer
points to CSM or HR instead — because of outside interest, a target
employer, or prior experience from elsewhere — that's a legitimate
choice too. The point isn't to force everyone toward the same
specialization; it's to choose based on real depth and real interest,
not on unverified salary comparisons.
