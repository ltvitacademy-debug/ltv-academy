# Lesson 25 — Salary Negotiation & Next Steps

**Chapter 7 · Interview & Job Search Preparation · Lesson 25 of 26**

## What you'll learn

- Why this course can't responsibly state specific ServiceNow salary figures, and what to use instead
- How to research a market range using public, verifiable sources before you ever hear a number
- Why negotiating the full offer — not just base salary — usually matters more than people expect
- How to set your own walk-away point before a negotiation starts, not during it

## Why no numbers appear in this lesson

ServiceNow compensation varies enormously by region, company size, industry, consulting versus
in-house, and individual experience level — enough that any specific figure or range stated here
would be misleading for a meaningful share of the people reading it, and stale within a year
regardless. Rather than offer a number that sounds authoritative but isn't grounded in your
specific market, this lesson teaches the method for finding your own honest number and the
negotiation habits that apply regardless of what that number turns out to be.

## Research the market range before you hear a number

Walk into any compensation conversation already knowing a defensible range for your role, level,
region, and specialization, built from sources you can actually point to — public salary-survey
sites, postings for comparable roles that list a range, professional communities and forums where
people in similar roles discuss real figures, and conversations with people actually doing the
job if you have access to any. Triangulating across several sources gets you closer to reality
than trusting any single site's number, since self-reported salary data is noisy and these sites
often skew by who chooses to report. The goal isn't a single exact figure — it's a defensible
range you could explain the sourcing for if asked.

## Know your own walk-away point, decided in advance

Before any offer conversation, decide privately what number (and what combination of terms) you
would genuinely decline below — not as a negotiating tactic, but as a real decision made with a
clear head, before stress or excitement about an offer can distort it. Deciding this in the moment,
during the call, is how people either accept something they'll resent or ask for something
unrealistic out of panic. A walk-away point set in advance, based on your researched range and
your own actual financial situation, keeps the conversation calm either way.

## Negotiate the whole offer, not just the base number

Base salary is the number most people fixate on, but it's one piece of a larger package: signing
or relocation bonus, annual bonus structure and its real historical payout (not just the target
percentage), healthcare and retirement contributions, paid time off, remote-work flexibility, and
start date. A company with limited room to move on base salary often has real room to move on one
of these other terms, and asking about the full package signals that you're thinking about the
offer the way an experienced professional does. The honest version of "negotiate" here isn't an
aggressive tactic — it's simply asking, clearly and professionally, for the things that matter to
you and being prepared to explain why, using the market research you already did.

## After the offer: thinking past this one role

Whatever happens with a specific offer, the job search itself doesn't end when one process does.
Keep the portfolio and resume work from Chapters 3 and 4 current as you gain real experience, and
treat every interview — even ones that don't result in an offer — as practice for the tradeoff-
defense skill Lesson 24 covered. That skill compounds with real project experience in exactly the
way Lesson 7 described for the path toward Technical Architect: each real engagement gives you
another decision to be able to explain clearly next time.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Market range | A defensible compensation range for your specific role, level, and region, built from multiple sourced references |
| Walk-away point | The minimum offer terms you'd genuinely decline, decided before a negotiation begins |
| Full offer / total compensation | Base salary plus bonus, benefits, equity (if any), and other terms — not base salary alone |
| Triangulation | Cross-checking several independent sources rather than trusting one site's number |
