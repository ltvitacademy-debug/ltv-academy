## Fourteen courses, one real skill set

This is the last lesson of the entire ServiceNow Administrator → Developer → Technical Architect
path, and it's worth walking back through what that path actually built, course by course, rather
than letting it blur into one long list. Course 1 gave you the ITSM and ITIL 4 foundations
underneath everything else on the platform. Course 2 (Platform Fundamentals) and Course 3 (System
Administration) gave you the base platform literacy — navigation, data model, configuration —
that every later course assumed you already had. Course 4 (Flow Designer) made you fluent in the
platform's native automation model. Courses 5 and 6 (JavaScript for ServiceNow Developers, then
Scripting & Application Development) took you from general JavaScript into real server-side
ServiceNow development: Business Rules, Script Includes, a genuine scoped application. Course 7
(IntegrationHub & REST APIs) connected the platform to the outside world. Course 8 (ITSM
Implementation) put the Course 1 foundations into real configured practice. Course 9 (CMDB, CSDM
& Discovery) took on one of the platform's genuinely hardest, most overlooked skills — real
configuration-item data modeling. Course 10 (Security & Access Controls) and Course 11 (DevOps,
Testing & Deployment) rounded out the skills a production instance actually depends on day to
day: access control design and a real application lifecycle. Course 12 (Performance &
Troubleshooting) built the diagnostic instincts that separate someone who can configure the
platform from someone who can keep it healthy under real load. Course 13 (Technical Architecture)
raised the altitude to enterprise-scale decisions — instance strategy, governance, Architecture
Decision Records. And this course, Course 14, closed the loop: certification planning, an honest
read on the real job market, a portfolio built from genuine artifacts, and the Meridian Health
Network capstone that ties the whole path together into one demonstrable body of work.

## The capstone is your proof, not a claim

Everything in the paragraph above is a list of topics — the kind of list that, on its own, sounds
exactly like a syllabus you could describe without having done the work. The Meridian Health
Network capstone is the opposite of that: a specific, built artifact — requirements gathered
against a realistic three-hospital-system consolidation, service workflows and integrations
actually configured, test plans actually run, deployment documentation actually produced, and
architecture decisions actually presented and defended under real follow-up questions in Chapters
4 through 6. When an interviewer asks what you've actually built, this is the honest, specific
answer, built on a Personal Developer Instance exactly the way Lesson 9 described — real applied
skill, clearly labeled as a capstone project and not oversold as production deployment experience.
That honesty is itself part of what makes the story credible.

## Where continued learning honestly goes from here

Finishing this path is a real milestone, not a finish line, and three things genuinely continue
to matter after today. First, ServiceNow ships new platform releases on its own regular cycle, and
staying current with what changes in each one — new Flow Designer capabilities, platform UI
shifts, deprecated APIs — is an ongoing professional habit, not a one-time task this path could
front-load for you. Second, Lesson 2 laid out the real CIS specialization landscape honestly: this
path built genuine depth in CIS-ITSM and CIS-Discovery specifically, and choosing one of those (or
a different one, with eyes open about starting closer to scratch) is a concrete next step worth
real consideration. Third, and most important: Lesson 7 was direct about this, and it's worth
repeating one last time here — real experience across multiple real engagements, not another
certification, is the actual path toward Technical Architect candidacy. Treat your first job or
project after this path as the beginning of that experience, not a formality on top of what you
already finished.

## A closing word

You started this path somewhere short of knowing what ServiceNow even was as a platform, and
you're finishing it having planned certification pathways, understood the real job market, built
a genuine portfolio, and completed a full enterprise capstone project you can describe, show, and
defend. That's real, earned progress — fourteen courses' worth of it. Take the portfolio, the
resume, and the capstone story you built in this course out into real interviews, and keep
building from here.
