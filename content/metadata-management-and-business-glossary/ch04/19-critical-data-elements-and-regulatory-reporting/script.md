# Lesson 19 — Critical Data Elements and Regulatory Reporting · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Data Governance Foundations covered the regulatory landscape broadly.
This is the sharp point of that topic: a regulator auditing a financial
institution isn't checking whether every column everywhere is
documented — they're checking whether the organization controls the
small number of elements that feed regulatory filings.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (the regulator's view)

That's exactly the CDE concept from Lesson 16, viewed from the
regulator's side of the table.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (two regulatory examples)

BCBS 239, in banking, explicitly requires the capability to identify
and govern critical data elements used in risk reporting, with assigned
ownership and documented lineage. Sarbanes-Oxley, for publicly traded
US companies, requires documented, auditable controls over financial
reporting — functionally, SOX's own version of a CDE list.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (four audit checks)

A regulatory audit typically checks four things. Does a documented CDE
list actually exist, not just known informally by one person. Does
every CDE have a named, accountable owner. Is there evidence the
validation rules and lineage are actually being run. And can the
organization show a remediation history when a check failed.
