# Lesson 24 — Catalog Adoption

**Chapter 5 · Data Catalogs · Lesson 24 of 25**

## What you'll learn

- Why building a technically excellent catalog is not the same problem as getting people to use it
- Three concrete adoption barriers that sink well-built catalogs
- A practical adoption tactic for each barrier
- Why adoption, not feature completeness, is usually the real bottleneck

## Building it isn't the hard part

By this point in the course, you know how to build a technically sound catalog: scan for technical metadata (Lesson 22), curate business context, link to the glossary (Lesson 11) and dictionary (Chapter 3), make it searchable (Lesson 21). None of that guarantees anyone actually uses it. Adoption is a separate, harder problem — and it's the one that determines whether all the work in Chapters 1 through 5 pays off or sits unused.

## Three adoption barriers

1. **Habit** — people already have a working method (asking a colleague, checking an old spreadsheet, querying the database directly) that's faster for them *right now* than learning a new tool, even if the catalog is objectively better. Old habits don't lose to a better tool; they lose to a tool that's faster for the specific task someone needs done this minute.
2. **Trust** — if someone searches the catalog once and gets a stale or wrong result (exactly the correctness failure from Lesson 23), they conclude "the catalog isn't reliable" and go back to asking a colleague — often permanently, after just one bad experience. This is why metadata quality (Lesson 23) isn't just a technical nicety; it's the entire basis for whether adoption happens at all.
3. **Visibility** — a catalog nobody knows exists gets zero adoption regardless of quality. This is the publish stage from the metadata lifecycle (Lesson 4) and the exact failure Lesson 10's glossary-building process warned about, now at the scale of an entire catalog.

## A tactic for each barrier

- **For habit**: integrate the catalog into existing workflows rather than asking people to adopt a new one — a catalog search widget embedded directly in the BI tool people already open daily beats a separate destination nobody remembers to visit.
- **For trust**: launch with a smaller, genuinely high-quality scope (the same scoping logic from Lesson 10 and Lesson 14) rather than a comprehensive but spotty one. A hundred reliable entries that always deliver a correct answer build more trust than a thousand entries of mixed quality.
- **For visibility**: actively announce it where people already look — team meetings, onboarding materials, pinned links in the tools people use daily — rather than passively publishing it and hoping for discovery.

## Why adoption is usually the real bottleneck

Most failed metadata programs aren't failed because the technology was wrong — they're failed because nobody accounted for the fact that getting a hundred people to change a habit is a harder problem than getting a database to return search results correctly. The technical work in this course (Chapters 1 through 5) is necessary but not sufficient; treating adoption as a deliberate, separate workstream — not an afterthought once the technical build is "done" — is what separates a catalog that gets used from one that gets built and then quietly ignored.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Adoption | Whether people actually use a catalog, as distinct from whether it's technically well-built |
| Habit barrier | People's existing, faster-for-now workaround competing against a new tool |
