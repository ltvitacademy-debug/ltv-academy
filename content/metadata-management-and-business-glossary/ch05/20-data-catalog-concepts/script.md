# Lesson 20 — Data Catalog Concepts · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

A data catalog is a searchable inventory of an organization's data
assets — tables, reports, dashboards, files — that pulls together
business and technical metadata from multiple sources into one place
someone can actually search.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (glossary + dictionary + usage)

Where the glossary covers business terms and the dictionary covers
physical structures, the catalog is the layer that surfaces both
together, plus usage information neither of them tracks on its own.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (an integration point)

Lesson 5 warned about repository sprawl. A poorly designed catalog just
adds a fourth place metadata has to be separately maintained. A
well-designed catalog instead pulls from the others — reading business
definitions from the glossary, structural metadata from the
dictionary, lineage from pipeline tools.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (three things surfaced)

A catalog entry surfaces three things together. What it is — the
technical structure from the dictionary. What it means — the business
definition from the glossary. And how it's actually used — which
reports query this table, how often, by whom. That third one is what
makes a catalog genuinely different from a glossary and dictionary
stapled together.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

This is the practical resolution to Lesson 5's sprawl problem — a
catalog that actually integrates, rather than becoming its own fourth
silo. Next lesson: catalog capabilities and search — what makes a
catalog actually usable, not just comprehensive.
