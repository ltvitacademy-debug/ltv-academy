# Lesson 7 — Writing Good Definitions · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

The single most common failure in glossary writing is the circular
definition — one that only makes sense to someone who already
understands the term. "Active Customer: a customer who is active."
That's not a definition, it restates the term using itself.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (why it fails)

It passes a quick skim but fails the moment someone genuinely
unfamiliar with the business tries to use it — which is exactly who a
glossary is supposed to serve.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (four-part test)

A usable definition passes four tests. Specific — someone applying it
gets the same answer as anyone else. Self-contained — understandable
without already knowing the term. Measurable — if there's a threshold,
state the number. And implementation-free — no column names or queries,
that belongs in the dictionary.

## S4 · CODE CARD (worked rewrite)

Here's a worked rewrite. Weak: "a customer who is currently active and
engaged with our products" — vague, no threshold, borderline circular.
Better: "a customer with at least one completed purchase in the trailing
90 days, measured from the nightly refresh" — specific, measurable,
self-contained, and implementation-free.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

Watch for three traps: circular definitions, implementation leakage —
writing an actual query instead of a business rule — and vague
qualifiers like "significant" or "recently" hiding an undefined
threshold. Next lesson: glossary governance and approval — writing a
good definition is only step one.
