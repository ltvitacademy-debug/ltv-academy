# Lesson 9 — Glossary Terms and Hierarchies · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

A glossary with ten terms works fine as a flat, alphabetical list. A
glossary with three hundred terms doesn't — without structure, someone
searching for Revenue has no way to discover that Net Revenue and
Gross Revenue are closely related terms they might actually need.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (why flat lists fail)

Flat lists make browsing nearly impossible, which defeats a large part
of what a glossary is for. Glossaries typically model three
relationship types to fix this.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (three relationship types)

Hierarchy — broader and narrower, Revenue is broader than Net Revenue.
Synonym — two terms meaning exactly the same thing, with one marked as
official and the other pointing to it. And related term — connected,
but not a hierarchy and not a true synonym.

## S4 · CODE CARD (worked hierarchy)

Here's a worked hierarchy. Revenue branches into Gross Revenue, Net
Revenue, and Recognized Revenue — and Net Revenue branches further into
North America and International. Each child term still needs its own
specific, measurable, self-contained definition, not just "Net Revenue,
but regional" left implicit.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

Watch for the synonym trap: Customer and Account often get treated as
the same thing colloquially, but a customer might have multiple
accounts. Marking them as true synonyms would silently lose a
distinction the business relies on — related term is the honest
relationship there. Next lesson: building a business glossary — putting
all four lessons together into one real artifact.
