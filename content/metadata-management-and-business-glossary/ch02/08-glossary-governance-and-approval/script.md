# Lesson 8 — Glossary Governance and Approval · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

A glossary anyone can edit freely sounds democratic, but in practice it
degrades fast. Two people disagree about what Active Customer means,
both edit the same entry back and forth, and within weeks the
definition drifts back into something vague, with no record of who
changed what.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (why open editing fails)

A glossary without governance isn't really a glossary — it's a wiki
page with extra steps, and it inherits a wiki page's reliability
problems.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (standard lifecycle)

Most mature programs use a four-state lifecycle. Draft — proposed,
flagged as unofficial. In review — submitted with the proposing
steward's rationale. Approved — the official, current definition.
Deprecated — retired, but the old definition stays visible, clearly
marked, so historical references still resolve.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (who approves it)

Approval authority should sit with the business steward accountable
for that domain — not IT, and not whoever happens to be the most
active editor. IT can maintain the tool, but deciding what Net Revenue
means to Finance is a business judgment, not a technical one.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

This whole workflow is just the capture and maintain stages of the
metadata lifecycle from Lesson 4, made concrete for one artifact. A
glossary with no governance has skipped straight to "approved" for
everything — exactly the failure mode that lesson warned about. Next:
glossary terms and hierarchies — terms don't live alone, they relate to
each other.
