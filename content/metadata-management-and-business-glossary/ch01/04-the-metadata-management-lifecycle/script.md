# Lesson 4 — The Metadata Management Lifecycle · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Metadata isn't written once and left alone — it moves through a
lifecycle, the same way the data quality lifecycle does. Five stages:
capture, store, publish, maintain, and retire.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (five stages)

Capture: metadata gets created, automatically or by a steward. Store:
it's recorded somewhere searchable. Publish: it's made visible to the
people who need it. Maintain: it gets reviewed and updated as things
change. Retire: when the thing it describes is gone, the entry is marked
retired, not silently abandoned.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (the stage everyone skips)

A glossary entry written three years ago, for a column that's since been
renamed and repurposed, is now actively wrong — worse than no entry at
all, because a wrong definition gets trusted. Maintain is the stage
everyone skips, because nothing visibly breaks the day an entry goes
stale. It just quietly drifts: no review date, an owner who left, a
definition that no longer matches reality.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (mapping onto the course)

Here's how this maps onto the rest of the course. Capture and store get
covered in Chapters 2 and 3, writing definitions and building a
dictionary. Publish is Chapter 5, data catalogs. Maintain specifically
comes back in Lesson 8, glossary governance, and Lesson 23, metadata
quality.

## S5 · OUTRO CARD

A deprecated term or column should always be marked retired, not deleted
outright, so historical references still resolve. Next lesson: metadata
repositories — where all of this actually gets stored.
