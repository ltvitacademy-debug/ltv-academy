## S1 · TITLE CARD

Data Governance Foundations covered governance broadly. This course goes
deep on one specific piece of its infrastructure: metadata — data about
data, and everything you build out of it.

## S2 · STEPS CARD (sharper definition)

The textbook definition undersells it. Here's the sharper version:
metadata is what makes data findable, understandable, and trustworthy,
without having to open it first. Most of the pain in "we have the data
somewhere, nobody knows where" organizations isn't a data problem — it's
a metadata problem.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (library card catalog)

Before computers, libraries used card catalogs — one card per book,
listing title, author, subject, and shelf location. The card wasn't the
book, it was metadata about the book. You searched the cards to decide
which book to pull, without pulling every book off the shelf first. A
modern data catalog, in Chapter 5, does exactly the same job.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (three examples)

You already rely on metadata constantly. A file's Date Modified column —
technical metadata, generated automatically. A nutrition label —
structured, standardized metadata about the product. An email's subject
line and sender — metadata your inbox sorts and searches on, separate
from the message itself.
