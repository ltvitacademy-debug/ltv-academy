# Lesson 2 — Reviewing the Fictional Company · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Meet the company this entire capstone is built for. Everything from here through Lesson 20 happens inside one fictional, illustrative Salesforce org, for one company — never a real business.

## S2 · STEPS — Meet Cascade

Cascade Foodservice Equipment Co. designs, sources, and sells commercial kitchen equipment — ranges, ovens, walk-in coolers, dishwashing systems, and ventilation hoods — to other businesses. It's headquartered in Portland, Oregon, with regional sales offices in Denver and Columbus, employs roughly two hundred forty people, and sells to restaurants, hotels, healthcare and institutional kitchens, school districts, and catering companies.

## S3 · STEPS — Cascade's departments

Cascade is organized into several departments you'll see referenced throughout this build. Sales splits into two teams: New Business, which finds and closes new accounts, and Key Accounts, which grows the large, multi-location customers already on the books. Service and Installation handles on-site work, and Customer Success owns the relationship after the sale.

## S4 · STEPS — The sales process

Every deal moves through the same five open stages. Qualifying confirms a real budget, authority, and timeline. Needs Analysis documents the exact equipment required. Equipment Proposal is a written, priced proposal. Negotiation slash Review covers terms under discussion. And every deal ends Closed Won with a signed order, or Closed Lost with a recorded reason. You'll build these exact stage names onto the Opportunity object in Lesson 8.

## S5 · CODE — The people

These seven people appear throughout the rest of this capstone. Monica Reyes is VP of Sales. Derek Oyelaran manages the New Business team; Priya Nair is the senior account executive who owns Cascade's largest Key Accounts. Tom Baptiste works New Business deals, Jordan Kessler qualifies inbound leads as the SDR, Angela Wu runs Customer Success, and Marcus Webb leads Service and Installation.

## S6 · STEPS — Why custom objects

Two business realities at Cascade don't fit the standard objects. An installation has its own lifecycle after a deal is won — a site, a date, a status, an installer. And many customers pay separately for ongoing maintenance through a recurring service contract, independent of any single Opportunity. Those become your two custom objects in Lesson 9.
