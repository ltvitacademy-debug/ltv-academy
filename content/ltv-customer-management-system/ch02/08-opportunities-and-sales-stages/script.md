# Lesson 8 — Opportunities and Sales Stages · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Now the object everything in Chapter 2 points toward: the Opportunity. Let's configure Cascade's five sales stages and get a real equipment package onto a deal.

## S2 · CODE — Stage configuration

The Stage picklist gets six values — five open stages plus Closed Lost, each with a probability and forecast category. Qualifying starts at ten percent, climbing through Needs Analysis, Equipment Proposal, and Negotiation Review, up to Closed Won at a hundred percent. Closed Won and Closed Lost both get flagged as Closed stages, which matters for both reporting and automation.

## S3 · CODE — Opportunity custom fields

Three custom fields round it out. Loss Reason captures why a deal didn't close — price, a competitor, no budget, timing, or cancellation. Target Install Quarter feeds scheduling. And Competing Vendor tracks what else the customer is evaluating, when known.

## S4 · STEPS — Price Book and Products

An Opportunity needs real products attached, not just a dollar amount. This representative slice of Cascade's catalog covers all four equipment categories: a six-burner commercial range, a walk-in cooler, a conveyor dishwasher, and a Type One ventilation hood — each added to the standard price book.

## S5 · STEPS — Walked through end to end

Here's one deal walked through end to end. A converted Lead becomes a new Opportunity in Qualifying, owned by Tom Baptiste. After a site visit he adds the range and the hood and moves it to Needs Analysis. A written quote moves it to Equipment Proposal. And when the customer signs, Tom sets it to Closed Won.

## S6 · STEPS — Why "Closed" matters

Marking Closed Won and Closed Lost as Closed stages isn't just bookkeeping. It's what lets reports correctly exclude them from open pipeline forecasts, and it's the exact trigger Chapter 3's record-triggered Flow watches for to automatically spin up an Installation Project.
