# Script — Subledger Reports: Journal Entries and Account Analysis

## Segment 1 (title)

Chapter four was about producing and moving accounting. Chapter five is about reading it back out, at scale - the reports you actually run instead of clicking into one entry at a time. This lesson covers the two most foundational: the Journal Entries Report and the Account Analysis Report.

## Segment 2 (steps)

The Journal Entries Report lists subledger journal entries for a ledger, application, and date range or batch - everything you could see one entry at a time on Review Journal Entries, but across thousands of entries at once. Run it to review a whole period's Payables accounting, sample-check a batch before transfer, or hand a complete listing to an auditor.

## Segment 3 (steps)

The Account Analysis Report organizes by GL account instead of by transaction. For a chosen account and period, it lists every subledger line that posted there - answering "show me everything that hit this account" rather than "show me this invoice's accounting." It's what a controller reaches for when a balance looks unexpected.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Both reports are only as useful as chapter two's configuration choices. A well-built description rule means every line says something specific - which supplier, which invoice - instead of a repeated generic label. A thoughtful accounting class means Account Analysis can meaningfully group lines by type. None of that is automatic.

## Segment 5 (code)

Pick the right one for the task. Journal Entries Report when your starting point is a transaction, batch, or period. Account Analysis Report when your starting point is a GL account. In practice you often move between both - starting broad to spot something unusual, then narrowing to the specific account.

## Segment 6 (outro)

So remember: Journal Entries Report organizes by transaction, Account Analysis Report organizes by account, and both depend on the rule discipline from chapter two. Up next, lesson twenty-two: open account balances listings, focused specifically on what's still open.
