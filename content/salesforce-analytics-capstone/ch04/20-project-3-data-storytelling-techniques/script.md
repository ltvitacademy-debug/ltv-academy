# Script — Data Storytelling Techniques

## Segment 1 (title)

Your dashboard from the last lesson was accurate, but you probably spent a lot of time explaining what each chart meant. Storytelling techniques move that explaining into the design. Four of them: a narrative arc, one message per chart, annotation, and removing chartjunk. Figures are illustrative.

## Segment 2 (steps: narrative arc)

A briefing has three beats. Setup: the context everyone agrees on, we're 5.4 million into a 9.0 million year. Tension: what threatens it, we're on pace to land about 1.7 million short. Resolution: the action, re-engage 19 stalled deals. If you can't find the tension, the audience won't know why they're listening.

## Segment 3 (code: message titles)

Test every chart by its title. Opportunities by Stage describes. 19 deals worth 2.1 million have gone quiet, states a message. Use the component title, subtitle and footer to carry the message and the definition. If you can't write a message for a chart, ask whether it belongs on the dashboard at all.

## Segment 4 (code: annotation)

Annotation tells viewers where to look: the outlier, the target, the change point, the definition. Native dashboards give you limited annotation, so lean on titles, footers, and conditional colors on metric and gauge components. Tableau supports annotations directly on the view, one honest reason to consider it for a polished story.

## Segment 5 (steps: chartjunk)

Chartjunk is decoration that carries no data. Three-dimensional effects, rainbow palettes, heavy gridlines, pies with many slices, and truncated axes that exaggerate change. Use one accent color for the thing that matters and neutral colors for everything else. Red should mean look here.

## Segment 6 (code: storytelling pass)

Now do a storytelling pass on Project 3. Write the setup, tension and resolution in three sentences. Rewrite every title as a message or move the chart to a report. Add each definition to a footer. Strip anything that carries no data, and highlight only stalled deals, backlog and the quota gap.
