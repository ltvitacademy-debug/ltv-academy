# Presenting to a "VP of Sales"

A dashboard nobody understands does not change a decision. In this lesson you present the Alder & Vale executive dashboard to a VP of Sales, played by your instructor. The VP is busy, skeptical, and will interrupt. That is realistic, and it is the skill: lead with the answer, support it with three findings, make one recommendation, and handle pushback without bluffing. All figures are illustrative.

## What you'll learn

- How to open with the answer instead of the method
- How to choose exactly three findings
- How to make one recommendation the VP can say yes to
- How to handle the questions executives usually ask
- How to prepare and rehearse

## Open with the answer

Executives read the last page first. Your first sentence is the conclusion, not the story of how you built the dashboard. Compare:

- **Weak:** "I pulled leads, opportunities and cases and built a dashboard with six KPIs."
- **Strong:** "We are on pace to finish about $1.7M short of the $9.0M quota, and the fastest way to close that gap is the 19 stalled deals worth $2.1M."

The strong version has a number, a verdict and a hint of the action. You can add the rest of the detail afterward, because the VP now knows why they are listening.

## Three findings, no more

People hold about three points in their head after a meeting. Pick the three that matter most and rank them.

1. **Pipeline:** $5.4M is closed won, 60% of quota. Open pipeline is $7.2M, 2.0x coverage of the $3.6M gap. At the 27% win rate (by count, a rough rule) that converts to roughly $1.9M, landing near $7.3M.
2. **Stalled deals:** 19 open deals worth $2.1M have had no logged activity for 30 days or more.
3. **Service health:** the open case backlog grew by 42 to 187. 71% of cases were resolved within target, and 17 high-priority cases are over five days old, 6 of them escalated.

Everything else on the dashboard is backup, ready if asked.

## One recommendation

Findings without an ask leave the VP guessing what you want. Give one specific, owner-and-date recommendation: "Have the six reps re-engage the 19 stalled deals this week, starting with the largest, and I will report the movement on Friday." If you have several ideas, choose the one with the biggest effect and mention the others as follow-ups.

## Handling pushback

Expect at least these questions:

- **"Is this current?"** Point to the refresh time and the running user. Know both before the meeting.
- **"That win rate looks low."** Define it: 27% is by count, not by amount. Offer to add the by-amount view rather than defending the number.
- **"Those deals are not stalled."** Explain the definition, no activity in 30 days or more, and offer to test a different threshold. A rep who disagrees may be right, and the definition is where to discuss it.
- **"Can you break it down by region?"** If you cannot, say so, note it, and say when you will follow up.

The pattern is listen, restate the question, answer with the data or the definition, and park anything you cannot answer. Never make up a figure in the room.

## Prepare and rehearse

Write your opening sentence, three findings and recommendation on one card. Rehearse it aloud in under five minutes, so the VP has time to interrupt. Have the definitions of every KPI ready, including date ranges and filters. Ask a classmate to play the skeptic and time yourself.

## Recap

Lead with the verdict, give three ranked findings and one recommendation, and treat pushback as a chance to sharpen definitions. Next, you will look at storytelling techniques that make the visuals themselves carry the message.
