# Script — Presenting to a "VP of Sales"

## Segment 1 (title)

Now you present. Your instructor plays the VP of Sales at Alder and Vale: busy, skeptical, and quick to interrupt. That's realistic, and it's the skill. Lead with the answer, support it with three findings, make one recommendation, and handle pushback without bluffing. Figures are illustrative.

## Segment 2 (steps: four moves)

The structure is four moves. The answer, in one sentence. Three findings, ranked. One recommendation with an owner and a date. And what you'll do next, so the meeting ends with movement.

## Segment 3 (code: the opening)

Open with the conclusion, not the method. Don't say you pulled leads, opportunities and cases. Say: we're on pace to finish about 1.7 million short of the nine million quota, and the fastest fix is 19 stalled deals worth 2.1 million. A number, a verdict, a hint of the action.

## Segment 4 (code: three findings)

Then three findings. One: 5.4 million closed won, 60 percent of quota, with 2.0 times pipeline coverage. Two: 19 deals worth 2.1 million with no activity for 30 days. Three: service backlog up 42 to 187, with 17 high-priority cases over five days. Everything else is backup.

## Segment 5 (steps: handling pushback)

Pushback has a pattern. Listen to the whole question. Restate it so the VP knows you heard it. Answer with the data or the definition. And if you can't answer, park it with a follow-up date. Never invent a figure in the room.

## Segment 6 (code: likely questions)

Practice the likely questions. Is this current? Point to the refresh time. Win rate looks low? It's by count, and you can add by amount. Those deals aren't stalled? Explain the 30-day definition and offer to test another threshold. By region? Not in this version; note it and follow up.
