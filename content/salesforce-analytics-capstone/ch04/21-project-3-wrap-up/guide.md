# Project 3: Wrap-Up & Presentation

Project 3 is finished when a stranger can open your work, understand the problem in one minute, and trust the numbers. This lesson closes the project: a one-page result, a final review checklist, a portfolio write-up and a final presentation to your instructor, the VP of Sales at Alder & Vale Systems. It is also the last of your three portfolio projects, so you will take stock of the whole set. All figures are illustrative.

## What you'll learn

- How to boil Project 3 down to a one-page result
- A review checklist to run before you present
- The five sections of a portfolio write-up
- How to run the final presentation
- How to be honest about limits and next steps

## The one-page result

Before any slides, write the result on a single page. For Alder & Vale, year to date through September:

| Sales | Service |
|---|---|
| $5.4M closed won, 60% of the $9.0M quota | 640 cases opened, 598 closed |
| $7.2M open pipeline, 2.0x coverage of the $3.6M gap | Backlog up 42 to 187 |
| 27% win rate by count | 71% resolved within the 2-day target |
| 19 stalled deals worth $2.1M | 17 high-priority cases over 5 days, 6 escalated |
| 1,480 new leads this quarter | 4 agents handling the queue |

If you cannot state each side in one sentence, the dashboard is not finished.

## The pre-presentation checklist

Run this before anyone else sees the work:

1. **Definitions.** Every KPI has a footer stating how it is calculated and the date range.
2. **Filters.** Every component honors the filters it should. Test each one.
3. **Running user and refresh.** You know who the dashboard is viewed as and when it last refreshed.
4. **Data quality.** The cleaning steps from earlier in this chapter are documented, including anything you excluded.
5. **Story.** The setup, tension and resolution fit in three sentences, and each chart title is a message.
6. **Mobile.** You have previewed it on a phone-sized screen.

## The final presentation

Give it in about five minutes, then take questions. The structure is the one you practiced: the answer first, three findings, one recommendation, and what you will do next. Practice the pushback pattern too: listen, restate, answer with data or a definition, and park what you cannot answer. Have the one-page result available as your backup slide.

## The portfolio write-up

Structure the write-up in five sections:

1. **Problem and audience:** who asked, and what they needed to decide.
2. **Data and approach:** the objects, report types and cleaning steps, and why.
3. **Design decisions:** layout, KPIs, filters, running user, native versus Tableau.
4. **Results:** screenshots of your own dashboard and the one-page result.
5. **Limits and next steps:** what native could not do and what you would do about it.

Build it from a Developer Edition org or sample data. Never include confidential employer or customer data, and replace any real names.

## Honest limits and next steps

Naming limits builds trust. Typical ones from this project: cross-object math is limited because each component draws on one report; the quota may be a value you maintain by hand; and history, such as a daily backlog trend, needs trend reporting or snapshots to be set up. Each limit is evidence for something you can do next: a stored quota field, snapshots, or a tool such as CRM Analytics or Tableau. You will meet those tools in the Advanced stage of this path.

## Recap

You now have three portfolio projects, from a sales pipeline to customer service to an executive dashboard, each with a result page and a write-up. The next chapter turns them into interview answers.
