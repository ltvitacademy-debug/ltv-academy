# Salary Negotiation Basics

You have the skills, the portfolio and the interview stories. The offer conversation is the last step, and it is where many candidates leave money on the table because nobody taught them how it works. This lesson covers the basics: researching a range, anchoring, looking at total compensation, and responding to an offer. None of this is financial or legal advice, and every figure here is a general market range that varies by city, company size, industry and year.

## What you'll learn

- How to research a realistic pay range for the role
- What an anchor is and how to state your range
- How to weigh total compensation, not only base pay
- A calm way to respond to an offer
- What not to do

## Know the range first

For this career path, LTV Academy's career overview describes typical pay of roughly $65K to $95K to start as a Salesforce or CRM data analyst. With CRM Analytics, Data Cloud and Tableau Next skills at a senior or analytics engineer level, the ranges can reach roughly $150K to $200K or more, with some postings in select markets going higher. These are broad guides, not promises. Your own number depends on location, employer, seniority and how you interview.

To research your specific range:

- **Job postings that list pay.** Several US states and cities require pay ranges in postings, so many now include them. Rules vary by location.
- **Salary sites and surveys.** Public sites and government occupational data give ballpark figures, but treat any single source with caution.
- **People.** Recruiters, mentors and instructors can tell you what is realistic for your market.

Collect at least three sources and write down a range for the exact role, location and level.

## Anchoring

An anchor is the first number in the conversation, and it pulls later numbers toward it. Two guidelines:

1. If you can, let the employer share their range first. Many will.
2. If you must go first, give a range whose bottom you would happily accept, based on your research. For example: "Based on my research for analyst roles like this in this market, and on the projects in my portfolio, I'm looking for something in the range of X to Y."

Some places restrict employers from asking about your past salary, and you are generally free to redirect: "I'd rather focus on the value of this role than my past pay." Never state a false number, and never invent a competing offer.

## Think in total compensation

Base salary is one part of the package. Compare:

| Component | Ask about |
|---|---|
| Base pay | Range for the level, review cycle |
| Bonus | Target, how it is calculated, how often it pays out |
| Equity | Type, vesting schedule (mainly for some employers) |
| Benefits | Health insurance cost, retirement match |
| Time off | Vacation, holidays, flexibility |
| Growth | Training budget, certification reimbursement, remote or hybrid work |

If the base is firm, you can often negotiate other pieces, such as a signing bonus, a start date, or a certification budget that helps you toward the Advanced-stage skills in this path.

## Responding to an offer

When the offer arrives, follow a calm sequence:

1. **Thank them and show enthusiasm.** "Thank you, I'm excited about this role."
2. **Ask for time.** A day or two to review is normal.
3. **Compare to your range and total package.**
4. **Make one clear counter with a reason.** "Given the scope of the role and my portfolio work, could we do X?"
5. **Get the final version in writing** before you accept.

Negotiate the whole package together instead of one item at a time, and be polite. Employers generally expect a counter, though outcomes vary and entry-level roles can have less room.

## What not to do

- Do not accept or decline on the spot.
- Do not lie about salary history, offers or skills.
- Do not make ultimatums you are not willing to keep.
- Do not forget that a small increase now compounds over the years.

## Recap

Research a range from several sources, state a range you would be happy with, look at total compensation, and respond to offers with time, a reason and a clear counter. Markets vary, so treat every number here as a starting point. One lesson remains: what to build after this course.
