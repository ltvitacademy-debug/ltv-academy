# Script — Project 1: Wrap-Up & Presentation

## Segment 1 (title)

You have five answers. Now they become the two things people actually consume: a dashboard that stands on its own, and a short readout that leads with the conclusion.

## Segment 2 (steps: dashboard components)

Every widget reads from a source report, so your reports become the components. A gauge for closed won against the two-point-two-five million quota. A funnel for open pipeline by stage. A bar for attainment by rep. A chart for conversion by lead source. And a table of the twenty-one stalled deals.

## Segment 3 (screenshot: real dashboard)

Here's a real Salesforce sales dashboard from Trailhead. Notice the layout: pipeline by stage, top accounts, a table of open opportunities, and win rate over time. Different data than ours, same idea.

## Segment 4 (screenshot: add widget)

Adding a component is the Add Widget dialog: choose the source report, a display type, a value, and a field to slice by. This example uses a Leads by Lead Source report as a donut.

## Segment 5 (code: reconcile)

Reconcile before you share. Rep totals must sum to one-point-seven-one million, matching the gauge. Stage totals sum to four-point-eight million. Lead sources sum to a thousand leads. Any mismatch means a filter differs between reports. Fix it before anyone sees it.

## Segment 6 (code: the readout)

Now the readout. Lead with the headline: seventy-six percent of quota, a five-forty gap, and pipeline likely to close about four seventy. Then three findings, each with one number. Then recommendations tied to decisions: review the stalled deals, coach Tomas and Priya on pipeline, test shifting Purchased List spend.

## Segment 7 (steps: portfolio write-up)

Finally, the portfolio write-up. The question. The data, labeled illustrative. The method with screenshots. Findings and recommendations. And limitations, stated plainly. No real employer data, ever.

## Segment 8 (outro)

Project One is done. Next, Project Two: customer service, with less hand-holding.
