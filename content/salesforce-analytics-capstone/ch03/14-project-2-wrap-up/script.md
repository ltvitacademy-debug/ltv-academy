# Script — Project 2: Wrap-Up & Presentation

## Segment 1 (title)

You have reports. Now you need an answer. The Head of Support doesn't want twelve reports. She wants to know if the queue is growing, if service is fast enough, and whether to hire. Let's wrap Project 2.

## Segment 2 (screenshot)

Assemble the dashboard. This is a real Trailhead dashboard as viewers see it, with a link back to its source report. Yours mirrors the three business questions, and every tile should reconcile to its source when you click through.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Layout: headline numbers on top. Backlog 210, SLA compliance 88 percent, average resolution 38 hours, and CSAT 4.3 with its response count. Charts in the middle: opened versus closed by month, and breach rate by priority. The agent table goes at the bottom. Title each tile with its point.

## Segment 4 (code)

Lead with the answer. One: the queue is growing. Backlog rose from 150 to 210, up 40 percent, as monthly intake climbed from 380 to 450. Two: speed is uneven. High priority breaches about 23 percent of the time. Three: after adjusting for case mix, the agent gap is small.

## Segment 5 (code)

Back the recommendation with math. An agent closes about 98 cases a month. September brought 450 against about 393 closed, a shortfall of 57, which is roughly 0.6 of an agent. So process first: a High-priority path and better triage for Low. Then capacity, if intake stays high. State your assumptions.

## Segment 6 (steps)

Structure the five-minute readout. The headline answer. Three findings, one slide each. One recommendation with its calculation. Caveats, such as measures the org doesn't track. And the ask: what decision you need, and by when. Practice out loud, and if you run long, cut a finding, not the caveats.

## Segment 7 (code)

Package it for your portfolio. A one-page README with questions, definitions, method, findings and recommendation. Screenshots with real customer names removed. Use a Developer Edition or sandbox with sample data, never confidential employer data. Note what you decided yourself this time, because interviewers ask.
