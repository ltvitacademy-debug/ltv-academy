# Project 2: Wrap-Up & Presentation

You have reports. Now you need an answer. The Head of Support at Alder & Vale Systems doesn't want twelve reports, she wants to know whether the queue is growing, whether customers are being served fast enough, and whether the team needs a hire or a better process. This lesson turns your analysis into a dashboard, a recommendation, and a five-minute readout. All figures are illustrative.

## What you'll learn

- How to assemble a service dashboard that mirrors the three business questions
- How to write findings that lead with the answer
- How to build a recommendation with a capacity calculation behind it
- How to package Project 2 as a portfolio piece

## Assemble the dashboard

Use the layout rule from earlier: headline numbers on top, charts in the middle, detail at the bottom. For this project:

- **Top:** open backlog (210), SLA compliance (88 percent), average resolution (38 hours), and CSAT (4.3, with its response count)
- **Middle:** cases opened versus closed by month, and breach rate by priority
- **Bottom:** the agent scorecard as a table, and escalations by priority

Title each component with its point, such as "Backlog up 40% this quarter", rather than the report name. Choose the running user deliberately: everyone viewing sees what that user sees. Then reconcile: every tile should match its source report when you click through.

## Lead with the answer

Write findings in the order a busy manager needs them.

1. **The queue is growing.** Backlog rose from 150 to 210 (+40 percent). Monthly intake climbed from 380 to 450 (+18 percent) while the team closed about 393 a month on average.
2. **Speed is uneven.** Overall resolution averages 38 hours and 88 percent of cases meet their SLA target, but High-priority cases breach about 23 percent of the time and escalate 28 percent of the time.
3. **The agent gap is small.** After adjusting for case mix, the four agents are within a few hours of expected performance.

## Build a recommendation with math

"Hire someone" is an opinion until you quantify it. The team closes about 295 cases per agent per quarter, about 98 a month. September's 450 arrivals against a 393 monthly average closure rate leaves a gap of about 57 cases per month, which is roughly **0.6 of an agent's capacity**. So:

- **Process first:** a dedicated path for High-priority cases, and better triage or self-service for the large volume of Low-priority cases
- **Then capacity:** if intake stays at 450 after process changes, a fifth agent, or part of one, is justified

State the assumptions, because intake could fall, and the numbers come from one quarter.

## The five-minute readout

1. The headline answer, in one sentence
2. Three findings, one slide each, each with a chart
3. One recommendation with its calculation
4. Caveats: what the data can't tell you, such as any measure the org doesn't track
5. The ask: what decision you need, and by when

Practice out loud and time it. If you need more than five minutes, cut a finding, not the caveats.

## Package it for your portfolio

- A one-page README: business questions, definitions, method, findings, recommendation
- Screenshots of the dashboard, with any real customer names removed
- A short note on your data source. Use a Developer Edition or sandbox with sample data you generated, never a real employer's data or anything confidential

Compare against Project 1: what did you decide yourself this time that the lesson used to decide for you? Note it in the README, because interviewers ask about exactly that.

## Recap

A wrap-up converts analysis into a decision. Answer first, back it with reconciled numbers and a capacity calculation, name your caveats, and package the work so someone else can follow it.

## Check yourself

If September's intake is 450 cases a month, average closure is 393, and an agent closes about 98 a month, how many agents' worth of capacity are you short, and what would you try before hiring?
