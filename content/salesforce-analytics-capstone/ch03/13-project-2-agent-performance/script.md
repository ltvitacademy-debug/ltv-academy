# Script — Agent Performance Analysis

## Segment 1 (title)

The Head of Support asked whether the team needs a hire or a better process. To answer, you'll compare four agents, without making the classic mistake of treating every agent's work as identical.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Build a scorecard. Cases report type, closed this quarter, grouped by case owner. Add record count, average resolution hours, and satisfaction if the org collects it. Then reconcile: closed counts should add to the team total from your earlier reports.

## Segment 3 (code)

Illustrative results. Ines closed 340 cases at 34 hours. Ravi 310 at 36. Hannah 290 at 40. Omar 240 at 45. The weighted average lands at about 38, matching the team figure. At a glance, Omar looks eleven hours slower than the team.

## Segment 4 (code)

But look at case mix. Group by owner and priority in a matrix. Omar's closed cases were 45 percent low priority, versus about 25 percent for Ines and Ravi. Low priority takes about 61 hours for everyone. He was handed the slowest kind of work.

## Segment 5 (code)

So compare expected against actual. Weight the team's average hours for each priority by each agent's mix. Omar's expected time is about 43 hours, versus 45 actual, a two hour gap, not eleven. Ines is about three hours faster than expected. Hannah is about two slower. Check the math yourself.

## Segment 6 (steps)

Mind the pitfalls. Case owner can be a queue. Reassignments credit the current owner. And satisfaction samples per agent are small, so always show response counts. Present the data as workload and coaching, never as a public ranking, and never rely on one metric alone.

## Segment 7 (outro)

Your turn. Build the scorecard and the mix matrix, compute one expected value by hand, and write two sentences for the Head of Support: what the data says, and what it can't say. Up next, lesson fourteen: project two wrap-up and presentation.
