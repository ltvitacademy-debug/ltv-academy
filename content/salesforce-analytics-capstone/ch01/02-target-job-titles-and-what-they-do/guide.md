# Target Job Titles & What They Actually Do

A job title tells you very little. Two postings both titled "Salesforce Data Analyst" can describe completely different work: one is a reporting seat inside a sales operations team, the other is closer to a business intelligence developer. This lesson gives you a working map of the titles this path targets, what people in them actually do in a normal week, and how to read a posting so you can tell the difference before you apply.

## What you'll learn

- The four core target titles and the day-to-day work behind each
- Adjacent titles that use the same skills but go by different names
- How to decode a posting into must-haves, nice-to-haves, and buzzwords
- How your earlier courses map onto the keywords you will see

## The four core titles

**Salesforce Data Analyst.** A typical week: a sales leader asks why pipeline dropped, you check filters and data quality, build or fix a report, write a SOQL query for the part reports cannot answer, and present the result in a dashboard or a short readout. Expect a lot of stakeholder conversation and a lot of definition work ("what exactly counts as a qualified lead?").

**Senior Salesforce Data Analyst.** The same work plus ownership. You maintain the trusted metric definitions, review other people's reports, decide what the analytics team should and should not build, and present to executives. Postings tend to emphasize stakeholder management, mentoring, and a track record of decisions your analysis influenced.

**CRM Analytics Developer.** Less ad hoc questioning, more building. You design datasets and recipes, write dashboards in Salesforce's advanced analytics tooling, tune performance, and deploy between environments. Postings tend to mention specific tooling by name. The next advanced course in this path is aimed at this title.

**Salesforce Analytics Engineer.** Your work product is the data pipeline and model rather than the dashboard. Postings mention a warehouse (often Snowflake), a modeling tool (often dbt), Git-based workflows, testing, and sometimes Data Cloud. Analytics still matters, but the emphasis is reliability and reuse.

## Adjacent titles worth searching

Many people who do this work are not titled "Salesforce" anything. Current postings suggest you should also search for **Sales Operations Analyst**, **Revenue Operations (RevOps) Analyst**, **Business Systems Analyst**, **CRM Analyst**, and **Reporting Analyst**. A **Salesforce Administrator** posting sometimes carries heavy reporting duties, and is a common way in. Read the responsibilities, not just the title, and treat titles as company-specific labels rather than standard definitions.

## How to read a posting

Sort every line into three buckets:

- **Must-haves:** the skills repeated in both the summary and the requirements, or tied to the first responsibilities listed. If you can honestly say you have these, apply.
- **Nice-to-haves:** anything introduced with "preferred," "a plus," or "familiarity with." Postings are wish lists; few candidates match all of them.
- **Buzzwords:** vague phrases like "data-driven mindset" that tell you little. Look for the concrete verbs and tools instead.

Then translate the concrete tools into your training:

| Keyword in a posting | Where you learned it |
|---|---|
| SQL, joins, aggregation | T-SQL Development |
| SOQL, Data Loader, data quality | SOQL & Salesforce Data Management |
| Reports, dashboards, formulas | Salesforce Reports & Dashboards |
| Tableau, visualization | Tableau |
| Excel, pivot tables, lookups | Advanced Excel |
| CRM Analytics, SAQL, Tableau Next | The next advanced course |
| Snowflake, dbt, Git | The Analytics Engineer courses |

Certifications sometimes appear as preferred qualifications. This path's listed credential is the Salesforce Certified Tableau Data Analyst; a Salesforce Administrator certification is also frequently listed as preferred in postings, so check what your target employers actually ask for.

## Recap

Titles vary by company, so read the responsibilities. The four core titles move from answering questions, to owning definitions, to building reusable assets, to engineering pipelines. Search adjacent titles like Sales Operations and RevOps Analyst too. Sort each posting into must-haves, nice-to-haves, and buzzwords, and map its tools to the courses you have finished.
