# The Salesforce Analytics Career Ladder

You have now spent several courses building a real toolkit: T-SQL, an understanding of what Salesforce data means, SOQL and data management, native reports and dashboards, Tableau, and Advanced Excel. This final course turns that toolkit into evidence. Before you build three portfolio projects, it helps to know exactly what those projects are supposed to prove, and to whom. That starts with the career ladder you are climbing.

## What you'll learn

- The five rungs of the Salesforce analytics ladder and what changes at each one
- Why the real jump is from *answering* questions to *building* what lets others answer them
- How to read the pay picture honestly, without treating any single number as a promise
- Where this course, and the courses after it, fit on the ladder

## The five rungs

**1. Salesforce / CRM Data Analyst.** You answer specific business questions from Salesforce data. Day to day that means native reports and dashboards, SOQL for the questions reports cannot answer, Tableau or Excel for presentation, and a lot of conversations with sales, service, and operations leaders about what a number is supposed to mean. This is the entry point for the path.

**2. Senior Salesforce Data Analyst.** Same tools, bigger scope. You own the analytics agenda for a function, define the metrics everyone else reuses (what counts as pipeline, what counts as a win), mentor newer analysts, and push back when a request will produce a misleading number.

**3. CRM Analytics Developer.** Here the job changes shape. Instead of answering one question at a time, you build reusable analytics assets inside Salesforce's advanced analytics tooling: datasets, recipes, and dashboards that hundreds of users explore on their own. The next advanced course in this path covers that skill set.

**4. Salesforce Analytics Engineer.** You move the data itself. Salesforce data flows into a warehouse such as Snowflake, gets modeled with a tool such as dbt, is version-controlled in Git, and feeds dashboards that span Salesforce and everything else the company runs.

**5. Senior Salesforce Analytics Engineer.** You design the platform: data models, testing standards, deployment practices, and the decisions other engineers follow. Beyond this rung sit architecture roles, which are a different conversation.

## What actually changes as you climb

Look at the verbs. A Data Analyst *answers*. A Senior Analyst *defines and prioritizes*. A CRM Analytics Developer *builds for reuse*. An Analytics Engineer *engineers the pipeline*. Nothing on the ladder asks you to abandon what you already know. SQL, an understanding of the Salesforce data model, and clear communication are load-bearing at every rung; each new rung adds a layer beneath or above them.

## Reading the pay picture honestly

Path research suggests Salesforce and CRM Data Analyst roles often start in the range of roughly $65K to $95K. The ceiling is much higher, in the range of $150K to $200K or more, for people who combine Salesforce knowledge with CRM Analytics, Data Cloud, Tableau Next, Snowflake, and dbt at the senior or analytics engineer level. One current senior posting that combines exactly that stack listed a base salary above $200K in select markets.

Treat those as directional, not guaranteed. Pay depends on your city, the company's size and industry, and the specific posting. Current postings suggest the pattern (general Salesforce reporting pays modestly, and the premium comes from engineering skills) but always check postings in your own market before you set expectations.

## Where this course fits

This course closes the Job-Ready stage of the path. It does not teach new tools. It asks you to apply the ones you have, on three projects of increasing independence, and then to talk about them convincingly in an interview. The advanced courses that follow (CRM Analytics and Tableau Next, then Snowflake, dbt, and Git-based workflows) are what move you up from rung one.

## Recap

Five rungs: analyst, senior analyst, CRM Analytics developer, analytics engineer, senior analytics engineer. The core shift is from answering questions to building what lets others answer them. Pay is a range that depends on the market, and the premium goes to people who add engineering skills to Salesforce fluency.
