# Script — The Salesforce Analytics Career Ladder

## Segment 1 (title)

You've built a real toolkit: SQL, SOQL, native reports and dashboards, Tableau, and Excel. This course turns that toolkit into evidence. First, the career ladder those projects are meant to prove you can climb.

## Segment 2 (steps: five rungs)

There are five rungs. A Salesforce Data Analyst answers business questions with reports, dashboards, and SOQL. A Senior Analyst owns the metric definitions and the analytics agenda. A CRM Analytics Developer builds reusable datasets and dashboards inside Salesforce. A Salesforce Analytics Engineer moves and models the data in a warehouse. And a Senior Analytics Engineer designs the platform everyone else builds on.

## Segment 3 (code: the verbs)

Look at the verbs. An analyst answers. A senior analyst defines and prioritizes. A developer builds for reuse. An engineer engineers the pipeline. The big shift is from answering questions to building what lets other people answer their own. Nothing here asks you to abandon SQL or the Salesforce data model. Both stay load-bearing at every rung.

## Segment 4 (code: pay, hedged)

Now the money, hedged. Current postings suggest Salesforce data analyst roles often start around sixty-five to ninety-five thousand dollars. Combining Salesforce with CRM Analytics, Data Cloud, Tableau Next, Snowflake, and dbt can reach one-fifty to two hundred thousand or more. Directional, not a promise. Your city, industry, and posting decide the real number.

## Segment 5 (steps: where this course fits)

Where does this course fit? It closes the Job-Ready stage. No new tools, just three projects and interview practice. The advanced courses come next: CRM Analytics and Tableau Next, then Snowflake, dbt, and Git. Whichever rung you target first, the projects in this course are how you show you're ready for it.

## Segment 6 (outro)

Now that you know the ladder, let's look at the actual job titles and what people in them do all day. Along the way, notice which rung each project feels closest to. That tells you where to aim next.
