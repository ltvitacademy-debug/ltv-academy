# Script — Building a Portfolio Strategy

## Segment 1 (title)

A résumé says what you claim you can do. A portfolio shows it. For a career changer, it's often the deciding factor. So before the projects, here's the strategy behind them.

## Segment 2 (steps: what reviewers ask)

Hiring managers are asking four questions. Can this person understand a business problem, not just a tool? Can they get correct answers from messy Salesforce data? Can they present findings a busy leader can act on? And are they honest about limits? A folder of screenshots answers none of those.

## Segment 3 (code: the one-page structure)

Give every project the same skeleton. The question, in the stakeholder's words. The data, and how you prepared it. The method. Three to five findings, each with its number. A recommendation. And limitations. Naming your limits builds trust rather than weakening the work. If a reviewer can skim it in two minutes and still know what you decided, it's working.

## Segment 4 (steps: where to publish)

Publish where reviewers will actually look. A GitHub repository with a README per project. A short PDF or slide deck to send directly. Annotated screenshots, because Salesforce dashboards live inside an org. Tableau Public, but only for data you fully own. Two or three polished projects beat ten half-finished ones.

## Segment 5 (code: protecting real data)

Now the rule career changers break most. Never publish real customer, prospect, employee, or revenue data from a current or former employer. Build in a free Developer Edition org with data you generate, and label every number as illustrative. If you describe real work, generalize the company, scale the figures, and remove every name. Handling data carefully is itself a hiring signal.

## Segment 6 (outro)

That's the strategy. Next, Project One: a sales pipeline analysis for our fictional company. Treat every number as illustrative unless it's yours to publish.
