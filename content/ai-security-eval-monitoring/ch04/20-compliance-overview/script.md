# Script — Compliance, Overview: AI-Specific Regulations

## Segment 1 (title)

A quick hedge first: this isn't legal advice, and AI regulation moves fast. The goal here is conceptual fluency — knowing the shape of the landscape so you can ask the right questions and recognize when a project needs real legal review.

## Segment 2 (steps: GDPR concepts)

GDPR predates generative AI but applies directly to any system processing personal data. Data minimization — collect only what's needed. The right to explanation. Purpose limitation — data collected for one purpose can't silently be reused for another.

## Segment 3 (code: AI Act risk tiers)

The EU AI Act's core idea is risk-tiering: obligations scale with potential harm, not the technology. Unacceptable risk is banned outright. High risk gets heavy obligations. Limited risk gets transparency rules. Minimal risk stays largely unregulated.

## Segment 4 (steps: why this connects back)

This isn't a separate checkbox exercise. Lesson 18's bias testing is part of what high-risk obligations actually ask for. Lesson 19's PII handling is data minimization in practice. Lesson 21's model cards are close to what documentation requirements describe.

## Segment 5 (outro)

The same model can face completely different obligations depending on what it's used for — which is why "is my AI compliant" is an incomplete question on its own. Next up: documentation — model cards, and what they're actually for.
