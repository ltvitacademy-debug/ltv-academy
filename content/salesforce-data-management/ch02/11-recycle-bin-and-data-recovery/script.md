# Script — Recycle Bin and Data Recovery

## Segment 1 (title)

Every tool in this chapter has been about preventing data loss. This lesson covers the safety net that catches everything else: the Recycle Bin, where a normal delete actually goes, and how to get records back out before they're gone for good.

## Segment 2 (screenshot: App Launcher, search Recycle)

If Recycle Bin isn't already in your navigation bar, open the App Launcher, search Recycle, and select it. This works in any app, and you don't need to be an admin to find your own deleted records this way.

## Segment 3 (screenshot: My vs Org Recycle Bin)

The list view toggle matters. My Recycle Bin shows only records you personally deleted. Org Recycle Bin, visible to admins and users with the right permission, shows everything deleted org-wide — useful for recovering something a colleague deleted.

## Segment 4 (screenshot: Restore and Delete)

Select records with the checkboxes and click Restore, and they come back exactly as they were at the moment of deletion. Delete on this screen does something different, though — it permanently removes the record from the Recycle Bin immediately, the same irreversible outcome as a permanent mass delete.

## Segment 5 (steps: 15-day window)

Records sit in the Recycle Bin for fifteen days before Salesforce purges them automatically, or sooner if deleted-data storage fills up. That's exactly why exports and scheduled backups from earlier in this chapter exist as a second layer behind it, not a replacement for it.

## Segment 6 (screenshot: add to navigation)

If the App Launcher search gets old, an admin can add Recycle Bin directly to an app's navigation bar through the Lightning App Builder — thirty seconds of setup that saves a search every time someone needs it.

## Segment 7 (outro)

That closes out getting data in and keeping it safe. Chapter 3 turns to data quality — duplicates, validation, and standardization.
