# Script — Creating a Purchase Order

## Segment 1 (title)

Marcus now builds out the standard purchase order for fifty pump bearings. Let's walk its structure and see where each piece of Dana's requisition actually landed.

## Segment 2 (steps)

A purchase order has a header - the supplier, buyer, and payment terms. A line - the item, carried over from the requisition. A schedule - quantity, price, and delivery date, which can even split into multiple shipments. And a distribution, the same accounting from lesson eight, now attached to the schedule.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Because Marcus processed Dana's requisition directly into this document, most of it is already populated - item, quantity, need-by date, deliver-to location, even the charge account. The buyer's job is to add what a requester couldn't provide, not retype what's already there.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Some fields only exist at the PO level, because they're about the supplier relationship. The supplier site, the payment terms like net thirty, and the match approval level - whether the eventual invoice matches against just the PO, or the PO and a receipt too.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Once Marcus submits it and it's approved, it reaches status Open, meaning it's now available for receiving and invoicing. Nothing before this point ever reached Meridian at all. Up next, lesson twelve: how this purchase order gets approved and actually communicated to the supplier.
