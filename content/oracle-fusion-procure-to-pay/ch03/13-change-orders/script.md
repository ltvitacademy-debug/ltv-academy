# Script — Change Orders

## Segment 1 (title)

The purchase order has been sent to Meridian. Real purchasing rarely stays perfectly static after that point. Let's cover what happens when something about an already-approved purchase order needs to change.

## Segment 2 (steps)

A change order updates an approved purchase order, tracked as a new, numbered version rather than a silent edit. Oracle Fusion keeps the full history, so you can always see exactly what the document looked like before and after, and who made the change.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Not every change gets the same scrutiny. Changes to quantity, price, or a cancellation are usually significant enough to trigger a new approval cycle. Minor changes, like a text note, may apply immediately without reapproval. The exact thresholds are configurable per implementation.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Say Meridian calls Marcus about a small raw material cost increase before they can ship. Marcus creates a change order updating the unit price. Because that's a financial change, it routes back through approval before taking effect, and the purchase order becomes a new version once approved.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Some changes can even apply retroactively, after a receipt has already happened - powerful, but risky, since it can create mismatches that surface later as matching holds. Up next, lesson fourteen: what happens when a purchase order reaches the end of its life - closing and cancelling.
