# Procure-to-Pay Overview

Welcome to Oracle Fusion Procure-to-Pay, the first course in the End-to-End Business Processes stage of the Oracle Fusion Financials Consultant path. You already know General Ledger, Payables, Receivables, Cash Management, Fixed Assets, and Expenses as individual modules. This course does something different: it follows **one purchasing transaction** through every module it touches, in order, so you see how the pieces actually connect in a live Oracle Fusion environment.

## What you'll learn

- What "Procure-to-Pay" (P2P) means as an end-to-end business process, not a single screen
- The five major stages every purchase travels through
- Which Oracle Fusion module owns each stage
- The fictional company and transaction this course follows for all 25 lessons

## The P2P process, stage by stage

Procure-to-Pay is the chain of events between a business deciding it needs something and that business paying for it, with the accounting recorded correctly along the way. In Oracle Fusion Cloud, the chain has five stages:

1. **Requisition** — someone inside the company asks for goods or services (Self Service Procurement).
2. **Purchase order** — a buyer converts an approved request into a formal, legally binding order with a supplier (Purchasing).
3. **Receipt** — the goods arrive, or the service is performed, and someone confirms it (Receiving).
4. **Invoice** — the supplier bills for what was ordered and delivered, and the invoice is matched and validated (Payables).
5. **Payment and posting** — the company pays the supplier, and the transaction's accounting lands in the General Ledger.

Each stage is owned by a different work area and, often, a different person. A requester never touches Payables. An AP processor rarely opens a requisition. Part of what makes P2P hard to learn is that no single person sees the whole thing — which is exactly why this course follows it end to end.

## Why follow one transaction instead of one module at a time

You already learned Payables and General Ledger as standalone courses, focused on configuration and transaction entry inside each module. That is necessary, but it hides a fact every working consultant eventually learns the hard way: a problem that shows up in Payables ("why won't this invoice validate?") is very often caused by a decision made back in the requisition or the purchase order. Following a single transaction across all five stages is how you build the instinct to trace a problem back to its source instead of only staring at the screen where the symptom appeared.

## The company and transaction this course follows

To keep every lesson concrete, this course follows a single fictional company and a single fictional transaction from start to finish:

- **Company:** LTV Manufacturing Corporation, a fictional industrial manufacturer used elsewhere in this path's capstone.
- **The need:** the maintenance team at LTV's plant is down to its last few spare bearings for a production line motor and needs to replace its stock.
- **The item:** Industrial Pump Bearing, Model PB-4400, a stocked maintenance, repair, and operations (MRO) item.
- **The supplier:** Meridian Bearing Supply Co., a fictional, preferred supplier already set up in LTV's Oracle Fusion instance.
- **The people:** Dana Whitfield (maintenance supervisor, the requester), Marcus Ibarra (the buyer), Priya Nandan (the receiving clerk), and Chen Liu (the AP processor).

All of these names, the company, and the transaction are invented for teaching purposes. No real company's data, screenshots, or records are used anywhere in this course — Oracle Fusion Cloud is login-gated software with no public screenshots to show, so every lesson in this course uses diagrams, process steps, and plain description instead.

## How the next 24 lessons are organized

Chapter 1 finishes the groundwork (roles, work areas, and the setup you need to recognize, not configure). Chapter 2 creates and approves Dana's requisition. Chapter 3 turns it into Marcus's purchase order. Chapter 4 receives the bearings at the dock. Chapter 5 matches and pays Meridian's invoice and follows the accounting into the General Ledger. Chapter 6 replays the whole cycle and adds exception scenarios, the kind every real consultant eventually has to untangle.

## Recap

Procure-to-Pay is the five-stage journey — requisition, purchase order, receipt, invoice, payment and posting — that every purchase takes through Oracle Fusion Cloud, crossing multiple modules and multiple people along the way. This course follows one fictional transaction, LTV Manufacturing Corporation's purchase of pump bearings from Meridian Bearing Supply Co., through all five stages. Next up, lesson 2: the specific roles and work areas involved in procurement.
