# Following One Transaction: The Plan

This is the last lesson before the transaction actually starts moving. Here is the complete map of where LTV Manufacturing Corporation's bearing purchase goes, lesson by lesson, so you always know where you are in the cycle and what to watch for.

## What you'll learn

- The full transaction, stated once, end to end
- Which lesson in this course covers which step
- The key number and status fields you will track across chapters
- What "good" looks like at each checkpoint, so you can recognize when something has gone wrong later

## The transaction, stated in full

LTV Manufacturing Corporation's plant maintenance team, led by Dana Whitfield, is down to a dangerously low stock of Industrial Pump Bearing, Model PB-4400, used in several production line motors. Dana submits a requisition for 50 units. It routes for approval, and once approved, Marcus Ibarra, the buyer, processes it into a standard purchase order with Meridian Bearing Supply Co., LTV's preferred supplier for this part. The purchase order is approved and transmitted to Meridian. Several days later, a truck arrives at LTV's dock; Priya Nandan, the receiving clerk, receives the bearings, which go through an inspection step before being put away into inventory. Meridian then sends an invoice for the shipment. Chen Liu, the AP processor, matches the invoice to the purchase order and the receipt, validates it, and it is accounted and paid. The accounting entries created in Payables and Receiving flow into the General Ledger, where they become part of LTV's financial statements.

## The map, chapter by chapter

- **Chapter 2 — Requisitions (Lessons 5–9):** Dana creates her requisition through Self Service Procurement, it routes through approval, its accounting distribution is determined, and it is processed into a purchase order.
- **Chapter 3 — Purchase Orders (Lessons 10–14):** Marcus builds a standard purchase order, it is approved and communicated to Meridian, and you learn how change orders and closing/cancelling work using this same PO as the example.
- **Chapter 4 — Receiving (Lessons 15–18):** Priya receives the shipment, it is routed for inspection, and you learn how corrections and returns work, plus how the receipt generates an accrual.
- **Chapter 5 — Invoice to Ledger (Lessons 19–23):** Chen matches Meridian's invoice, it is accounted and paid, the transaction's journals land in the General Ledger, and you learn to reconcile and troubleshoot the whole cycle.
- **Chapter 6 — Practice (Lessons 24–25):** the complete cycle is replayed end to end in one sitting, followed by a set of exception scenarios that stress-test what you have learned.

## Key fields to track across the whole course

A handful of identifiers connect every document in this transaction, and recognizing them is how you will trace the transaction across chapters:

- **Requisition number** — created in Chapter 2, referenced on the purchase order line.
- **Purchase order number** — created in Chapter 3, referenced on every receipt and invoice distribution that follows.
- **Receipt number** — created in Chapter 4, the link between what was ordered and what was actually matched on the invoice.
- **Invoice number** — created in Chapter 5, matched back to both the purchase order and the receipt.
- **Quantity and amount** — 50 units at an agreed unit price; watching this number stay consistent (or deliberately not, in exception scenarios) is how you will understand matching tolerances later.

## What "good" looks like at each checkpoint

At each stage, there is a clean, expected outcome: the requisition is approved and shows status "Approved"; the purchase order is approved and shows status "Open"; the receipt is created with no discrepancy and the item is put away; the invoice matches within tolerance and validates without a hold; the payment clears and the accounting posts to GL without a reconciling difference. Chapter 6 deliberately breaks several of these checkpoints on purpose so you can practice recognizing when a real transaction has gone off this path.

## Recap

The whole course follows one 50-unit purchase of pump bearings from Meridian Bearing Supply Co., mapped chapter by chapter: requisition, purchase order, receiving, invoice to ledger, and practice. A small set of document numbers and a quantity/amount figure are what tie every stage together. Next up, lesson 5: Dana's requisition actually begins, starting with Self Service Procurement.
