# Script — Returns and Corrections

## Segment 1 (title)

LTV's fifty bearings passed inspection cleanly, so let's step briefly outside the main transaction to cover what Priya would do if they hadn't. Returns and corrections come up often enough in real receiving work to understand both.

## Segment 2 (steps)

A return to supplier reverses a receipt, in whole or in part, sending material back - usually because it failed inspection or arrived damaged. If five bearings had been rejected for a cosmetic defect, Priya would return those five, dropping the received quantity to forty-five until Meridian ships replacements.

## Segment 3 (steps)

A correction is different - it fixes a recording mistake on LTV's side, not a physical decision about the goods. Maybe Priya typed fifty-five instead of fifty, or picked the wrong line. A correction adjusts the receipt transaction itself to reflect reality.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Both change the quantity available for matching later. If the received quantity is lower than what's invoiced because of an unprocessed return, the invoice won't match within tolerance and lands on hold instead of getting paid - exactly the kind of exception chapter six works through.

## Segment 5 (outro)

LTV's transaction needs neither, since the shipment arrived correctly and was entered correctly. But now you know the mechanism before chapter six's exception scenarios put it to use. Up next, lesson eighteen: how the receipt itself generates accounting through receipt accounting and accruals.
