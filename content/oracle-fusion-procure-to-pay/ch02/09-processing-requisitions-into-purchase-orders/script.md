# Script — Processing Requisitions into Purchase Orders

## Segment 1 (title)

Dana's requisition is fully approved, with its distribution set. It's still just a request. This is the handoff - how it becomes Marcus's problem, and how it turns into an actual purchase order.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Once approved, a requisition lands in a buyer's Process Requisitions worklist. Marcus, as LTV's assigned procurement agent, sees Dana's line waiting for action. Lines can route to a specific buyer automatically by category, or sit in a pool a team can pick up from.

## Segment 3 (steps)

For many lines, the supplier is already chosen. Dana's bearing came from a catalog tied to Meridian as the negotiated source, so Marcus mostly confirms it's still correct rather than sourcing from scratch. A non-catalog line would require actual sourcing work first.

## Segment 4 (steps)

One real advantage of separating requisitions from purchase orders is consolidation. A buyer can pull multiple approved lines from different requesters that share a supplier and combine them onto one purchase order - fewer documents, better leverage. This transaction keeps it simple with just Dana's one line.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Marcus manually processes Dana's line into a new draft standard purchase order - not yet approved, not yet sent to Meridian. Up next, chapter three: building that purchase order in full, starting with the purchase order types available.
