# Script — Self Service Procurement and Requisitions

## Segment 1 (title)

This is where LTV Manufacturing's transaction actually begins. Dana needs more pump bearings, and she's going to ask for them the same way almost every employee asks for anything in Oracle Fusion Cloud - through Self Service Procurement.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Self Service Procurement is built to feel like shopping: a search bar, a cart, catalogs by category, and a checkout step. It lets ordinary employees request goods and services directly, instead of routing every request through a dedicated buyer who has no idea what a bearing even looks like.

## Segment 3 (steps)

There are a few ways to add something to a cart. Catalogs hold pre-loaded items with known prices, like Dana's bearing. Smart forms guide structured requests, like a laptop configuration. Non-catalog requests cover items with no catalog entry at all. And punchout sends the requester to a supplier's own website and brings the cart back.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Structurally, a requisition is a header plus one or more lines - each line has an item, a quantity, a need-by date, and a distribution that decides what cost center gets charged. On its own, a requisition does nothing to a supplier. It has to be approved and processed into a purchase order first.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Self service doesn't mean no rules. Dana can request bearings because her catalog access allows it, but she can't approve her own request, and unusual purchases still route for scrutiny. Up next, lesson six: actually creating Dana's requisition.
