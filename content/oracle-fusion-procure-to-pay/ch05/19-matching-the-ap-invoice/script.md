# Script — Matching the AP Invoice

## Segment 1 (title)

Meridian sends its invoice for the fifty bearings. Chen, the AP processor, now has to confirm what Meridian is billing for actually matches what was ordered and what was received, before LTV pays a dollar.

## Segment 2 (steps)

Matching compares an invoice against the purchase order, and often the receipt, before payment is allowed. Without it, a company would just trust whatever a supplier billed, with no independent check on price, quantity, or whether the goods even arrived.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Two-way matching only checks the invoice against the purchase order. Three-way matching adds the receipt - quantity billed can't exceed quantity received. Four-way adds a formal acceptance document on top of that. LTV's purchase order requires three-way matching, since physical delivery really matters here.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Matching doesn't demand perfect agreement. Tolerances allow a small, configured percentage of variance in price or quantity through without flagging a problem - normal rounding and shipping variance, not fraud.

## Segment 5 (outro)

If an invoice falls outside tolerance, Payables places a matching hold, and it can't be paid until someone resolves it. Meridian's invoice here matches cleanly against both the purchase order and the receipt - no hold needed. Up next, lesson twenty: what happens once the invoice is validated - payment and accounting.
