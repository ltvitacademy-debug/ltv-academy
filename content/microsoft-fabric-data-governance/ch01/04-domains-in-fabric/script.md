# Lesson 4 — Domains in Fabric · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Last lesson previewed the Domains page. This lesson walks it end to end.

## S2 · STEPS — What a domain groups

A domain groups workspaces by business area — Finance, Health, Education — for org-wide discovery and governance. That's different from a workspace's own permissions, and different from a capacity, which groups by compute and region, not business meaning. Subdomains exist for further segmentation inside a domain, like Health splitting into Clinics and Hospitals.

## S3 · SCREENSHOT — A domain's own page

Opening a domain shows its workspace list. Here, Clinics and Hospitals are subdomains; US South and Alaska are workspaces assigned directly. Domain settings — Microsoft's own highlight box — is where the rest of this walkthrough happens.

## S4 · SCREENSHOT — General settings

Domain settings opens to General settings first: just a name, and an optional description. The domain's identity, nothing governance-specific yet.

## S5 · SCREENSHOT — Admins

The Admins tab is the broadest level of control. Domain admins can change domain settings and add or remove workspaces.

## S6 · SCREENSHOT — Contributors

Contributors is narrower: specifically who can add or remove workspaces from the domain, not who can change its other settings. Three choices — the entire organization, specific users or groups, or tenant and domain admins only — and by default it's open to the whole org.

## S7 · SCREENSHOT — Assigning workspaces

Assigning workspaces has three methods. By workspace name, one at a time. By workspace admin, sweeping in everything a given person administers. Or by capacity — every workspace on a given capacity, assigned into the domain in one move.

## S8 · STEPS — Beyond membership

A domain can carry more than membership — a default sensitivity label, or a certification default, applied to everything assigned to it. Just a preview here; both get their own full lessons later in the course.
