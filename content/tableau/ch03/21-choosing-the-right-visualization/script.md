# Script — Choosing the Right Visualization

## Segment 1 (title)

Tableau's own documentation puts it plainly: form follows function. The chart you build should depend on the question you're asking and the properties of your data — not on which chart type looks the most impressive, or which one you reach for out of habit.

## Segment 2 (screenshot: magnitude category)

Tableau organizes chart selection around a set of common analytical questions. Magnitude is one of them — comparing the relative size of discrete items, like which region has the highest sales. That's what a bar chart is built for.

## Segment 3 (screenshot: correlation category)

Correlation is another — looking for a relationship between two measures, like whether higher sales tend to come with higher profit. That's a scatter plot's job, with a trend line to describe the relationship statistically.

## Segment 4 (steps: the three-question process)

Before opening Show Me, ask three questions in order: what's the actual question I'm trying to answer, which of Tableau's categories does it fall under, and which chart type in that category fits the dimensions and measures I actually have. The chart type alone never tells you the question — your framing does.
