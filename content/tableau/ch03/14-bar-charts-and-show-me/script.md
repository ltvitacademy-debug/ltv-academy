# Script — Bar Charts & Using Show Me

## Segment 1 (title)

A bar chart starts the same way most Tableau charts do: a dimension on one shelf, a measure on the other. Switch the mark type to Bar, and stack it by dropping a second dimension on Color — the bars still show the total, while their colored segments show what's inside it.

## Segment 2 (screenshot: stacked bar chart)

Here's a finished stacked bar chart, straight from Tableau's own documentation: Sales by year for the West region, split into colored segments by Ship Mode, with the total labeled above each bar. You can read the overall trend and the breakdown at the same time — that dual read is why bar charts are usually the first chart type you reach for when comparing category totals.

## Segment 3 (screenshot: Show Me panel)

This is Show Me, the panel in the top-right of the toolbar. It looks at the fields already in your view, or selected in the Data pane, and lights up the chart types those fields can support — hover over any icon and it tells you the minimum requirements. Show Me reads requirements, not intent. It can't tell you which chart best answers your business question; that judgment call is still yours.
