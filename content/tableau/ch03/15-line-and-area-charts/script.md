# Script — Line Charts & Area Charts

## Segment 1 (title)

Drop a date on Columns and a measure on Rows, and Tableau's default mark type is already Line — dates are the one field type that nudges Tableau toward a line automatically, because a line's whole job is showing a sequence.

## Segment 2 (screenshot: dual-axis line chart)

Add a second measure to Rows and Tableau gives each one its own axis along the left margin — this is a dual-axis view, two independent scales sharing one timeline. That's different from a blended axis, where you drag one measure onto the other's axis so both share a single scale — useful when the two measures are close enough in size to compare directly.

## Segment 3 (screenshot: stacked area chart)

An area chart is a line chart with the space beneath it filled in. Change the mark type to Area and add a dimension to Color, and the filled shape splits into stacked bands. The top edge is the running total; each band's thickness is that segment's share. A line draws your eye to the shape of change — an area chart draws your eye to cumulative volume, which is also why precise comparison in the middle of a stack gets harder.
