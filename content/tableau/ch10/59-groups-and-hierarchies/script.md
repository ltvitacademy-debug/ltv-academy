# Script — Groups & Hierarchies

## Segment 1 (title)

This lesson covers two ways to reorganize the dimensions you already have: groups, which manually combine specific members into one, and hierarchies, which nest fields together so you can drill up and down.

## Segment 2 (screenshot: Edit Group dialog)

This is the real Edit Group dialog. Here, Bookcases and Tables — two separate Sub-Category members — have been combined into a single group called Bookcases & Tables. Grouping doesn't touch your underlying data; it creates a new field, right alongside the original, that you can rename, ungroup, or use to bucket everything else into an "Other" member.

## Segment 3 (screenshot: hierarchy in the data pane)

This is a real hierarchy in Tableau's Data pane, named Products, built by dragging Sub-Category on top of Category. Once it exists, dragging just the top of the hierarchy into the view gives you a plus icon to drill down and a minus icon to roll back up — no manually swapping fields required. Tableau does this automatically for every date field too, breaking it into year, quarter, month, and day.

## Segment 4 (steps: group vs. hierarchy vs. set)

A group manually combines specific members into one new member. A hierarchy nests fields you already have so you can drill through them. A set, which is next lesson, adds something neither of these does: a dynamic, rule-based subset that can update itself as your data changes.
