# Script — Gathering Process Requirements

## Segment 1 (title)

Chapter 3 assumed you already had a clear requirement to design against. This chapter backs up one step and asks: how do you actually get that requirement out of a stakeholder in the first place?

## Segment 2 (code: a symptom vs. a root cause)

A stakeholder's first sentence is a symptom, not a requirement. "People are confused about approval" could mean almost anything. Discovery is what finds the actual root cause underneath it -- in this case, that no entry criteria exists at all, so every request gets the same informal review regardless of length.

## Segment 3 (steps: five discovery questions)

Five questions surface what a complaint leaves out on its own. What exactly triggers the problem -- get the real number, not "a lot." Who should actually be involved, by named role, not "someone in management." What happens today, step by step, when it goes wrong. And are there exceptions nobody's mentioned yet.

## Segment 4 (steps: how will we know it's fixed)

One more question matters just as much: how will we know this is actually fixed? That needs a measurable outcome -- zero scheduling conflicts, say -- not a vague sense that things feel better now.

## Segment 5 (code: where Mill Creek's numbers came from)

Lesson 14's lab brief stated specific numbers -- over five days, two scheduling conflicts this quarter. Those numbers are exactly what a real discovery conversation produces. HR wouldn't volunteer "five days" unprompted; a well-asked question is what gets a vague complaint to that level of precision.

## Segment 6 (outro)

Requirements gathered is only half the job. Next lesson covers putting the process on paper before building anything at all, so the design actually matches what was agreed to.
