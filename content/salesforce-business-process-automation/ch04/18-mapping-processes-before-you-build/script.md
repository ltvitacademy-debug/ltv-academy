# Script — Mapping Processes Before You Build

## Segment 1 (title)

Last lesson of this course. Requirements are gathered -- now there's one more step before anything gets built: putting the process down on paper.

## Segment 2 (steps: a map shows order)

A requirements list gives you the trigger, the approver, the exceptions. A map gives you something a list can't -- the actual order things happen in, and every branch along the way. That's often exactly where a stakeholder catches a gap: in the planning conversation, not after something's already been built.

## Segment 3 (code: a lightweight notation)

The notation here is deliberately simple. A box is a single action. A diamond is a decision point, with labeled paths coming out of it. An arrow, labeled with the outcome that leads there, connects one to the next. No diagramming software required -- a whiteboard or a shared doc works fine.

## Segment 4 (code: mapping Mill Creek's process)

Here's Mill Creek's time-off process mapped out in full. A request comes in. A decision on whether it's over five days. Two different paths depending on the manager's decision. Every box on this map becomes a final action in the eventual design; every diamond becomes entry criteria or an approval decision.

## Segment 5 (steps: the whole course together)

And that's really the whole course in one shape. Chapters one and two built the declarative toolbox. Chapter three applied all of it to real designs, with documentation and testing. Chapter four added the piece that actually comes first in practice -- asking good questions, then mapping the answer, before ever touching Setup.
