# Script — Business Process Automation Practice Lab

## Segment 1 (title)

Two worked examples are behind you now. This lesson hands you a brand-new scenario and asks you to design the approval process yourself, using the exact same method.

## Segment 2 (steps: the lab brief)

Mill Creek Design Studio, a fictional architecture firm, has a Time-Off Request object. HR's complaint, in their own words: requests over five consecutive days get approved by whoever happens to see the email first -- sometimes a peer, not a manager -- and it's already caused two scheduling conflicts this quarter.

## Segment 3 (steps: your lab tasks)

Your job has five parts. Write the entry criteria. Design the approval step or steps and decide if one tier is enough. Decide whether the record should lock while pending, and be ready to justify it. Write the final approval and rejection actions. And document the whole thing using Lesson 13's five-question template.

## Segment 4 (code: acceptance criteria)

Check your own work against this list before calling it done. Entry criteria that only catches requests over five days, not every request. One named approver tier, not just "someone." A locking decision that's actually justified. Both final actions written out. And all five documentation questions answered.

## Segment 5 (code: a common first-draft mistake)

Watch for the most common mistake here: entry criteria written as "more than zero days requested," which routes even a single sick day into the same approval queue HR specifically wanted reserved for longer requests. Re-read your own criteria against the actual complaint before moving on.

## Segment 6 (outro)

A design that checks every box on that list still hasn't been proven to actually work. Next lesson covers how to test business processes properly before they ever touch a real record.
