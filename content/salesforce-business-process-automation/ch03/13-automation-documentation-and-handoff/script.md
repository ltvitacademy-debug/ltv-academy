# Script — Automation Documentation and Handoff

## Segment 1 (title)

Two case studies are built now -- a quote approval and an SLA escalation. Here's the question that actually matters six months from now: how does the next person understand what you built, without you standing next to them explaining it?

## Segment 2 (steps: the builder always leaves)

The builder always leaves, eventually -- a new role, a different team, or just a faded memory of the details. What's left behind is either a document that answers the obvious questions, or an automation the next admin has to carefully reverse-engineer by clicking through Setup, hoping they don't break something nobody remembers the reason for.

## Segment 3 (steps: five things to write down)

Five things, every single time. The purpose, in one sentence. The exact trigger or entry criteria. Who it affects -- which users, profiles, or owners. What it depends on. And who owns it, with a date it was last reviewed against reality.

## Segment 4 (code: lesson 11's process, documented)

Applied to Harborline's quote approval from Lesson 11, that's under ten lines -- purpose, trigger, who's affected, dependencies, and an owner. Anyone inheriting this can answer "what would break if I changed the entry criteria" without opening Setup first.

## Segment 5 (code: a document isn't a handoff)

But a document nobody knows to look for isn't really a handoff -- it's just a file sitting somewhere. Real handoff means it lives in a predictable place, and the current owner actually walks the next person through it at least once.
