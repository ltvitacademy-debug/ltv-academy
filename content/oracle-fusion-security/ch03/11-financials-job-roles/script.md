# Script — Financials Job Roles

## Segment 1 (title)

Chapters one and two built the general vocabulary: roles, privileges, data security, provisioning. Chapter three applies all of it specifically to Financials, starting with the predefined job roles you'll actually encounter in GL, Payables, and Receivables.

## Segment 2 (steps)

General Ledger predefines General Accounting Manager, who oversees the accounting function and period close; General Accountant, who handles day to day journal entry, reconciliation, and inquiry; and Financial Analyst, focused on reporting rather than transaction entry.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Payables has a clearer supervisory hierarchy. Accounts Payable Manager manages the department and sets policy. Accounts Payable Supervisor oversees AP Specialists. Accounts Payable Invoice Supervisor focuses specifically on invoice processing oversight. And Accounts Payable Specialist enters invoices, ensuring accuracy and completeness — the role you first saw back in lesson one.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Receivables mirrors that structure: Accounts Receivable Manager handles policy, process, and reporting end to end, while Accounts Receivable Specialist runs day to day customer payment activities. Underneath, every one of these is really a bundle of duty roles — a manager role usually inherits everything the specialist role has, plus more, like approval-limit configuration.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Oracle ships these pre-built so most companies don't design access from scratch. At Castellan Robotics, the real implementation work went into which business units each role assignment covered, not redesigning what the roles could do.
