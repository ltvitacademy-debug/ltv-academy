# Lesson 16 — Applying Labels to Data · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

Getting a sensitivity label onto a Data Map asset isn't one step. It's a short chain — and skipping one link breaks the rest.

## S2 · SCREENSHOT (flow diagram)

Create labels, register the asset's source, scan it, classifications get found, and only then does a label actually get applied. The scan itself never applies a label directly — it only produces classifications.

## S3 · STEPS CARD (the missing piece)

What connects classifications to an actual label is an auto-labeling policy. Build one under Information Protection, Auto-labeling policies — name it, pick a label scoped to Files and other data assets, and choose your registered Data Map sources as the location.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (the trigger)

Define rules using Microsoft's prebuilt classifiers as the trigger — custom sensitive info types and trainable classifiers aren't supported here. Give it about 15 minutes to saturate, then scan the source again.

## S5 · SCREENSHOT (filter search results)

Once labels start landing, the Unified Catalog's search filters let you find assets by label directly — right alongside source type, classification, and glossary term filters.

## S6 · SCREENSHOT (labeled asset detail)

Open a labeled asset, and the label sits right next to its name, alongside the classifications that triggered it — a clear audit trail from raw data to applied protection.

## S7 · STEPS CARD (recap)

Five links: create the label, register, scan, classifications found, label applied through a policy. Every one of them has to hold for the chain to work.

## S8 · OUTRO CARD

That wraps up classifications and labels. Next chapter: the Data Catalog and Unified Catalog — making all of this actually discoverable.
