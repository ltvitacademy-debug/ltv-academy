# Lesson 15 — Sensitivity Labels · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

---

## S1 · TITLE CARD

A classification describes what kind of data something is. A sensitivity label describes how sensitive it is — and it can actually protect it, not just describe it.

## S2 · SCREENSHOT (sensitivity labels list)

Labels get created in Information Protection, Sensitivity labels. Priority matters — your most restrictive label belongs at the bottom of the list, least restrictive at the top.

## S3 · SCREENSHOT (define scope)

The single most important decision is scope. Files and other data assets, emails, meetings, groups and sites — each one controls what settings you get and where the label becomes selectable at all.

## S4 · STEPS CARD (why scope matters here)

For this course, Files and other data assets is the one that matters most — it's what makes a label reach Microsoft Purview Data Map, Fabric, Azure, AWS, not just Office documents.

## S5 · SCREENSHOT (publish label)

But a label isn't visible to anyone until you publish it through a label policy — choose which labels, which users, and whether there's a default or mandatory setting. Give it up to 24 hours to fully propagate.

## S6 · SCREENSHOT (label in Excel)

Here's what it looks like once published — right on Excel's ribbon and status bar, Confidential, All Employees, applied and visible in the title bar too.

## S7 · STEPS CARD (recap)

Create the label, define its scope, publish it through a policy. Three steps, and users see it show up right where they already work.
