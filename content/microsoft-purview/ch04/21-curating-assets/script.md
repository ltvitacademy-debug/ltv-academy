# Lesson 21 — Curating Assets · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Back in Lesson 18 we saw an asset with no description and no classifications — findable, but not actually useful. This lesson is curating assets: the work that closes that gap.

## S2 · STEPS — From found to trustworthy

Curation has three layers. Description and tags give a search engine plain-language context to match on. Classifications and glossary terms say what's actually inside the asset, and what it means in business terms. And rating and history add social proof — who trusts this asset, and a complete audit trail of every change made to it.

## S3 · SCREENSHOT — The same asset, curated

This is that same "Feature Usage and Adoption" asset from Lesson 18 — after curation. A real description explaining what it's for, five tags, three classifications including Email Address and Geolocation, two linked glossary terms, and a four-star rating from one reviewer.

## S4 · SCREENSHOT — Ratings are social proof

Selecting that star rating opens a full breakdown — the distribution by star count, and the reviewer's own written comment: "I love using this to track Fabric Adoption throughout my division." Curation isn't just metadata — it's trust, visibly earned.

## S5 · STEPS — Who can curate, and how

Not everyone can curate everything. Editing an asset's description, classifications, or terms requires the Data Curator role on the collection that asset lives in. Rating doesn't require that role — any reader can rate. And for scale, bulk edit lets a curator apply terms, classifications, or contacts to many assets at once, instead of one at a time.

## S6 · SCREENSHOT — Nothing is invisible

And every one of those curation actions is logged. Who added classifications, who assigned glossary terms, who updated the description, and exactly when — all the way back to the original automated scan that first brought this asset into the catalog.
