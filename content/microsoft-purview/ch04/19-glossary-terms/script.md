# Lesson 19 — Glossary Terms · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Every organization has its own private vocabulary — what exactly counts as "active customer," or "net revenue." This lesson is glossary terms, Purview's way of making that vocabulary explicit and attached to real data.

## S2 · STEPS — Terms carry policy now

Three things make glossary terms in Purview different from a spreadsheet of definitions. They're a shared vocabulary — the same meaning for "customer" everywhere it's used. They're governed per domain — a term belongs to a governance domain, like Sales or Finance. And they're active, not static — policies attached to a term apply automatically wherever that term gets used.

## S3 · SCREENSHOT — Starting a new term

Creating a new term starts with picking a template. System default gives you just the basic fields. An organization can also define its own richer templates — here, a Contoso Sales Glossary Terms template alongside the default.

## S4 · SCREENSHOT — What a term holds

Microsoft's own callout here marks the Status field — a term is Draft until a steward publishes it. Beyond status, the core fields are straightforward: Name, Definition, Acronym, any supporting Resources, and a Data Privacy classification.

## S5 · STEPS — The term lifecycle

Every term moves through the same lifecycle. It starts as a Draft, not yet visible to business users. It gets submitted for approval, where a steward or approver reviews it. And once approved, it's published — visible in the Enterprise Glossary that end users actually browse.

## S6 · SCREENSHOT — Closing the loop

Microsoft's own callout here marks Submit for approval — the step that moves a drafted, defined term out of someone's workspace and into the approval workflow, on its way to becoming visible to the business.
