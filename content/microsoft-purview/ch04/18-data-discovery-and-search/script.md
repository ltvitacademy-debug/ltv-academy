# Lesson 18 — Data Discovery and Search · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Once data is scanned into Purview, the next question is simple: can a person actually find it? This lesson is data discovery and search.

## S2 · STEPS — Search, or browse

There are two ways in. Search lets you type keywords, partial terms, or even natural language. Browse lets you explore by collection, by source type, or by governance domain when you don't know exactly what you're looking for yet. And governed assets search gives you a focused view of only the data that's actually been curated — described, classified, and trustworthy.

## S3 · SCREENSHOT — The catalog home page

This is the catalog's home page. A big central search bar, with Browse assets, Manage glossary, and Knowledge center shortcuts right below it. Microsoft's own numbered callouts mark the catalog's counts, the search bar, and these shortcut cards.

## S4 · STEPS — What you can search on

Search supports real query syntax, not just a single keyword. You can search on name, type, and classification. You can combine terms with AND, OR, NOT, and exact phrase quotes for precision. And once you have results, a filter pane lets you narrow further by collection, endorsement, owner, tag, or rating.

## S5 · SCREENSHOT — The portal's own search

This is the Purview portal's own top-level search — searching across navigation, people, and resources at once, and ranking results by relevance. The catalog's data-asset search works the same way: a relevance engine, not just a flat keyword match.

## S6 · SCREENSHOT — What a search result opens

Here's what you land on after a search: an asset's detail page. This particular one has no description, no classifications, and no glossary terms yet — nobody's curated it. That gap is exactly what curation, covered in Lesson 21, exists to close.
