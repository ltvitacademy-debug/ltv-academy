# Script — Asset Classes Overview

## Segment 1 (title)

Welcome to Financial Markets and Quantitative Finance, the fourth course on the Quantitative Developer and Researcher path. This course assumes no prior finance knowledge, and builds the vocabulary that quant research and trading roles expect on day one. This first lesson maps the territory: the major asset classes, and how they sit on the risk-return spectrum.

## Segment 2 (steps)

An asset class is a group of instruments that share risk and return characteristics and tend to be regulated the same way. Equities are ownership shares in a company. Fixed income instruments, bonds, are loans that pay scheduled interest. Cash and money market instruments are short-term and highly liquid — this is where capital sits when it isn't deployed elsewhere. Commodities, currencies, derivatives, and alternatives round out the picture, and each gets its own lesson soon.

## Segment 3 (steps)

Line these up by expected risk and you get a rough spectrum. Cash and Treasury bills sit at the low end, about as close to risk-free as a dollar-denominated asset gets. Investment-grade bonds sit above that. Equities carry higher expected return and higher volatility. Commodities and alternatives like real estate and private equity sit higher still, generally less liquid and harder to value day to day.

## Segment 4 (steps)

Derivatives don't fit neatly on that spectrum, because they're different in kind from the other six. Equities, bonds, commodities, currencies, cash, and alternatives are all direct claims on something real. A derivative's value is derived from an underlying asset instead — it has no independent existence. That also means a derivative's risk depends entirely on how it's used: the same option that speculates can instead be used to hedge an existing position.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Hold onto that map: seven groups, one spectrum, and derivatives standing apart because they derive value rather than holding it directly. Up next, lesson two: the exchanges, brokers, and other participants that make trading in any of this possible.
