# Script — Futures & Forwards

## Segment 1 (title)

Lessons six through eight covered equities and bonds, direct claims on something real. This lesson opens the derivatives world with its simplest member: a contract that fixes today the price of a trade that happens later.

## Segment 2 (steps)

A forward and a future are both agreements to buy or sell an asset at a set price on a set future date, but they're structured very differently. Forwards are over the counter — negotiated privately between two parties, customized to their exact needs, with no exchange involved, which means real counterparty risk if the other side defaults. Futures are exchange-traded instead: standardized, and cleared through a central counterparty, which largely removes that counterparty risk.

## Segment 3 (steps)

The other big difference is timing. A forward settles once, at expiration — all the profit or loss builds up silently until then. A future is marked to market daily: every day the exchange settles that day's gain or loss in cash into a margin account. If that account's balance falls too low, the holder gets a margin call and has to add funds or the position gets closed. That daily settlement is exactly why a clearinghouse can safely guarantee futures contracts.

## Segment 4 (code)

The fair futures price isn't a guess — it's set by arbitrage, the cost of carrying the underlying from today until delivery. Take a stock index at four thousand dollars, a four percent risk-free rate, no storage cost or dividend, and six months to delivery. Four thousand times e to the point-zero-two comes out to four thousand eighty dollars and eighty cents. The futures price sits above spot — that's contango, the normal state when financing cost dominates and there's no offsetting yield.

## Segment 5 (outro)

Forwards are private and settle once; futures are standardized and settle daily; and cost of carry pins the fair price to financing, storage, and yield. Up next, lesson ten: foreign exchange basics, closing out chapter two.
