# Quant Researcher, Developer & Trader Roles

Lesson 28 mapped the firms. This lesson maps the jobs inside them. "Quant" is a broad label covering at least three genuinely different roles — researcher, developer, and trader — each with a different mix of skills, and the lines between them blur more at smaller firms than at larger ones.

## What you'll learn

- What a quant researcher actually spends their time doing
- What a quant developer actually spends their time doing
- What a quant trader actually spends their time doing
- Where the three roles overlap, and how firm size changes the picture

## Quant researcher

A quant researcher designs and tests trading signals and models — forming a hypothesis about a pattern in markets, building a model to capture it, and rigorously backtesting whether it would have worked historically (while being careful about overfitting to the past). This role leans heaviest on statistics, probability, and increasingly machine learning, and the day-to-day output is often a signal or model handed off to be traded, not a trade itself.

## Quant developer

A quant developer builds and maintains the infrastructure that research and trading actually run on: data pipelines, backtesting frameworks, execution systems, risk and position-monitoring tools. This role leans heaviest on software engineering — strong coding practices, system design, and performance — and a quant developer may understand the finance well without necessarily originating the trading ideas themselves.

## Quant trader

A quant trader executes strategies and manages risk in real time — deciding how and when to enter and exit positions, managing intraday risk limits, and reacting to live market conditions. This role needs market intuition and decisiveness on top of enough technical fluency to understand (and sometimes extend) the models being traded; it's less about originating a model from scratch and more about running it well under live conditions.

## Where the lines blur

At a large bank or established hedge fund, these three roles are often distinct job titles with different reporting lines and even different required backgrounds. At smaller shops — and especially early-stage prop trading firms or small hedge funds — a single person frequently does two or all three: a researcher who also writes the production code for their own signal, or a trader who builds their own execution tools. Understanding all three skill sets, even if you specialize in one, makes someone more valuable and more flexible across firm sizes.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Quant researcher | Designs and backtests trading signals and models; heavy statistics/ML |
| Quant developer | Builds and maintains trading/research infrastructure; heavy software engineering |
| Quant trader | Executes strategies and manages intraday risk in live markets |
| Backtesting | Testing whether a model or signal would have worked on historical data |
| Overfitting | Building a model that fits past data too closely to generalize to the future |

## Recap

Quant researcher, quant developer, and quant trader are three distinct skill profiles — statistics-heavy, engineering-heavy, and market-intuition-heavy, respectively — and the smaller the firm, the more likely one person wears more than one of these hats.
