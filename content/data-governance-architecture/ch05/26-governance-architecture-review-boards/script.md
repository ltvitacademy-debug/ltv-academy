# Lesson 26 — Governance Architecture Review Boards · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

This closes Chapter Five. A review board is the standing group that keeps making and revisiting architecture decisions as the program evolves — not a one-time committee that disbands after kickoff.

## S2 · STEPS — Who needs a seat

A board that represents every stakeholder becomes too large to decide anything. A workable board is small: a governance lead or chief data officer who chairs it, a security or IT architecture representative, and one or two rotating business-domain reps. Everyone else presents an item — they don't hold a permanent vote.

## S3 · STEPS — What belongs on the agenda

The board should see significant ADRs, proposed operating model or platform changes, policy exceptions outside the standard, and roadmap changes that cross domains. It should never see routine data-quality fixes or individual access requests — those belong to existing steward roles, and sending them up just creates a bottleneck.

## S4 · STEPS — Decision rights and escalation

A board's authority has to be written down before the first disputed decision. At minimum: quorum, so a handful of people can't bind the whole program; decision rights, what it can approve versus only recommend; and an escalation path for when the board itself can't agree.
