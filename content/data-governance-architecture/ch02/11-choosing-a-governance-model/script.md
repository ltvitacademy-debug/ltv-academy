# Lesson 11 — Choosing a Governance Model · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

This lesson closes Chapter 2 by pulling together centralized, federated, decentralized, and data mesh into one decision framework.

## S2 · STEPS — Building on Foundations L12

Foundations Lesson 12 already gave the organizational version of this question — five questions about business units, cross-cutting data, maturity, sponsorship, and speed. This lesson adds the architectural layer: once you know roughly which model fits, which pattern can you actually build?

## S3 · STEPS — Five factors, part one

Three of the five factors: what the capability map already shows — fix the specific gap, don't rebuild what works. Which pattern the operating model already implies, as a starting point. And whether there's funded platform investment, because data mesh specifically needs a real self-serve platform before its governance can function.

## S4 · STEPS — Five factors, part two

Two more: how many genuinely distinct domains there are and how much of their data is cross-cutting. And whether data-product discipline is realistic to adopt now, or needs to be staged in first, regardless of domain count.

## S5 · CODE — The worked example

In a worked example: an insurance company where claims and underwriting share regulated data, but marketing is independent. Metadata is decent, lineage is weak, and there's no platform budget yet. The factors point to a federated rollout scoped to claims and underwriting — not a jump straight to mesh.

Chapter 3 picks up the capability this chapter leaned on throughout without building it out fully: metadata and catalog architecture itself, starting with how an enterprise metadata layer actually gets designed.
