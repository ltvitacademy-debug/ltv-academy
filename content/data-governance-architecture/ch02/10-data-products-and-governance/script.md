# Lesson 10 — Data Products and Governance · Voiceover script

Segments map 1:1 to slides. Target: ~2-3 minutes total.

## S1 · TITLE

Lesson 9 introduced data as a product as one of data mesh's four principles. This lesson goes deep on what that actually requires, architecturally.

## S2 · STEPS — Not just a table

A raw table isn't automatically a data product. A product has real consumers and has to earn that name — this lesson covers what that actually takes.

## S3 · STEPS — What earns the name

Four of the six characteristics: discoverable, so it shows up in a catalog. Addressable, with a stable way to access it. Trustworthy, with known, communicated quality. Self-describing, with its schema and meaning documented alongside it.

## S4 · STEPS — Two more, plus where governance attaches

Two more: interoperable, using shared conventions so it can join with other domains' data. And secure, with access control traveling with the product itself. All of this attaches at the product's output port, through a data contract.

## S5 · CODE — A data product, drawn out

Drawn out: however a domain builds a product internally is its own business. What matters for governance is the output port and its data contract — the schema, quality guarantee, classification, and ownership — which is all a catalog or policy engine actually needs to see.

Next lesson closes Chapter 2: choosing a governance model, pulling together everything from centralized through data mesh.
