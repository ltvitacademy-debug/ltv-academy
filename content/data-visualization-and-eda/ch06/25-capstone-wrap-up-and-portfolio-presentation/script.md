The analysis is done. Now we turn it into two deliverables: a short presentation for a decision-maker, and a project for your portfolio.

First, polish the key figure. The two-panel chart was for us. The audience needs one message, so we take tickets, the strongest finding, and apply Lesson 20: a title that states the finding, one highlighted bar, grey context, and a dashed baseline so every bar is judged against the overall rate.

Second, let code write the summary. On our seeded data, it prints three sentences. Of eight hundred customers, nineteen point six percent churned. Three or more tickets: forty-four point seven percent, versus eleven point three with none. First year: thirty point nine percent, versus ten point six after year three. Those sentences are the spine of the summary.

Third, structure the presentation with the four-part flow. Answer: customers with three or more tickets, and those in their first year, are most likely to leave. Evidence: the polished chart, and the tenure comparison. Caveat: these are associations in one dataset, tickets may be a symptom rather than a cause, and the East gap is weak evidence. Ask: a two-month pilot, with proactive outreach at the third ticket and a comparison group, so we can measure whether it works.

Fourth, package for a portfolio. A README opening with the question, the answer, and one image. A clean notebook that runs top to bottom. A data note, saying plainly if the data is generated. Seeds and versions. And the presentation. With a real dataset, check its license and remove personal information.
