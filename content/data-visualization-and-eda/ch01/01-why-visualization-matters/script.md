Welcome to Data Visualization and Exploratory Data Analysis, the fourth course on the Data Scientist path. You already know pandas and basic statistics. Now you'll learn to look at data, and to show it to other people.

Let's start with a famous warning. In nineteen seventy-three, the statistician Francis Anscombe built four small datasets, eleven points each. Compare their averages, their spread and their correlation, and they come out identical. An average y of seven point five. A standard deviation of two point oh three. A correlation of point eight two. On paper, it's the same data four times.

Now draw them. The first is a noisy straight line. The second is a smooth curve. The third is a perfect line with one outlier, and the fourth is a vertical stack of points with a single extreme value creating the whole correlation. Same numbers, four completely different stories. Summary statistics compress data, and compression loses shape. A chart puts the shape back.

Visualization does two different jobs. Exploratory charts are for you. They're fast and rough, made to find surprises, check assumptions and decide what to do next. Explanatory charts are for your audience. They're polished and focused on one clear message. Most of this course moves you from the first job to the second.

Here's the route. First, principles: choosing charts, using color and layout, and avoiding misleading visuals. Then matplotlib, seaborn and Plotly in Python.
