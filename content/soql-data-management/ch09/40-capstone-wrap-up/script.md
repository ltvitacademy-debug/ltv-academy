# Script — Capstone: Wrap-Up & Portfolio Presentation

## Segment 1 (title)

You've done the whole job on Summit Ridge Outfitters. This final lesson turns it into something you can show, and it closes out the SOQL and Salesforce Data Management course.

## Segment 2 (steps: results)

Start with the result. Leads went from forty-one thousand eight hundred sixty to thirty-eight thousand ninety. Duplicated email addresses, twenty-seven hundred sixty down to zero. Six Trade Show spellings down to one. Twelve hundred forty orphan Contacts down to zero. Sixty-one new Accounts. Every number was predicted before the load and verified by a query.

## Segment 3 (steps: skills used)

Each part rests on a chapter of this course. SOQL aggregates sized every problem. Data Loader and Workbench moved and explored the data. Data quality techniques found duplicates and standardized values. And migration discipline gave you the load order, the upsert, and the validation. The project didn't rely on cleverness. It relied on backups, predictions, a sandbox, and reading every result file.

## Segment 4 (steps: portfolio presentation)

Here's a five-part presentation for a hiring manager. The problem, with the counts. The approach, your five steps. Two or three key queries, each explained in a sentence. The safety design. And the result, with the before-and-after table. Because Summit Ridge is fictional, all of it is safe to show. With real employer work, strip out anything identifying first.

## Segment 5 (code: prevention)

End with prevention. Matching and duplicate rules flag new duplicate Leads. A restricted picklist stops new Lead Source spellings. A validation rule can require an Account on every Contact, where the business needs it. Fixing data once is temporary unless the org stops producing the mess.

## Segment 6 (outro)

That completes SOQL and Salesforce Data Management, the second course in the Salesforce Data Analyst path. You can query, move, clean, and migrate Salesforce data. The next course is Salesforce Reports and Dashboards, where you'll present that trustworthy data to the people who use it.
